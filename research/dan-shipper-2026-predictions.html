One agent per company, not one per person

03SaaS survives, CLIs don't

SaaS survives, CLIs don't

  • Shipper calls the 'SaaS apocalypse' narrative 'dumb': he'd buy SaaS stocks, arguing agents increase the number of users of SaaS products rather than replacing them, since users bring their own agent's tokens instead of the vendor paying for AI.
  • Separately, he predicts the CLI-first era (typified by early Claude Code) is 'over,' with GUI-based agent surfaces replacing terminal-based workflows even for most technical staff at Every.

04Automation requires more humans, not fewer

Automation requires more humans, not fewer

  • Despite being what he calls one of the most AI-forward startups, Every doubled headcount to about 30 people over the past year.
  • Shipper's framing: 'automation is a lie' in the sense that every automated process still needs a human maintaining it, and models make 'yesterday's human competence' cheap and commoditized rather than eliminating the need for people who can push past the default output.

05Benchmark progress vs. hiring decisions

Benchmark progress vs. hiring decisions

  • Shipper built his own 'senior engineer benchmark' by having two human engineers independently rewrite a broken vibe-coded app, then scoring new models against that rewrite.
  • Every model before GPT-5.5 scored around 30/100; GPT-5.5 scored 62/100 (or 60/100 using an Opus 4.7 plan), against a human senior engineer's 85-92/100.
  • He argues rising benchmark scores don't capture unscoreable judgment calls, which is part of why he still hires engineers even as scores climb.

Commentary (ours)

(ours) Shipper's evidence base is almost entirely Every's own internal usage and his own benchmark -- a real, detailed dataset, but a self-selected one from a company built around AI-forward workflows, so the generalization to companies without that culture (or without Shipper's hands-on tool access) is his extrapolation, not a measured result.
