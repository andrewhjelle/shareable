01AI reshaping the lab

AI reshaping the lab

  • Sinclair says his lab now relies on AI for drug design, screening billions to trillions of candidate molecules for anti-aging effects, and for rapidly classifying whether cells are old or young from images.
  • He also describes a collaboration with a group called Cadence that fed transcriptomic data into an AI system and surfaced a new way to assess biological age that the lab hadn't considered.

“We we can't survive without it in my lab and it's accelerating our work exponentially.”

“We fed it a lot of transcriptomic data and it actually made a discovery that we hadn't thought of about how to look at biological age with that data.”

02GLP-1's overlooked risk

GLP-1's overlooked risk

  • Sinclair says GLP-1 drugs show benefits to the heart and brain beyond weight loss, but names one downside besides the already-known risks: a rare, drug-linked blindness affecting roughly 20-30,000 people a year in the US.
  • He says this condition is one of the diseases his lab's ER100 therapy will be tested against for reversal.

“It's still rare. It's about 20 to 30,000 people in the US a year, which is still horrible. And that's one of the conditions that ER100 therapy will be tested to see if we can reverse blindness.”

said at 04:33
03How much of aging is genetic

How much of aging is genetic

  • Asked why longevity clinics underuse genetics, Sinclair cites a recent paper that challenges the long-standing estimate that only 10-15% of lifespan is genetically determined, putting the real figure closer to 50/50.
  • He concludes that science supports at least half of lifespan being within a person's control.

“at a minimum I can say science says at least half of your lifespan is up to you and the rest may be fairly determined.”

said at 10:10
04Levers Sinclair points to: mindset, plants, LDL

Levers Sinclair points to: mindset, plants, LDL

  • Sinclair cites a National Academy of Sciences study finding optimists live 15% longer than pessimists.
  • On atherosclerosis, he points to nattokinase and to evidence that sufficiently lowering LDL, especially alongside reduced inflammation, can produce some reversal effects.
  • He also traces his xenohormesis hypothesis, developed with Conrad Howitz roughly 15-20 years ago, back to how plant polyphenols like resveratrol, quercetin, and fisetin activate SIRT1.

“There was a National Academy of Sciences study done that looked at those who are optimist versus pessimist and optimist were living 15% longer than pessimist.”

05What to look for in a longevity investment

What to look for in a longevity investment

  • Asked what KPIs investors should track in longevity companies, Sinclair says the criteria are the same as for any biotech: team quality above all else, then cash on hand, valuation, and runway, since running out of money is the leading cause of biotech company failure.

“it it should be the same for all companies in biotechnology. Ah, one of the the non-KPIs is who's the team. Ah, the team counts for probably”
