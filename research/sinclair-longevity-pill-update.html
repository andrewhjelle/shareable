01From mice to a human eye trial

From mice to a human eye trial

  • Sinclair's lab published the OSK partial-reprogramming technique in 2020, and the same three-gene formula that reversed blindness in mice and monkeys is now going into human patients to treat blindness, with recruitment already underway.
  • He says the eye was chosen not because it was the easiest tissue but because his team wanted to start somewhere the FDA would allow; the liver is the likely next target.
  • A separate, cheaper small-molecule cocktail proof-of-concept is aimed at clinical trial within a couple of months as part of an X Prize competition.

“we have three of the genes. We call them OSK for short. They're going into the eye of a patient shortly to see if we can cure blindness”

said at 03:04

“those we hope to put into a clinical trial into humans uh within the next couple of months as part of our Xprize uh competition.”

said at 09:14
02No known ceiling, and a 2026 marker

No known ceiling, and a 2026 marker

  • Asked directly whether there's an upper limit to human lifespan, Sinclair says no law of biology sets one, while declining to claim humans can live forever.
  • He floats 2026 as a possible year in which age reversal gets demonstrated as possible in humans, framed as a guess rather than a fixed prediction.

“there is no law that says we have to age. That anyone who says that there is a limit doesn't know what they're talking about.”

said at 15:23

“It may be 2026 is the year we learn that age reversal is possible in humans.”

said at 12:49
03Cost path: from AAV gene therapy to a cheap pill

Cost path: from AAV gene therapy to a cheap pill

  • The AAV-delivered gene therapy going into trials now costs in the hundreds of thousands to low millions per treatment to manufacture at kilogram scale.
  • Sinclair frames small molecules as the route to bringing that down, eventually hoping for something priced closer to metformin.

“the three factor molecules, they're expensive to make. They're hundreds of thousands of dollars right now to make it in the kilograms scale.”

said at 10:16
04Sinclair's own protocol

Sinclair's own protocol

  • He describes a 15-year daily stack of resveratrol (taken with fat/protein for absorption) and NMN, plus a glucose-lowering drug he cycles between metformin and its natural analog berberine.
  • He also flags that even one drink a day now correlates with a smaller brain in the data he's seen, and says he's added nattokinase, which he says is the only supplement shown in large trials of 1,000+ people to reverse arterial plaque.

“before the data came in, which is now damning for alcohol, even one glass of alcohol a day uh correlates with a smaller brain.”

said at 29:43

“the the only thing that's been very clearly shown in large trials of thousand plus people to reverse plaque in the body”

said at 27:40
05Funding model: Friends of Sinclair Lab

Funding model: Friends of Sinclair Lab

  • After a federal funding cut of roughly $2-3M/year tied to a White House-Harvard dispute forced Sinclair to let go of graduate students, a donor community (Friends of Sinclair Lab) was formed and raised about $6M in private annual support within a few months, now funding specific projects within weeks of being proposed rather than the multi-year grant cycle.

“we were able over the course of the next few months to bring in I think on the order of six million dollars of private annual support.”

said at 19:32

Commentary (ours)

(ours) Sinclair's own timeline language is more hedged than the framing around it ("Wright brothers moment," "singularity") suggests -- the concrete, checkable claims are the eye-trial recruitment and the small-molecule trial timeline, not the lifespan-ceiling or 2026 predictions.
