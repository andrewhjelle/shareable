01The AI doom cycle framework

The AI doom cycle framework

  • Whittemore introduces a framework he calls the AI doom cycle, explicitly modeled on Gartner's technology hype cycle chart.
  • Where Gartner's chart describes how a technology diffuses through society, his version describes the emotional and cognitive states individuals move through in their relationship with AI: skepticism and disbelief, AI psychosis (peak conviction AI will change everything), doom desperation, real-world recalibration, and finally enlightened excitement.

02Doom desperation, from the inside

Doom desperation, from the inside

  • As his case study for the 'AI psychosis' turning into 'doom desperation,' he traces Citadel CEO Ken Griffin's public reversal: in January, at the World Economic Forum, Griffin dismissed AI as hype meant to justify infrastructure spending; by a recent Stanford Business School talk he said Citadel is seeing a 15-25% productivity boost and that finance work formerly done by people with master's and PhDs is now done by AI agents in hours.
  • Whittemore notes Griffin described this not with excitement but personal trepidation.
  • Separately, a Menlo Ventures partner's viral post (about 11 million views) described a stark wealth divide in the Bay Area tech scene as evidence of the same desperation.

03Real-world recalibration: layoffs and token pricing

Real-world recalibration: layoffs and token pricing

  • Whittemore points to two concurrent real-world developments as evidence the doom narratives are colliding with physical and economic constraints.
  • Meta is cutting roughly 10% of its staff, timed alongside continued AI infrastructure investment.
  • At the same time, AI providers -- Anthropic, GitHub, and others -- are moving enterprise customers off flat-rate subscriptions onto token-based usage billing, which he argues is a structural response to a compute shortage rather than a temporary pricing experiment.

04Enlightened excitement: a different narrative

Enlightened excitement: a different narrative

  • Whittemore frames the cycle's endpoint, enlightened excitement, as a state that makes more specific and productive conversations possible.
  • He cites Sam Altman's public shift away from doomsday messaging toward 'augment, not replace' language, and holds up Mark Cuban's proposed federal tax on AI tokens as the kind of concrete policy discussion this state enables, in contrast to what he calls the 'permanent underclass' discourse of the desperation stage.

Commentary (ours)

(ours) The five-stage framework is Whittemore's own synthesis of a week's news cycle rather than a tested model -- it works better as a lens for parsing AI discourse (why an exec's reaction or a viral post lands the way it does) than as a predictive tool, and 'enlightened excitement' is presented as a state he's advocating for, not a description of where most people currently sit; he says as much when he places most of the world still in the first two stages.
