  • Kurzweil reaffirms AGI by 2029 and the singularity (a millionfold capability increase) by 2045 -- the same dates he set in 1999, when a Stanford conference of AI experts guessed 100 years instead of his 30 (c3, c4)

The 2029 and 2045 predictions

  • Kurzweil says he predicted in 1999 that AGI would arrive by 2029 and that the singularity -- which he defines as a millionfold increase in capability -- would arrive by 2045.
  • He notes that after his book The Singularity Is Near came out, Stanford hosted a conference of several hundred AI experts who examined his prediction and concluded human-level AI was real but would take about 100 years, not the 30 years he forecast.

03What's still missing for AGI

What's still missing for AGI

  • Asked whether AGI needs a further breakthrough, Kurzweil says two things are still needed: genuine physics understanding (he cites a Google project he expects to take until 2029) and robotics, which he says lags language models and currently can't handle everyday variable tasks like post-dinner cleanup at any price point.
  • Separately, on the decades-old debate over whether a neural-net parameter maps onto a brain synapse, Kurzweil says the two land within an order of magnitude of exact parity in the resulting intelligence.

Commentary (ours)

(ours) Kurzweil's headline 86%-accuracy-across-147-predictions figure is self-graded by his own methodology (a prediction within a year or two counts as correct), so it's worth treating as a self-reported track record rather than an independently audited one.

His 2029/2045 dates have stayed fixed since 1999 despite very different AGI definitions circulating among other researchers in the interim, which reads either as unusual consistency or as evidence his own goalposts never moved.

The robotics-cost claim -- that no robot can handle general household cleanup at any price today -- is probably the single most checkable prediction in the conversation over the next few years.
