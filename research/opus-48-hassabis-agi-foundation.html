01Opus 4.8 reclaims the coding-benchmark lead

Opus 4.8 reclaims the coding-benchmark lead

  • Six weeks after Opus 4.7, Anthropic shipped Opus 4.8, which the hosts say now leads the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 61.4, 1.2 points ahead of GPT-5.5.
  • On SWE-Bench Pro it scored 69.2 versus 58.6 for GPT-5.5, and it is reported to be four times less likely than its predecessor to overlook bugs in its own code, completing every case end-to-end on Anthropic's internal super-agent benchmark.
  • Anthropic also previewed additional models coming in the next few weeks and shipped a Claude Code feature called dynamic workflows for running hundreds of parallel sub-agents on large codebases.

02Hassabis moves his AGI clock to 2029

Hassabis moves his AGI clock to 2029

  • Demis Hassabis has aligned his AGI timeline with Ray Kurzweil's long-standing 2029 projection, about three years out from this recording.
  • He is reported to have called today's AI agents "a practice run" and said society has only a few years left to prepare for what's coming.
  • He has also proposed an "Einstein test" for AGI: train a model on knowledge only through 1901 and see whether it can independently derive special relativity, something he says current systems can't do.
  • Co-host Alex pushed back on air, noting Gemini is not currently winning the model race and calling it self-serving for a lab that isn't in the lead to frame AGI as still three to four years away.

03OpenAI's nonprofit foundation becomes the largest in the world

OpenAI's nonprofit foundation becomes the largest in the world

  • Following OpenAI's restructuring into a public benefit corporation, the OpenAI Foundation retained a 26% ownership stake in the PBC.
  • The hosts value that stake at somewhere between $130 billion and $260 billion, which they say makes it the largest foundation in the world, ahead of Novo Nordisk ($150B), the Tata Trust (roughly $100B), and the Gates Foundation ($75B).
  • Its first grant, the 2025 People First AI Fund, distributed $40 million across roughly 28 nonprofits, before larger later grants aimed at public wealth funds, worker ownership models, and AI dividends as the labs prepare for possible job displacement.
