Did Modern Humans Help Create Neanderthals? – David Reich

Science

If you only read one thing

  • Reich floats that Neanderthals may be genetic cousins of Denisovans but cultural/toolkit cousins of modern humans
  • A ~5% interbreeding event 200-300,000 years ago fully replaced Neanderthal mitochondrial DNA and Y chromosomes -- a statistically odd pattern
  • His explanation is a wave-of-advance model: modern humans expanding into Europe were mostly genetically absorbed by local archaic populations while keeping their stone-tool technology
01A puzzle that started in 2010

A puzzle that started in 2010

  • The 2010 sequencing of the Denisovan genome showed that, on average, Denisovans are genetically closer to Neanderthals than to modern humans -- even though Neanderthals and modern humans look far more archaeologically similar to each other than either does to Denisovans.
  • That mismatch is the starting puzzle for Reich's talk.

02The 5% that shouldn't work

The 5% that shouldn't work

  • Standard estimates put modern-human-to-Neanderthal gene flow at about 5% of the genome, from an interbreeding event roughly 200-300,000 years ago.
  • But that same small fraction appears to have fully replaced Neanderthal mitochondrial DNA (maternal line) and Y chromosome (paternal line) -- a scenario Reich says is statistically unlikely to occur by chance from only a 5% admixture.

03A wave-of-advance replacement model

A wave-of-advance replacement model

  • Reich's working explanation: a population carrying the new Middle Stone Age toolmaking tradition expanded out of a source region into both Europe and Africa.
  • In Europe, that expanding population interbred with local archaic humans and was mostly genetically swamped by them (roughly 95% replaced) while keeping its mitochondrial DNA, Y chromosome, and cultural technology.
  • Reich also proposes modern humans themselves are the product of an earlier admixture between two divergent lineages.

Cousins by culture, not just by genome
