01Anthropic drops its no-train-without-safety pledge

Anthropic drops its no-train-without-safety pledge

  • The panel opens on Anthropic revising its 2023 responsible scaling policy, which had pledged not to train advanced AI unless safety was guaranteed.
  • Alex Wissner-Gross argues the safety-from-a-single-heroic-lab premise was always flawed, and that safety, to the extent it emerges, will come from competition and a balance of power between labs rather than unilateral restraint.
  • The replacement standard, as described on the pod, is simply to be as good or better than competitors.

02Amazon's contingent $35 billion into OpenAI

Amazon's contingent $35 billion into OpenAI

  • Amazon's offer is structured as contingent on OpenAI both going public and achieving AGI, and the panel notes OpenAI's earlier deal with Microsoft reportedly defined AGI as generating $100 billion in earnings or revenue rather than a capability threshold.
  • Public details of the Amazon announcement include a requirement that OpenAI train using Amazon's Trainium or Trainium 2 chips.

Commentary (ours)

(ours) The through-line the panel doesn't quite name is that both halves of the episode, Anthropic's safety retreat and the Amazon/OpenAI AGI clause, turn 'AGI' and 'safety' into contract language (a dollar figure, a competitive-parity bar) rather than capability thresholds, which is a different kind of claim than the one usually implied by the term.
