01SpaceX files the largest IPO in history

SpaceX files the largest IPO in history

  • SpaceX filed for what the hosts describe as the largest IPO ever, raising $75 billion at a valuation north of $1.75 trillion -- more than 2.5 times Saudi Aramco's.
  • Elon Musk is preserving super-voting rights, with insiders controlling 86% of the vote, giving him a publicly traded entity he actually controls (unlike Tesla).
  • The prospectus also discloses that Anthropic is now paying SpaceX $15 billion per year for data center access across both Colossus 1 and Colossus 2, which the hosts read as SpaceX pivoting toward owning compute infrastructure rather than its own frontier model.

Two AI research milestones: forecasting and an 80-year-old math problem

  • OpenAI's GPT-5.5 is leading all frontier models at 25% accuracy on the Future Sim forecasting benchmark and beat Polymarket's crowd prediction for the Super Bowl.
  • Separately, an unreleased internal OpenAI model disproved a roughly 80-year-old conjecture posed by mathematician Paul Erdos about the maximum number of unit-distance point pairs in a plane, which Wissner-Gross calls the first mathematical breakthrough due to an AI on a genuinely well-known open problem rather than a brute-forceable one.

04The AI backlash: campuses, workplaces, and a proposed tax

The AI backlash: campuses, workplaces, and a proposed tax

  • Graduating students booed Eric Schmidt for mentioning AI at a University of Arizona commencement, which the hosts frame as evidence the generation entering the workforce is angry and scared about AI disrupting their careers.
  • A Stanford survey found 49% of 849 computer science majors would rather cheat than fail, prompting Stanford to reinstate proctored in-person exams.
  • Meta installed software on employee computers to track mouse movements, clicks, and screen activity for AI training purposes, triggering employee protests the same week Meta cut 10% of its global workforce.
  • And Mark Cuban proposed a federal tax on AI tokens at the provider level, arguing it would push labs to optimize tokenization and generate new revenue.

Commentary (ours)

Our read: the SpaceX prospectus detail that Anthropic is now paying for both Colossus 1 and Colossus 2 compute is the most concrete data point in this episode -- if that reading holds, it suggests SpaceX is positioning itself as an infrastructure and energy play (Dyson swarm ambitions) rather than a model developer, ceding the model layer to Anthropic.

The Erdos-conjecture claim is the strongest research signal here, though it rests on an unreleased internal model and secondhand mathematician commentary rather than a public paper, so the result hasn't been independently verified.

What's least resolved: the token-tax discussion undercuts its own premise on air, with Wissner-Gross and Blundin noting tokens aren't a stable taxable unit since labs can swap tokenization schemes -- which reads more like an example of an unserious policy proposal than a considered forecast.
