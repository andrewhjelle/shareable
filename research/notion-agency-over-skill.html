AI era skills: Why cultivating agency matters more than job titles | Max Schoening (Notion)

SpeakerMax Schoening · Notion Watchyoutu.be/mCO-D3pkviM · 87 min SourceRecording of this talk at AI Engineer (2026), published on YouTube by the conference. Quotes are verbatim from the talk transcript with the time they were said.

If you only read one thing

  • Notion's head of product Max Schoening says agency, not raw skill, is now the differentiator, and he'd rather hire a PM who understands agent loops than one who can only tweak a traditional UI (c1, c2)
  • He calls the SaaS apocalypse overstated even as the first 10% of any project becomes free to build (c4, c5)
  • He flags that fast-inference frontier models can already run $3,000/day in token spend, with a real ROI reckoning likely in 6-12 months, and offers a hot take that knowledge work already functions as universal basic income (c6, c7, c10)
01Agency over skill, agent-loop fluency over syntax

Agency over skill, agent-loop fluency over syntax

  • Schoening argues that as AI closes skill gaps, agency (understanding that the world around you is malleable and acting on it) becomes the real differentiator between people who thrive and those who don't.
  • Concretely, he says he'd rather staff a designer or PM who deeply understands how agent loops work than one who can write traditional, hand-tweaked UI code.

“I would much rather take the designer or PM that deeply has an affinity for understanding how agent loops work and can design those than someone who can sort of write traditional software”

said at 09:10

“the thing that matters is agency and I don't think agency is very evenly distributed in the world and uh I think people who have true agency and they understand that the world around them is malleable will do great”

said at 11:11
02Malleable software

Malleable software

  • Schoening defines his long-running interest, malleable software, as software that serves the interests of the people using it rather than the corporation that built it.

“Malleable software is the idea that software works closer to the interest of the people that use it”

said at 17:54
03The SaaS apocalypse is exaggerated, but the first 10% is free

The SaaS apocalypse is exaggerated, but the first 10% is free

  • Schoening pushes back on predictions that AI will let companies rebuild all their own SaaS tools, arguing most people don't want to maintain a full software stack.
  • At the same time, he says the first 10% of any project, the janky first version, now takes almost no effort to build.

“I think to some degree the SAS apocalypse is greatly exaggerated. At the same time, are things going to stay the same? Of course not.”

said at 26:36

“I think the first 10% of every project are now free. That's how I would describe it.”

said at 28:40
04Token spend: cheap enough to ignore, for now

Token spend: cheap enough to ignore, for now

  • Schoening notes that fast-inference frontier models (like an accelerated Opus variant) can run up a bill of roughly $3,000/day for an individual, and Notion currently treats token spend as effectively unlimited because it's the wrong thing to optimize for yet.
  • He predicts that within 6-12 months, companies broadly will start asking uncomfortable ROI questions about that spend.

“you very quickly run up a bill of like $3,000 a day. Uh but um the speed of inference really changes things.”

said at 31:44

“I do suspect in six to 12 months from now a lot of companies are going to actually start asking questions around RARI and I think that will be an uncomfortable conversation for for a lot of folks”

said at 34:46
05Coding's exponential lead, taste, and a UBI hot take

Coding's exponential lead, taste, and a UBI hot take

  • Schoening says AI models are improving at coding at an exponential rate that isn't slowing, while he's unimpressed by progress in other domains, arguing much of what looks like non-coding progress is really coding principles applied elsewhere.
  • Separately, he says designers with genuinely high taste tend to be the ones running full side projects end-to-end, and offers a closing hot take: knowledge work already functions as universal basic income.

“models are getting better at coding at some exponential rate right and I don't think that's changing now. I'm not that impressed with the progress in the any other domain.”

said at 42:27

“the designers that I think have at least in software design high taste are the ones that both have side projects that they build where they're responsible of the full thing in end”

said at 59:23

“my my take is that we already have universal basic income. It's called knowledge work. Uh and I don't exclude my job from it.”

said at 67:36

Commentary (ours)

(ours) Schoening's 'unlimited for now, ROI conversation in 6-12 months' framing on token spend is a useful counterpoint to run against Nate B Jones's token-economy talk in this same corpus: one treats token budgets as a near-term management discipline to build now, the other as a discipline companies haven't needed to build yet because the bill hasn't come due.
