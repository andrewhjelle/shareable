01The case for a free, secular Iran

The case for a free, secular Iran

  • Pahlavi describes Iran as one of the most untapped economic opportunities of the century, estimating roughly $1 trillion in benefit to the US market over the first 10 years of an open Iran, on top of billions in direct investment.
  • He names separation of religion from state as the second of several prerequisites for democracy, alongside territorial integrity, equality under the law, and an elected constitutional process.
  • Asked what form of government should follow, he points to parliamentary monarchies like Japan, Sweden, and Spain as existing models, alongside republics like the United States, France, or India, saying the choice belongs to the Iranian people.

02"My focus is on the process, not the outcome"

"My focus is on the process, not the outcome"

  • Pressed on whether he wants to become president or restore a monarchy, Pahlavi says he sees his role as a neutral transitional arbiter, not an eventual officeholder, whose function is to bridge Iran to a system chosen through free elections.

“My focus is on the process, not the outcome. And in as a neutral arbiter, I'm not taking sides with one form versus the other.”

The military campaign's toll and Trump's ultimatum

04Conditions for a stable transition

Conditions for a stable transition

  • Pishevar frames territorial integrity as a hard red line for both Pahlavi and the Iranian public, warning that any separatist movement would cost the campaign its broad public support.
  • He says a secure system for military and civilian contacts to reach Pahlavi's team has drawn responses from more than 50,000 people in the military, which he presents as the groundwork for defections as the regime's leadership is dismantled.
