01Is Anthropic building a god, or just a very good safety pitch?

Is Anthropic building a god, or just a very good safety pitch?

  • Discussing Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah's 'Constitution' document, chief philosopher Amanda Askell's podcasts, and Dario Amodei's essay 'Machines of Loving Grace,' Bill Gurley says the people building these systems aren't just writing software, they're midwifing something god-like.
  • Chamath responds that this is not a compliment: he calls it the belief that a small group of people can construct a perfectly benevolent superhuman god, and names that belief the ultimate form of narcissism and delusion of grandeur.
  • Chamath separately lays out a game-theoretic reading of why labs would push this framing: get a handful of entities in a room, dominate the others, and set rules an outside regulator can't fully evaluate.

02The Pope's AI letter, and Sacks's counter-warning about government power

The Pope's AI letter, and Sacks's counter-warning about government power

  • The hosts discuss Pope Leo's document 'Magnificate Humanitas,' which calls for AI regulation and was joined by Anthropic's Chris Olah despite Olah's not being Catholic.
  • Sacks says he agrees with the Pope's central worry, that AI could be used to centralize and misuse power, but locates the primary threat in government rather than individual actors.
  • He argues that creating an FDA-style approval body for AI models would let regulators give notes to developers, and that the definition of 'safety' would expand the way 'trust and safety' did during the social media era.

03Open source in the crosshairs

Open source in the crosshairs

  • Sacks argues the Washington 'regulatory capture agenda' is ultimately headed toward banning open source or open-weight models, and that the predicate case is already being built: he cites Anthropic's own blog posts repeatedly framing open models as dangerous because their guardrails can be stripped out.
  • He notes a US ban wouldn't stop the technology, just shift the infrastructure and the rest of the world's usage elsewhere, likely toward Chinese models.

04The jobs narrative flips, then gets contested

The jobs narrative flips, then gets contested

  • Jason relays Goldman Sachs CEO David Solomon's New York Times op-ed, which argues AI will automate roughly 25% of work hours rather than eliminate 25% of jobs, with workers absorbed into higher-level tasks.
  • Sacks says this, plus Sam Altman and Dario Amodei walking back their job-apocalypse predictions, vindicates his January prediction that AI produces net job gains, not losses, and cites Yale Budget Lab data showing no discernible labor-market disruption and rising software job postings.
  • Chamath disputes this directly, pointing to Amazon's stated plan to eliminate 600,000 future positions and telling Sacks the people losing cab-driving, trucking, and package-sorting jobs are real, not anecdotes to be waved away.
