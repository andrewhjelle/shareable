When to Automate, Build, Buy, Hire, or Wait on AI

SpeakerNate B Jones · Nate B Jones Watchyoutu.be/LIkYVsxMpS8 · 27 min SourceRecording of this talk at AI Engineer (2026), published on YouTube by the conference. Quotes are verbatim from the talk transcript with the time they were said.
Business & strategyAI industryStartups & venture

If you only read one thing

  • Gartner projects over 40% of agentic AI projects will be killed by 2027, driven by cost, unclear business value, and weak risk controls -- not the underlying tech
  • Jones's core rule: 'do not automate what you cannot describe' -- most failed AI investments start from vague, bundled workflow definitions
  • A specificity/market-maturity grid decides between automate, build, buy, and hire; waiting is a legitimate fifth option when a workflow isn't a priority yet

02AI investment is a workflow question, not a model question

AI investment is a workflow question, not a model question

  • His central reframe: which model to use, which vendor to pick, and what dashboard to build are all downstream of understanding the actual shape of the work -- its inputs, exceptions, and ownership.
  • He warns teams not to be 'religious' about automating everything; automation only makes sense where routine cases dominate and exceptions are well understood.

03Buy: know if the work is 'shaped' like the vendor's product

Buy: know if the work is 'shaped' like the vendor's product

  • Jones warns that vendor demos showcase the routine case, but production traffic is often mostly exceptions, so buyers end up disappointed by low accuracy on work they never actually diagnosed.
  • For an all-in-one vendor solution (his example: Harvey for legal), the key question is whether your own workflow is shaped enough like the vendor's to get an 80-90% fit.
