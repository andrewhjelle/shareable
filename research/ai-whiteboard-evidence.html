02Why a polished artifact no longer proves skill

Why a polished artifact no longer proves skill

  • Jones argues the deeper issue is that AI makes more people look productive, so the same kinds of evidence, a clean memo, a working prototype, a sharp-sounding resume, an organized project plan, no longer show whether the person understood the situation well enough to make a good decision.
  • He frames this as an evidence problem rather than a resume problem: production quality used to correlate with expertise because production itself was hard, and AI breaks that link.

“productive, and the old evidence does not carry the same signal”

03The whiteboard test for judgment

The whiteboard test for judgment

  • Jones's proposed fix is what he calls 'the age of whiteboards': put a person in front of a real problem with someone strong enough to push back, and watch how they reason live, since that reasoning is harder to fake than a finished artifact.
  • He says the standard advice to build a portfolio is incomplete because a portfolio only proves you can ship, which is exactly the part AI already makes easier.
  • He structures the whiteboard conversation around four things a person should be able to show: the situation, the decision made and the options rejected, the risk taken or avoided, and the change that resulted.

04Talent board and starting a new role

Talent board and starting a new role

  • Jones frames his 'talent board' project around comprehension over generation, turning a whiteboard conversation into a work sample, promotion note, or hiring packet that preserves evidence of the thinking, not just the credential.
  • For people starting a new job, he recommends asking for a whiteboard session with someone who understands the domain deeply, presenting an early point of view, and letting that person push back on it.

Commentary (ours)

Our read: the diagnosis (AI erodes artifact-based signal) rests on Jones's own extrapolation from two Microsoft usage stats, not on any study of hiring outcomes, so it's plausible but unverified at that scale. Jones is also promoting his own 'talent board' product here, which doesn't invalidate the argument but means the specific prescription is less independently tested than the underlying observation.

The four-part frame, situation, decision, risk, change, reads as a reusable structured-interview or self-review rubric on its own, separable from the platform he's pitching.
