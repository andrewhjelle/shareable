        The timeline

          Peri-peri chicken

          Marinate (min 2 hr, ideal 8–24 hr)

          1. Pat 4–6 boneless skinless chicken thighs fully dry (bone-in works too, ~5 min longer to cook).
          2. Salt both sides (~1 tsp kosher salt per lb). Rest 15 min. Bone-in with skin: score the skin 3 shallow slashes per thigh first.
          3. Coat in ¼–⅓ cup peri-peri sauce per lb (mix medium + hot for adult heat). Zip-top bag, air out, seal.
          4. Reserve a separate clean bowl of sauce for basting — never re-dip a brush into raw-marinade sauce.
          5. Refrigerate. Overnight is best; 2 hr floor. Past 24 hr, acid starts to break down the texture.

          Cook day

          1. Pull chicken from fridge 20 min before cook. Pat dry, keep the marinade cling.

          Grill (boneless skinless thighs)

          1. Two zones on the grill (bone-in uses the indirect side; boneless stays on direct). Grate clean; oil rag on tongs.
          2. Rounded side down over direct, 4–5 min. Don’t chase char — the basted sauce paints it later.
          3. Flip. Stay direct for boneless: 3–5 min more until thickest part reads 170–175°F. Brush with reserved sauce the last minute for lacquer.
          4. (Bone-in with skin: after the flip, move to indirect + close lid for 8–12 min instead; baste the last 2 min per side; final 60 sec direct for lacquer.)
          5. Rest 5 min tented under foil. Second bowl of hot sauce at the table for adults.

          Nando’s-style garlic mash

          1.5 lb Yukon golds · 5–6 garlic cloves (jarred minced works) · 4 tbsp butter · ½ cup whole milk (or heavy cream) · salt · white pepper · small pinch smoked paprika
          1. Peel + cut potatoes into 1.5-inch chunks.
          2. Cover with cold salted water in a pot (1 tsp salt per quart). Bring to boil, then simmer 15–18 min until a paring knife slides in with zero resistance.
          3. While they cook: finely mince or grate the garlic.
          4. Small saucepan, low heat: melt 4 tbsp butter with the garlic. 2–3 min — fragrant, not brown.
          5. Pour in ½ cup milk or cream. Warm through, keep warm.
          6. Drain potatoes. Return to the empty hot pot for 30 sec shaking — dries them, makes silkier mash.
          7. Push potatoes through a ricer back into the pot (ricer = the Nando’s smooth texture; a masher + hand whisk works too).
          8. Fold in the warm garlic butter + cream in three additions. Don’t whip — overworked mash goes gluey.
          9. Salt to taste (start 1 tsp), a pinch of white pepper, small pinch smoked paprika for the Nando’s warm undertone.
          10. Serve straight away. A little melted butter on the plate if showing off.

          Kid mild

          Pull the kids’ portions from the mildest thighs (skin side, less baste) before the final hot-sauce basting round. Serve their sauce on the side, not brushed on. Mash is mild by default.
