Char siu pork tenderloin

Cantonese BBQ pork, tenderloin sliced lengthwise into strips · ~20 min active cook once marinated · feeds 4 with rice · kid-mild, sweet-savory

TIMING TRIAGE · PICK A PATH

How much time do you have?

Char siu is more forgiving than most marinades because the sticky glaze does the flavor work — not the soak. Pick your path:

  • 8+ hours out: Full overnight marinade. Best result. Marinate now, roast at dinner.
  • 3–6 hours out: Cut the tenderloin lengthwise into 2 strips, fork-poke aggressively, then marinate. Compensate with extra glaze coats while roasting.
  • Under 2 hours: Skip the soak. Glaze-on-the-fly method — sear it, then paint the marinade on as glaze every 4–5 min while it roasts. Total time = 25 min. 80% of the flavor.

            Full recipe

            Marinade (do first, then move to cook)

            1. Whisk together: 3 tbsp hoisin, 2 tbsp light soy, 1 tbsp dark soy, 2 tbsp Shaoxing wine (or dry sherry), 2 tbsp brown sugar, 2 tbsp honey, ½ tsp Chinese five-spice, 2 tsp minced garlic, 1 tbsp grated ginger, ¼ tsp white pepper.
            2. Reserve ¼ cup of the marinade in a small bowl for glazing later — DON’T dip a brush into raw-meat marinade.
            3. Slice the tenderloin lengthwise into 2 long strips (2-3" thick loin halves to ~1-1.5" thick strips). Faster marinade penetration + faster cook + more surface area for lacquer.
            4. Add pork strips (1–1.25 lb total) to a zip-top bag with the rest of the marinade. Massage, seal, refrigerate.
            5. Marinate: overnight best, minimum 2 hr. Strips take marinade faster than a whole loin, so 2-3 hr is genuinely fine.

            Roast

            1. Preheat oven to 425°F. Line a sheet pan with foil, set a wire rack on top. Add 1 cup water to the pan (catches drippings, prevents burn).
            2. Pull strips from marinade, let excess drip off. Lay on the rack, not touching each other.
            3. Roast 10 min. Pull, brush both sides with the reserved ¼ cup glaze (add 1 tbsp honey to it first for extra stick).
            4. Roast another 6 min. Brush again.
            5. Broiler on HIGH: 2 min for lacquer char. Watch it — goes from sticky to burnt in 30 seconds.
            6. Pull at internal 140°F (usually right after the broil). Rest 5 min — strips carry over fast.
            7. Internal temp target: 140–145°F. Rest 5 min tented in foil (carries to 145–150°F).
            8. Slice thin against the grain, ~¼ inch pieces. Serve over rice with steamed bok choy or cucumber salad.

            Glaze-on-the-fly variant (no marinade time)

            Salt the tenderloin, sear all sides in a screaming-hot cast iron pan (~5 min total). Move to a 400°F oven on a foil-lined pan. Paint the char-siu mixture on every 4–5 min, flipping once. Pull at 140°F internal, rest, slice.

            Kid-mild note

            Char siu is naturally kid-friendly — five-spice is warm but not spicy, and the sticky-sweet glaze is the crave-back-for-it factor. No adjustments needed for Kai/Zada.
