Film2026–27 · one a month, together · streaming unchecked
  1. 1
    The Wizard of Oz
    Sep · 1939 · G
    Musical fantasy · Both kids, no risk. If they’ve seen it, swap in Mary Poppins. Notice: the film starts in sepia and turns to color the second Dorothy opens the door. Ask why.
  2. 2
    The Nightmare Before Christmas
    Oct · 1993 · PG
    Stop-motion musical · Zada’s Wednesday lane; Kai gets the craft. Pairs with Edward Scissorhands in the queue. Notice: 24 frames a second, every one a hand-moved puppet. Three years to shoot. Count the mouth shapes.
  3. 3
    Fantasia
    Nov · 1940 · G
    Animation + orchestra · The film-and-music bridge. Skip the segments that drag (they will); Sorcerer’s Apprentice, Night on Bald Mountain, the dinosaurs are the keepers. Notice: you already heard these at breakfast in October and November. Which picture did you imagine before you saw Disney’s?
  4. 4
    It’s a Wonderful Life
    Dec · 1946 · PG
    Christmas drama · The ancestor of Groundhog Day, which they liked: one man shown a world without him. Notice: it flopped in 1946 and became a classic because TV showed it for free every December. Ask what else got a second life that way.
  5. 5
    The Hidden Fortress
    Jan · 1958 · subtitles
    Kurosawa adventure · Kai lead. Star Wars is built on this: two bickering peasants (R2 and 3PO), a princess in disguise, a general, a fortress. Notice: the screen wipes between scenes. Lucas copied those too. Zada can bail at the hour mark without guilt.
  6. 6
    Singin’ in the Rain
    Feb · 1952 · G
    Musical comedy · Andrew’s pick from the queue, moved to a month with a reason. Notice: it’s a movie about movies, set in the year films learned to talk (1927). The bad-microphone scene is a real problem studios had.
  7. 7
    E.T.
    Mar · 1982 · PG
    Spielberg · They’ve seen four Spielbergs (Raiders, Doom, Crusade, Jurassic Park). This is the gentle one. Notice: the camera stays at kid height and you barely see an adult face until the last third. That’s a choice.
  8. 8
    Modern Times
    Apr · 1936 · G
    Chaplin comedy · Zada liked Sherlock Jr.; this is the next silent, and it’s short. Factory, feeding machine, roller skates. Notice: sound films had existed for nine years. Chaplin made a silent anyway, and the only voices you hear come out of machines. Ask why.
  9. 9
    The Sting
    May · 1973 · PG
    Con-man caper · National Treasure was a hit; this is the grown-up puzzle version. Both kids can follow it. Notice: title cards split it into chapters like a book, and the piano is Scott Joplin from 1902 (June breakfast music). You’re conned too. Don’t warn them.
  10. 10
    Jaws
    Jun · 1975 · PG
    Summer thriller · Kai first; Zada optional (it’s scarier than Jurassic Park because you can’t see it). Notice: the mechanical shark kept breaking, so Spielberg hid it and used two notes of music instead. The limitation made the film.
  11. 11
    Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse
    Jul · 2018 · PG
    Animation · Modern, both kids, and a real piece of film education. Notice: the animation runs at 12 frames a second on purpose, and the shading is printed halftone dots, the same trick as Seurat’s painting you saw in September.
  12. 12
    Free Solo
    Aug · 2018 · PG-13
    Documentary · You climb every week. This is the climbing film, and the language is the only reason for the rating. Notice: the crew argues on camera about whether filming him makes a fall more likely. That’s the ethics question every documentary has, said out loud.
Kai extension · PG-13 and older films, his pace
  1. ·
    The Empire Strikes Back
    Kai · 1980 · PG
    Star Wars · They liked A New Hope. This is the one that’s better. Notice: the heroes lose. A sequel that ends on a defeat is rare; ask whether it works.
  2. ·
    Rear Window
    Kai · 1954 · PG
    Hitchcock · Before North by Northwest in the classics card. One apartment, one window, the whole film. Notice: you only ever see what Jimmy Stewart can see from his chair. Every shot obeys that rule.
  3. ·
    King Kong
    Kai · 1933 · unrated
    Monster adventure · The grandfather of Jurassic Park, which he liked. Notice: Kong is an 18-inch model moved one frame at a time. The same technique as Nightmare Before Christmas, 60 years earlier.
  4. ·
    The Adventures of Robin Hood
    Kai · 1938 · PG
    Swashbuckler · Indiana Jones’s grandfather. Errol Flynn, sword fights on staircases, the castle. Notice: three-strip Technicolor, the most expensive color process ever. Every green is greener than real life.
  5. ·
    Superman
    Kai · 1978 · PG
    Superhero origin · Iron Man and Ragnarok landed. This is what those are quoting, slow first hour and all. Notice: no computers. Wires, front-projection, a man on a crane. Ask how they made him fly, then look it up.
  6. ·
    A Hard Day’s Night
    Kai · 1964 · G
    Beatles comedy · Music education disguised as a comedy. 87 minutes. Notice: the handheld, jump-cut style was invented here and every music video since copies it.
Zada extension · her lead
  1. ·
    Paddington 2
    Zada · 2017 · PG
    Comedy · A modern classic. Hugh Grant as the villain. Both kids, but she leads. Notice: the pop-up-book sequence; a whole scene made of paper. Kindness is the plot engine, not a lesson tacked on.
  2. ·
    Kiki’s Delivery Service
    Zada · 1989 · G
    Ghibli · After Totoro and Castle in the Sky. A 13-year-old’s first job; there is no villain. Notice: nothing bad happens and it’s still a story. Ask what Kiki is actually afraid of.
  3. ·
    The Red Balloon
    Zada · 1956 · 34 min
    Short film, no dialogue · Paris, a boy, a balloon that follows him. One sitting. Notice: no words at all, and you understand everything. Ask her how she knew what the balloon wanted.
  4. ·
    Safety Last!
    Zada · 1923 · silent
    Harold Lloyd comedy · She liked Keaton. Lloyd is the third silent clown; this is the one with the clock. Notice: the building climb is real (a set built on a roof, but a real drop). Compare Lloyd’s glasses-guy to Keaton’s stone face.
  5. ·
    Babe
    Zada · 1995 · G
    Talking-animal farm story · Chicken Run fans. Real animals, not cartoons. Notice: real pigs plus animatronic pigs, cut together so you can’t tell. 48 piglets played Babe because they kept growing.
  6. ·
    Mary Poppins
    Zada · 1964 · G
    Musical · The Oz backup, and the music-month tie-in. Notice: live actors dancing with drawn penguins in 1964. The same problem Roger Rabbit solved 24 years later.
  7. ·
    Matilda
    Zada · 1996 · PG · gated
    Dahl adaptation · After she reads (or you read her) the book. Same rule as Coraline for Kai. Notice: what the movie cut from the book, and whether the Trunchbull is scarier on the page or on screen.
Books2026–27 · the reading year · state lives on the Books page
Kai · 6th grade · real books, one a month, after the Riordan run
  1. 1
    D’Aulaires’ Book of Greek Myths
    D’Aulaire · 1962 · illustrated
    Myth collection · He just finished seven Percy Jacksons. This is the source. Notice: where Riordan changed the story. Then find the same heroes on the Greek vases at the Art Institute in January.
  2. 2
    The Odyssey (Gillian Cross retelling)
    Cross / Packer · 2012
    Epic, retold · The real thing, at his level, with Neil Packer’s drawings. Emily Wilson’s translation is the next rung in a few years. Notice: it starts in the middle and tells the early part as a flashback. Percy Jackson does the same thing.
  3. 3
    The Hobbit
    Tolkien · 1937
    Fantasy quest · Dad’s five-star. Read aloud, or the Andy Serkis audiobook on the drive to climbing. Notice: it was told out loud to Tolkien’s kids before it was written. Listen for the narrator talking to you.
  4. 4
    Treasure Island
    Stevenson · 1883
    Adventure · Every pirate thing he’s ever seen comes from this book. Parrots, maps with an X, one-legged men. Notice: Long John Silver is the villain and you like him anyway. That was new in 1883.
  5. 5
    The Westing Game
    Raskin · 1978
    Puzzle mystery · Sixteen heirs, one will, one murder. The book National Treasure fans deserve. Notice: keep a suspect list as you go. The clues are fair; you can solve it.
  6. 6
    Holes
    Sachar · 1998
    Novel, three timelines · Already on the movie queue; book first. Notice: three stories from three centuries. Mark the page where they snap together.
  7. 7
    Hatchet
    Paulsen · 1987
    Survival · Already top of his queue. One boy, one hatchet, a Canadian lake. Notice: almost no dialogue. Short sentences on purpose. The whole book is inside his head.
  8. 8
    A Wrinkle in Time
    L’Engle · 1962
    Science fantasy · A girl hero in 1962, a tesseract, a planet where everyone bounces the ball at the same time. Notice: the scariest thing in the book is sameness, not a monster. Ask what IT actually wants.
  9. 9
    The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes (five stories)
    Doyle · 1892
    Detective stories · Enola Holmes landed. Pick five: Speckled Band, Red-Headed League, Blue Carbuncle, Scandal in Bohemia, Silver Blaze. Notice: Watson tells it, not Holmes. Ask why the smartest character never narrates.
  10. 10
    Watership Down
    Adams · 1972
    Epic, with rabbits · A long one, for when he wants a big book after the Riordan run. Notice: the rabbits have their own language and their own myths. It’s the Odyssey shape again: leave home, find home.
  11. 11
    Ender’s Game
    Card · 1985
    Sci-fi · Dad’s five-star; he’s the right age now. Skip the sequels (Dad rated those 1 and 2). Notice: who is using whom. Reread the last chapter after the twist.
  12. 12
    Bone (complete edition)
    Jeff Smith · 1991–2004 · graphic
    Graphic novel epic · One 1,300-page graphic novel he’ll finish in a week. Counts as a real book. Notice: cartoon characters drawn into a realistic world. Ask why the Bones look different from everyone else.

    Win lane · In her queue. One sitting. Notice: the pictures tell the joke, the words set it up. She’s reading both.

    Early chapter book · A pig who loves buttered toast. Six books, all short, all funny. Not in her queue yet. Notice: a picture on every page, chapters two pages long. It’s a chapter book that doesn’t feel like one.
  4. 4

    Hale · 2014
    Early chapter book · In her queue. Secret identity. Notice: why does she hide it? The oldest superhero question, at 90 pages.
  5. 5

    Dahl · 1970
    Dahl · After The Magic Finger. Then the movie as the reward. Notice: a plan that fails, then a better plan. Compare to the film afterward; they’re different stories.
  6. 6
    George’s Marvellous Medicine
    Dahl · 1981
    Dahl · Funny-gross. The grandma. The one reluctant readers pick. Notice: Dahl lists the ingredients for a whole chapter and it’s still funny. Lists can be stories.
  7. 7
    Where the Sidewalk Ends
    Silverstein · 1974 · poems
    Poems · One poem a night. Most are under a page; she reads the short ones, you read the long ones. Notice: rhyme tells you what word is coming. Let her guess the last word of each line.
  8. 8
    Ramona Quimby, Age 8
    Cleary · 1981
    Realistic, funny · She’ll be 8 this year and in third grade, same as Ramona. The egg in the hair. Notice: nothing magic happens and it’s still a book. Ask if Ramona’s school feels like hers.
  9. 9
    My Father’s Dragon
    Gannett · 1948 · 80 pp
    Early classic · A map in the front, ten short chapters, a talking cat. The first chapter book for three generations of kids. Notice: the map. Trace the route as you read. Maps are a way of reading too.
  10. 10
    Pippi Longstocking
    Lindgren · 1945
    Read-aloud · Strongest girl in the world, lives alone with a horse and a monkey. Each chapter stands alone, so stopping is easy. Notice: the grown-ups are always wrong. Same trick as Dahl, 20 years earlier, from Sweden.
  11. 11
    Charlotte’s Web
    E.B. White · 1952
    Read-aloud, together · You read it to her. The first book that makes a kid cry; it’s also the best-written one on this list. Notice: read the first sentence out loud twice. “Where’s Papa going with that ax?” Ask why the book starts there.
  12. 12
    Greek Myths (Marcia Williams)
    Williams · comic-strip
    Myths, comic format · So she gets Kai’s Percy Jackson world in panels. Museum tie-in with the vases in January. Notice: the same story as Kai’s D’Aulaires, drawn differently. Put the two side by side.
MusicBreakfast · one composer or idea a month
  1. ·
    Instruments are characters
    Sep
    Prokofiev, Peter and the Wolf · Plus Saint-Saëns, Carnival of the Animals; Britten, Young Person’s Guide to the Orchestra. Game: name the instrument before the narrator does. Bird = flute, wolf = horns, Peter = strings.
  2. ·
    Spooky
    Oct
    Mussorgsky, Night on Bald Mountain · Plus Grieg, In the Hall of the Mountain King; Saint-Saëns, Danse Macabre; Dukas, The Sorcerer’s Apprentice. Game: what’s the scary trick? Getting faster, going lower, the xylophone as bones. They’ll see two of these in Fantasia next month.
  3. ·
    Fantasia month
    Nov
    Beethoven, Symphony No. 6 (the storm) · Plus Stravinsky, Rite of Spring (opening); Tchaikovsky, Nutcracker Suite; Bach, Toccata and Fugue in D minor. Game: draw what you see while it plays. Then watch Fantasia and compare to Disney’s picture.
  4. ·
    December
    Dec
    Tchaikovsky, The Nutcracker (whole ballet) · Plus Handel, Messiah (Hallelujah); Bach, Christmas Oratorio (opening chorus); Vince Guaraldi, A Charlie Brown Christmas. Game: the Nutcracker has a dance for each country. Guess the country from the sound before you tell them.
  5. ·
    Bach
    Jan
    Bach, Brandenburg Concerto No. 3 · Plus Cello Suite No. 1 (Yo-Yo Ma); Goldberg Variations, Aria (Glenn Gould, 1981 recording). Game: count the voices. A fugue is the same tune chasing itself; hold up fingers when a new one enters.
  6. ·
    Mozart and Beethoven
    Feb
    Beethoven, Symphony No. 5 · Plus Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik; Queen of the Night aria (The Magic Flute); Für Elise. Game: the four notes. Count how many times da-da-da-DUM comes back in the first movement. It never stops.
  7. ·
    Spring
    Mar
    Vivaldi, The Four Seasons: Spring · Plus Copland, Appalachian Spring; Stravinsky, Firebird (finale). Game: Vivaldi wrote a poem for each season and put the birds, the dog, the storm in the music. Find the dog.
  8. ·
    Pictures in sound
    Apr
    Mussorgsky, Pictures at an Exhibition · Plus Debussy, Clair de lune and La Mer; Ravel, Boléro; Holst, The Planets (Mars, Jupiter). Game: Pictures is a walk through an art show, one movement per painting. Same as your museum missions. Mars is where Star Wars got its marching sound.
  9. ·
    Jazz
    May
    Dave Brubeck, Take Five · Plus Louis Armstrong, West End Blues; Duke Ellington, Take the A Train; Miles Davis, So What; Ella Fitzgerald, anything. Game: count to five with Take Five (it’s in 5, not 4). Then find where the solo starts and the tune stops.
  10. ·
    America
    Jun
    Gershwin, Rhapsody in Blue · Plus Copland, Rodeo (Hoe-Down); Scott Joplin, Maple Leaf Rag and The Entertainer; Bernstein, West Side Story (Mambo). Game: Joplin is the piano in The Sting (May film). Hoe-Down is the beef commercial. Name where else they’ve heard these.
  11. ·
    World
    Jul
    Ali Farka Touré & Ry Cooder, Talking Timbuktu · Plus Ravi Shankar (any raga, 10 minutes of it); Buena Vista Social Club; a Balinese gamelan recording. Game: which instrument is doing the job a drum does? Which one is doing the job a singer does? Different answers in each.
  12. ·
    Now
    Aug
    Steve Reich, Clapping Music · Plus Philip Glass, Glassworks (Opening); Max Richter, Vivaldi Recomposed (Spring); Arvo Pärt, Spiegel im Spiegel; John Williams, Star Wars Main Title. Game: do Clapping Music at the table; it’s two people clapping the same pattern, one shifted. Then play Holst’s Mars again next to Williams and hear the borrowing.
ArtArt Institute · one mission a month, after climbing · galleries rotate, check the map
  1. ·
    The big three
    Sep · Impressionism + American galleries
    Seurat, A Sunday on La Grande Jatte · Plus Hopper, Nighthawks; Grant Wood, American Gothic. Do: stand six inches from the Seurat, then thirty feet. Dots become a picture (Spider-Verse does the same thing). Nighthawks: nobody’s talking; make up what happened. American Gothic: it’s his sister and his dentist.
  2. ·
    Spooky
    Oct · American + Modern, Arms & Armor
    Ivan Albright, Picture of Dorian Gray · Plus Magritte, Time Transfixed (train out of a fireplace); Albright, That Which I Should Have Done I Did Not Do (the door); the Deering arms and armor hall. Do: the Dorian Gray was painted for the 1945 film, and the movie flips to color for the one shot of it. Kai reads the book in a few years; Andrew has it in his queue now.
  3. ·
    Tiny worlds
    Nov · lower level
    The Thorne Miniature Rooms · 68 rooms at one inch to the foot, 1930s, built by hand. Plus the Rubloff paperweight collection next door. Do: each kid picks the room they’d live in and the one with the best fake view out the window. Find the room with a tiny newspaper you can read.
  4. ·
    Light
    Dec · Impressionism; the Chagall windows
    Monet, Stacks of Wheat (the series) · Plus Caillebotte, Paris Street; Rainy Day; Chagall, America Windows (the blue glass from Ferris Bueller). Do: the Monets are the same haystack at different hours. Put them in time order. Caillebotte: find the vanishing point with your finger. Check whether the Thorne rooms are decorated for the holidays.
  5. ·
    Ancient
    Jan · Greek, Roman, Egyptian galleries
    Greek vases · Plus the mummy case of Paankhenamun; Roman portrait heads. Do: Kai finds a myth he knows from D’Aulaires on a vase (Heracles and Odysseus are the common ones). Zada finds hers from the Marcia Williams comic. Then the mummy.
  6. ·
    Faces
    Feb · European painting
    Picasso, The Old Guitarist · Plus Van Gogh, Self-Portrait and The Bedroom; Rembrandt, Old Man with a Gold Chain; Cassatt, The Child’s Bath; Toulouse-Lautrec, At the Moulin Rouge. Do: there’s a woman’s face hidden under the Old Guitarist; Picasso painted over an earlier picture and you can see her ghost above his neck. Van Gogh: his eyes follow you; ask why painters do that.
  7. ·
    Japan
    Mar · Arts of Asia; the Ando Gallery
    Japanese woodblock prints · Plus the Ando Gallery (the dark room of pillars with the screens). Hokusai’s Great Wave if it’s on view; prints rotate every few months, so check. Do: Ghibli household. Find the Totoro trees and the Castle in the Sky clouds in the screens. A print was carved in wood and stamped, so there are many copies; ask which is “the real one.”
  8. ·
    Is it art?
    Apr · Modern Wing
    Kandinsky, Improvisation No. 30 (Cannons) · Plus Pollock, Greyed Rainbow; Lichtenstein, Brushstroke with Spatter; Warhol, Mao; a Calder mobile; Joan Mitchell, City Landscape. Do: each kid argues one piece IS art and one ISN’T, and has to give a reason. Mussorgsky’s Pictures is this month’s breakfast music; the connection is the whole point.
  9. ·
    Chicago
    May · American art; architecture galleries; then outside
    Archibald Motley, Nightlife · Plus the Chicago Stock Exchange Trading Room (Louis Sullivan, rebuilt inside the museum); Frank Lloyd Wright windows and chairs; the architecture fragments. Do: Motley painted Bronzeville in 1943, a few miles from the building. Then walk out: Cloud Gate, Calder’s Flamingo, the Picasso in Daley Plaza. Art that isn’t in a museum.
  10. ·
    America, big
    Jun · American galleries; top of the main stairs
    Georgia O’Keeffe, Sky Above Clouds IV · Plus Winslow Homer watercolors (if up); Sargent; Whistler; Jacob Lawrence; Kerry James Marshall, Many Mansions. Do: the O’Keeffe is 24 feet wide, painted at 77 from an airplane window. Ask what the picture is of; the answer (clouds from above) took most people a minute in 1965.
  11. ·
    Other worlds
    Jul · Arts of Africa; Ancient Americas; Arts of Asia
    African masks · Plus the Maya and Aztec objects; the Buddha heads in the Indian galleries. Do: a mask in a case is a mask with the music, the dance and the person taken away. Ask what’s missing. The Ancient Americas room has ballgame gear: a sport with rules we only half know.
  12. ·
    Sketch day
    Aug · their pick
    One work each, ten minutes, pencil · Each kid picks anything from the year and draws it. Pencil and a small pad only (museum rule). Postcard of it from the shop goes on the fridge. Do: drawing a thing makes you look at it for ten minutes. That’s the entire lesson of the year in one sitting.
How it runsRules · keep it small
  • Film: the monthly pick is the Friday family night for that month; everything else stays on the Movies queue. One “notice” line per film, said before it starts, never a quiz after.
  • Books: Kai’s list is a year of real books at one a month; he reads faster than that and the gaps are his. Zada’s list is wins first, classics as read-alouds. The rule for her stays: quit anything after 20 pages, no penalty.
  • Music: 15–20 minutes at breakfast, one piece named out loud (“this is Bach”), the game is optional. Search the title on Spotify or YouTube; no playlist to maintain. Kids DJ on Fridays.
  • Art: one mission, 45 minutes, after climbing. Each kid gets a “last stop” pick. Kids under 14 are free at the Art Institute; if the adults go more than three times a year, check whether a membership is cheaper than tickets. Check the free-evening schedule for Illinois residents before paying.
  • Tracking: tell Claude what landed and what didn’t the same way you do for movies. Verdicts change the next month’s pick.
