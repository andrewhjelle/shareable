Film2026–27 · one a month, together · streaming unchecked
  1. 1
    Mary Poppins
    Sep · 1964 · G
    Musical · Oz is done (both really liked it), so the other big musical takes September. Unseen. Notice: live actors dancing with drawn penguins in 1964. Ask how; it’s the same problem Roger Rabbit solved 24 years later.
  2. 2
    Edward Scissorhands
    Oct · 1990 · PG-13
    Burton fairy tale · Nightmare Before Christmas is done (both liked it); this is the Burton they haven’t seen, and Andrew’s pick from the queue. Gentle-sad, one scary moment at the end. Notice: the pastel suburb versus the black castle on the hill. Burton tells the whole story with color before anyone speaks.
  3. 3
    Fantasia
    Nov · 1940 · G
    Animation + orchestra · The film-and-music bridge. Skip the segments that drag (they will); Sorcerer’s Apprentice, Night on Bald Mountain, the dinosaurs are the keepers. Notice: you already heard these at breakfast in October and November. Which picture did you imagine before you saw Disney’s?
  4. 4
    It’s a Wonderful Life
    Dec · 1946 · PG
    Christmas drama · The ancestor of Groundhog Day, which they liked: one man shown a world without him. Notice: it flopped in 1946 and became a classic because TV showed it for free every December. Ask what else got a second life that way.
  5. 5
    The Hidden Fortress
    Jan · 1958 · subtitles
    Kurosawa adventure · Kai lead. Star Wars is built on this: two bickering peasants (R2 and 3PO), a princess in disguise, a general, a fortress. Notice: the screen wipes between scenes. Lucas copied those too. Zada can bail at the hour mark without guilt.
  6. 6
    Singin’ in the Rain
    Feb · 1952 · G
    Musical comedy · Andrew’s pick from the queue, moved to a month with a reason. Notice: it’s a movie about movies, set in the year films learned to talk (1927). The bad-microphone scene is a real problem studios had.
  7. 7
    E.T.
    Mar · 1982 · PG
    Spielberg · They’ve seen four Spielbergs (Raiders, Doom, Crusade, Jurassic Park). This is the gentle one. Notice: the camera stays at kid height and you barely see an adult face until the last third. That’s a choice.
  8. 8
    Modern Times
    Apr · 1936 · G
    Chaplin comedy · Zada liked Sherlock Jr.; this is the next silent, and it’s short. Factory, feeding machine, roller skates. Notice: sound films had existed for nine years. Chaplin made a silent anyway, and the only voices you hear come out of machines. Ask why.
  9. 9
    The Sting
    May · 1973 · PG
    Con-man caper · National Treasure was a hit; this is the grown-up puzzle version. Both kids can follow it. Notice: title cards split it into chapters like a book, and the piano is Scott Joplin from 1902 (June breakfast music). You’re conned too. Don’t warn them.
  10. 10
    Duck Soup
    Jun · 1933 · 68 min
    Marx Brothers comedy · Jaws is done (both liked it). This is the next comedy rung after Keaton, Chaplin and Lloyd: the first great talking comedy, and it’s barely over an hour. Notice: the mirror scene has no dialogue at all; it’s a silent gag inside a talkie. Ask which brother never speaks and why it works.
  11. 11
    Wallace & Gromit: The Curse of the Were-Rabbit
    Jul · 2005 · G
    Aardman claymation · They liked both Chicken Runs; this is the same studio’s best feature. (Spider-Verse is done; both were indifferent.) Notice: fingerprints on the clay. Compare to Nightmare Before Christmas: both stop-motion, but clay moves differently from puppets.
  12. 12
    The Dawn Wall
    Aug · 2017 · PG
    Climbing documentary · Free Solo is done (Andrew kinda liked it). This is the other one: Tommy Caldwell’s 19 days on El Cap, with a partner, and it’s rated PG. If it’s seen too, Meru. Notice: Free Solo asked whether the camera makes him less safe; this one asks whether he should leave his partner behind to finish. Same wall, opposite question.

    Riordan · 2014 · illustrated
    Myths in Percy’s voice · The bridge book: Percy retells the actual myths, jokes intact, with John Rocco’s paintings. Greek Heroes is the companion.   3. 3
    The Odyssey (Gillian Cross retelling)
    Cross / Packer · 2012
    Epic, retold · The real thing, at his level, with Neil Packer’s drawings. Emily Wilson’s translation is the next rung in a few years. Notice: it starts in the middle and tells the early part as a flashback. Percy Jackson does the same thing.
  4. 4
    Black Ships Before Troy
    Sutcliff / Alan Lee · 1993
    The Iliad, retold · The Trojan War in Rosemary Sutcliff’s prose with Alan Lee’s paintings (he did the Lord of the Rings films). The Wanderings of Odysseus is her sequel if Cross’s Odyssey lands. Notice: there’s no clear good guy. Ask who he’s rooting for at the start and whether it changes by the end.

    Oct
    Mussorgsky, Night on Bald Mountain · Plus Grieg, In the Hall of the Mountain King; Saint-Saëns, Danse Macabre; Dukas, The Sorcerer’s Apprentice. Game: what’s the scary trick? Getting faster, going lower, the xylophone as bones. They’ll see two of these in Fantasia next month.

    Nov
    Beethoven, Symphony No. 6 (the storm) · Plus Stravinsky, Rite of Spring (opening); Tchaikovsky, Nutcracker Suite; Bach, Toccata and Fugue in D minor. Game: draw what you see while it plays. Then watch Fantasia and compare to Disney’s picture.

    December
    Dec

  5. ·
    Bach
    Jan
    Bach, Brandenburg Concerto No. 3 · Plus Cello Suite No. 1 (Yo-Yo Ma); Goldberg Variations, Aria (Glenn Gould, 1981 recording). Game: count the voices. A fugue is the same tune chasing itself; hold up fingers when a new one enters. Greek bonus for museum month: Offenbach, Orpheus in the Underworld (the can-can is from it) and Gluck, Dance of the Blessed Spirits; both are the Orpheus myth Kai knows.
  6. ·
    Mozart and Beethoven
    Feb
    Beethoven, Symphony No. 5 · Plus Mozart, Eine kleine Nachtmusik; Queen of the Night aria (The Magic Flute); Für Elise. Game: the four notes. Count how many times da-da-da-DUM comes back in the first movement. It never stops.
  7. ·
    Spring
    Mar
    Vivaldi, The Four Seasons: Spring · Plus Copland, Appalachian Spring; Stravinsky, Firebird (finale). Game: Vivaldi wrote a poem for each season and put the birds, the dog, the storm in the music. Find the dog.
  8. ·

    Apr
    Mussorgsky, Pictures at an Exhibition · Plus Debussy, Clair de lune and La Mer; Ravel, Boléro; Holst, The Planets (Mars, Jupiter). Game: Pictures is a walk through an art show, one movement per painting. Same as your museum missions. Mars is where Star Wars got its marching sound.

How it runsRules · keep it small
  • Film: the monthly pick is the Friday family night for that month; everything else stays on the Movies queue. One “notice” line per film, said before it starts, never a quiz after.
  • Books: Kai’s list is a year of real books at one a month; he reads faster than that and the gaps are his. Zada’s list is the Dahl run plus her Wednesday lane; the early-reader picks were aimed too young (she finished Matilda). The rule for her stays: quit anything after 20 pages, no penalty.
  • Music: 15–20 minutes at breakfast, one piece named out loud (“this is Bach”), the game is optional. Search the title on Spotify or YouTube; no playlist to maintain. Kids DJ on Fridays.
  • Art: one mission, 45 minutes, after climbing. Each kid gets a “last stop” pick. Kids under 14 are free at the Art Institute; if the adults go more than three times a year, check whether a membership is cheaper than tickets. Check the free-evening schedule for Illinois residents before paying.
  • Tracking: tell Claude what landed and what didn’t the same way you do for movies. Verdicts change the next month’s pick.
