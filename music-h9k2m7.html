This month · Prokofiev

SEP 2026 · Russian · program music · the kid’s entry point to the whole tradition

Play one piece per weekday morning at breakfast. Cycle Mon–Fri all month — by week 4 they’ll know Prokofiev on sight. Say his name each morning. On weekends the kids pick their own (BTS etc.).

Why him first: Peter and the Wolf was literally written to teach kids the orchestra. Every instrument gets a character. It’s the on-ramp.

  1. MON
    Peter and the Wolf27 min · narratedSPOTIFY
    The whole point of the piece is each character = one instrument: Peter = strings, bird = flute, duck = oboe, cat = clarinet, grandpa = bassoon, wolf = horns, hunters = timpani. Ask them: which instrument for which animal?
  2. TUE
    TroikaLieutenant Kijé Suite · 3 minSPOTIFY
    Sleigh bells and a big melody. They’ve heard it in commercials without knowing. Short and catchy.
  3. WED
    Dance of the KnightsRomeo & Juliet · 5 minSPOTIFY
    The heavy, stomping theme every action movie trailer steals. Kai will recognize it instantly.
  4. THU
    Classical Symphony · mvt 1Symphony No. 1 · 5 minSPOTIFY
    Prokofiev writing in Haydn’s style but sneaky-modern. Playful. Good for showing them “classical” is a specific era, not just “old music.”
  5. FRI
    Cinderella · Grand Waltzballet · 4 minSPOTIFY
    Sweeping romantic ballet closer — Zada’s favorite kind of thing.

How this works

READ ONCE · NEVER THINK ABOUT IT AGAIN
  • 10–15 min at breakfast, weekdays only. Weekends the kids pick.
  • One composer per month. Five pieces. Cycle Mon–Fri all month. By week 4 they know them cold.
  • Say the composer’s name every morning. That’s the whole cultural literacy trick — recognition, not analysis.
  • Monday of a new month = introduce. Read the “why him/her” line from this page. Play piece 1.
  • Tue–Fri = just play. Don’t explain. Kids ask questions — answer them. They don’t — that’s fine.
  • The year covers Russia, France, Germany, jazz, American folk-classical, global roots, musical theater. Real breadth by next August.
  • No quiz. No worksheets. This is exposure, not homework.

Kids’ own listening

SEPARATE FROM THIS · NOT REGULATED

The kids are into BTS right now. They can listen to that whenever — car, weekend mornings, their own time. This page is only the parallel diet Andrew runs at breakfast so classical + jazz + global roots don’t disappear from their ears entirely.

If a kid says “can we do BTS instead” at breakfast: say no, save it for after school. Kai and Zada will get plenty of pop on their own. Only Andrew can hold this slot open.

The year · Sep 2026 → Aug 2027

12 COMPOSERS · 5 PIECES EACH · MONTH-LONG CYCLES
  1. SEP
    Sergei Prokofiev
    RUSSIAN20TH C. Peter and the Wolf is the on-ramp. Program music — instruments as characters. The best possible opening.
  2. OCT
    Camille Saint-Saëns
    FRENCHROMANTIC Carnival of the Animals (written for kids). Add Danse Macabre for Halloween. Aquarium is one of the loveliest 2 min of music ever written.
  3. NOV
    J.S. Bach
    GERMANBAROQUE Toccata & Fugue in D minor, Cello Suite Prelude, Brandenburg 3. Structure and math you can hear. The bedrock.
  4. DEC
    Tchaikovsky
    RUSSIANROMANTIC Nutcracker season. Sugar Plum, Trepak, March, Waltz of the Flowers, 1812 finale. The Christmas month plays itself.
  5. JAN
    Beethoven
    GERMANCLASSICAL → ROMANTIC Symphony 5 opening, Ode to Joy, Für Elise, Moonlight Sonata mvt 1, Symphony 6 Pastoral. The greatest-hits month.
  6. FEB
    Duke Ellington
    JAZZAMERICAN Take the A Train, Mood Indigo, It Don’t Mean a Thing, Caravan, Satin Doll. Black History Month. Their first taste of jazz-as-composition, not background music.
  7. MAR
    Vivaldi
    ITALIANBAROQUE Four Seasons — Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter, one per week + Gloria opening. Vivid, tight, kid-friendly.
  8. APR
    Aaron Copland
    AMERICAN20TH C. Fanfare for the Common Man, Rodeo’s Hoe-Down, Appalachian Spring (Simple Gifts variations), Billy the Kid. American open-plains sound.
  9. MAY
    Mozart
    AUSTRIANCLASSICAL Eine kleine Nachtmusik, Rondo alla Turca, Symphony 40 mvt 1, Queen of the Night aria, Horn Concerto 4 finale. Pure melodic craft.
  10. JUN
    George Gershwin
    AMERICANJAZZ × CLASSICAL Rhapsody in Blue opening, Summertime, An American in Paris, I Got Rhythm, S’Wonderful. The bridge between the classical + jazz months.
  11. JUL
    Global roots sampler
    WORLD One artist per week: Ravi Shankar (Indian sitar), Ali Farka Touré (Malian desert blues), Astor Piazzolla (Argentine tango — Libertango), Fela Kuti (Nigerian afrobeat), Miriam Makeba (South African). Passport month.
  12. AUG
    American musical theater
    MUSICALS Bernstein (West Side Story — Somewhere, America), Rodgers & Hammerstein (Do-Re-Mi), Sondheim (Send in the Clowns), Lin-Manuel Miranda (Alexander Hamilton). Bring it home to what they’ll actually sing in car rides.
