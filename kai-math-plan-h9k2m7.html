1 Open
Framing

Thank you for making time. Kai has had two good years with Beata, and we’re grateful for her. This isn’t a critique of the classroom. We’re here because we want to work with the school on Kai’s math trajectory, and we think there’s a small, low-risk step that would help him and give the school a clean path forward.

We know Middle School math placement is a sensitive topic and we don’t want to make anyone’s job harder. Our hope is to find something that works for Kai and gives the school a clear way to handle these questions for other families going forward.

Our goal for Kai

High-90th percentile MAP by the fall placement window, and on track for selective-enrollment high schools — Jones, Whitney Young — or the advanced tracks at OPRF. All of those effectively require ~95th percentile. We want to see where Kai stands this year and make sure the trend line stays there through 7th and 8th, when placement is decided.

Our request
  1. A six-week trial in the Middle School math group, starting this month. A short written plan, a defined check-in with Beata, Sally, and us at week six, and a no-penalty return to the UE group if it isn’t working.
  2. Written criteria for MS math placement going forward. The same set for all UE families. This protects Kai now and Zada in fall 2027, and it makes the current placements easier to explain to any family who asks.
  3. The MS math curriculum and books, so we can support Kai at home during the trial and after.
Why we prefer this to a “let’s see” approach
  • It’s small. Six weeks. It tests one thing: whether Kai can keep up in the group.
  • It’s reversible. A no-penalty return if it isn’t working. The risk is bounded.
  • The path already exists. Three sixth graders are in the MS math group this year. We’re asking for Kai to be evaluated by the same process, and for those criteria to be made visible to other UE families.
  • We’ve already tried the alternative. Independent study inside the same group was proposed last year. Under that approach Kai’s MAP percentile has moved 93rd → 86th → 79th.
  • Real classroom evidence is a better signal. Six weeks of performance in the group tells us more than another single-point MAP score months from now.

Does that seem like a reasonable approach?

2 One point of context

We do want to be direct about one thing. In February 2025 we asked in writing for more math challenge for Kai and were pointed to extensions and independent work. A Middle School math path existed at that time, and exists now, and it wasn’t named to us. We only learned about it, and about the MAP threshold that determines it, this month.

We’re not looking to relitigate that. We’re looking to make sure it doesn’t happen again — for Kai this year, and for Zada when she enters Upper Elementary in fall 2027. Written criteria and a clearer communication pattern around the MS math path would address it.

4 How Kai learns
  • Kai does his best work in a classroom, with peers, and against clear expectations. Independent study inside the same group was proposed last year, and his percentile hasn’t moved under it.
  • We have not seen him struggle with math when he’s given the opportunity to work at that level.

  • By our read, a classroom setting with a clear standard is the path most likely to raise his score.
A low-stakes trial for one additional student, with the option open to peers on the same criteria, is a small step. Clear criteria on top of it make the current placements easier to explain to any family who asks.
5 Questions we expect, and how we’re thinking about them
  • “It’s a rare pathway.”
    • Three sixth graders are in it this year in a small cohort. What was the process that led to those placements, and can Kai be evaluated by the same one?
  • “None of our top 8th graders participated.”
    • What does the data tell us about Kai specifically? His MAP has moved 93rd → 86th → 79th under the current path. What trend would change the answer?
  • “It sends the message our UE teachers aren’t capable.”
    • That isn’t our message. Kai has had two good years with Beata. The trial is about matching his math ceiling, not about the classroom. We’re happy to say that to Beata directly.
  • “Let’s try another similar opportunity — differentiation, an enrichment track.”
    • Independent study was proposed last year and Kai’s percentile hasn’t moved. What would be different about this? How would we measure whether it’s working, and by when?
  • “Let’s monitor and reassess in a few weeks.”
    • That’s the path we’ve been on and the data hasn’t moved. Could we start the trial now and use the week-six check-in to reassess with real classroom evidence?
  • “Long-term high-school placement is the goal.”
    • That’s exactly why we’re asking. Our goal is high-90th percentile going into 7th and 8th. What is the school’s plan for how Kai gets there?
  • “Montessori — we don’t want children under external pressure.”
    • Understood, and we share that. Kai works best against clear expectations. The Chinese program in this same building has that structure, and the school has affirmed it by naming Ms. Xiang Director of Target Language Education. We’re asking for the same clarity on the math side.
  • “This raises questions about whether the current three should be there.”
    • Removing them doesn’t help Kai. Adding a clear written path in and out is what we’re proposing, and it makes the current placements easier to defend.
