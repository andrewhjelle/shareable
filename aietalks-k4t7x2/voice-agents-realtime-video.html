Voice agents with Realtime Video

SpeakerSidney Primas · LemonSlice Watchyoutu.be/z1dqv74SpUs
Voice & realtimeGenerative media

If you only read one thing

  • LemonSlice trains a video world-model focused on humans rather than a traditional rigged avatar pipeline, betting the harder upfront training pays off in emergent full-body movement, object interaction, and micro-expressions. (c2)
  • Making the model real-time required switching from bidirectional to causal (past-only) attention and collapsing denoising from about 30 steps to 1. (c3, c4)
  • Error accumulation over long sessions (the model repeatedly generates from a video block that already contains error) is called an unsolved, industry-wide problem for real-time video/world models, though LemonSlice claims a proprietary fix that lets sessions run 8-16 hours with no noticeable accumulation. (c5, c6)
01The goal: pass the Avatar Turing Test

LemonSlice states its mission as making a video avatar indistinguishable from a human on a video call, which requires solving a long tail of problems beyond photorealism: correct emotions, object interactions, micro-expressions, and an internal state for the avatar. As a public demo, LemonSlice partnered with Microsoft to bring a Teddy Roosevelt avatar to life in a replica Oval Office, where President Trump interacted with it live for roughly 10 minutes.

“Lemon Slice is on a mission to break the Avatar Turing test”

02World-model approach instead of rigged avatars

LemonSlice's technical bet is to take general world models and focus them specifically on humans, rather than using the rigging/driving pipeline most avatar companies use. This is described as harder to train and deploy initially, but yields emergent behaviors (full-body movement, object interaction, physics on details like earrings and water) that don't come for free with the alternative approach.

“Essentially what we do is we take these world models and we focus them on humans”

▶ watch at 05:16
03Making a bidirectional video model real-time

Video models are normally bidirectional, generating by looking at both past and future latents at once. LemonSlice trains with an attention mask so the model can only look into the past, since at inference time the future (e.g. audio not yet spoken) doesn't exist. Separately, real-time speed required collapsing the denoising process from roughly 30 steps down to a single step.

“we basically train a model with an attention mask so that the model can only look into the past”

▶ watch at 10:45

“we need to do is go from like 30 steps, bring it out to one step”

▶ watch at 11:18
04Error accumulation is the hard unsolved problem

Because the model only looks backward at its own previously generated video (which already contains some error), errors compound over time — a problem Primas says anyone in real-time video or world models will recognize. This is especially difficult because LemonSlice's avatars run continuously for hours (one demo ran 8 hours straight, another planned for 16, with no reset). LemonSlice claims to have a new, undisclosed approach to this problem that removes noticeable accumulation over long sessions.

“this problem here on the left, which is called error accumulation, which is a problem anybody in like real-time video generation and world model is familiar with”

▶ watch at 11:48

“the teddy avatar is generating continuously non-stop frame by frame for 8 hours straight with like no reset throughout the entire process”

▶ watch at 12:18
05Cost is roughly at parity with voice models

Despite video being far more pixel-heavy to stream than audio, LemonSlice says its costs are about the same as a voice model, which Primas calls surprising. The company expects costs to keep falling from both algorithmic improvements and hardware gains, and predicts a longer-term shift toward a single end-to-end model that directly ingests user video/audio and outputs avatar video/audio, functioning as an 'EQ layer' paired with a separate 'IQ' model for reasoning and tool use, arriving within roughly two to three years.

“the costs are about the same as a voice model”

▶ watch at 13:51

“I feel strongly that within two or three years you'll be seeing these kinds of end-to-end EQ models coming on the market”

Commentary (ours)

The claim of a proprietary fix for error accumulation is asserted without detail ('can't talk about that yet'), so it should be read as a vendor claim rather than a verified technical result (ours) — the demo footage shown is consistent with the claim but isn't independent proof, and the field-wide framing of error accumulation as unsolved suggests some skepticism is warranted until the method is published or independently tested over comparably long sessions.
