Voice agents with Realtime Video

patternVoice & realtimeGenerative media

LemonSlice is building real-time video avatars as a bet on world-model architecture rather than the more common rigged/driven avatar approach, and is selling it as an API layer that sits on top of a customer's own LLM and voice stack

If you only read one thing

  • LemonSlice trains a video world-model focused on humans rather than a traditional rigged avatar pipeline, betting the harder upfront training pays off in emergent full-body movement, object interaction, and micro-expressions. (c2)
  • Making the model real-time required switching from bidirectional to causal (past-only) attention and collapsing denoising from about 30 steps to 1. (c3, c4)
  • Error accumulation over long sessions (the model repeatedly generates from a video block that already contains error) is called an unsolved, industry-wide problem for real-time video/world models, though LemonSlice claims a proprietary fix that lets sessions run 8-16 hours with no noticeable accumulation. (c5, c6)

02World-model approach instead of rigged avatars

World-model approach instead of rigged avatars

  • LemonSlice's technical bet is to take general world models and focus them specifically on humans, rather than using the rigging/driving pipeline most avatar companies use.
  • This is described as harder to train and deploy initially, but yields emergent behaviors (full-body movement, object interaction, physics on details like earrings and water) that don't come for free with the alternative approach.

“Essentially what we do is we take these world models and we focus them on humans”

03Making a bidirectional video model real-time
Slide at 12:00 · click any slide to enlarge

Making a bidirectional video model real-time

  • Video models are normally bidirectional, generating by looking at both past and future latents at once.
  • LemonSlice trains with an attention mask so the model can only look into the past, since at inference time the future (e.g. audio not yet spoken) doesn't exist.
  • Separately, real-time speed required collapsing the denoising process from roughly 30 steps down to a single step.

“we basically train a model with an attention mask so that the model can only look into the past”

said at 10:45

“we need to do is go from like 30 steps, bring it out to one step”

said at 11:18
04Error accumulation is the hard unsolved problem
Slide at 12:00 · click any slide to enlarge

Error accumulation is the hard unsolved problem

  • Because the model only looks backward at its own previously generated video (which already contains some error), errors compound over time — a problem Primas says anyone in real-time video or world models will recognize.
  • This is especially difficult because LemonSlice's avatars run continuously for hours (one demo ran 8 hours straight, another planned for 16, with no reset).
  • LemonSlice claims to have a new, undisclosed approach to this problem that removes noticeable accumulation over long sessions.

“this problem here on the left, which is called error accumulation, which is a problem anybody in like real-time video generation and world model is familiar with”

said at 11:48

“the teddy avatar is generating continuously non-stop frame by frame for 8 hours straight with like no reset throughout the entire process”

05Cost is roughly at parity with voice models
Slide at 15:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

Cost is roughly at parity with voice models

  • Despite video being far more pixel-heavy to stream than audio, LemonSlice says its costs are about the same as a voice model, which Primas calls surprising.
  • The company expects costs to keep falling from both algorithmic improvements and hardware gains, and predicts a longer-term shift toward a single end-to-end model that directly ingests user video/audio and outputs avatar video/audio, functioning as an 'EQ layer' paired with a separate 'IQ' model for reasoning and tool use, arriving within roughly two to three years.

“the costs are about the same as a voice model”
