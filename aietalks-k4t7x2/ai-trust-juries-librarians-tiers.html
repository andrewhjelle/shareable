Describes three concrete patterns for making agent output trustworthy in go-to-market workflows: an anchor-asset scaffold to ground content generation, a retrieval layer (Radiant Librarian) that supplies business-specific definitions bef...

If you only read one thing

  • AI's hallucination problem has become a trust problem: agents give confidently wrong answers instead of saying they're unsure (c1).
  • Scaffold agents with anchor assets and a retrieval/librarian layer before turning them loose, rather than YOLO-prompting from scratch (c5, c6).
  • For ambiguous judgment calls, use independent analyst agents plus a consensus judge instead of one agent's single-pass answer; and don't run important agent work on crowbarred, cheap subscription tiers (c7, c8, c9).

01Hallucination's successor is a trust problem

Hallucination's successor is a trust problem

  • The speaker argues that the AI hallucination problem discussed a couple of years ago has been replaced by a trust problem: when asked to do something like report on revenue, a model doesn't flag uncertainty, it confidently returns a wrong answer that looks identical to a correct one.

“doesn't say, "I'm not sure." It says, "Here you go." And it gives you a wrong answer that looks exactly like being right.”

02Manage agents like people, with commander's intent
Slide at 08:20 · click any slide to enlarge · 2 more from this section below

Manage agents like people, with commander's intent

  • The talk's main thesis is to manage AI agents the way you would manage a team of humans.
  • The single practical tip offered is to use commander's intent when prompting (borrowed from armed forces doctrine): tell agents why you want something done, not just what to do, because they perform better with the reasoning behind a task.
  • The speaker warns that agents, having been trained on human-generated material, tend to default to micromanaging themselves and need to be pulled back toward the underlying why.

“the main thesis of the talk today is actually, when in doubt, manage your agents like other humans. If you were helping a team of humans figure out how to do something, great.”

03Scaffold before turning an agent loose
Slide at 11:20 · click any slide to enlarge · 1 more from this section below

Scaffold before turning an agent loose

  • A YOLO-mode attempt to have Claude rebuild the company website from raw sources failed even with plan mode.
  • The fix was defining structure first: a maintained set of anchor assets (including a product capabilities reference with citations back to source systems) that the agent could ground itself in before generating content, rather than starting from nothing.

“the step here was define the structure first, and then turn Claude loose. Don't try and YOLO it”

said at 11:30
04Radiant Librarian: just-in-time company knowledge
Slide at 12:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 2 more from this section below

Radiant Librarian: just-in-time company knowledge

  • Upside's Radiant Librarian is consulted before an agent answers a data question, giving it access to documentation, a library of company knowledge items, and the schema of prior failed queries.
  • This supplies business-specific definitions on demand, for example that the company's fiscal year runs February through April rather than the calendar quarter an agent would otherwise assume.

“it basically gives your agent a just-in-time memory of all the important things. So we know that the fiscal year here is actually February through April.”

said at 12:32
05Jury-of-analysts pattern, and don't skimp on agent tier
Slide at 14:20 · click any slide to enlarge · 2 more from this section below

Jury-of-analysts pattern, and don't skimp on agent tier

  • For questions without a single empirically correct answer, such as multi-touch attribution, Upside's workflow spins up independent analyst agents that research separately and produce evidence-cited opinions, which a consensus judge treats as input rather than fact, weighing reasoning quality to produce a final result.
  • Separately, the speaker warns against running important agent work through cheap, subscription-tier AI products crowbarred with MCP support, and recommends at minimum a tier-two setup with a powerful model, sub-agents, plan mode, and full MCP support.

“I'm not treating these as fact. I'm treating them as input, and I'm going to weigh the reasoning quality of each of these analysts, and then I'm going to help you come up with the final version here.”

said at 14:37

“any AI product where it's been crowbarred into a pre- subscription model is probably not something that you should be using for anything important”

said at 15:39

“find something that is at least tier two here and tier two means it's got to be a powerful model. You need it to have attributes like sub agents, plan mode, full MCP support.”

said at 16:10

Commentary (ours)

The through-line across all three examples is that trust is manufactured by adding a retrieval or review layer between the raw prompt and the final answer, not by improving the base model's confidence; that's a cheaper and more portable fix than waiting for better models, which may be why a small GTM-tooling vendor can ship it before large platforms do (ours).
