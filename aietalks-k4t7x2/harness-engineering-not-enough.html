Harness Engineering is not Enough: Why Software Factories Fail

SpeakerDex Horthy · HumanLayer Watchyoutu.be/Ib5GBkD555M
Coding agentsEvals & benchmarksAgentic engineering

If you only read one thing

  • Real-world data (Faros AI) shows PR review quality and merge-without-review rates worsening since teams adopted coding agents broadly.
  • This is a model-training limitation, not a skill issue: RL rewards tests passing, with no way to penalize eroded maintainability.
  • Fix is re-adding upfront product/architecture/program-design review; ~30 minutes of planning can save hours of PR review time.
01The 'lights-off' factory is producing worse code

Horthy cites a Faros AI report showing PR review quality dropping and more PRs merging with no review at all since teams adopted coding agents broadly, alongside rising incidents and bugs per developer. He references StrongDM's lights-out software factory, where no one reads the generated code, as an example of the trend he's pushing back on.

“pull request code review quality is way down. We're having more comments, longer comments, and tons of PRs being merged without any review at all”

“StrongDM built a lights-out software factory where nobody even reads the code”

02This is a training problem, not a prompting problem

He argues no amount of harness engineering or looped prompting can fix what is fundamentally a model-training issue: RL on coding benchmarks like SWE-bench rewards a binary pass/fail on tests, with no mechanism to penalize architectural erosion or shotgun-surgery-style coupling. Because bad-architecture costs surface months later, the reward signal can't propagate back to the training episode that caused it.

“this is in fact not a skill issue. That no amount of harness engineering or loops maxing can solve what is fundamentally a model training issue”

▶ watch at 02:34

“models have a shortcoming. Um they can't maintain and improve codebase quality over time, not without a a decent amount of human steering”

▶ watch at 08:44
03Why Claude Code broke out first

Horthy attributes Claude Code's early lead to being the first case of a model lab training a model directly against the harness it would be distributed in, rather than a third party building a harness on top of someone else's model weights. He cites Claude Code's revenue growth (from nothing to roughly 4, then 9 billion in under a year) as evidence of how much that training-harness alignment mattered.

“this was the first time that a model lab trained a model against the harness that they were going to distribute it to users in”

▶ watch at 10:18

“Why did Claude Code go from nothing to 4 billion and I think now they're at 9 billion in revenue in under a year”

▶ watch at 09:47
04The fix: put planning and review back in front

His recommended workaround is re-adding upfront product review, system architecture, and program design (types, method signatures, call stacks) before code generation, so review time shrinks because alignment already happened. He estimates roughly 30 minutes of pre-planning can save hours of PR review, and closes by telling engineers to treat model limitations as a known constraint to design around rather than a reason to stop reading code.

“The main idea here is 30 minutes over here in pre-planning and alignment can save you hours in review”

▶ watch at 16:27

“we're engineers, and these are just constraints, and models are good at certain things, and they're not good at other things. And so go figure out how to solve problems given a set of constraints”
