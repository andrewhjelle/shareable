Malleable Evals: Why Are We Evaluating Adaptive Systems with Static Tests?

SpeakerVincent Koc · OpenClaw Watchyoutu.be/4VhbYlfC7Gs · 15 min SourceRecording of this talk at AI Engineer (2026), published on YouTube by the conference. takeEvals & benchmarksAgentic engineeringCoding agents

Argues that static benchmark suites are the wrong tool once a harness or agent starts modifying itself at runtime, and sketches an alternative: self-curated eval suites drawn from live traces plus telemetry-driven self-correction

If you only read one thing

  • AI applications are no longer static, but most teams still evaluate them the way they evaluate static software: hand-crafted test sets and offline regression suites.
  • Koc frames the shift as moving from prompt engineering to context engineering to "intent engineering," where the agent self-optimizes toward a defined end state instead of being graded against fixed answers.
  • He warns that the well-covered 80% of agent behavior isn't the risk; the unpredictable 20% of edge-case usage is what breaks production, a problem he calls "eval calcification."

02How teams evaluate today

How teams evaluate today

  • He describes the current default: write a set of examples and unit tests, build a manual regression suite to catch known bad outcomes, run offline evaluation cycles before deployment.
  • This mirrors classic software QA but misses the "chaos engineering" instinct of deliberately stress-testing a system to see where it breaks.

“we might do like a manual regression suite, which is like, hey, when we do A and B, sometimes C happens, and C is unfavorable”

03From prompt engineering to intent engineering

From prompt engineering to intent engineering

  • Koc traces a progression: prompt engineering (wordsmithing instructions and hoping for improvement), then context engineering (RAG, tool calling, steering agents with structured input), and now what he calls intent engineering, where the machine self-optimizes based on a stated goal rather than being scored against a fixed expected answer.

“what that brings us to is like intent engineering. Um this kind of concept of like machines can self-optimize based on intent”

said at 08:20

“You set a goal, you set a target and it kind of tunes itself, it tweaks itself”

said at 12:26
04The 20% that breaks production, and eval calcification
Slide at 11:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 5 more from this section below

The 20% that breaks production, and eval calcification

  • He argues roughly 80% of agent behavior can be defined and tested intentfully, but the remaining 20% keeps shifting as users ask unexpected questions or use the product in new ways, and that's what causes production failures.
  • He names the resulting difficulty of keeping evals current "eval calcification" and proposes telemetry-in-the-loop self-correction as a partial answer.

“that 20% is always going to keep changing and it's that 20% that's going to mess up your business”

said at 13:27

“I'm kind of calling this like a calcification problem, like the or eval calcification”
