The Rise of CaaS: Context-as-a-Service for Agentic AI

SpeakerOmer Primor · Bright Data Watchyoutu.be/Ot4OPrPH4xY
Context engineeringInfra & servingWeb automation

If you only read one thing

  • In a 100-run test enriching company data across 25 fields, AI search and CaaS providers converged on similar coverage, but CaaS providers actually trailed because they only know what they already collected (c2, c3).
  • Renting context (search/CaaS) means every repeated query costs the same as the first, even for unchanged data; owning it via direct scraping had zero AI/token cost per query after a ~$5,000 setup investment (c5, c7).
  • The tipping point where build-it-yourself beats renting was just over 15,000 entities/queries in their test, and frequency of repeat queries (not raw entity count) is what drives that number (c6, c8).
01Web data is becoming 'context', not just search results

Bright Data's Omer Primor frames the web as decaying fast: social media content is stale in under a day, and news/finance/retail data is largely irrelevant 30 days after publication. He traces web-search dominance shifting from Google to LLM-embedded search to a new wave of AI-search companies (Perplexity, Tavily, You.com, plus Amazon and Microsoft building their own agent-facing indexes), and argues search alone under-serves agents that need structured, longitudinal context (e.g., price history, headcount over time), not just a single answer.

“So social media, it's easy for us to understand, it's far less than a day, but also news, finance, retail, 30 days later, data”

02CaaS: a new vertical-search layer for agents

A category he calls CaaS (Context-as-a-Service) has emerged: vendors that crawl, structure into knowledge graphs, dedupe entities, and expose the result via MCP/CLI/API so agents can retrieve rather than search. Examples span finance, retail, e-commerce, GTM, and sales intelligence, and legacy data-as-a-service players (ZoomInfo launching "gtm.ai") are repositioning to serve agents directly.

03Head-to-head test: coverage and cost across 100 companies

Bright Data ran an agent loop (using GPT-4o as harness) to enrich 25 fields per company across 100 sponsor companies, 100 times, comparing AI search, native LLM search, CaaS providers, and plain Google/SERP retrieval. Coverage converged for most methods; the two CaaS providers tested actually underperformed because they're limited to data they already collected and can't chase novel fields. On cost, most methods converged except native LLM search ("obscenely expensive") and one high-cost CaaS provider, while a cheap niche CaaS also existed for lower-quality/lower-cost use.

04The real cost driver is frequency, and owning context changes the math

Primor argues per-record cost understates the real cost because knowledge work re-asks the same questions repeatedly (e.g., due diligence revisiting companies for new hires or news); every repeat query costs the same as the first even when nothing changed. As a counter-test, Bright Data built direct scrapers (via a tool called Scraper Studio) against LinkedIn, LinkedIn Jobs, and Crunchbase, hitting comparable coverage with zero AI/token cost per query after roughly a week (priced at $5,000) of setup. They calculate a tipping point of just over 15,000 entities/queries where the self-built approach becomes cheaper than renting via search or CaaS, driven primarily by query frequency rather than absolute entity count.

Commentary (ours)

The 15,000-query tipping point is specific to Bright Data's own test (25 fields, 100 companies, a week of scraper build time priced at $5,000) and Primor says as much ("maybe it's 30, maybe it's 100,000") — treat it as an order-of-magnitude sanity check for a build-vs-rent decision, not a number to import directly (ours).
