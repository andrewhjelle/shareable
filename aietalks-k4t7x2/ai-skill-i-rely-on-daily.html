Tracking her own prompts revealed comprehension, not generation, dominates

04Built a personal 'catch me up' comprehension skill

Built a personal 'catch me up' comprehension skill

  • Because her comprehension prompts kept repeating, she created a local skill called 'catch me up,' structured as a markdown file with clear goals across six exploration modes: architecture, convention, feature trace, syntax, testing, and history.
  • She demonstrated using it on an unfamiliar repository to get a structured summary of what a component does before contributing to it.

“that is structures those comprehension questions in two six exploration modes. Architecture, convention, feature trace, syntax, testing, and history”

05The takeaway: the biggest AI unlock in a large codebase is comprehension
Slide at 15:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

The takeaway: the biggest AI unlock in a large codebase is comprehension

  • Citing Armin Ronacher's observation that people increasingly don't know what code is in their own codebase, the speaker argues understanding the agent's research and aligning a mental model before prompting for implementation is the step often skipped in vibe-coding workflows, and frames AI as a tireless teammate for questions rather than primarily a code generator.

“the biggest unlock from AI in a large code base isn't generation. It's comprehension”

said at 15:26

Commentary (ours)

The 67%/2% comprehension-vs-generation split is a self-reported single-person sample size, but the underlying claim -- that legacy-codebase leverage comes from faster reorientation, not faster typing -- lines up with why 'catch me up'-style context-loading skills tend to get reused daily rather than one-off (ours).
