01AI adoption at Sentry, from hackathon tools to daily workflow

The speaker, a senior engineer now informally an 'agent manager,' says she has not written code by hand since December 2025 and only prompts, including for this presentation. A recent internal hackathon produced tools like Abacus (tracks internal AI usage), Warden (a PR code-review agent), and Junior (a Slack bot that turns bug reports into PRs), alongside a pre-existing AI SDK testing repository.

02The codebase is large, old, and constantly moving

Sentry was founded in 2010, has over 400 employees globally, and roughly 100,000 organizations depend on the codebase running correctly. About 100 PRs are merged per day, and the speaker says returning from vacation typically means her open PRs have merge conflicts requiring reorientation to recent changes.

03Tracking her own prompts revealed comprehension, not generation, dominates

The speaker had Claude analyze 116 of her own coding sessions and classify them into six categories (comprehension, modification, process, review, generation, other). The result showed 67% of her AI usage was comprehension-related versus only 2% code generation, which she says surprised her.

“it analyzed it 116 sessions and it classified everything in six categories. Comprehension, modification, process, review generation, and other”

04Built a personal 'catch me up' comprehension skill

Because her comprehension prompts kept repeating, she created a local skill called 'catch me up,' structured as a markdown file with clear goals across six exploration modes: architecture, convention, feature trace, syntax, testing, and history. She demonstrated using it on an unfamiliar repository to get a structured summary of what a component does before contributing to it.

“that is structures those comprehension questions in two six exploration modes. Architecture, convention, feature trace, syntax, testing, and history”

▶ watch at 10:47
05The takeaway: the biggest AI unlock in a large codebase is comprehension

Citing Armin Ronacher's observation that people increasingly don't know what code is in their own codebase, the speaker argues understanding the agent's research and aligning a mental model before prompting for implementation is the step often skipped in vibe-coding workflows, and frames AI as a tireless teammate for questions rather than primarily a code generator.

“the biggest unlock from AI in a large code base isn't generation. It's comprehension”

▶ watch at 15:26

Commentary (ours)

The 67%/2% comprehension-vs-generation split is a self-reported single-person sample size, but the underlying claim -- that legacy-codebase leverage comes from faster reorientation, not faster typing -- lines up with why 'catch me up'-style context-loading skills tend to get reused daily rather than one-off (ours).
