01MLX's growth and origin

The speaker started contributing to MLX, Apple's array framework for Apple silicon, in 2023 after his father lost his sight and cloud-based AI subscriptions weren't a practical fit for where his father lives. Three years later, MLX has over 1.5 million downloads and more than 4,000 ported models, with day-zero support for major open-source releases.

“million downloads, over 4,000 models ported, and we work with some of the best frontier labs to deliver to you day zero support for all of your open source models”

02Vision and accessibility use cases

The talk frames MLX VLM (vision-language models) as an accessibility tool: because his father can speak but not type, voice-controlled camera understanding became the design target. Live demos included real-time object detection and background blurring running fully offline on a MacBook.

“For my dad in particular, typing is not really a reality, but he can speak”

03Large models now fit on-device

The speaker says models with hundreds of billions of parameters can run on an original M1 MacBook, and gives Gemma 4 26B running on an iPhone using storage as a specific example of large-model on-device inference at reasonable speed.

“You can now run models of hundreds of billions of parameters even on your on your initial M1 MacBook”

04Audio pipeline: sub-100ms speech generation

MLX Audio's custom TTS model, Marvis, generates audio in under 100 milliseconds, and the stack also supports speech-to-text and speech-to-speech, in both Python and Swift. The speaker also demoed pointing Claude Code or Codex at MLX Audio to build a real-time transcription app in about 10 minutes.

“We started off with text-to-speech and then that on became Marvis, uh one of our custom models that can generate audio in less than 100 milliseconds”

05GPU vs. Neural Engine, and context scaling

In Q&A, the speaker confirmed MLX currently uses the GPU rather than Apple's Neural Engine, since enabling the Neural Engine requires Core ML, which he says is not yet an easy developer experience pending private-API fixes expected around WWDC. He also described a recent breakthrough, Turbo Quant, that compresses KV cache 4x and extends usable on-device context toward roughly a million tokens.

“MLX uses the GPU, not the neural engine. For you to enable the neural engine, you need Core ML”
