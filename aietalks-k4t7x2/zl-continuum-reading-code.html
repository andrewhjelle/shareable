Should AI Engineers Still Read Code in 2026? The Z/L Continuum

takeCoding agentsAgentic engineeringEvals & benchmarks

Gives a decision rule for where to spend human review attention as agents write most code: read every line of authentication, money movement, permissions, and irreversible-data paths, and let agents decompose and self-verify everything else

If you only read one thing

  • A 22,000-engineer Faros AI survey found 861% more code deletion per PR alongside 31% more PRs merged with zero review and 242% more incidents per PR (c4, c6, c7).
  • The right question isn't whether to read code, it's what proof each change needs: read every line of auth/money/permissions/irreversible-data, let agents decompose and self-verify the rest (c8).
  • Capability drift keeps moving where review happens -- from reading every line, to directing agents to the right code, to (soon) inspecting the loops that grade their own work (c9, c10).

Output is up, but so are incidents and unreviewed merges

  • Drawing on an April 2026 Faros AI survey of 22,000 engineers (the 'acceleration whiplash' report), the talk lists an 861% increase in code deletion per PR and Anthropic shipping eight times more code per quarter than in 2025.

03Reframe: route review by task, not by person
Slide at 14:20 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

Reframe: route review by task, not by person

  • The speaker argues the two conference speakers he built the continuum around (Ryan LeFebvre of OpenAI and Mario Zechner) agree more than they appear to: LeFebvre wants humans to inspect the system that catches repeated mistakes rather than every line, while Zechner wants every line read only for critical code.
  • The resulting rule: read every line of authentication, money movement, permissions, and irreversible data; decompose everything else into atomic PRs and let agents verify, trace, eval, and shadow-mode test.

“You read every line of authentication, money movement, permissions, and irreversible data.”

said at 14:20
04Where the review layer moves next
Slide at 16:30 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Where the review layer moves next

  • Andrej Karpathy is quoted saying it has never felt so tempting to stop looking at code at all, but warning not to do that in production.
  • The speaker frames this as capability drift: the review layer keeps moving up a level, from reading code, to directing agents toward the right proof, to -- with the rise of self-looping agents that write, run, and grade their own work -- inspecting the loops themselves.

“It's never felt so tempting to stop looking at code at all. But don't do this in production.”

said at 16:25

“If yesterday we inspected the outputs and we read the code, and today we inspect the task direction and kind of like directed to the right proof, maybe tomorrow we're inspecting the loops.”

said at 17:27

Commentary (ours)

The framing quietly assumes review capacity scales down as capability moves up a layer, but the survey stats he cites (more incidents, more bugs, more unreviewed merges) are evidence of the opposite happening in practice right now -- the routing table is a prescription for where review should go, not proof that teams are actually doing it (ours).
