Scaling the Next Paradigm of Heterogeneous Intelligence

SpeakerAdrian Bertagnoli · Callosum Watchyoutu.be/WRBNDpUhsJQ · 15 min SourceRecording of this talk at AI Engineer (2026), published on YouTube by the conference. patternAgentic engineeringInfra & servingEvals & benchmarks

Argues that mapping different sub-tasks to different model sizes and chips (not just picking one frontier model) delivers large cost and latency wins, with concrete numbers from recursive-language-model and web-navigation benchmarks

If you only read one thing

  • On the ULong benchmark, routing sub-context to different chips/models instead of one model on one chip was 7x cheaper and 5x faster on Cerebras, or 12x cheaper and 3x faster on SambaNova, versus GPT-5.2.
  • A heterogeneous mixture of video-action-language models beat GPT-5.2 and Gemini 2.5 on VideoWebArena by 18% and 25% respectively, while also being cheaper and faster.
  • Offloading simple sub-tasks like zooming to less-intelligent models was 11x faster and 43x cheaper than using ChatGPT for that sub-task alone.

The talk's core move is splitting one task into sub-tasks and routing each to a differently sized model or chip instead of using one frontier model for everything; the benchmark wins shown here are the vendor's own figures (ours).

01From one model on one chip to mixed models on mixed chips

From one model on one chip to mixed models on mixed chips

  • The talk contrasts today's dominant 'homogeneous' paradigm (scaling a single model architecture on a fleet of identical chips) with a 'heterogeneous' approach that mixes model architectures, sizes, and hardware for different sub-tasks.
  • The speaker says real-world problems decompose into sub-problems requiring different types of intelligence, so scaling one model type to solve all of them is inefficient.

02Recursive language models extended across hardware
Slide at 10:20 · click any slide to enlarge

Recursive language models extended across hardware

  • Building on MIT's recursive language model concept (treating context as an environment a coding agent explores via Python REPL rather than stuffing it all into one prompt), the speaker's team mapped sub-context generated during recursion onto different chips and models.
  • On the ULong benchmark this matched GPT-5.2's performance while cutting cost and latency sharply.

“recursive language models solve this problem by actually treating the context as a um environment, rather than putting it all into the prompt”

said at 07:43

“we are seven times cheaper and five times faster”

said at 09:15
03Heterogeneous mixtures beat single frontier models on web navigation
Slide at 10:30 · click any slide to enlarge · 5 more from this section below

Heterogeneous mixtures beat single frontier models on web navigation

  • For visual web navigation, mixing open and closed video-action-language models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct with Kimi K2.5) outperformed both a single strong model and GPT-5.2, because the task decomposes into visual and textual reasoning sub-steps that benefit from different models.
  • Offloading simple sub-tasks like zooming to smaller models drove large additional savings.

“we managed to beat uh the state-of-the-art of video web arena beating GPT 5.2 and Gemini uh 2.5 by 18 and 25% respectively”

said at 10:20

“we mapped certain subtasks like zooming and and creating a different visual um reasoning for the agent, we offloaded that into less intelligent models”

04Where compute is headed
Slide at 12:30 · click any slide to enlarge · 4 more from this section below

Where compute is headed

  • The speaker frames three eras of compute scaling: CPU-era speed, GPU-era massive parallelism, and a coming heterogeneous era where models, workflows, and silicon co-evolve.
  • The company has a 3 million grant with Aria (the UK's ARIA institute) to operate what it calls the first heterogeneous collocated cluster in the UK.

“compute is going to become heterogeneous mapping onto multi-agentic workloads and optimally um mapping these workloads onto different chips”

said at 12:59

Commentary (ours)

The savings numbers are compelling but come from the vendor's own benchmark setup rather than an independent evaluation, so the specific multiples (7x, 12x, 43x) should be treated as best-case figures for these particular tasks rather than general expectations (ours).
