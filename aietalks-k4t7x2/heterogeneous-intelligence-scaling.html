Scaling the Next Paradigm of Heterogeneous Intelligence

SpeakerAdrian Bertagnoli · Callosum Watchyoutu.be/WRBNDpUhsJQ
Agentic engineeringInfra & servingEvals & benchmarks

If you only read one thing

  • On the ULong benchmark, routing sub-context to different chips/models instead of one model on one chip was 7x cheaper and 5x faster on Cerebras, or 12x cheaper and 3x faster on SambaNova, versus GPT-5.2.
  • A heterogeneous mixture of video-action-language models beat GPT-5.2 and Gemini 2.5 on VideoWebArena by 18% and 25% respectively, while also being cheaper and faster.
  • Offloading simple sub-tasks like zooming to less-intelligent models was 11x faster and 43x cheaper than using ChatGPT for that sub-task alone.
01From one model on one chip to mixed models on mixed chips

The talk contrasts today's dominant 'homogeneous' paradigm (scaling a single model architecture on a fleet of identical chips) with a 'heterogeneous' approach that mixes model architectures, sizes, and hardware for different sub-tasks. The speaker says real-world problems decompose into sub-problems requiring different types of intelligence, so scaling one model type to solve all of them is inefficient.

“homogeneous intelligence in in terms of AI mainly refers to scaling single models on a fleet of identical chips”

“They decompose into sub-problems, which require vastly different types of intelligences. So, scaling a singular type of intelligence to solve these is very inefficient”

02Recursive language models extended across hardware

Building on MIT's recursive language model concept (treating context as an environment a coding agent explores via Python REPL rather than stuffing it all into one prompt), the speaker's team mapped sub-context generated during recursion onto different chips and models. On the ULong benchmark this matched GPT-5.2's performance while cutting cost and latency sharply.

“recursive language models solve this problem by actually treating the context as a um environment, rather than putting it all into the prompt”

▶ watch at 07:43

“we are seven times cheaper and five times faster”

▶ watch at 09:15
03Heterogeneous mixtures beat single frontier models on web navigation

For visual web navigation, mixing open and closed video-action-language models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct with Kimi K2.5) outperformed both a single strong model and GPT-5.2, because the task decomposes into visual and textual reasoning sub-steps that benefit from different models. Offloading simple sub-tasks like zooming to smaller models drove large additional savings.

“we managed to beat uh the state-of-the-art of video web arena beating GPT 5.2 and Gemini uh 2.5 by 18 and 25% respectively”

▶ watch at 10:20

“we mapped certain subtasks like zooming and and creating a different visual um reasoning for the agent, we offloaded that into less intelligent models”

▶ watch at 11:54
04Where compute is headed

The speaker frames three eras of compute scaling: CPU-era speed, GPU-era massive parallelism, and a coming heterogeneous era where models, workflows, and silicon co-evolve. The company has a 3 million grant with Aria (the UK's ARIA institute) to operate what it calls the first heterogeneous collocated cluster in the UK.

“compute is going to become heterogeneous mapping onto multi-agentic workloads and optimally um mapping these workloads onto different chips”

▶ watch at 12:59

Commentary (ours)

The savings numbers are compelling but come from the vendor's own benchmark setup rather than an independent evaluation, so the specific multiples (7x, 12x, 43x) should be treated as best-case figures for these particular tasks rather than general expectations (ours).
