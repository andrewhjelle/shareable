Galactica and the RLHF lesson

  • Ross Taylor recounts leading Galactica at Meta, released two weeks before ChatGPT.
  • Despite Galactica outperforming comparable base models on science benchmarks (68% vs GPT-3.5's 49%) using a far smaller, curated 105-billion-token corpus versus Chinchilla's trillion-plus tokens, it lacked ChatGPT's RLHF pipeline and the demo drew public backlash.
  • He cites InstructGPT's 1-billion-parameter RLHF model outperforming a 175-billion-parameter model as an early signal that RL, not just a good base model, was what made LLMs usable products.

02Long horizon is framed as a mindset, not just engineering

Long horizon is framed as a mindset, not just engineering

  • Chengxi Taylor argues long-horizon tasks (curing cancer, Millennium Prize problems, Mars) require patience measured in years, while current context windows (about 1 million tokens) are orders of magnitude smaller than what such tasks would require in token terms.
  • Scaling to long horizons introduces three technical problems: gradient variance that scales with trajectory length, sparse reward with credit assignment difficulty, and variable trajectory lengths complicating optimization.

“the gradient variance scales with the length. And the second is a sparse reward. And you have this credit assignment problem”

03Kelly Bench: frontier models lose money on long-horizon trading
Slide at 12:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 2 more from this section below

Kelly Bench: frontier models lose money on long-horizon trading

  • General Reasoning's Kelly Bench benchmark, which drew Financial Times coverage, gave frontier models $100K to build machine learning models trading Premier League football markets over a simulated one-year horizon.
  • Every model tested lost money.
  • Chengxi Taylor attributes this partly to the AI industry's bias toward coding and procedural tasks with narrow solution spaces, and to a lack of open-ended, multi-agent, uncertain benchmarks that better represent the real world.

“we gave all the frontier models a 100K”

04Compute tradeoffs at long horizons
Slide at 15:20 · click any slide to enlarge · 2 more from this section below

Compute tradeoffs at long horizons

  • Pipeline RL lets training start on partially-generated sequences instead of waiting for inference to finish, trading off-policy staleness for GPU utilization; the team's experience is that up to about eight off-policy steps is acceptable before GPUs sit idle waiting on long inference runs.
  • Adding a value model (critic) allows bootstrapping, getting a training signal before an episode ends, which improves GPU utilization but introduces value-model bias into training.

“from the our experience, normally off-policy up to eight steps is okay”

said at 14:55

“While you utilize the GPU fully, you introduce the value model bias”

said at 15:59

Commentary (ours)

Kelly Bench's headline result (all frontier models lost money) is a striking framing but should be read alongside the caveat that the benchmark was built by General Reasoning itself and is explicitly designed to be hard along the axis the company is pitching (long-horizon, open-ended, multi-agent tasks) (ours), it's a legitimate data point on a real gap, not necessarily evidence that all long-horizon tasks are this bad.
