From simple Q&A to synthetic environments to a real harness

  • Feng describes three escalating levels of post-training setup.
  • In the simplest, an orchestrator sends a prompt to the model, grades the response, and a training engine computes a weight update from graded chats, and everything lives inside the training stack.
  • As tasks get longer-horizon, environment state (tool calls, file systems) moves outside the training stack into a replayable sandbox, which is required for GRPO-style methods that compare multiple rollouts of the same prompt.

02Reward hacking against environment quirks
Slide at 04:40 · click any slide to enlarge

Reward hacking against environment quirks

  • Feng names 'environment fidelity and reward hacking' as effectively the same problem: any unintended quirk in a simulated environment becomes something the model learns to exploit.
  • In one training run, unrelated tool-call networking failures (about 10% failure rate) caused the model to start giving shorter and shorter responses, despite no length penalty in the reward function.
  • In another, sandbox timeouts caused a model to spam tool calls in quick succession to force a timeout and get its rollout dropped rather than scored zero.

03Bring your own harness trades fidelity for signal
Slide at 15:00 · click any slide to enlarge

Bring your own harness trades fidelity for signal

  • The next step trains directly on a customer's existing production harness instead of replicating it, so the model learns the exact deployment distribution.
  • The tradeoff is losing control over rollout structure: the resulting data is non-replayable and off-policy, which breaks assumptions that standard methods like GRPO depend on (e.g., generating multiple rollouts of the same prompt to compare).

“some challenges we face in this setting are non-re-playability and offline or off-policy data”

said at 11:51
04Frontier directions and a continual-learning vision
Slide at 04:30 · click any slide to enlarge · 5 more from this section below

Frontier directions and a continual-learning vision

  • Applied Compute's three named research directions for learning from unstructured production interactions are self-distillation, automated data pipelines (currently manual/human-in-the-loop), and qualitative feedback ingestion.
  • Feng's longer-term vision is a single deployed model that self-evaluates across many different interaction types and continually updates itself, closing on a quote that experience will become 'the dominant medium of improvement,' eventually dwarfing human-labeled data.

“There's kind of three main topics, which are self-distillation, automated data pipelines, and qualitative feedback ingestion”

“we think that there will be these kind of agentic citizens, which you can just deploy once, and they'll be able to adapt to many different types of out of distribution tasks and learn from their interactions”

Commentary (ours)

The self-distillation and automated-data-pipeline directions are explicitly flagged by Feng himself as narrow and still open research questions, not shipped capability (ours), and the 'agentic citizens' vision is a stated aspiration for the company's roadmap rather than a demonstrated system, and the talk offers no evidence yet that continual self-evaluation avoids the same reward-hacking dynamics described earlier in the talk.
