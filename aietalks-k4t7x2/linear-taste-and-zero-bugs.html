  • Linear reports about 10% of incoming bugs are automatically fixed and landed by a single-shot AI instance with no engineer involvement, a share expected to keep rising (c1).

01Faster shipping risks lower-quality software

The speaker warns that as agentic tools make it trivial to ship almost any requested feature, teams risk shipping low-quality, convoluted software simply because they now can, echoing the pre-AI dynamic he saw during Uber's hypergrowth phase where speed was prioritized over polish.

“you will effectively ship you know a software that that is not great”

02Automated bug fixing and the zero-bug policy

About 10% of bugs reported into Linear are automatically fixed and landed by a single-shot AI instance with no engineer involvement, a figure the CTO expects to climb over the coming years. Under Linear's zero-bug policy, every reported bug is automatically assigned via agents to whoever likely created or owns the area, becomes that engineer's top priority, and is typically resolved within two to three hours, never later than seven days.

03Why AI still lacks taste

The speaker argues AI coding agents don't feel or have taste, so quality and AI agents rarely belong in the same sentence. Concretely, he says AI has no concept of time when interacting with a browser -- it can look at a DOM or take a screenshot, but it doesn't experience whether a two-second click delay feels slow.

“they they don't feel they they have no taste um they they simply don't”

“AI doesn't have a concept of time and currently how it sort of interacts with your browser”

04Quality Wednesdays: hunting hidden defects manually

Linear runs a weekly ritual where every engineer must find and fix one quality issue in the product themselves. It started after the CTO gathered the team to deep-dive on a single small view-option menu and the group found 35 separate quality problems in it, prompting a company-wide push to audit the rest of the product the same way.

“what we found in it was one of the view option menus uh we found like 35 problems with with that tiny UI”

▶ watch at 13:58
05Everyone becomes a product engineer

Looking a year ahead, the CTO predicts that as AI continues to handle more implementation work, engineers will increasingly need to become product-focused -- understanding customers and good UX -- rather than simply moving data between systems.

“I think everybody will become a product engineer um in in some sense”
