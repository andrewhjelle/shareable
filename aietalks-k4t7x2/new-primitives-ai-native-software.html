If you only read one thing

  • Kramer's team built Pipecat, described as the most widely used open-source voice-agent framework, used by AWS, Nvidia, and Anthropic among others (c1)
  • He frames today's tool-calling harnesses (echoing Satya Nadella's model+data+tools loop) as the current stage in an arc that runs from web pages to web apps, and argues agents are headed toward a similarly bigger successor (c2, c3)
  • As a concrete example of that successor, his team's multiplayer game runs hundreds of simultaneous inference calls with async context compression and long-running sub-agents sharing context -- patterns he says weren't buildable a year ago (c6, c7)

02Today's agents are 1995's web pages
Slide at 02:30 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Today's agents are 1995's web pages

  • Kramer plays a clip of Satya Nadella describing current agent-building as harnessing models, data, and tools in a loop.
  • He argues this is the direct descendant of last year's AI Engineer talks about loops, tool calls, and context engineering, and compares it to the early Web: just as web pages gave way to full web and native mobile applications, he expects agents and "agents plus plus" (multi-model harnesses embedded in every piece of software) to give way to something categorically new.

“you kind of want to harness to define the models, the the data, uh and the tools. And so that you have a loop across those three”

“just like we went from web pages to full-blown web and native mobile, clearly we're going to chart a path to a new fully AI native software that comes after agents and agents plus plus”

03VisiCalc as a historical lens
Slide at 09:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

VisiCalc as a historical lens

  • Kramer traces a line from Vannevar Bush's 1945 essay through the 1980s personal computer, using VisiCalc as a case study: he argues its durability (Google Sheets and Excel are direct descendants) shows how a new abstraction can make specialized work broadly accessible without eliminating the underlying profession, offering it as a counter-argument to fears that AI will cause mass unemployment.
  • He also argues the web's defining property, in hindsight, was being multimodal (text, audio, video, data together) from its inception.

“VisiCalc is a potentially a counter-argument to the argument or the fear or the concern that AI is going to lead to mass unemployment. Because VisiCalc didn't put accountants out of business.”

said at 09:54

“with the benefit of hindsight, I now think that the single most important thing about the web was that it was multimodal from the very beginning”

said at 12:30
04A working prototype of what's next
Slide at 18:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 4 more from this section below

A working prototype of what's next

  • Kramer closes with Gradient Bang, a multiplayer game built with LLMs at the core of every interaction, running hundreds of inference calls at any given moment.
  • He says it demonstrates several sub-agent patterns -- asynchronous non-blocking context compression, long-running sub-agents that share context, progressive skill loading, dynamic UI generation -- that he says would not have been buildable even a year earlier.

“At every moment in the game, there are hundreds of inference calls happening and we couldn't have built anything like this even a year ago”

said at 19:01

“Gradient Bang demonstrates several patterns for AI sub agents such as asynchronous non-blocking context compression”

said at 19:32

Commentary (ours)

Kramer's own product (Pipecat, voice-agent infrastructure) sits squarely inside the "agents plus plus" stage he says is already being superseded, which is worth keeping in mind when weighing how much of this is forward-looking argument versus scene-setting for Daily's next move.
