If you only read one thing

  • Tan says his own coding output went from ~15 lines/day to ~400X this year on the same models everyone else uses; even heavily discounted, he estimates the real gain at 8X at the floor, 80X in the middle.
  • His model for an AI-native org: a skill file is an employee, a resolver table is an org chart, and trigger evals are performance reviews.
  • The core discipline is "never do one-off work" — once you fix something with an agent, skillify it so the org compounds instead of re-asking the same thing.

The 400X claim, and what he says it isn't

  • Tan says he wrote about 15 usable lines of code a day at his most productive in 2013, and estimates his current output at roughly 400X that, using the same Claude weights and API available to everyone.
  • He pre-empts skepticism by saying even under the most pessimistic discount for bloat and scaffolding, the gain is still 8X at the floor and 80X in the middle, and argues the leverage comes from how the work is wired, not model capability.

02Skill files, resolver tables: an org chart made of markdown

Skill files, resolver tables: an org chart made of markdown

  • Tan maps organizational structures directly onto agent tooling: a skill file is a one-capability, one-job "employee," and a resolver table (the kind built when Claude.md gets too large) functions as an org chart routing incoming tasks.
  • He describes this as the management layer that was previously missing when working with coding agents.

“A skill file is an employee. It has one capability, one job, written”

03Working memory: seven items versus a million tokens
Slide at 11:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 4 more from this section below

Working memory: seven items versus a million tokens

  • Tan contrasts human working memory ("seven plus or minus two" items, per the classic cognitive psychology finding) with an AI agent holding roughly a million tokens, which he describes to his child as being able to hold about three Harry Potter books open at once and search across them instantly.

“we only hold about seven things in our head at once. Uh, seven plus or minus two.”

04A company brain is a library plus a librarian
Slide at 12:20 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

A company brain is a library plus a librarian

  • Tan defines context engineering as the decision of which information gets loaded for a given task, and calls the resulting system (retrieval plus curation) a company brain: "the library plus the librarian." He warns that an uncurated brain becomes, in his words, a garbage dump with great search, surfacing stale facts with high confidence.

“That's context engineering. And this is what a company brain is. It's the library plus the librarian.”

“a brain nobody curates becomes a garbage dump with great search”

05The discipline: never do one-off work
Slide at 15:20 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

The discipline: never do one-off work

  • Tan's stated rule, which he says he repeats to every YC company, is to never let a fixed task remain one-off: once an agent's output is corrected and approved, the operator should convert that fix into a reusable skill file rather than re-explaining it next time.

“you actually need to at the end of that task, uh, skillify it”
