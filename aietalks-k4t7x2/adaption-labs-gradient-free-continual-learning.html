Adaption Labs: Gradient-Free Continual Learning

SpeakerSara Hooker · Adaption Watchyoutu.be/XEd_SRVHBgU
Continual learningPost-training

If you only read one thing

  • Auto Scientist, an automated model-training system, outperforms Adaption's own research staff by searching a broader space of architectures and sizes than humans typically explore.
  • It only produced real gains once the data pipeline was co-optimized alongside the model, not treated as a separate step.
  • Pre-training scale is no longer the most lucrative axis for performance (per Hooker's 'Death of Scaling' paper), which shifts advantage toward recipe/algorithm work not gated by having the most GPUs.
01A narrow historical path to the frontier

Hooker traces how becoming a frontier AI researcher has required a specific narrow path (right PhD program, right industry lab), professionalized and increasingly gated by access to compute. She frames the talk around who gets to participate in building frontier AI, noting whole regions are largely absent from documented AI breakthroughs.

“modern computer science as a field has only existed for the last 77 years”

▶ watch at 00:30
02Auto Scientist: automating the training loop itself

Adaption's Auto Scientist automates training-model design across the full loop, from data to alignment, and self-evolves its approach based on domain and data type. Hooker states it outperforms the company's own research staff, partly because it searches across many more model architectures and sizes (dense and mixture-of-experts) than a human team typically would. She reports the gains only materialized once the data pipeline was co-optimized together with the model, rather than treated as a fixed input.

“what's interesting as well is that it actually outperforms research staff”

“This only worked when we co-optimized the data”

03A quirk in the reported win rates

Hooker notes the win-rate charts shown are capped near 60% because the genetic search process was configured to exit once it crossed that threshold; that stopping condition has since been removed, and performance keeps climbing past it.

“that's because we put the budget stopping it stopping it above 60”

▶ watch at 06:41
04The end of pre-training-scale dominance

Citing her own paper on the topic, Hooker argues pre-training size is no longer the most lucrative axis for scaling model performance, evidenced by smaller models increasingly outperforming larger ones on leaderboards. Because pre-training compute needs to be co-located at large volume while inference and other compute can be distributed, this shift changes who can realistically compete on training recipes rather than raw compute access.

“empirically, we do now know that um pre-training size in particular is not your most lucrative axis of scale”

▶ watch at 10:47
05Coverage and access choices

Auto Scientist was built to cover 242 languages from day one and to target non-verifiable tasks, which Hooker says make up the bulk of everyday tasks and are where much of the near-term progress will come from.

“we decided okay, we're going to cover languages from day one, 242 languages”

▶ watch at 08:44
06Q&A: fewer than 5,000 people know how to train frontier models, and open access carries real risk

Asked how AI becomes more democratized, Hooker estimated realistically fewer than 5,000 people in the world know how to train frontier models at scale, calling that knowledge 'secret' and passed down like an apprenticeship; automating it accelerates innovation cycles by lowering the cost of asking a question. On the safety counterpoint, she said she does not believe open access carries no risk, describing it as a delicate balance between real risk and who gets to participate.

“there's very few people who know how to train frontier models. I would say realistically probably less than 5,000 in the world at scale”

“when you make a tool more readily available, there's a profile of risk associated with it”
