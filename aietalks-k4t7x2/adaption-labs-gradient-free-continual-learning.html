A narrow historical path to the frontier

  • Hooker traces how becoming a frontier AI researcher has required a specific narrow path (right PhD program, right industry lab), professionalized and increasingly gated by access to compute.
  • She frames the talk around who gets to participate in building frontier AI, noting whole regions are largely absent from documented AI breakthroughs.

02Auto Scientist: automating the training loop itself
Slide at 05:30 · click any slide to enlarge

Auto Scientist: automating the training loop itself

  • Adaption's Auto Scientist automates training-model design across the full loop, from data to alignment, and self-evolves its approach based on domain and data type.
  • Hooker states it outperforms the company's own research staff, partly because it searches across many more model architectures and sizes (dense and mixture-of-experts) than a human team typically would.
  • She reports the gains only materialized once the data pipeline was co-optimized together with the model, rather than treated as a fixed input.

“what's interesting as well is that it actually outperforms research staff”

A quirk in the reported win rates

  • Hooker notes the win-rate charts shown are capped near 60% because the genetic search process was configured to exit once it crossed that threshold; that stopping condition has since been removed, and performance keeps climbing past it.

“that's because we put the budget stopping it stopping it above 60”

04The end of pre-training-scale dominance
Slide at 11:30 · click any slide to enlarge

The end of pre-training-scale dominance

  • Citing her own paper on the topic, Hooker argues pre-training size is no longer the most lucrative axis for scaling model performance, evidenced by smaller models increasingly outperforming larger ones on leaderboards.
  • Because pre-training compute needs to be co-located at large volume while inference and other compute can be distributed, this shift changes who can realistically compete on training recipes rather than raw compute access.

“empirically, we do now know that um pre-training size in particular is not your most lucrative axis of scale”

said at 10:47
05Coverage and access choices
Slide at 08:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Coverage and access choices

  • Auto Scientist was built to cover 242 languages from day one and to target non-verifiable tasks, which Hooker says make up the bulk of everyday tasks and are where much of the near-term progress will come from.

“we decided okay, we're going to cover languages from day one, 242 languages”

said at 08:44
06Q&A: fewer than 5,000 people know how to train frontier models, and open access carries real risk
Slide at 13:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 2 more from this section below

Q&A: fewer than 5,000 people know how to train frontier models, and open access carries real risk

  • Asked how AI becomes more democratized, Hooker estimated realistically fewer than 5,000 people in the world know how to train frontier models at scale, calling that knowledge 'secret' and passed down like an apprenticeship; automating it accelerates innovation cycles by lowering the cost of asking a question.
  • On the safety counterpoint, she said she does not believe open access carries no risk, describing it as a delicate balance between real risk and who gets to participate.

“there's very few people who know how to train frontier models. I would say realistically probably less than 5,000 in the world at scale”

said at 13:54

“when you make a tool more readily available, there's a profile of risk associated with it”

said at 16:02
