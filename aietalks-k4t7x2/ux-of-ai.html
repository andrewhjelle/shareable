The UX of AI: Making AI-Powered Apps Your Users Don't Hate

takeProduct & UXAgentic engineering

Gives concrete UX patterns (source citations, pre-execution action plans, visible stop/undo controls, granular permissions) for designing agentic and AI-chat features so users trust and adopt them instead of abandoning them after one bad...

If you only read one thing

  • AI products face high stakes: users disengage after just a few bad results, so trust and UX design become the differentiator, not raw model performance.
  • Recommends citing sources, showing agent action plans before execution, and labeling AI-generated content to build trust.
  • Recommends a visible stop/undo control and granular, non-binary permissions to keep users in control.

01AI has a trust problem that compounds fast

AI has a trust problem that compounds fast

  • Nanz argues AI products currently have a serious UX problem because users don't understand how the systems work and have all seen hallucinations.
  • She says each bad result makes a user less likely to try the feature again, so builders get very few chances to earn trust before users disengage entirely.

“And right now, AI has a kind of serious UX problem.”

said at 02:02

“The more times they try an AI feature and get sub-par results, the less likely they are to engage with it again in the future.”

said at 03:04
02Build trust with citations and pre-approval of agent plans
Slide at 14:20 · click any slide to enlarge · 1 more from this section below

Build trust with citations and pre-approval of agent plans

  • Rather than telling users a product is trustworthy, she recommends citing sources and linking back to source material so users can verify AI output themselves.
  • For agentic workflows, she recommends showing the agent's action plan and getting user approval before it begins executing.

“By citing the references in an AI-generated response and linking users back directly to the source material, we give users the information they need to validate AI output themselves.”

said at 12:22

“show an action plan to the user and allow them to approve it before the agent begins work.”

said at 14:56
03Label AI content, show your work, give users an emergency stop
Slide at 18:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 6 more from this section below

Label AI content, show your work, give users an emergency stop

  • She recommends explicitly marking AI-generated content as such, and using streaming/live generation so users can see partial output and intervene early rather than waiting on a blank loading state.
  • She also recommends a clear, always-accessible stop control so users can halt an in-progress AI action at any time.

“One of the easiest things is to just mark any AI-generated content as AI-generated.”

said at 16:59

“One of the most obvious ways for us to do this is with streaming text or similar live generation of content.”

said at 19:05

“One of the most reassuring things we can offer nervous users is a clear and prominent emergency brake that they can slam to bring everything to a halt for any reason.”

said at 23:46
04Permissions need to be granular, not binary
Slide at 27:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 6 more from this section below

Permissions need to be granular, not binary

  • Nanz argues that agent permissions are rarely a simple yes/no decision — an agent might be allowed to read a database but not delete tables, or access one folder but not all folders — and that products need to expose this granularity rather than a single access toggle.

“permissions are often more than just a binary yes or no”

said at 27:55
05The differentiator is now UX, not model performance
Slide at 35:30 · click any slide to enlarge · 1 more from this section below

The differentiator is now UX, not model performance

  • She closes by arguing that since underlying model capability keeps improving and converging across vendors, the quality of the experience built around a model is what will differentiate AI-powered software going forward.

“the differentiator for the AI-powered software we build isn't performance anymore. It's the quality of the experiences that we can build around them.”

said at 34:37

Commentary (ours)

The five-pillar framing (trust, clarity, control, transparency, meaningful benefit) reads as a checklist more than a novel insight, but the specific mechanisms — pre-execution action plans, granular non-binary permissions, an always-visible stop control — are concrete enough to audit an existing agentic product against (ours).
