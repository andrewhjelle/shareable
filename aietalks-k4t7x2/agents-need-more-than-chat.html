Agents need more than a chat

SpeakerJacob Lauritzen · Legora Watchyoutu.be/XNtkiQJ49Ps · 14 min SourceRecording of this talk at AI Engineer (2026), published on YouTube by the conference. Quotes are verbatim from the talk transcript with the time they were said.

Useful for teams building agents on open-ended, hard-to-verify work (legal, and by extension other document-heavy domains): gives a concrete way to decompose a task by verifiability, an escalation path for control (planning to skills to ...

If you only read one thing

  • Planning and reviewing work, not doing it, is now the bottleneck; the verifier's rule says AI solves what's easy to verify, and legal tasks sit at very different points on that spectrum -- checking contract definitions is easy, writing a contract is easy to generate but hard to verify, and litigation strategy has no objective truth at all (c1, c2, c3, c4).
  • Trust is increased by decomposing tasks into verifiable sub-steps and adding guardrails that limit what an agent can touch; control escalates from low (judging only the finished output of a work tree) through skills (encoding judgment with contingencies) to elicitation, where an agent unblocks itself and logs its decision for later human review (c5, c6, c7, c8).
  • Chat is a low-bandwidth, one-dimensional interface that collapses a large work tree into a single thread; Legora instead builds durable artifacts like a tabular contract review where a person can flag specific items and a document where a person can highlight and edit one clause at a time (c9, c10).

01Planning and reviewing are now the bottleneck, not doing the work

Planning and reviewing are now the bottleneck, not doing the work

  • The talk opens by arguing that the economics of completing complex work have shifted in the past 6-12 months: doing the work itself has become cheap, while planning (getting requirements and specs right) and reviewing the output are now the slow, expensive parts.

“right now planning work and reviewing work is the new bottleneck. So doing the actual work is extremely cheap. It's very easy to do”

said at 02:35
02Verifier's rule: legal tasks sit at different points on the spectrum

Verifier's rule: legal tasks sit at different points on the spectrum

  • Citing a term attributed to Jason, the verifier's rule states that a solvable task easy to verify will get solved by AI.
  • Applied to legal work, checking contract definitions is easy to verify; writing a contract is easy for AI to generate but hard to verify (only a court can ultimately confirm the language works); and litigation strategy is nearly impossible to verify because different lawyers give genuinely different answers with no objective truth.

“verifier's rule is a a term that was coined by Jason, which states that if it's a task is solvable and it's easy to verify, then it's going to get solved by AI”

said at 03:05

“Writing a contract is very easy to solve, but actually extremely difficult to verify cuz if you think about it, when you write a contract”

said at 04:07

“if you ask five lawyers, "What should be the right strategy for this litigation case?" they're going to give you different answers”

said at 04:38
03Increasing trust and control

Increasing trust and control

  • Trust (how much a human needs to review) is increased by decomposing a task into verifiable sub-parts (e.g., formatting, definition-checking) and by adding guardrails that restrict what an agent can edit, read, or search.
  • Control (how well a human can steer the approach) escalates through a spectrum: a low-control work tree where judgment can only be applied to the finished root-level output, then planning, then skills that encode human judgment into specific nodes of work with built-in contingencies, then elicitation, where an unsure agent makes a decision, unblocks itself, and writes the decision to a log for later human review.

“guardrails is essentially a way to increase trust by limiting what the agent can do. So instead of being able to do all of this, you're just going to say you can only do these”

said at 07:14

“This is extremely low control because essentially, I can only impose my judgment at the root level”

said at 08:14

“Skills are really, really, really good. They are really good because the skills allow you to encode human judgment into essentially the nodes of work”

said at 09:47

“you tell the agent "If you're unsure about something, make a decision, unblock yourself, but write this to a decision log." So then the human can review the decision log afterwards”

said at 10:49
04Chat doesn't scale to a full work tree

Chat doesn't scale to a full work tree

  • As a work tree grows large, an open-ended chat becomes a low-bandwidth, one-dimensional interface that forces a person to answer dozens of questions without the context to do so well.
  • Legora instead builds durable, high-bandwidth artifacts -- a document where a person can highlight and edit a single clause, and a tabular review where the agent flags specific items for a person's take.

“you don't want this in a chat. You don't want to open up a chat and then it's infinitely long. You have to answer 50 questions”

said at 11:20

“You can highlight clause three and it will only change clause three. You can add comments. You can tag your agents. You can tag your collaborators”

said at 11:52
