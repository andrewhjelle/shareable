If you only read one thing

  • Arize instruments everything through OpenTelemetry traces and spans, plus session-level tracking of back-and-forth state, as the observability layer underneath evals.
  • Signal for evals comes in five flavors -- LLM-as-judge, human feedback, golden datasets, deterministic/logic checks, and cost -- and can be scoped at a single span, across multiple spans, over a full trajectory, or at the session/state-machine level.
  • Arize's stated end goal is to automate observability, evals, and experimentation via an AI system (Alex) callable from coding agents, shipped as open-source Phoenix (single container) or enterprise Arize AX.

Observability runs on OpenTelemetry traces and sessions

  • Arize builds its observability layer entirely on OpenTelemetry: an auto-instrumenter adds one line of code to a framework or SDK and produces traces and spans that serve as an audit record of what an agent did, since the speaker argues code itself can't audit agents or harnesses.
  • Beyond traces, sessions track the back-and-forth state of a conversation or run, which the speaker ties to a recent Anthropic paper on managed agents.

Five flavors of eval signal

  • The speaker breaks eval signal into five types: LLM-as-judge, human feedback from end users or product managers, golden datasets curated by trusted domain labelers, deterministic/logic-based checks (e.g., valid JSON schema), and cost, framed around one of three business metrics -- make money, save money, or save time.

“The way I like to break it down is is kind of these five flavors of signal. Um I think everyone here in the room has heard of LLM as a judge”

“We've all heard of golden data sets. They're extremely valuable because it's if the third column here represents quality, you know, you trust the person who who labeled this data because they know the domain”

said at 07:16

“when we think about costs, you don't always have to use an LLM call or even humans. Determinism is super nice, so think about logic or deterministic based evals”

03Eval scope: span, multi-span, trajectory, session
Slide at 05:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Eval scope: span, multi-span, trajectory, session

  • Evals can run on a single component's input/output (a span-level eval), across data passed between several components (multi-span), over an agent's whole path through the system (trajectory), or over the full session state machine, e.g. whether a user was ever frustrated.
  • The speaker gives an example where a trajectory eval surfaced that two components were called out of order because one had an undeclared dependency on the other.

“the root cause issue was that these two components are actually out of order. I did B before A and actually B has a dependency on A”

said at 05:44

“we also have like multi-span evals. So we can think of that as like hey, in order to run the eval that I want, it actually requires data across many different components in in the system”

said at 10:54
04Where Arize is headed, and its two products
Slide at 15:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 2 more from this section below

Where Arize is headed, and its two products

  • Arize's stated goal is to automate the observability-eval-experimentation-improvement flywheel so users don't have to choose their own evals; an AI system called Alex, callable from coding agents like Claude Code, can inspect traces and propose or run evals.
  • The company ships this as Arize Phoenix, an open-source single-container deployment, and Arize AX, used by large enterprises including Uber, Booking, and Reddit.

“our ultimate goal as a company is actually to automate you out of this process. Observability, evals, experimentation and improvement”

said at 14:31

“for for more of the engineering first folks, we have Arize Phoenix, which is open source. The really nice thing about Phoenix is it's single single container. You can deploy it locally”

said at 15:33

Commentary (ours)

The push to make evals themselves agent-generated (Alex proposing evals from traces rather than engineers hand-picking them) is a meaningful bet, but it also means eval quality inherits whatever blind spots the automating model has -- worth checking whether Arize exposes a way to audit what Alex chose not to test (ours).
