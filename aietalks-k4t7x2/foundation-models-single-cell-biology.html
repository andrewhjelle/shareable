takeHealthcare AIVertical AIEvals & benchmarks

Reports benchmark results showing current single-cell transformer foundation models (SCGPT-style) sometimes lose to simple linear models despite heavy compute cost, and that flow-matching models outperform them

If you only read one thing

  • NeurIPS benchmarking last year found transformer-based single-cell foundation models sometimes underperform simple linear models despite far higher training cost.
  • Flow-matching models like PrimeFlow do better than transformer/autoencoder approaches because they match the data distribution rather than compressing to a latent vector and predicting a mean.
  • RNA-seq is the dominant training modality because it's the easiest to scale (tens of millions to ~1 billion cells), but scaling data alone won't fix the field without also improving data quality and realism.

The talk's argument is that scaling RNA-seq data alone doesn't fix single-cell foundation models, since compression-based transformers lose to simple baselines while distribution-matching flow models do better on the same noisy data (ours).

Drug development isn't speeding up despite AI advances

  • Unlike Moore's Law, the number of new drugs developed per year is declining, and the drug development pipeline from research through clinical trials can take up to 10 years and cost billions with an acceptance rate around 5% or less.
  • The goal of virtual cell/tissue/human modeling is to compress this timeline, but the speaker notes gains only in early research stages won't help the full pipeline without breakthroughs across every stage.

“the number of drugs that developed each year is kind of declining, which is surprising with all the advances in technology, in AI”

03RNA-seq dominates because it's easiest to scale, but data is noisy

RNA-seq dominates because it's easiest to scale, but data is noisy

  • Of the measurement modalities (genome, RNA-seq/transcriptomics, proteomics, morphology), RNA-seq is used most for foundation model training because PCR-based technology makes it easiest to scale, with datasets reaching hundreds of millions to about 1 billion cells.
  • But identical cells don't read the same, measurements are single snapshots of continuous biological processes, and cross-lab/cross-machine measurements are inconsistent, making the data highly heterogeneous and noisy.

04Transformer models underperform relative to their cost
Slide at 12:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 2 more from this section below

Transformer models underperform relative to their cost

  • Transformer-based models like SCGPT treat each cell as a sentence and genes as tokens, using BERT-style masked gene-count prediction, but compressing single-cell data into a latent vector loses information.
  • Comprehensive NeurIPS benchmarking on multimodal data and perturbation-response modeling found these expensive-to-train models are not performing as well as comparable models in language and imaging domains, and are sometimes matched or beaten by simple linear models.

05Flow matching models perform better

Flow matching models perform better

  • Flow-matching models such as PrimeFlow, which predict the data distribution starting from Gaussian noise rather than compressing and decoding, outperform transformer/autoencoder-based models like CPA in benchmark comparisons, because autoencoder approaches tend to just predict the mean rather than capture the true distribution.

Commentary (ours)

The pattern of transformer scaling underperforming simple baselines in single-cell biology (unlike language/vision) suggests the field may need a distribution-modeling paradigm shift before scale alone helps -- worth watching whether flow-matching approaches get the compute investment that autoregressive transformers currently receive (ours).
