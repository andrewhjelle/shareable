The Golden Age of AI Engineering

SpeakerAlexander Embiricos, Romain Huet & Peter Steinberger · OpenAI Watchyoutu.be/pMggiOb18tc
Agentic engineeringCoding agentsContext engineering

If you only read one thing

  • GPT-5.6 model releases now ship roughly every 6 weeks instead of every 15 months, with Terra matching 5.5-level intelligence at half the cost and Soul running at 750 tokens/sec on Cerebras (c1, c2, c4).
  • OpenAI's own Codex team has shifted from managing many terminal sessions to running a long-lived manager agent that delegates to worker agents, enabled by server-side compaction (c5, c6, c7).

01Model cadence and pricing claims

OpenAI says its release cadence has gone from roughly every 15 months to about every 6 weeks. On terminal-bench-style comparisons, GPT-5.6 Terra is presented as matching GPT-5.5-level intelligence at half the cost, and a lower-cost model (Luna) is priced at $1/million input and $6/million output tokens. Separately, GPT-5.6 Soul is shown running on Cerebras hardware at 750 tokens/second.

“we used to ship a new model every 15 months or so, and now it's about roughly every 6 weeks”

02From terminal-juggling to manager/worker agents

Peter Steinberger describes his own workflow shift: he used to run 10+ terminal windows and manually poll/steer/queue work for each agent. He now talks primarily to one long-running manager agent that delegates to worker agents, reviews finished PRs, and steers at the outer loop rather than watching code generate in real time.

“I was juggling 10 or more terminal windows, always waiting for one of them to finish, so I could steal the agent and queue new work”

▶ watch at 19:10

“The manager wakes up, reads it against the project's goals, notes, and vision, and decides whether it might be a fit”

▶ watch at 22:19
03What made the manager/worker loop possible

Steinberger attributes the shift to three changes: server-side compaction that made long-running tasks reliable enough to stop optimizing around first sessions, coordination that lets one thread steer multiple projects, and automation/triggers that can wake the manager when something happens.

“server-side compaction made long-running tasks reliable enough that I stopped optimizing around first sessions”

▶ watch at 20:14
04The new constraint is attention, and harnesses lag models

Steinberger says his constraint moved from tokens, to compute, to attention -- and unlike tokens or compute, attention can't simply be scaled up. He states that models are currently advancing faster than the harnesses and organizations built around them, calling harness/org design the next engineering problem.

“Now, I'm primarily constrained by attention. And unlike tokens or compute, I can't simply add more of it”

▶ watch at 21:16

“Models are advancing faster than the harnesses and organizations around them. Designing those things is the next engineering problem”

▶ watch at 24:26

Commentary (ours)

The manager-of-managers framing matches a broader pattern across AI Engineer talks this cycle (Zed's ACP, Poolside's self-testing agents) where the bottleneck keeps moving from model capability to harness/orchestration design -- worth tracking whether OpenAI's own internal tooling (app server, Agents.md) becomes the reference implementation other teams converge on (ours).
