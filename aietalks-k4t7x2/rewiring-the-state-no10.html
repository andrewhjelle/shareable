No. 10 Downing Street built an insurgency-model data team: outsider-only hiring, embedded forward-deployed engineers, AISI Inspect agent-autonomy evals, and sycophancy red-teaming before rollout.

If you only read one thing

  • No.10's data science team frames UK public service delivery as a systemic productivity crisis (7 and 1/4 million on NHS waiting lists, only one in five planning applications decided on time) with a cited 40 billion pound annual AI productivity opportunity.
  • Their response is an 'insurgency model': a small team with political backing, market-rate pay, and an outsider-only hiring bar (about 0.7-0.8% acceptance) that embeds forward-deployed engineers directly with policy, legal, and operational teams.
  • Early wins include replacing a 1.5 million pound external legal review of the UK statute book with an in-house tool, plus agent-autonomy evals (AISI's Inspect tool) and AI-tutor safety benchmarking, alongside an acknowledged sycophancy risk that they mitigate through red-teaming before user rollout.

Why this talk is a blueprint, not a take (ours)

Scope: No.10's insurgency-model data science team: how it is staffed, how it picks projects inside government, and the evals/red-teaming gate before tools reach users.

A repeatable staffing and delivery pattern (political mandate, market-rate pay, outsider-only hiring, forward-deployed engineers embedded with existing teams) paired with a portfolio of shipped tools, from a statute-book analyzer to a delivery-report red-teaming PMO tool to planning-application digitization. Riskier tools, like AI policy simulation and AI tutors, go through model red-teaming and benchmark evaluation before rollout.

Prerequisites the talk implies:

  • Executive-level political backing to grant the team unusual autonomy and market-rate pay inside a hierarchical organization
  • A hiring process built specifically to filter for technical skill rather than standard institutional recruitment criteria
  • Existing teams (legal, policy, delivery, comms) willing to have an engineer embedded and observe their workflows directly
  • A partner body or process for evals and red-teaming (the talk names AISI's Inspect tool) before an AI tool reaches real users
  • Tolerance for a team that operates outside normal rules as an explicit, temporary proof point rather than standard practice

What the talk does not say:

  • Total headcount of the team or budget beyond the market-rate pay framing
  • How long the 0.7-0.8% hiring pipeline takes end to end, or its applicant volume
  • Quantified impact of the delivery red-teaming tool or the policy simulation tool, unlike the statute-book tool's cited savings
  • Detail on how the AI-tutor cognitive-load benchmark is scored or what threshold counts as safe
  • What happens when red-teaming does flag a sycophancy or safety problem, since the speaker only describes it as rare so far
  • How the insurgency model is meant to formally become business-as-usual rather than staying a bypass of normal rules

If you were to replicate one thing first: Start by securing an explicit political or executive mandate for a small team to operate outside standard hiring and process rules, since the speaker frames that mandate as the precondition for everything else.
