01A productivity crisis and a systemic bet

The speaker cites 7 and 1/4 million people on NHS waiting lists and roughly 350,000 backlogged court cases as evidence of a public-sector productivity crisis, alongside a Tony Blair Institute estimate of a 40 billion pound annual productivity opportunity from AI in government. He frames government as a 400,000-person 'industry' rather than a single organization, ripe for disruption but historically poor at building technical teams due to pay, hierarchy, and bureaucracy.

“there are 7 and 1/4 million people that are on NHS waiting lists. There are I think about 350,000 court cases that are stuck in a backlog.”

02An 'insurgency model': recruiting outsiders

The team operates with a mandate and political backing from No.10, pays market rates, and recruits exclusively from outside government through a selection process with roughly a 0.7-0.8% success rate. As of the talk, the team has forward-deployed engineers embedded for the first time in No.10's history with policy advisers, lawyers, and comms teams, observing workflows and shipping solutions in weeks.

“we've got a success rate of about 0.7, 0.8%. Um and most interestingly of all, and this is what differentiates us, uh we recruit exclusively outsiders.”

03Low-hanging fruit that also saves money

One example: the Cabinet Office was about to pay an outside law firm 1.5 million pounds to analyze the entire UK statute book; instead, an embedded engineer built an in-house tool with the in-house legal team in a couple of weeks, saving money and making the analysis repeatable as laws change. The team also points to the underlying problem such tools target: only one in five planning applications in the UK are currently decided on time.

“the cabinet office was about to spend 1.5 million pounds on getting an outside firm of lawyers to come in and do analysis of the entire UK statute book”

04Scaling to partners: agent evals and a sycophancy risk

Team alumni now sit inside partner bodies like the AI Safety Institute (AISI), which built the Inspect tool -- described as an isolated environment for testing what AI agents actually do when given autonomy and tools -- and inside AI-tutor safety work benchmarking frontier models on classroom-relevant metrics. Asked directly about sycophancy in a policy-simulation tool, the speaker calls it 'a very real risk' that the team addresses by red-teaming models before putting them in users' hands.

“there's a CFI isolated environment for testing what AI agents actually do when you give them autonomy and tools”

“that's a very real risk. Um so, it's it's not something that we've encountered too much, but it's only because we have um sort of red team the models for that before we've put it into the hands of users.”
