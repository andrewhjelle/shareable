Building Generative Image & Video models at Scale

SpeakerSander Dieleman · Google DeepMind Watchyoutu.be/xOP1PM8fwnk
Generative mediaData curationPost-training

If you only read one thing

  • Diffusion models train on learned latent representations rather than raw pixels, cutting tensor size by roughly two orders of magnitude, which is what makes 1080p-scale video training feasible at all (c2).
  • Classifier-free guidance trades sample diversity for quality by amplifying the difference between conditional and unconditional predictions, and it is described as now essentially always left on since one 2021 paper (c4, c5).
  • New conditioning signals like camera control or reference images are typically introduced in a post-training phase rather than pretraining, because that structured conditioning data is rarely available at pretraining scale (c7).
01Why data curation gets underrated

The speaker argues data curation is essential to high-quality results but is structurally under-invested in, because research training incentivizes using standard benchmark datasets rather than inspecting and improving one's own data. He frames time spent improving data as sometimes a better investment than tweaking the model itself.

“it's really important to emphasize just how important data curation is in training these these large-scale models”

▶ watch at 02:33
02Compressing pixels into a latent space

Rather than feeding raw pixel grids into a diffusion model, teams train an autoencoder to compress images and video into a smaller latent representation that preserves spatial/topological structure while discarding fine texture detail. For video especially, the extra time dimension adds redundancy that this compression exploits, shrinking the data the model must handle by about two orders of magnitude.

“you're often able to reduce the size of these tensors that you have to work with by by two orders of magnitude”

▶ watch at 07:43
03Diffusion as coarse-to-fine generation

Using Fourier analysis of image spectra, the talk describes diffusion's noise-adding process as progressively obscuring higher-frequency detail first, then lower frequencies, meaning sampling effectively generates images from coarse structure to fine detail. The speaker calls this 'spectral auto-regression' — an approximate analogue to token-by-token autoregression, but in frequency space.

“diffusion is basically spectral auto-regression, right? Because it's essentially allowing you to generate images from coarse to fine.”

▶ watch at 19:06
04Guidance: trading diversity for quality

Classifier-free guidance amplifies the difference between a model's conditional (with prompt) and unconditional (without prompt) predictions, and turning up the guidance scale sharply reduces sample diversity while improving perceived quality. Since a 2021 scaling paper demonstrated the effect, the speaker says the field has treated guidance as essentially mandatory.

“if you crank up what's called the guidance scale, if you crank up this hyperparameter, um diversity of your samples is going to greatly reduce”

▶ watch at 24:15

“After this paper, everyone just always uses guidance. Like nobody ever turns it off anymore cuz it's so essential to getting good samples from these models.”

▶ watch at 26:52
05Distillation, sharding, and post-training control

For diffusion models, distillation targets sampling steps rather than parameter count — the goal is fewer denoising passes, not a smaller model, unlike the usual LLM sense of the term. Training itself relies on JAX for automatic model sharding across chips once data parallelism alone is insufficient. Conditioning signals beyond text (camera motion, reference images, timing) are usually added in post-training, since that data is scarce during pretraining.

“we're not going to try and make the the model smaller, which is usually what distillation refers to in the context of large language models”

▶ watch at 27:32

“often you don't have this kind of conditioning information for most of your pre-training data set. So, it can actually be quite useful to do that in post-training.”

▶ watch at 31:00

“we tend to use JAX to build these models. JAX has some really good tooling that sort of does that for you, right?”
