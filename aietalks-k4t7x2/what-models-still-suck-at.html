What Do Models Still Suck At?

If you only read one thing

  • On BullshitBench (nonsense questions), the latest Claude/Sonnet models push back most; GPT and Gemini models go along with the nonsense roughly half the time, and even models that appear to push back often still partly accommodate the false premise (c1, c2).
  • Reasoning mode isn't a reliable fix: on some puzzles it makes performance worse, and GPT-5.4 was observed to flag that a question doesn't make sense yet still spend paragraphs trying to solve it anyway (c3, c4).
  • On LMArena's 'both responses bad' dissatisfaction-rate metric across ~40,000 expert-category votes among top-25 models, the rate keeps dropping (about 9% now) but progress is uneven: software's rate fell from 23.5% to 13%, while gaming shows little improvement (c6, c7, c8, c9).

The talk's argument: inconsistent pushback on nonsense and unreliable reasoning gains motivate a stricter metric (dissatisfaction rate on hard expert prompts), which shows real but uneven progress across categories (ours).

BullshitBench: what happens when you ask nonsense

  • The speaker's own BullshitBench (155 questions) asks models nonsense questions and grades responses with an LLM judge.
  • The latest Sonnet/Claude models push back clearly most often, with Qwen and the latest Grok also doing reasonably well; GPT and Gemini models go along with the nonsense about half the time.
  • Even responses graded as a clear pushback often still partly try to accommodate the false premise on closer reading of the traces.

02Reasoning isn't a reliable fix

Reasoning isn't a reliable fix

  • Cranking up reasoning effort is often assumed to fix a model's failure on a hard puzzle, but the speaker's data shows reasoning sometimes makes performance worse rather than better.
  • Reading GPT-5.4's traces, he found the model would sometimes note a question's premise doesn't make sense in a single line, then still spend twenty paragraphs trying to solve it anyway.

“reasoning often actually goes in reverse and doesn't help. It actually makes it worse”

03Arena's dissatisfaction-rate metric

Arena's dissatisfaction-rate metric

  • LMArena lets users flag when both anonymous model responses in a battle are bad, which the speaker calls the dissatisfaction rate.
  • Narrowing nearly 6 million prompts to the hardest 'expert category' battles between the top 25 models yields about 40,000 prompts to analyze by subcategory.

04Uneven progress by category

Uneven progress by category

  • The overall dissatisfaction rate for software-related expert prompts fell from 23.5% (Q2 2024) to 13% (Q1 2026), which the speaker calls a nice improvement, but not all categories moved: gaming showed little sign of improvement, and he argues LLMs still don't understand how to build an actual game.
