01The caching economics behind compaction

Providers cache previously sent tokens, making reused context dramatically cheaper to resend, up to 50x cheaper on some APIs like DeepSeek. Because compacting or summarizing context produces a new, uncached context, compaction only pays for itself if it compresses the context by more than 50x — otherwise summarization becomes a net cost, which the speakers call "potentially a trap."

02The main finding: keeping full history wins

Across 11 presets tested on their production AI tutor (full history, sliding window, summarization, selective retention, and others), leaving the context entirely untouched was, unexpectedly, the best strategy for recalling facts across a multi-turn session.

“we weren't expecting this, but basically not touching um the context was actually the best the best strategy for recovering this fact over time over multiple uh messages.”

▶ watch at 43:32
03DeepSeek confirms it: compaction costs recall

In a follow-up evaluation on DeepSeek v4 Flash, keeping full conversation history answered student questions correctly 95% of the time, versus only 32% when the context had been summarized or compacted first. The full-history approach also held up to 800,000 tokens of context without measurable context rot.

“whereas if I summarize first or if I compact um the context I had it only gave me the answer back 32% of the time.”

▶ watch at 51:54

“we went up to 800k tokens and you know we did not um see any sort of context rot in that case.”

04Cost at scale and the production decision

At an estimated volume of about 1,000 students, running the tutor on Gemini would cost roughly $40,000 a month versus about $1,900 a month on DeepSeek. The team's takeaway is not to compact by default but to first identify the actual constraint driving the decision; their shipped AI tutor keeps full history and only starts compacting after 30,000 tokens.

“it scales up for like let's say if we have like thousand students uh you know Gemini costs us about like $40,000 40,000 a month whereas Deep Seek Deep Seek was around 1,900 a month”

Commentary (ours)

This result comes from one team's specific evaluation — an AI tutor with 11-13 turn sessions, tested mainly on Gemini and DeepSeek — so it's worth treating "don't compact by default" as a claim to re-test against your own session lengths and provider caching behavior rather than a universal rule (ours).
