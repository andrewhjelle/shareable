patternContext engineeringMemoryEvals & benchmarks

Informs the build decision for any team running an LLM agent or chatbot with multi-turn memory: whether to invest in compaction and summarization machinery at all

If you only read one thing

  • The workshop's central, counterintuitive finding: leaving full conversation history untouched beat sliding windows, summarization, and other compaction techniques on cost, latency, and recall in their tests. (c1,c6)
  • Compaction breaks prompt caching, so it only pays off if it compresses context by more than 50x; otherwise summarization is "potentially a trap" that dropped recall from 95% to 32% in their DeepSeek tests. (c2,c3,c4,c5)
  • Production guidance: don't compact by default, name your actual constraint first. Their shipped tutor keeps full history until 30k tokens, running DeepSeek v4 Flash at roughly $1,900/month versus Gemini's estimated ~$40,000/month at the same scale. (c8,c9,c10)

The caching economics behind compaction

  • Providers cache previously sent tokens, making reused context dramatically cheaper to resend, up to 50x cheaper on some APIs like DeepSeek.
  • Because compacting or summarizing context produces a new, uncached context, compaction only pays for itself if it compresses the context by more than 50x — otherwise summarization becomes a net cost, which the speakers call "potentially a trap."

The main finding: keeping full history wins

  • Across 11 presets tested on their production AI tutor (full history, sliding window, summarization, selective retention, and others), leaving the context entirely untouched was, unexpectedly, the best strategy for recalling facts across a multi-turn session.

“we weren't expecting this, but basically not touching um the context was actually the best the best strategy for recovering this fact over time over multiple uh messages.”

DeepSeek confirms it: compaction costs recall

  • In a follow-up evaluation on DeepSeek v4 Flash, keeping full conversation history answered student questions correctly 95% of the time, versus only 32% when the context had been summarized or compacted first.
  • The full-history approach also held up to 800,000 tokens of context without measurable context rot.

“whereas if I summarize first or if I compact um the context I had it only gave me the answer back 32% of the time.”

Cost at scale and the production decision

  • At an estimated volume of about 1,000 students, running the tutor on Gemini would cost roughly $40,000 a month versus about $1,900 a month on DeepSeek.
  • The team's takeaway is not to compact by default but to first identify the actual constraint driving the decision; their shipped AI tutor keeps full history and only starts compacting after 30,000 tokens.

Commentary (ours)

This result comes from one team's specific evaluation — an AI tutor with 11-13 turn sessions, tested mainly on Gemini and DeepSeek — so it's worth treating "don't compact by default" as a claim to re-test against your own session lengths and provider caching behavior rather than a universal rule (ours).
