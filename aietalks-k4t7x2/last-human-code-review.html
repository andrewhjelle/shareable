For teams deciding when it's safe to let AI-generated code skip human review, this talk names the actual blocker as codified context and governance rather than model capability, and gives a concrete failure mode to watch for: shipping AI...

If you only read one thing

  • Friedman splits engineering orgs into two camps: those requiring human review of every line, and those that ship fast and fix bugs after they reach production (c3)
  • He argues code-review model quality has plateaued — the real bottleneck is fragmented context (agents.md, tribal knowledge, Slack threads) with no shared governance layer (c1, c4)
  • Teams already shipping AI-generated code faster than humans can review it are not ahead of the curve — they're already exposed, unless the context and governance layer is built first (c5)
▣The system in one picture (ours, from the slides and the transcript)

Friedman's argument, as we read it: the two camps split over a question that context scarcity, not model quality, actually answers, and skipping that governance layer is what turns fast shipping into exposure rather than progress (ours).

Two camps on AI-introduced bugs

  • Friedman poses to the audience whether human review of every PR diff will still be mandatory by the end of 2026.
  • He describes two schools of thought he heard from engineering leaders: one insists every line must be human-trusted before it ships, the other accepts that bugs will reach production and get fixed quickly afterward because velocity matters more.

Context, not model quality, is the real bottleneck

  • He argues that code-review model benchmarks haven't moved much across recent model generations — the limiting factor is context.
  • Teams' rules and standards are scattered across agents.md, cloud.md, skills.md, and Slack threads with no shared source of truth, which he calls a missing governance layer standing between current tooling and trustworthy automated review.

Where this is headed

  • Friedman frames the end state as moving from artificial intelligence to what he calls artificial wisdom — codifying the judgment senior developers currently hold in their heads so both agents and humans can act on it.
  • Qodo's stated target is reaching 2027 with zero outages and zero critical or high-severity production bugs.

Commentary (ours)

Qodo sells a code-governance platform, so the framing here — a missing governance layer as the real blocker, a 2027 zero-outage target — doubles as a product pitch. The two-camps observation and the plateaued-model-quality claim are worth weighing on their own merits separately from that incentive.
