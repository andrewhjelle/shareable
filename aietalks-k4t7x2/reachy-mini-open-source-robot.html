Reachy Mini: the $300 open source robot you can actually hack

SpeakerAndres Marafioti · Hugging Face Watchyoutu.be/0jeZfjJMfmo
Voice & realtimeOpen sourceProduct & UX

If you only read one thing

  • Reachy Mini ships at $300 or $450 (vs $50K+ for humanoid robots) and unassembled, explicitly targeting hackers and students rather than enterprise buyers (c1, c2, c10).
  • 7,500 units shipped, and the voice-chat app is Hugging Face's most-used app by far, running VAD, Parakeet speech-to-text, an LLM, and Coqui TTS (c3, c4).
  • Optimizing Coqui TTS with a static KV cache and CUDA graph capture raised its real-time factor from 0.8x to 5.8x, cutting time-to-first-audio to milliseconds (c6, c7).
01Robots are improving but staying expensive

The speaker frames current humanoid robots and self-driving cars as improving rapidly but still priced at minimum in the mid five-figure range (Waymo mid six figures), a price point that isn't coming down, which he says makes them impractical to prototype with at, e.g., a high school.

“these things are really, really expensive. They're all at least in mid five-figure range, so 50K up”

02Reachy Mini: two price points, ships unassembled

Reachy Mini sells at $450 (with a Raspberry Pi and battery) or $300 (without), and ships unassembled so the buyer builds and can later repair it themselves. The design deliberately avoids a humanoid form so people approach it as a new kind of interaction rather than a human replacement.

“we sell two of these robots. Uh they are both uh 450 and 300 USD”

“it's an expressive robot that you can talk to it, but it doesn't look like a human”

03Deployment scale

Hugging Face has shipped 7,500 Reachy Mini units, and the built-in voice-chat app is the most-used app on the robot by a wide margin.

“we have now shipped 7,500 of these robots, which means we have a pretty sizable fleet of people talking to the robot. It is our most used app by far”

▶ watch at 10:03
04Voice pipeline architecture

The speech-to-speech pipeline runs voice activity detection, then Parakeet for speech-to-text (transcribing every 150 milliseconds and sending partial transcriptions), an LLM for the reply and tool calling, and Coqui TTS for speech output. Conversation nodes and LLM inference are scaled separately so that heavy conversational load on one node doesn't spike latency for the others.

“we are using Parakeet because it's super fast. So, we transcribe every 150 milliseconds and we send back the partial transcriptions”

▶ watch at 10:34

“we scale that differently because each of the conversation nodes can have an amount of concurrent users without the latency spiking up too much”

▶ watch at 11:38
05Optimizing Coqui TTS for real-time speed

The released Coqui 3 TTS model generated full output non-streaming and ran 500 autoregressive steps per audio packet with constant CPU-GPU round trips due to a dynamic KV cache. Switching to a static KV cache enabled CUDA graph capture, moving the real-time factor from 0.8x (slower than real time) to 5.8x and cutting time-to-first-audio from several seconds to a few milliseconds.

“We went on a real-time factor from being below real time, so at 0.8”

▶ watch at 15:12

“We changed that for a static KV cache. We used more RAM from the get-go, but that makes it faster”
