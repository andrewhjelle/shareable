Reachy Mini: the $300 open source robot you can actually hack

It is a product narrative (pricing, shipped units, design philosophy) with one technical aside on TTS optimization, not a full system walkthrough (ours).

If you only read one thing

  • Reachy Mini ships at $300 or $450 (vs $50K+ for humanoid robots) and unassembled, explicitly targeting hackers and students rather than enterprise buyers (c1, c2, c10).
  • 7,500 units shipped, and the voice-chat app is Hugging Face's most-used app by far, running VAD, Parakeet speech-to-text, an LLM, and Coqui TTS (c3, c4).
  • Optimizing Coqui TTS with a static KV cache and CUDA graph capture raised its real-time factor from 0.8x to 5.8x, cutting time-to-first-audio to milliseconds (c6, c7).

Reachy Mini: two price points, ships unassembled

  • Reachy Mini sells at $450 (with a Raspberry Pi and battery) or $300 (without), and ships unassembled so the buyer builds and can later repair it themselves.
  • The design deliberately avoids a humanoid form so people approach it as a new kind of interaction rather than a human replacement.

04Voice pipeline architecture
Slide at 10:30 · click any slide to enlarge · 4 more from this section below

Voice pipeline architecture

  • The speech-to-speech pipeline runs voice activity detection, then Parakeet for speech-to-text (transcribing every 150 milliseconds and sending partial transcriptions), an LLM for the reply and tool calling, and Coqui TTS for speech output.
  • Conversation nodes and LLM inference are scaled separately so that heavy conversational load on one node doesn't spike latency for the others.

“we are using Parakeet because it's super fast. So, we transcribe every 150 milliseconds and we send back the partial transcriptions”

said at 10:34

“we scale that differently because each of the conversation nodes can have an amount of concurrent users without the latency spiking up too much”

said at 11:38
05Optimizing Coqui TTS for real-time speed
Slide at 14:30 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Optimizing Coqui TTS for real-time speed

  • The released Coqui 3 TTS model generated full output non-streaming and ran 500 autoregressive steps per audio packet with constant CPU-GPU round trips due to a dynamic KV cache.
  • Switching to a static KV cache enabled CUDA graph capture, moving the real-time factor from 0.8x (slower than real time) to 5.8x and cutting time-to-first-audio from several seconds to a few milliseconds.

“We went on a real-time factor from being below real time, so at 0.8”

said at 15:12

“We changed that for a static KV cache. We used more RAM from the get-go, but that makes it faster”

said at 14:41
