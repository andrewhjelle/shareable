01On-device throughput numbers

Running Gemma 4 (sourced at 8-bit, quantized down to 4-bit) through MLX on a recent iPhone produces roughly 40 tokens per second, demonstrated live and offline in the talk. On older iPhone hardware, throughput drops to around 20 tokens per second, which the speaker still considers usable for most application use cases.

“if you take Gemma 4 8-bit quantized in 4-bit it's extremely fast. Like it can run easily at 40 token 40 token per second.”

02Quantization sweet spot

For on-device deployment, the speaker recommends staying between 4-bit and 8-bit quantization, using 8-bit mainly for already-small models. Going below 4-bit starts to visibly degrade output quality, so 4-bit is presented as the practical floor.

“4-bit is the lower I would go. And 8-bit is the higher I would go if you're using really small models.”

▶ watch at 05:11

“under like 4-bit it's getting it's starting to get a lot of impact on the output. And and the model are not that great”

03How fast new models reach on-device form

Hugging Face's MLX community hosts pre-quantized weights (4-bit, 6-bit, full precision, etc.) for thousands of models, and new releases from labs typically appear there quantized within about 30 minutes of release, according to the speaker.

“When a model is is released by a by a lab you will directly have it almost 30 minutes after release quantized in 4-bit, 6-bit and everything”

04Tool calling and current limits

MLX Swift LM, the framework the Locally AI app is built on, supports tool calling but not yet structured generation, though the speaker notes third-party packages are working to add that on top.

“So, it support tool calling. Um not yet custom not yet structured generation.”
