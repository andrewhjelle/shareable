01AI companies are growing three times faster than SaaS, straining pricing

Pant cites Stripe data showing the top 100 AI companies reached $20M ARR in 20 months, versus 65 months for the top 100 SaaS companies. He frames this speed as the source of new pricing difficulty: companies scale and go global faster than their pricing models can keep up with.

“The top 100 AI companies took 20 months to get to 20 million ARR versus the top 100 SaaS companies which took 65 months.”

02Why neither pure subscription nor pure usage-based pricing works

Unlike traditional SaaS's stable 80-85% gross margins, AI margins move with usage. He warns that 5-10% of power users can consume 80% of compute, creating margin risk under flat subscriptions, while pure usage-based pricing makes customers hesitant to experiment because they can't predict their bill.

“5 to 10% of your users can use 80% of your compute.”

▶ watch at 02:04
03Survey data: top concerns, and the move to hybrid pricing

Pant cites survey figures: 33% of AI-powered businesses cite unpredictable compute costs as a concern, 41% cite difficulty defining delivered value, and 84% say pricing isn't keeping pace with product velocity. Against that backdrop, hybrid pricing adoption rose from 6% in 2024 to 41%, hypergrowth companies (100%+ YoY growth) change pricing 3+ times within 2 years far more often than low-growth peers, and 53% of hypergrowth companies offer clear value-based pricing versus 26% of low-growth peers.

“33% of our AI-powered business site unpredictable compute costs as their concern. 41% say that defining the value that is being delivered is is a concern”

04A five-step framework: value, metric, model, guardrails, iterate

Pant lays out a five-step framework, starting with defining the value the customer perceives (not what the product technically does), then picking a charge metric (consumption, workflow, or outcome-based), then choosing a pricing model (subscription, usage, or hybrid), then building guardrails, and finally iterating. On guardrails specifically, he recommends usage caps (e.g. stopping after 100 credits until the customer pays more or waits for the next cycle) and automated notifications at 50%, 70%, and 90% of allocated limits to build trust before a surprise bill erodes it.

“we've got a five-step uh a five-step framework for this and the step one of that framework is you define your value”
