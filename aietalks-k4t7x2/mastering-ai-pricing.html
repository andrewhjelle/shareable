If you only read one thing

  • AI companies reach $20M ARR in 20 months versus 65 months for SaaS, but 5-10% of power users can burn 80% of compute, and pure subscription or pure usage-based pricing each fail to protect margins on their own.
  • Hybrid pricing adoption jumped from 6% in 2024 to 41%, and hypergrowth companies (100%+ YoY growth) change pricing 3+ times in 2 years versus only 22% of low-growth companies.
  • Pant's five-step framework -- define value, pick a charge metric, choose a pricing model, build guardrails, then iterate -- is presented as the approach behind the 78% of AI companies Stripe says build their billing on its platform.

Why neither pure subscription nor pure usage-based pricing works

  • Unlike traditional SaaS's stable 80-85% gross margins, AI margins move with usage.
  • He warns that 5-10% of power users can consume 80% of compute, creating margin risk under flat subscriptions, while pure usage-based pricing makes customers hesitant to experiment because they can't predict their bill.

“5 to 10% of your users can use 80% of your compute.”

Survey data: top concerns, and the move to hybrid pricing

  • Pant cites survey figures: 33% of AI-powered businesses cite unpredictable compute costs as a concern, 41% cite difficulty defining delivered value, and 84% say pricing isn't keeping pace with product velocity.
  • Against that backdrop, hybrid pricing adoption rose from 6% in 2024 to 41%, hypergrowth companies (100%+ YoY growth) change pricing 3+ times within 2 years far more often than low-growth peers, and 53% of hypergrowth companies offer clear value-based pricing versus 26% of low-growth peers.

04A five-step framework: value, metric, model, guardrails, iterate
Slide at 06:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

A five-step framework: value, metric, model, guardrails, iterate

  • Pant lays out a five-step framework, starting with defining the value the customer perceives (not what the product technically does), then picking a charge metric (consumption, workflow, or outcome-based), then choosing a pricing model (subscription, usage, or hybrid), then building guardrails, and finally iterating.
  • On guardrails specifically, he recommends usage caps (e.g. stopping after 100 credits until the customer pays more or waits for the next cycle) and automated notifications at 50%, 70%, and 90% of allocated limits to build trust before a surprise bill erodes it.

“we've got a five-step uh a five-step framework for this and the step one of that framework is you define your value”
