Explains the actual hardware reason text diffusion models are faster than autoregressive ones (fewer memory transfers, not raw compute), and where the real trade-off bites: much lower latency per user but worse throughput at large batch ...

If you only read one thing

  • Text diffusion trades throughput for latency: it needs far fewer memory transfers per response than autoregressive decoding, but multiple forward passes over a batch make it more expensive to serve at scale (c3, c2).
  • Gemini diffusion hit ~2,000 tokens/second in its research preview and could self-correct mistakes mid-generation because it sees future tokens, something GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 Flash could not do on the same math problem (c4, c5).
  • No lab is putting text diffusion into flagship hosted models yet because of the throughput cost, but it's already used in on-device/robotics settings where batching isn't the bottleneck (c7, c8).

01How text diffusion differs from next-token generation
Slide at 02:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

How text diffusion differs from next-token generation

  • Standard models generate one token at a time, conditioned on prior tokens.
  • Text diffusion instead initializes an entire block of tokens as random noise and iteratively denoises the whole canvas over several passes, attending bidirectionally rather than only to the past.
  • Google DeepMind's Gemini diffusion research preview, released about a year before this talk, was a Gemini variant using this approach and was opened to roughly 100,000 people.

“a research demo release 1 year ago now called Gemini diffusion which was a variant of a Gemini model which did text diffusion instead of auto aggressive next token generation”

Why it's faster: memory bandwidth, not compute

  • GPUs/TPUs are bandwidth-constrained relative to their flops, so autoregressive generation is memory-bound: each token requires streaming the full model and KV cache through the tensor core.

03Bidirectional self-correction and adaptive computation
Slide at 10:50 · click any slide to enlarge

Bidirectional self-correction and adaptive computation

  • Because diffusion sees the full canvas including not-yet-finalized future tokens, it can revise an earlier wrong answer once later reasoning steps reveal the mistake.
  • On a math problem shown at Google I/O, both GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 Flash (larger models) got the final answer wrong, while the much smaller Gemini diffusion model corrected itself across passes.
  • The model also adapts computation to difficulty, spending as few as 4 denoising steps on an easy prompt and 31 on a harder one, with no external signal telling it which is which.

“So, both Chat GPT 4o, which was new at the time, and Gemini 2.5 Flash, which was brand new at the time, both made an error on this on this exact problem”

said at 10:49

“And then some something more complicated is explained quantum mechanics in a single paragraph, and that took 31 denoising steps”

04The throughput trade-off keeps it out of flagship serving today
Slide at 05:20 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

The throughput trade-off keeps it out of flagship serving today

  • The core disadvantage is lower throughput for large batches: autoregressive serving amortizes cost across many concurrent queries, while diffusion's multiple forward passes hit compute limits faster per request, raising cost to serve even though per-user latency is lower.
  • That's why, as of this talk, no lab is deploying text diffusion into its large hosted models despite the latency win.

“no one's landing text diffusion into any of these big models primarily because of that disadvantage”

said at 05:39
05Where it fits today, and open design choices
Slide at 21:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 1 more from this section below

Where it fits today, and open design choices

  • The speaker frames on-device and robotics applications (within the Alphabet ecosystem) as the current fit, since those settings don't batch thousands of concurrent queries and therefore don't pay diffusion's throughput penalty, while quality is described as comparable to same-size autoregressive models.
  • Response length is currently handled with a fixed window, generated block-wise; audience questions surfaced that starting denoising from a smaller model's draft (instead of pure noise) doesn't work well because the model is trained to expect noise as input.

“we are in a couple of on-device applications already. Um and within like the Alphabet ecosystem. So, robotics, things like that, where you want to run a a model on the device itself”

“The easiest way is to just fix some some window length and then just iterate on that. So, it's like auto aggressive, block-wise auto aggressive”

“The problem with that is if you train it with noise, it expects noise. You'd have to train it with the small model”

Commentary (ours)

The framing that text diffusion is fundamentally a latency-for-throughput trade rather than a quality improvement suggests it will show up first in interactive/on-device products (voice coding, robotics) rather than replacing chat-style hosted serving, until batching-aware diffusion inference techniques close that throughput gap (ours).
