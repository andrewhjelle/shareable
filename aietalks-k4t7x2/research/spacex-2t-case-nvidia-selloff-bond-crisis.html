01Karpathy joins Anthropic to chase recursive self-improvement

The show opens on Andrej Karpathy leaving his solo run to lead a new pre-training team at Anthropic focused on recursive self-improvement — using Claude to accelerate Claude's own pre-training research. Chamath frames this alongside Anthropic's first (Wall Street Journal-reported) profitable quarter as evidence the AI narrative has shifted from pure spend to demonstrated ROI, with Gavin Baker estimating $200-400B of combined LLM-company ARR by year end.

02The AI backlash and a pulled executive order

The panel debates why public sentiment on AI has turned negative, with Friedberg arguing technology that concentrates leverage and benefit in a small group triggers a deep, historically-recurring backlash (he draws a parallel to the Copernican revolution's disruption of established power centers). Separately, a planned presidential AI executive order — reportedly requiring federal review of frontier models before release — was announced and then pulled at the last minute, which the panel reads as the administration balking at building in government oversight of model releases.

03SpaceX's S-1 and the Anthropic compute deal

SpaceX filed its S-1 targeting a $1.75 trillion valuation on a $75 billion raise, which would make it the largest IPO ever. The filing disclosed that Anthropic is paying SpaceX $1.25 billion a month — a $45 billion deal over three years — to rent Colossus 1 and part of Colossus 2, a contract the panel says single-handedly added the equivalent of a second Starlink-sized revenue stream to SpaceX's AI compute business.

04What that compute bought: Composer 2.5 and Grok's harness

05Nvidia's beat versus its stock reaction

Nvidia reported $81.6 billion in revenue, up 85% year-over-year and 20% quarter-over-quarter, with 75% gross margins and an additional $80 billion buyback. Gavin Baker argues the 'Nvidia is losing share to custom ASICs' narrative can't be reconciled with the numbers — Nvidia is demonstrably gaining share against comparable hyperscaler/AI-cloud spend — and attributes the muted stock reaction partly to rival chipmakers avoiding public benchmarks where he believes they'd lose.

06Bond yields and the debt backdrop

The panel closes on macro warning signs: the 10-year Treasury yield hit 4.6%, Japan's 30-year yield hit a record 5.1%, UK yields are at their highest since the financial crisis, and Germany's are at their highest since 2011. Friedberg puts this against a global debt-to-GDP ratio of 310%, framing rising yields against that debt load as a potential catalyst for a credit crisis, while Chamath and Gavin argue America's energy and food self-sufficiency make it relatively better positioned than the rest of the world even if the macro picture broadly deteriorates.
