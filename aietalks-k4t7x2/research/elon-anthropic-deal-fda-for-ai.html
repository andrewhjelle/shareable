Elon Musk's SpaceX leased Anthropic the entire capacity of the Colossus 1 data center, resolving what the panel frames as Anthropic's compute constraints. 02Anthropic's growth curve and the monopoly debate

David Sacks cites Anthropic's annualized revenue growing from roughly $10 billion to $30 billion between January 1st and March 31st, describing the pace as unprecedented even by Silicon Valley standards. He goes on to argue that if the trajectory continues, Anthropic will become the most powerful monopoly in human history, a framing Chamath and Brad push back on given the company's still-small absolute revenue relative to incumbents.

03The White House 'FDA for AI' story and KYC as an alternative

The panel discusses New York Times reporting that the White House is considering an executive order creating an AI working group to review new models before release, with the report attributing the catalyst to Anthropic's Methuselah model spooking officials. Sacks pushes back against a formal pre-approval regime and instead floats requiring frontier labs to implement a KYC (know-your-customer) identity check before granting access to powerful models.

“the catalyst was wait for it, Anthropic's Methuselah model, which reportedly scared, spooked, made people really nervous at the White House.”

04Cloud revenue and the market's 'reckoning' question

The panel runs through hyperscaler cloud run-rates, with AWS at $150 billion, Azure at $108 billion, and Google Cloud at $80 billion. Chamath argues that despite this growth, there's a coming 'reckoning moment,' framed as roughly 500 days out, when AI spending will need to show up as measurable benefit for the companies paying for the tokens. Brad counters with S&P 500 operating-margin data, showing margins rising from about 11.8% in Q1 2024 to 13% this year.
