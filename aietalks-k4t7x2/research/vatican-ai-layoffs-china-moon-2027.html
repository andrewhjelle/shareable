01The Pope takes a stance on AI

Pope Leo XIV published his first encyclical, a roughly 42,000-word document on AI titled Magnifica Humanitus, before which Google, Anthropic, Meta, and OpenAI had reportedly been lobbying the Vatican. The hosts read it as the first major-religion position taking an explicit stand against AI personhood, a stance they note sits oddly next to Anthropic's own 'soul' documents, which describe its models as having something like an inner life. The document also condemns AI supply-chain labor conditions as a new form of slavery and calls for AI ownership to be redistributed away from a small number of private companies.

02Tech layoffs: AI's fault or a hiring freeze?

The hosts cite roughly 134,000 tech-sector layoffs since the start of 2026, a Mercer survey finding 99% of CEOs expect AI-driven layoffs within two years, and March as the worst month for tech layoffs since the pandemic. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang pushes back, calling the AI-blame narrative 'lazy' and arguing the underlying data is more nuanced than headlines suggest. The hosts also note Sam Altman walking back his earlier warnings of a white-collar 'job apocalypse,' and cite a Dallas Fed report finding the employment decline tied to AI exposure shows up only among younger workers, which the hosts read as a hiring freeze rather than mass layoffs.

Commentary (ours)

(ours) The apparent contradiction the hosts flag, a frontier lab's own 'soul' language treating models as having inner life while the religious institution it's reportedly closest to denies AI personhood outright, is worth watching as a fault line in how AI rights get argued publicly, though it's speculative to extrapolate from one encyclical to a broader religious realignment. The layoffs discussion is a useful corrective against taking 'AI killed my job' headlines at face value, but the Dallas Fed finding the hosts cite (a younger-worker-specific decline) is itself just one study and shouldn't be read as a settled resolution of the AI-jobs debate.
