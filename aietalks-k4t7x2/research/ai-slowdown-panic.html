01New coding benchmark separates the leaders

Data Curve released a new coding benchmark called Deep SWE, built from scratch rather than scraped from GitHub issues, specifically to avoid the memorization and triviality problems the show's host has previously criticized in benchmarks like SWE-bench. GPT-5.5 topped the leaderboard, with scores trailing off quickly after the top handful of models and Chinese models scoring far behind (DeepSeek-V4 at just 8%). A qualitative evaluation harness found the top models' edge came largely from self-verification (writing their own tests), while Claude models showed a specific failure pattern of dropping one part of multi-part requirements.

Goldman Sachs CEO David Solomon made a similar point in a New York Times op-ed, citing his own firm's data alongside Goldman economists' longer-run forecast that a quarter of work hours will be automated over the next decade.

04The annual summer bubble panic returns

The host traces a yearly pattern of AI slowdown narratives (2023's ChatGPT summer dip, 2024's pre-training wall, 2025's MIT '95% of projects fail' study) and argues this year's version arrived early, driven by companies like Uber and Microsoft pulling back on token spending. Research firm Epoch AI estimates token supply is tripling each year while demand is growing roughly 10x per year. Separately, the U.S. government has begun rationing access to the most powerful models: the White House opposed Anthropic's plan to expand access to its Mythos model partly because it wanted first access to those tokens itself.

05Debunking the VS Code slowdown chart

A viral chart showing plateaued daily install counts of AI coding assistants in VS Code was cited as evidence that coding-tool growth is decelerating. Developer Simon Willison countered by showing NPM installs of Codex's terminal interface grew from about 100,000 a day in January to over a million a day by late May, arguing usage has shifted to CLI and desktop interfaces the VS Code chart doesn't capture.
