How 3,800 DNA Sites Proved Us Wrong – David Reich

SpeakerDwarkesh Patel + David Reich · Dwarkesh Podcast Watchyoutu.be/dEYA-zKAPfc
Science

If you only read one thing

  • Reich says his research keeps disproving his own priors — most notably discovering strong Neanderthal-interbreeding evidence he first assumed was a mistake.
  • As recently as 2024, the best-resourced ancient-DNA study found only 21 genome positions under selection; Reich's team, using 14x more data and a new relatedness-based method, finds 479 to about 3,800 candidate sites.
  • The signal is cross-validated against UK Biobank GWAS data: sites with a high selection-statistic score are enriched roughly fivefold for affecting a real measured human trait, versus 15% with no filter.
01A career of disproven priors

Reich frames this work in the context of a pattern in his career: results that repeatedly contradict his own starting assumptions. He cites the Neanderthal genome project, where he and colleagues had established non-Africans as a simple subset of African genetic variation with no interbreeding evidence, and then found the opposite.

02A decade of near-null results

The first ancient-DNA selection studies, starting in 2015, found only a handful of candidate positions. Even the best-resourced follow-up, a 2024 Copenhagen-led study with far better data, found only 21 positions with a strong selection signal, suggesting the approach might be hitting a hard ceiling.

03New method, 14x more data

Reich's team, led by Ali Akbari, increased the ancient-DNA dataset roughly 14-fold and applied a relatedness-based prediction method borrowed from disease-risk studies. The result: at least 479 genomic positions under selection at 99% confidence, and about 3,800 at a lower confidence threshold — far beyond the couple dozen typical of a single prior scan.

04Validating against UK Biobank

To check whether the new selection sites were real, the team compared them to an independent dataset: UK Biobank genome-wide association data covering about 500,000 people and hundreds of traits. Filtering for a high selection-statistic score raised the rate of sites tied to a real trait from a 15% baseline to roughly fivefold enrichment.
