01Google's usage and spending numbers

At Google I/O, Sundar Pichai said monthly token processing across Google's products has grown roughly sevenfold year-over-year to 3.2 quadrillion tokens per month, and that 2026 AI capital expenditure will land around $180-190 billion, about six times 2022's $31 billion. Google also launched Gemini 3.5 Flash as its new default model, which it says generates output tokens four times faster than other frontier models. Co-host Alex Wissner-Gross said the model trails top-tier competitors like GPT-5.5 High on raw capability and reads as optimized for throughput and tool use rather than frontier benchmarks.

02Karpathy leaves for Anthropic's pretraining team

Andrej Karpathy, an OpenAI co-founder who left in 2017 to run Tesla's Full Self-Driving effort, returned to OpenAI in 2023-24, then founded Eureka Labs, announced he has joined Anthropic to work on a new initiative using Claude to accelerate Claude's own pretraining research. In his own post on X, he said he expects the next few years at the LLM frontier to be especially formative. The hosts and Cerebras CEO Andrew Feldman connected this to Karpathy's earlier comment, on the No Priors podcast, that staying outside a frontier lab risks his judgment on the field drifting.

03Cerebras goes public

Cerebras's IPO closed up 68%, giving the wafer-scale chipmaker a $95 billion market cap, which the hosts described as the biggest US IPO since Uber in 2019. CEO Andrew Feldman said Cerebras signed a compute supply deal with OpenAI worth north of $20 billion over several years, plus a term sheet with AWS for data-center deployment. He also cited a benchmark on the open-source, trillion-parameter Kimi K2 model where Cerebras ran at roughly 1,000 tokens per second versus about 70 tokens per second he attributed to GPU-cloud provider Fireworks.

04OpenAI beats Musk in court

A federal jury rejected Elon Musk's lawsuit against OpenAI after roughly two hours of deliberation, ruling that Musk had waited too long to sue and was outside the statute of limitations. Musk's legal team said it plans to appeal. Feldman said he expects the appeal to fail because the ruling rested on a factual, timing-based finding that courts rarely overturn.

05Compute geopolitics: token resale and a chip-fab prediction

Wissner-Gross said recent public reporting shows China running proxy services that resell American model tokens to Chinese users at steep discounts, in order to harvest reasoning traces for training domestic models, and said Anthropic is pursuing action on it. Separately, discussing Elon Musk's proposed Terafab chip-manufacturing project, Feldman predicted it would take 15-20 years rather than the 5-10 years sometimes assumed, citing fab construction timelines of 5-6 years and $40-50 billion per facility even for experienced builders like TSMC and Samsung.

Commentary (ours)

(ours) The episode's numbers cut two ways: Google's capex and token-volume figures are self-reported without independent verification, and Cerebras's Kimi K2 comparison names one GPU-cloud competitor (Fireworks) rather than a broader baseline, so both are best read as vendor benchmarks rather than audited figures. The throughput emphasis running through both segments, Gemini 3.5 Flash's speed claims and Cerebras's tokens-per-second framing, suggests competitive attention is shifting toward serving economics rather than raw capability scores, though that reading is our inference, not something any speaker stated directly.
