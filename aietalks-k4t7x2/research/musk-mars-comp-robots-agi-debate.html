01Musk's Mars-shot comp package

SpaceX's board approved a compensation package for Musk built around two extreme triggers: 200 million super-voting shares (10x normal voting weight) if he establishes a Mars colony of a million people or more at a $7.5 trillion market valuation, and a separate tranche of 60.4 million restricted shares for bringing online 100 terawatts of space-based compute. The panel frames the super-voting structure as following, not originating, a pattern used by Mike Saylor, Google, and Meta.

“You get to 200 million super voting shares 10 to 1 when he hits a Mars colony of a million people or more.”

03Is AGI already here? Hassabis vs. Wissner-Gross

Demis Hassabis is reported saying AGI may not need a major breakthrough, putting roughly 50/50 odds on needing one more advance in world models versus betting on continued foundation-model scaling. Alex Wissner-Gross goes further, restating his standing position that AGI was effectively achieved in summer 2020 with the publication of the GPT-3 few-shot-learning paper.

“I think there's a 50/50 chance that we could still that we still need a breakthrough maybe in world models. But my bet is still strongly on foundation models because of how successful they've been.”

“no later than with the discovery of or the publication rather of large language models or few shot learners, which was the GPT-3 paper.”

04China rules AI-driven demotion illegal

A Hangzhou court sided with a tech worker who refused a pay cut after his employer automated part of his job with AI, ruling that adopting AI was a business choice rather than an unavoidable external event that voided the employment contract.

“The court logic was simple. Adopting AI was a business choice, not an unavoidable external shock that made the employment contract impossible.”

▶ watch at 49:07
05Kotler's dissent: AI writing has regressed

Steven Kotler, working daily with AI as a science writer, argues that ChatGPT's writing quality has gotten worse over the past year rather than better, pushing back on the panel's broader optimism about model self-improvement.

“ChatGPT is so much worse now as a writer than it was a year ago.”
