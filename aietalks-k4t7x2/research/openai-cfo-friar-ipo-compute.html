Friar says that if OpenAI were optimizing purely for today's unit economics, it would route tokens to the API business, which she says earns an order of magnitude more revenue per token than consumer use. Instead, she says OpenAI is building toward what she calls an AI infrastructure layer, serving consumers, small businesses, large enterprises, and governments, and will keep an ad-free paid tier alongside an ad-supported free tier that is already being tested.

Commentary (ours)

(ours) Friar's framing suggests OpenAI is treating near-term unit economics as subordinate to distribution: the ChatGPT-as-"noun and verb" argument implies she's betting consumer lock-in compounds into pricing power later, the way search dominance did for Google. The multi-chip, multi-CSP diversification she describes reads as explicit hedging against both Nvidia dependency and any single supplier's capacity ceiling, which lines up with her own admission that compute remains scarce well past 2026. Whether the ad platform she describes can actually rival Meta or Google's targeting is unverified here -- she's describing intent and a stated principle (model results won't be sponsored), not measured ad performance.
