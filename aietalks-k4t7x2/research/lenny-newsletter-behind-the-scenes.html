A behind-the-scenes interview with Lenny Rachitsky on building his newsletter and podcast

SpeakerLenny Rachitsky & Michelle Rial · Lenny's Podcast Watchyoutu.be/HEqrvF7ztBE
Media & cultureProductivity & learning

If you only read one thing

  • Lenny's newsletter has grown to 1.2 million subscribers since 2019; he credits a 'Lindy effect' bet (writing weekly for 9 months, then committing to 9 more) and a paywall that started making meaningful money within about a month of launch.
  • A giveaway offering a free year of Cursor, Lovable, Bolt, Replit, and v0 drew organized fraud rings, mostly based in China, exploiting API holes; his engineer Este worked without sleep for a week patching them.
  • Each newsletter post goes through roughly 50 editing passes before publication, while Michelle Rial says her viral charts get stripped of attribution and sold on unlicensed merch.
01The newsletter's growth and origin

Lenny confirms the newsletter, started in 2019, has reached 1.2 million subscribers. He describes committing to a weekly Substack cadence for 9 months, then reasoning from the 'Lindy effect' that since it had lasted 9 months it would likely last roughly that much longer again, which led him to add a paywall. The paywall started generating meaningful income within about a month of launch.

“your you started your newsletter 2019. And it now has over a million subscribers.”

02A fraud attack on the free-tools giveaway

About a year before this recording, Lenny ran a promotion offering subscribers a free year of five AI coding tools -- Cursor, Lovable, Bolt, Replit, and v0. The offer attracted organized fraud rings, mostly based in China, that found exploits in the underlying API, forcing his engineer Este to work without sleep for a week to close the holes.

“I launched you get a free year of Cursor and Lovable and Bolt and Replit and V0. I think that's five. And so uh that was way too good an offer.”

03Iteration behind the work

Lenny says a typical newsletter post goes through roughly 50 rounds of his own revision before it also passes through an editor, a copy editor, and a designer.

“when I write a newsletter post, I go through it probably, let's say 50 times making it better. Like I start, there's like something that's the beginnings and then read through it and add to it.”

▶ watch at 63:33
04Attribution and virality of Michelle's charts

Michelle Rial describes her charts being widely shared with attribution stripped out, and reproduced without credit on merchandise like pillows and mugs sold by third-party websites.

“They make pillows and mugs and there's like all these websites that sell all these charts you've made just like on swag that you get no credit for.”

▶ watch at 20:48
05Where the best material comes from

Lenny says a key driver of the newsletter's success is that it's grounded in real practitioner experience rather than commentary from the sidelines, which is why most posts are now guest posts where someone shares the single best thing they learned in their career.

“I think has been key to my newsletter success is uh it is based on like real life doing the thing.”
