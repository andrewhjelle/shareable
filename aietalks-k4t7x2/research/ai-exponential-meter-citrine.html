The Perils of the AI Exponential

SpeakerNathaniel Whittemore · The AI Daily Brief Watchyoutu.be/dztw1yctjI4
AI researchMarkets & economyAI industry

If you only read one thing

  • Opus 4.6 hit an ~14.5-hour time horizon on Meter's benchmark, more than tripling Opus 4.5's and implying the doubling rate is now 1.5 months, down from the original 7-month rate
  • Meter researchers themselves caution the newest results are noisy and possibly saturated (confidence interval upper bound of 98 hours), and warn against treating this update as proof of a faster progress regime
  • Citrine Research's '2028 Global Intelligence Crisis' piece, predicting AI consuming the whole economy while capital owners benefit and workers are left jobless, is getting unusually wide acceptance from investors who already believed some version of the thesis
01What the Meter chart actually measures

Meter's continuous study, first released in March of last year, found agentic task time horizons doubling roughly every 7 months, going back to GPT-2. The host stresses a common misreading: the metric is not how long an agent can work continuously, but how difficult a task is in terms of how long a human would take to solve it, at a 50% agent success rate.

“Meter conducted their study going all the way back to GPT-2 and found that the time horizon of agentic tasks was reliably doubling roughly every 7 months.”

02From GPT-5.1 Codex to Opus 4.6

Meter's November result for GPT-5.1 Codex showed a 2-hour-40-minute time horizon, barely better than GPT-5. December's Opus 4.5 result nearly doubled that to 4 hours 49 minutes. On the Friday results covered here, GPT-5.3 Codex reached 6.5 hours and Opus 4.6 reached roughly 14.5 hours -- more than tripling Opus 4.5 and implying the doubling rate has compressed to about 1.5 months.

03Meter's own caveats

Amid the viral reaction, Dean Ball wrote that he doesn't read the new chart as much of an update, noting Meter itself had been signaling declining confidence in the benchmark due to saturation and a thin set of long-duration tasks.

“Dean Ball wrote, "For what it's worth, I don't take the meter chart that's been going around as much of an update. Meter itself has been signaling their decrease in confidence in the benchmark for a while now”

04Citrine's 2028 Global Intelligence Crisis

Citrine Research's new piece applies Dario Amodei's 'country of geniuses in a data center' idea to the real economy, predicting AI consumes the entire economy rather than staying sector-specific. Its thesis: capital owners reap the gains while workers across every level of the economy end up jobless, shifting economic activity from a household basis to a capital basis.

“the piece predicted that we'll see AI start to consume the entire economy, moving from sector specific to broad application of cheap machine intelligence.”

▶ watch at 07:41

“Citrine's thesis is essentially that capital owners are about to reap the massive benefits of AI, while workers in every strata of the economy will be left jobless and purposeless.”

▶ watch at 07:41
05Pushback on the marketplace-disruption claim

Critics like Dan Hawkins pushed back on specific claims in the Citrine piece, arguing that the idea of AI agents easily building competitors to DoorDash or Uber Eats misunderstands why those marketplaces are defensible in the first place -- the hard part was never the app, but building liquid two-sided supply and reliability over years.

“Anyone could have done that at any time in the last 10 years. Why was no one able to? Because the hard part has nothing to do with building the app or attracting the drivers.”
