Does Gemini 3.1 Pro Matter?

SpeakerNathaniel Whittemore · The AI Daily Brief Watchyoutu.be/of85FKh_6yY
Models & releasesAI industryBusiness & strategy

If you only read one thing

  • Gemini 3.1 Pro jumps from 31.1% to 77.1% on ARC-AGI-2, ahead of Opus 4.6's 68.8%, at under a dollar a task (c1, c2, c3)
  • Artificial Analysis ranks it #1 on its overall intelligence index at less than half the cost of Opus 4.6 to run, even as it still trails rivals on the real-world GDPval agentic benchmark (c6, c5)
  • Commentary frames the release as proof that raw benchmark scores are becoming table stakes, with Google's distribution (2 billion Chrome users) read as the real advantage (c8)
01A large jump on ARC-AGI-2 specifically

Gemini 3.1 Pro's score on ARC-AGI-2 rose from 31.1% for Gemini 3 to 77.1%, a bigger jump than on other benchmarks covered in the episode. That score is reported as ahead of Claude Opus 4.6's 68.8% on the same test, even though Gemini trailed Opus 4.6 narrowly on SWE-bench verified.

“The jump between Gemini 3 Pro and Gemini 3.1 Pro was from a 31.1% with Gemini 3 to 77.1% on Gemini 31 Pro.”

“The biggest jump and the one that a lot of folks are talking about was on ARC AGI 2 while Opus 4 6 scored a 68.8% on that test.”

02The number that matters more is cost per task

On the ARC leaderboard, which tracks cost alongside score, Gemini 3.1 Pro reached its 77.1% result at under a dollar per task, while Gemini 3 Deepthink scored higher but at more than ten times the cost. Artificial Analysis separately reports Gemini 3.1 Pro jumping from sixth to first place on its overall intelligence index while running at under half the cost of Opus 4.6.

“3.1 Pro achieved that score at less than a buck a task.”

▶ watch at 05:06

“Google went from 31.1% to 77.1% in 3 months while keeping pricing at $2 per million input tokens, the same pricing as Gemini 3 Pro. They doubled the intelligence and charged zero incremental cost.”

▶ watch at 07:10

“On artificial analysis's overall intelligence index, Google jumped all the way from the sixth spot behind various versions of Claude, GPT, and even a Chinese model GLM5 all the way up to number one.”

▶ watch at 05:06
03It still lags on real-world agentic tasks

On the GDPval benchmark, which focuses on real-world agentic performance rather than static reasoning problems, Gemini 3.1 Pro improved meaningfully over Gemini 3 Pro but still finished behind Sonnet 4.6, Opus 4.6, GPT-5.2, and GLM5. Commentators cited in the episode flagged this as evidence that work-task reliability isn't where Google is currently focused.

“While Gemini 3.1 Pro did jump up meaningfully from Gemini 3 Pro, it was behind Sonnet 4.6, Opus 4.6, GPT 5.2, and GLM5.”

▶ watch at 06:08
04Google's own framing centers reasoning and multimodal output

Google VP Josh Woodward's launch post describes Gemini 3.1 Pro as a significant leap in complex reasoning, pointing again to the ARC-AGI-2 result, and pitches it for agentic tasks, intricate coding, and data synthesis with fewer errors and better SVG output.

“Gemini 3.1 Pro has arrived. It's a significant leap in complex reasoning. Once again, he points to ARGI 2. So, it's great adentic tasks, intricate coding, and data synthesis projects. You should see fewer errors, better logic, and surprisingly good SVGs.”

▶ watch at 03:34
05Benchmarks read as table stakes, distribution as the real moat

One cited commentator argues the frontier is commoditizing fast enough that benchmark leadership now lasts weeks rather than quarters, with OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google converging on comparable intelligence while diverging on distribution. Google's installed base across Chrome, Android, Workspace, and Cloud is framed as the more durable advantage than any single score.

“The frontier is commoditizing so fast that benchmark leadership lasts weeks, not quarters. Open AAI, Anthropic, and Google are all within singledigit percentage points of each other on most evals. The three labs are converging on comparable intelligence, but diverging on distribution.”

Commentary (ours)
