04Month by month — in their words

Narrative (ours, hedged) with the evidence it leans on, then three talks per month chosen mechanically: the most-viewed talks whose strongest claim is a prediction or a warning. Quotes are verbatim and time-linked.

The arc (ours)

This corpus draws from 97 of 355 manifest episodes, sampled in two stratified extraction waves; May reads dense because the transcript store was fetched most heavily then, and the window ends Jun 4 because the store was not refreshed after that. Publication dates drive placement; each show sets its own cadence. February opens with agent pricing tiers and a frontier-commoditization thesis. March widens the aperture to society (Yang, Pahlavi) and physical work (Younis). April is a small, off-topic month dominated by Sinclair on longevity and Singhal on PM restructuring. May is the loudest, because a single All-In roundtable set the SpaceX-Anthropic deal, an Anthropic-monopoly warning, and open-weight regulation risk into circulation. The partial June sees named-voice bets from Kurzweil and Friar rather than a broadly held view.

Jun 2026

7 talks · 68 claims · top themes: AI industry (5), Business & strategy (4), Markets & economy (3)

June partial: Kurzweil's dates and Friar's compute admission dominate

Only 7 talks land in the June partial, drawn from two shows and three named guests. Sarah Friar (OpenAI CFO, All-In Podcast) says OpenAI will still not have enough compute in 2026 despite early, heavily-criticized purchases, and pre-announces an unnamed consumer hardware device to be unveiled by the end of 2026 with a broader release in early 2027. Ray Kurzweil (Moonshots) reaffirms his 1999 prediction that 'AGI would happen by 2029' and defines the singularity as a millionfold increase in capability arriving by 2045; he also predicts that by 2029 people will not be able to tell whether a decision was human or AI. Bill Ackman (All-In Podcast) calls a $1.75 trillion SpaceX IPO one of the lowest-cost-of-capital equity transactions in history. Extrapolation (ours): with only three headliners on the record, the sample is too thin to call a field-level trend and skews toward named-voice bets.

May: SpaceX-Anthropic deal, and All-In turns on Anthropic

May carries 59 talks, and the most-cited claims cluster on one All-In roundtable. Brad Gerstner (All-In Podcast) estimates the SpaceX-Anthropic compute deal will generate an incremental $45 billion in revenue this year for Elon's emerging hyperscaler business. David Sacks predicts that if Anthropic's growth continues, it will become 'the most powerful monopoly ever created in human history,' and in a later episode argues the current regulatory-capture push is pointed at banning open source or open-weight models. Chamath Palihapitiya tells the same audience markets have roughly 500 days before AI infrastructure spending faces a reckoning over measurable business benefit. Jason Calacanis predicts always-on LLM querying will require a hardware upgrade to the average desktop. David Freiberg cites 11 million extra terawatt-hours stored in the oceans against the planet's 25,000 terawatt-hour annual use as the basis for forecasting a record-breaking El Nino. Apr 2026

7 talks · 65 claims · top themes: AI industry (3), Business & strategy (3), Hardware & devices (2)

April: quieter month, longevity and PM restructuring take the floor

April lands only 7 talks, and the highlights move away from frontier-model news. David Sinclair (Moonshots) floats 2026 as the year age reversal is shown to be possible in humans and describes a small-molecule reprogramming cocktail aimed at a human clinical trial within a couple of months as part of an XPrize effort; he also cites the correlation between one alcoholic drink a day and a smaller brain as the reason he stopped drinking. Nikhyl Singhal (formerly Meta and Google) predicts a 12-24 month cycle in which companies shed large numbers of staff and rehire a smaller AI-first group, estimating 'about half' of current PMs are non-builders in trouble. Alex Wissner-Gross (Moonshots) extrapolates the roughly 1%-per-month gain on Frontier Math Tier 4 to essentially all professional research-grade problems solved in four or five years. Extrapolation (ours): four of the month's six surfaced claims come from Moonshots, so the longevity and XPrize tilt partly reflects one show's cadence.

Mar 2026

11 talks · 101 claims · top themes: AI industry (7), Geopolitics (4), Business & strategy (4)

March: guests widen the aperture from software to society and physical work

Andrew Yang (All-In Podcast) predicts AI will 'replace large numbers of white collar jobs' in 12 to 18 months and sketches a UBI-scale transfer of $50,000 a year to the bottom 200 million Americans. Reza Pahlavi, on the same show, estimates a free Iran would generate roughly $1 trillion in benefit to the US market over its first ten years. Qasar Younis (Lenny's Podcast) argues the real 5 to 10-year impact of AI is in farming, mining, and construction, not knowledge work. Jenny Wen (Lenny's Podcast) pushes back on designers treating taste and judgment as a permanent human moat. Nick Turley (OpenAI) says the goal is to productize reasoning so users never have to encounter the concept. Extrapolation (ours): March is the month named guests, not host commentary, supply most of the framing.

Feb 2026

13 talks · 116 claims · top themes: AI industry (10), Business & strategy (5), Policy & regulation (4)

February: agents get price tags and the frontier commoditizes

Nate B Jones (Nate B Jones) surfaces an unconfirmed OpenAI report on tiered agent pricing running from about $2,000/month for knowledge-worker agents up to $20,000 for AI research agents. He pairs it with the call that the minimum viable software team is 'approaching one' as token costs keep falling. Nathaniel Whittemore (The AI Daily Brief) aggregates a commentator arguing labs are within single-digit percentage points on most evals, so distribution rather than benchmark leadership now decides the frontier, and separately relays a Citrine Research piece predicting AI will start consuming the entire economy. He also reports the rumor that OpenAI's next model, internally called Garlic, will ship as GPT-5.3. Dave Blundin (Moonshots) predicts interactive AI tutor personas will exist within a year. Extrapolation (ours): the pricing tiers and the team-of-one thesis fit together as one commercial shape if they land.
