Why Scientists Missed Recent Human Evolution – David Reich

SpeakerDwarkesh Patel + David Reich · Dwarkesh Podcast Watchyoutu.be/hMNIlp--KZA
Science

If you only read one thing

  • Ancient DNA research spent 16+ years reconstructing human migration history but consistently failed to detect biological adaptation, largely due to small sample sizes.
  • The field's working assumption has been that human natural selection was quiescent for hundreds of thousands of years; Reich's team finds ~98% of allele-frequency change is drift/migration, leaving a thin but real selection signal.
  • The team treats ancient Europe and the Middle East as an archipelago of temporarily isolated populations — e.g. a 4,500-year-old steppe migration replaced 40-80% of European DNA — to separate real selection from migration noise.
01Why the ancient-DNA dream stalled

Reich says the ancient-DNA field set out, from its start over 16-17 years ago, to learn about biological change over time from ancient human remains. It succeeded at reconstructing migration and mixture history but not at detecting biological adaptation, mainly because sample sizes were too small to see subtle frequency shifts.

02The quiescent-selection consensus

The mainstream view for decades held that natural selection had been largely quiescent over the last several hundred thousand years of human history. Reich's own numbers still show that of roughly 10 million variable DNA positions, about 98% of frequency change is explained by drift and migration rather than directional selection.

03Isolated populations as natural experiments

To detect the remaining signal, the team treats ancient Europe and the Middle East as an archipelago of small populations that were isolated for a few hundred years at a stretch between migration events. A concrete example: roughly 4,500 years ago, steppe migration from north of the Black and Caspian Seas replaced a large share of European DNA, driving huge frequency swings that are migration, not selection.
