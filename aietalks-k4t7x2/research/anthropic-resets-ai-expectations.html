01The IPO race is suddenly close

The Wall Street Journal reported OpenAI has engaged investment bankers and expects to file IPO paperwork confidentially as soon as Friday, targeting readiness by September. Anthropic had been seen as the frontrunner with a reported October target, but is putting together one more private funding round instead, making it unlikely to move up its timeline to match OpenAI.

02A new AI executive order is close, with a timeline fight

Reports indicate a new AI executive order could arrive within days, with the White House proposing that labs share new models with the government 90 days ahead of release while the labs are reportedly pushing for a much shorter 14-day window. The order is also expected to direct the Pentagon to harden critical systems and the Treasury to set up an AI clearing house with the NSA handling model testing.

03Karpathy leaves solo work to join Anthropic

Andrej Karpathy, an OpenAI founding team member who had been working independently since mid-2024, announced he's joining Anthropic's pre-training team to help build tools using Claude to accelerate pre-training research itself. Commentators framed the move as part of a pattern (after Jan Leike and John Schulman) of researchers leaving OpenAI for Anthropic, and some read it as a signal that recursive self-improvement (RSI) is becoming real enough to matter competitively.

04Anthropic's first profitable quarter

Wall Street obtained financials showing Anthropic forecasting $10.9 billion in Q2 revenue at a $44 billion annualized rate, up from $4.8 billion the prior quarter, with a small operating profit — the first profitable quarter for any foundation lab. Anthropic had previously forecast no profitability until 2029 (OpenAI: 2030), though Nathaniel notes caveats: Anthropic counts top-line revenue before partner shares, and part of the profitability may reflect being too compute-constrained to spend more even if it wanted to.
