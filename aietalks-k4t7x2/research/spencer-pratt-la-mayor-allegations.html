01Wildfire response and reservoir failures

Pratt describes calling the fire department directly as the Palisades fire approached and being told no assets were available, despite living next to a reservoir used for regular helicopter dip drills. He says then-DWP head Janice Quinones had drained that and another reservoir heading into a known dry fire-weather year, without backup water or tankers in place.

“the Palisades reservoir. 5 million gallons next door to my house that the fire department would do almost, not weekly, but bi-weekly drills”

02NGO and nonprofit spending allegations

Pratt alleges a nonprofit he calls Weinart bought a Westwood building from the city for $29 million six days after it sold on the open market for $11 million, with no one housed there years later. He also says that of over 200 NGOs listed as recipients of a $100 million fire-relief fund, the organizations' own legal defense acknowledged only 'under 10' gave money directly to fire victims.

“6 days later, the city with our tax money gives Weineart 29 million, $28 million to buy this same building that was $11 million”

“fire aid puts out a legal letter to defend themselves in their own legal letter from the law firm. They say several several of these NOS's gave directly to fire victims”

03A rival city's model and LA's own numbers

Pratt cites San Francisco under Mayor Daniel Lurie as a model, saying car break-ins there are down 87% after the city began enforcing existing laws. He and the interviewer also cite LA figures: homeless spending up roughly 10x over a decade while the counted homeless population doubled, and LA USD spending $23,000 per student and a $101,000 average teacher salary while ranking 170th among California school districts.

“the last 10 years city of Los Angeles I think has increased homeless spending by 10x and the homeless population has doubled and clearly it's gotten a lot worse”

04Permitting and bureaucracy as a campaign target

Pratt cites an affordable-housing developer who says Mayor Bass promised to fast-track permits to 6 months but is at 2.5 years in the permitting process for a project.

“He said when Mayor Bass announced her initiative, she was going to rush it 6 months. He's at 2 and 1/2 years in the permit process”
