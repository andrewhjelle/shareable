01India's AI summit: an equity framing

The AI Impact Summit in New Delhi was the first edition held in a developing country, after prior years in the UK, France, and South Korea. A UN report released the same week warned that AI adoption is outpacing in the developed world, risking a permanent technological divide, and Secretary General Guterres used the summit to call for AI to belong to, and be accessible, beneficial, and safe for everyone. Separately, Adani and Reliance Industries each pledged over $100 billion in local data-center spending over the coming decade.

“This is the first time the event has been held in a developing country with previous iterations hosted in the UK, France, and South Korea.”

02Altman and Amodei's summit optics

In a moment caught on camera, more than a dozen tech leaders joined hands with Prime Minister Modi on stage, except for Altman and Amodei, who refused to hold hands with each other. Off stage, in a CNBC interview, Altman pushed back on the idea that AI is driving most current job losses, attributing some of it to companies using AI as cover for layoffs they would have made anyway.

“The leaders joined hand and raised their arms in celebration, save for Altman and Amodei who refused to hold hands.”

03Enterprises mandate AI adoption from the top

Walmart's earnings call highlighted its Sparky shopping assistant, used by roughly half of online customers, with those users ordering 35% more than non-users. Accenture went further, telling senior managers that promotion now requires regular AI tool use, following CEO Julie Sweet's earlier warning that non-adopters would be exited from the company.

“around half of Walmart's online customers have used Sparky, and that those using the assistant ordered 35% more than those who didn't.”

04The real adoption blocker: time, not tools

The host argues the dominant complaint across the show's surveys isn't tool quality but time: employees report they don't have time to learn tools that would eventually save them time, and few companies carve out dedicated time for that learning.

“People inside enterprises report that they don't have time to learn the technology that would save them time.”

Commentary (ours)

(ours) The mandate pattern across Walmart, Amazon, and Accenture landing in a single news cycle reads as a signal that large employers are shifting from encouraging AI adoption to compelling it, though the sample here is three companies in one week, not a broad survey.
