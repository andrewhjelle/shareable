01Prompt engineering is table stakes, not a differentiator

The speaker argues prompt engineering skills still matter but no longer earn credit on their own, calling it "table stakes" for 2026. He says the common lab advice to "just ask AI for what you want" only holds if the person already knows what they want, which breaks down as agentic workflows get more complex.

02Treat the model as a senior partner, not a junior one

His core mental-model shift: last year's models needed to be managed like a junior team member with careful, narrow instructions; this year's models should be addressed like a senior partner capable of exploring a problem with less hand-holding. He attributes part of this shift to what he calls a second capability jump following the release of Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5.

04The question method: intent, synthesis, and pushback

His first principle is to convey a directional thesis and its rough boundaries when asking a question, using a flashlight beam as the metaphor. He illustrates this with a worked marketing example: stating a hypothesis (that product-led growth is broken) and asking the AI to test it against multiple data sources and come back with its own thesis, even if it disagrees.

Commentary (ours)

(ours) The claim rests on two Opus/GPT releases changing agent behavior enough to justify a new prompting habit within roughly six months. That's a fast turnaround to generalize from personal observation alone, and the piece offers no benchmark or before/after comparison, so treat the "senior partner" framing as one practitioner's read rather than a measured result.
