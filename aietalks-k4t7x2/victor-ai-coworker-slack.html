If you only read one thing

  • Victor is a Slack-native, company-wide AI coworker with access to roughly 3,000 integrations and the ability to build its own connections when one doesn't exist, evolved from an earlier browser-automation agent (JCAI) and an email agent (Jace).
  • Scaling from one user to a company introduces two problems the team had to solve: shared memory fills up roughly a hundred times faster than single-user memory, and per-channel context (e.g. growth vs. engineering) must not leak across Slack channels or DMs.
  • In an A/B test, users preferred Opus's personality over a cheaper, comparably capable GPT-5.4 for tool calling and code generation, and a customer incident where a shared personal Gmail integration leaked one employee's emails to the whole team led the company to add per-integration access scoping.

01What Victor is and how it connects to tools

What Victor is and how it connects to tools

  • Victor is described as an AI employee that lives in Slack with no separate web app, participating in discussions, threads, and channels like a teammate.
  • It has access to roughly 3,000 integrations, and when an integration doesn't exist it can build its own connection, giving it broad tool access across a company.

“it has access to 3,000 integrations. And if for some reason it doesn't have access to your integrations, it can build its own connections”

02From browser agent to Slack coworker

From browser agent to Slack coworker

  • The product's predecessor, JCAI, worked by taking a lossless-minified snapshot of a webpage's DOM and deciding the next browser action from that snapshot and a stated goal.
  • Wiatrowski says this predates reliable tool calling and code-generating models, and the team later moved to an email agent (Jace) before launching Victor as a Slack-native company agent.

03Why Slack, and what breaks at company scale

Why Slack, and what breaks at company scale

  • A stated reason for choosing Slack as the interface is that powerful agent tasks don't complete instantly and can take around 10 minutes, which people tolerate from a human teammate on Slack but find frustrating when waiting on a web app.
  • At company scale, shared memory across a hundred users fills up roughly a hundred times faster than single-user memory, and per-channel context (e.g. a growth channel versus an engineering channel) has to be kept from leaking into channels or DMs where it doesn't belong.

“if Victor is like a very powerful agent and it's supposed to perform difficult tasks, then those tasks will not execute immediately. They they can take like 10 minutes to execute, right?”

“with Open Claw, there was a big concern about the memory getting cluttered or cluttered over time”

“So, it's probably running out of the memory a hundred times faster. It's a big challenge to be solved, and we have solved it”

04Model choice: users preferred Opus's personality

Model choice: users preferred Opus's personality

  • When the team tested replacing Opus with a cheaper GPT-5.4 that performed comparably on tool calling and code generation, users pushed back and preferred Opus, which Wiatrowski attributes largely to personality rather than raw capability.

“our users, can be due to our architecture, but they loved Opus, and they all started raging when we did we did did the AB test”

said at 12:30
05Positioning as a hire, not a tool

Positioning as a hire, not a tool

  • Wiatrowski frames Victor explicitly as "not a tool, it's a hire," illustrated by a customer story where a team admin connected their own personal Gmail as a shared integration and other employees started asking Victor about that person's private emails, prompting a complaint.
  • That incident led the team to add scoped, per-integration access controls.
  • He closes with three requirements for building a good AI coworker: getting work done easily via capable models and integrations, knowing the company through Slack context, and being made friendly.
