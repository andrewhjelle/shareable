Training Krea 2: What Matters in Generative Model Training

SpeakerSangwu Lee · Krea.ai Watchyoutu.be/-tviRdpmHvs
Data curationPost-trainingGenerative media

If you only read one thing

  • Krea open-sourced Create 2 medium, built to optimize for fast, stylistically diverse generation rather than the slower, highly consistent but mode-collapsed output the speaker attributes to big-lab models like ChatGPT and Nano Banana (c1, c2).
  • Data curation, not architecture, was the team's main lever: large-scale deduplication (2 to 10 billion images, hash-based then embedding-based), roughly 30 to 40 custom in-house classifiers/filters, and a deliberate refusal to train on AI-generated images (c5, c6, c7).

01Optimizing for diversity, not just reliability

Krea open-sourced the medium variant of its Create 2 image foundation model. The speaker contrasts this with production-grade models from large labs, which he says favor slower but highly reliable generation at the cost of stylistic diversity: to get consistency, those models tend to mode-collapse toward rendering the most average, centered version of a subject. Krea instead prioritized faster generation so users can iterate across different visual ideas.

02Latent diffusion as the default architecture

Most competitive production diffusion models, including Krea's, use an autoencoder to spatially compress images into a latent space before running the diffusion process there rather than on raw pixels, since diffusion transformers scale quadratically with sequence length and modeling every pixel directly would be too expensive.

“most of the production uh production diffusion models, at least the open-source ones that are very competitive, including ours, use a autoencoder”

▶ watch at 04:12
03Data curation is the real lever

The speaker repeatedly stresses that once an architecture is locked in, most of the work that actually determines model quality goes into data curation rather than modeling choices. For Create 2 that meant deduplicating a corpus of roughly 2 to 10 billion images (hash-based methods like pHash/MD5 first, then embedding-based methods like SigLip/SSCD for near-duplicates), building around 30 to 40 custom in-house classifiers and heuristics to filter or rebalance the data, and deliberately excluding AI-generated images from training because synthetic data is "sticky" and pulls the model toward a distillation-like ChatGPT/Nano Banana aesthetic.

04The training pipeline

Training progresses from low to high resolution, from 256 up to 1K, so the model first learns semantics before structure and detail, followed by mid-training, supervised fine-tuning, and preference optimization on pairwise comparisons. The final stage is reinforcement learning using a GRPO-inspired method: the model generates images that are scored by reward servers, and that feedback is used to improve things like text rendering and anatomical structure.
