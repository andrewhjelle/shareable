Relevant for anyone deciding whether to build internal tooling for a forward-deployed/solutions-engineering team: Varick's argument is that off-the-shelf frontier models fail specifically at judging what context matters in long enterpris...

If you only read one thing

  • Varick's core thesis is that AI has solved execution; the remaining bottleneck is how deeply an FDE can understand a customer's business without linearly adding headcount (c1).
  • Citing the MIT stat that 95% of generative AI pilots fail to reach production, Varick argues most enterprise AI projects fail because AI gets bolted onto broken processes instead of the process being redesigned around it, and that scoped point-solutions cap out around 5-10% ROI versus 25-75% for department-wide redesigns (c2, c4, c5).
  • Varick builds agents on top of customers' existing systems of record rather than asking them to migrate off, citing a client that reportedly spent $5 million and 5 years migrating to NetSuite (c3).

The next bottleneck is understanding the business, not executing tasks

  • Moza argues that end-to-end task execution is no longer the constraint on enterprise AI adoption; the harder problem is extracting and encoding enough context about how a specific business actually runs to make AI useful there, without linearly scaling headcount of forward-deployed engineers to do it.

Why AI pilots fail without process redesign

  • Moza cites publicly circulated statistics — the MIT finding that 95% of generative AI pilots fail to reach production, and a separate 87% figure for pilots that don't produce measurable ROI — as evidence that AI is commonly applied on top of unchanged, broken processes.
  • He contrasts narrow point-solutions, which he says deliver roughly 5-10% ROI in a single function like AP or prospecting, with Varick's department-wide transformations, which he claims deliver 25%, 50%, or 75% ROI.

“There's a lot of, you know, semi-outdated, but still very relevant statistics like the MIT review saying that 95% of generative AI pilots fail to reach production.”

Building on top of systems of record, not migrating off them

  • Varick's platform is designed to sit on top of a customer's existing systems of record (NetSuite, Dynamics, SAP, Salesforce) rather than requiring migration off them.
  • Moza quotes a client who said they spent $5 million and 5 years migrating to NetSuite as the reason enterprises won't tolerate an AI vendor asking them to migrate again.

“One of the quotes from our clients said that they spent $5,000,000 and 5 years migrating to uh NetSuite.”

04The FD agent: a knowledge graph plus custom-trained models
Slide at 15:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

The FD agent: a knowledge graph plus custom-trained models

  • Varick's head of engineering, JD Pruitt, describes building an internal 'FD agent' to reduce how much manual work forward-deployed engineers do gathering and synthesizing customer context.
  • The system represents a company's processes as a dependency graph, and Pruitt says frontier models like Claude, used directly, fail to produce output with the right level of detail, so Varick post-trains its own models on open-source bases and trains custom RL tools for traversing the knowledge graph to extract the right context.

“And frontier models have absolutely no concept of this. So, we started post-training our own models on top of on top of open-source models.”

said at 16:29

“but the point is we use a dependency graph. Um most of these workflows inside of enterprise are remarkably linear.”
