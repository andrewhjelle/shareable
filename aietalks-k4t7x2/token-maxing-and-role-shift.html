How AI is changing Software Engineering: A Conversation with Gergely Orosz

SpeakerGergely Orosz · The Pragmatic Engineer Watchyoutu.be/CS5Cmz5FssI

If you only read one thing

  • Token output is becoming an informal, sometimes weaponized performance signal at Meta, Microsoft, and Salesforce, pushing engineers to inflate usage even when it doesn't help the actual work (c1, c2, c3).
  • A cited METER study found developers felt 20% more productive using AI while measured results showed them 20% less productive, complicating simple productivity claims (c4).
  • Big tech companies are rebuilding internal AI infrastructure -- custom coding agents, MCP gateways -- partly because AI-branded projects get funded more easily and in-house tooling can handle code that never fits a vendor's context window (c7).
01Token maxing as a gamed metric

At companies like Meta and Microsoft, token usage shows up on internal leaderboards and, according to managers the speaker talked to, can factor informally into performance evaluations alongside other signals like diffs and code reviews. This creates an incentive to inflate token counts even at the cost of usefulness, such as asking an agent to summarize documentation instead of reading it directly. Salesforce reportedly set an explicit minimum monthly token-spend target.

“just like any data point, it can be weaponized. So, like a low performer with low impact and a low token count, clearly not even trying”

02Felt productivity versus measured productivity

The speaker cites a METER study in which developers using AI tools reported feeling about 20% more productive, while their actual measured output was about 20% less productive, a gap he uses to complicate the general claim that AI is straightforwardly speeding up engineering work.

“they felt 20% more productive, but their demonstrated results was actually they were 20% less productive”

▶ watch at 09:15
03Who benefits: engineers or their collaborators

The speaker argues the clearest productivity gain isn't necessarily for engineers themselves but for non-technical collaborators who can now write code directly rather than waiting on an engineer, removing a bottleneck. He also notes that unlike traditional tools, understanding the underlying theory of how these models work doesn't reliably translate into being better at using them.

“if you actually enable your non-coding your non-coding collaborators to code, actually they are more productive cuz they don't have to wait for you”

▶ watch at 10:18

“understanding the theory will not make you better at using the tools, which is an absolute mind honestly”

04Why big tech is rebuilding its own AI infra

Beyond buying tools like Cursor or Claude Code, companies such as Uber are building custom internal coding agents integrated into their monorepos and their own MCP gateways tied into service discovery. Shopify is cited as an early example of this pattern, having secured access to GitHub Copilot in 2021 before it was publicly available, in exchange for feedback from thousands of engineers.

“They're building their own custom background coding agents that is integrated into their mono repo. They are are having their own MCP gateway that is is now integrated into service discovery”
