Most Enterprise Agentic Projects Are Doomed, Here's Why

SpeakerJess Grogan-Avignon & Jack Wang · Accenture Watchyoutu.be/AGkzpxMdPn8
Agentic engineeringVertical AIProduct & UX

If you only read one thing

  • Only 12% of companies reach 'AI achiever' status; a solution the presenters built in 2 weeks took 12 months to reach production because of multi-team review chains (c1, c2).
  • Enterprises should fund AI like a VC portfolio rather than requiring predictable ROI business cases up front, since AI achievers see about 50% higher revenue growth from new capabilities, not cost cutting (c3, c4).
  • Agent deployment should follow a graduated trust ladder -- shadow mode, advisory mode, controlled autonomy, then wider autonomy -- gated by evidence rather than project-plan completion (c6).
01The enterprise scaffold, not AI capability, is the bottleneck

The speakers argue that large enterprises built control, process, and governance structures suited to human-speed operations, and these structures -- not data or API availability -- are now the primary drag on capturing AI value. Only 12% of companies reach what they call 'AI achiever' status, with most stuck piloting and spending without much return.

“Our research show that 12% of companies reach what we called AI achiever”

▶ watch at 01:31
02Approval infrastructure hasn't kept pace with AI-generated code volume

A project the speakers built for a large enterprise took about 2 weeks to build but 12 months to reach production because infrastructure, security, AI gateway, data governance, and application teams all needed to align through manual review chains. They cite GitHub data of 1 billion commits in 2025 and a run rate implying roughly 14 billion commits by the end of the current year as evidence that code supply is outpacing review/deployment infrastructure built for human speed.

“the whole application we built took about 2 weeks. It then took another 12 months to get that into production”

▶ watch at 04:34

“In 2025, they reported 1 billion commits. So far this year we're averaging 275 million per week, which means we're on track for 14 billion by the end of the year”

▶ watch at 05:50
03Business cases built for certainty don't fit AI's unlock-based value

Traditional business cases assume scope, expected value, and cost/time are knowable up front, but with AI the solution and its business case are often discovered by doing the work, and execution costs near zero unlock new categories of products rather than just efficiency. The speakers report AI achievers see about 50% higher revenue growth than peers, driven by new capabilities rather than cost cutting, citing Walmart's social-media trend scanner and JPMorgan's productized internal tool as examples.

“we see them achieving about 50% higher revenue growth than their peers. And that's not from cost cutting, that's from doing entirely new things”

▶ watch at 08:07
04Fund AI like a VC portfolio, not a single guaranteed-payback project

The speakers recommend enterprise finance functions back a portfolio of AI bets expecting most not to pay off but a few to compound exponentially, rather than demanding a fixed guaranteed payback timeline for each individual project, framing the key question as the cost of not doing the project rather than whether the project alone can be justified.

“a VC doesn't bet on one project and demand like 3 years fixed guaranteed payback because they know the certainty from a business case is a fantasy”

▶ watch at 09:47
05Build trust through a graduated autonomy ladder

Because agent behavior is emergent and can't be fully tested up front, the speakers recommend an exposure ladder: shadow mode (agent runs alongside humans without affecting outcomes), advisory mode (agent recommends, humans approve/reject), controlled autonomy (agent acts in narrow low-risk scenarios with kill switches), then wider autonomy -- with each step gated by evidence in outcomes rather than completion of project-plan activities.

“each step is gated by evidence in outcomes. It's not based on completion of activities in a project plan or pass-fail testing”

▶ watch at 16:34

Commentary (ours)

The VC-portfolio framing for AI funding is a useful pushback against ROI-gated business cases, but it assumes finance leadership is willing to accept a high failure rate per bet; worth probing whether that's realistic in more risk-averse regulated industries (ours).
