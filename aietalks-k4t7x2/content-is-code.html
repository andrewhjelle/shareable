TALKSlide brief · 10 min

Content Is Code

SpeakerMatt Palmer · Conductor Watchyoutu.be/yv6xovSsB1U
Generative mediaProduct & UX

If you only read one thing

  • Argues content creation (docs, product tours, marketing) is shifting from handcrafted or professionally-coded work to AI-generated code, citing his own Conductor product tour built entirely in React/Remotion.
  • Claims the scarce resource is no longer 'taste' but structure: consistent design tokens, clean PRs, and accurate internal documentation, since AI rewards conscientiousness and organizational excellence over raw coding skill.
  • Predicts 2027 will be 'the year of the content engineer,' with declarative, code-driven content pipelines replacing manual documentation and product-update workflows.
01Content has moved through three eras, ending in cheap code

Palmer frames content creation history as three eras: handcrafted (only time/money/skill as levers), expensive code (only professional engineers could build assets, so content wasn't prioritized), and now cheap code, where AI makes building any asset through code the fastest option.

“Era one, the handcrafted era of content. Era two, an era where code existed, but it was quite expensive. And era three, where code exists, but it's very cheap.”

02He built his own conference materials in React and Remotion

He demonstrates a Conductor product tour built entirely with React and the Remotion framework, and says every ancillary asset for his talk besides himself talking or writing was built in React or TypeScript, which he calls surprising given his data-engineering, Python background.

“I have a product tour for Conductor that was built entirely with React and Remotion.”

03What's expensive now is structure, not taste

Palmer argues the scarce resource in an AI-cheap-code world isn't design taste but structure: maintained brand guidelines, design tokens, clean and tagged PRs, and accurate internal documentation. He frames this as conscientiousness, defined as meticulousness and care guided by professional duty, rather than raw engineering skill.

“Now, you might think I'm going to say taste here. I'm not going to say taste because everybody says taste. Structure is expensive.”

▶ watch at 06:40

“the quality of being meticulous, careful, and guided by a strong sense of moral or professional duty”

04AI rewards organizational discipline

He states plainly that AI rewards conscientiousness, organizational excellence, and structure, and that assets shown in the talk are a byproduct of design tokens and structured code that gets exponentially harder to produce without that foundation.

“AI rewards conscientiousness. AI rewards organizational excellence. AI rewards structure.”

▶ watch at 08:13
05Predicts 2027 as the year of the content engineer

Palmer predicts that 2026 was 'the year of the creative technologist' in DevRel and that 2027 will be the year of the content engineer, with teams building declarative, robust content pipelines that produce documentation, walkthroughs, and product updates through code.

“I think 2027 is the year of the content engineer.”

Commentary (ours)

The 'structure over taste' claim is the most exportable idea here, but it's asserted rather than demonstrated with a counterexample of a well-structured codebase that still produced poor content — worth treating as a hypothesis to test against his own studio's output (ours).
