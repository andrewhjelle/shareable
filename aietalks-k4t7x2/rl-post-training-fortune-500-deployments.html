01Why GenAI pilots stall before production

Why GenAI pilots stall before production

  • The speaker cites a 95% failure rate for GenAI pilots reaching production, attributing it to what he calls the 'myth of the last mile': proprietary-model prompting and SFT-based fine-tuning both let a team demo well, but neither offers a systematic, mathematical way to integrate defects found in the field back into the model, so improvement stalls after the MVP.

“95% of GenAI pilots fail to reach production. Why that is the case? Uh I believe it's what we call the myth of the last mile.”

02RL vs. prompting and SFT for steering model behavior

RL vs. prompting and SFT for steering model behavior

  • RL is presented as disproportionately more effective than instruction fine-tuning or prompting at steering a model's behavior, which lets a much smaller model reach the same performance as a larger one trained with SFT.
  • The speaker gives AT&T's transcript-summarization use case, which costs millions of dollars at scale, and speech-to-speech customer support, which he says needs sub-half-second (ideally sub-third-of-a-second) latency that only small models can deliver, as concrete cases where a smaller RL-trained model changes whether a use case is viable at all.

“Reinforcement learning is disproportionately more effective than instruction fine-tuning, and likewise versus versus prompting. Reinforcement learning unlocks, let's say, outsized performance.”

03Agents raise the stakes for tokenomics and reliability

Agents raise the stakes for tokenomics and reliability

  • The speaker argues agentic use cases make the RL case stronger, not weaker: agents require more tokens and complexity, and there is less room for error because agents can directly change data in connected systems, which raises the bar for what can go into production and further stresses the tokenomics case for a smaller trained model.

“Agents require more tokens, more complexity. There is less room for errors because now agents will have access to the data, will change things in the database”

said at 06:47
04Training agents: reuse existing workflows as RL environments

Training agents: reuse existing workflows as RL environments

  • For clients like Manulife that already have a settled agent workflow, Adaptive ML plugs a new model directly into that existing environment rather than rebuilding it; where no environment exists, tools and even a mock user (itself an LLM) can be built.
  • Because agent training data doesn't exist in the wild, the speaker notes that an environment plus a reward function automatically produces a synthetic data set as a byproduct, via rejection sampling on good trajectories.

“we work for a Manulife and Manulife already had agents. So, they already have an actual workflow that has been settled.”

said at 07:50

“when you train a model with reinforcement learning and you have an environment and you have a reward in place, basically, you just build what is uh as a byproduct of your of your environment, you created a synthetic data set pipeline.”

said at 09:55
05Reward design in practice, and RL's real cost

Reward design in practice, and RL's real cost

  • Reward signals combine systematic checks (does the code run) with direct business KPIs -- client CCS's customer-support system optimizes directly for containment rate, the share of calls resolved end-to-end by the model -- and LLM-as-judge scoring for softer criteria like tone.
  • The speaker's stated catch with RL is that it's genuinely hard to implement: one of the most common algorithms, PPO, requires orchestrating four large language models at the same time.

“One of our client, CCS, the medical supply company I was mentioning before, as as a customer support system that as any other customer support system, what it's trying to maximize is containment rate.”

said at 12:30

“One of the most famous RL algorithms, which is a PPO, uh requires orchestrating not one, but four large language models at the same time.”

said at 14:38

Commentary (ours)

The pitch treats RL as the default post-training method for any production use case, agentic or not, which is a stronger claim than most of the industry currently makes -- the AT&T and CCS references are named as paying customers, so the cost/latency numbers carry more weight than a generic vendor benchmark, but they're also the vendor's own selected case studies (ours).
