State of the Union: Why Local, Why Now

SpeakerPanel: NVIDIA, Osmantic, Roboflow, EXO Labs (moderated by Matthew Berman) · Panel Watchyoutu.be/KB41dTlX1Uc
Local modelsInfra & servingOpen source

If you only read one thing

  • EXO Labs and NVIDIA engineers spent about three weeks tuning inference (VLLM backend, quantization) and got 10x throughput on the DGX Spark versus NVIDIA's existing playbook, without inventing new techniques (c2, c3).
  • Panelists argue the market is pulling toward many specialized, locally fine-tuned models per use case rather than one giant model, because businesses want cost control and sovereignty over model versions (c4, c5, c6).
  • Roboflow's CEO says vision had to build specialized, compute-constrained models years before language did, and cautions against naively fine-tuning a large open-vocabulary model like SAM 3 instead of distilling to a smaller specialized one (c7, c8).
01Local AI's 2026 inflection point

The panel opens by framing this as an inflection point where both models and the surrounding harnesses improved quickly, citing a shift in Andrej Karpathy's public stance on coding agents over roughly three months from needing constant supervision to struggling to keep up with their capability.

“you can't really trust these coding agents alone yet. You have to monitor them with an eye like a hawk.”

02NVIDIA and EXO Labs squeeze 10x more out of the DGX Spark

After a dinner conversation, NVIDIA pulled engineers from teams across the company (data center, VLM, Neatron) to work with EXO Labs for about three weeks on the DGX Spark. The result, reported to Jensen Huang and NVIDIA's executive staff, was a roughly 10x throughput improvement achieved mostly through tuning and configuration already proven in NVIDIA's data-center stack rather than new computer science. EXO Labs cites its own Neatron 3 Ultra demo, a 550-billion-parameter model running across four Sparks at 30 tokens per second, as an example of what that local hardware can now do.

“we did end up getting 10x performant improvements on the DGX Spark and we sent an update to Jensen, to the executive staff, to the teams that were helping”

03Multi-model routing as a cost lever

Panelists describe enterprises adopting a mixed-model pattern where a frontier model handles top-level planning and cheaper models execute subtasks, citing Coinbase publicly reporting that its token consumption grew while costs stayed flat because of this kind of routing.

“I know Coinbase and Brian Armstrong just came out with that just great post the other day talking about uh how their tokens are are exploding yet their costs are staying flat”

▶ watch at 13:57
04Why the panel expects specialized, locally trained models to win

Osmantic's founder says he has argued since 2024 that small, specialized, per-use-case models are the future, driven by businesses wanting sovereignty over model versions and behavior rather than depending on one general frontier model. He also predicts that continual learning, updating model weights directly rather than relying on markdown memory files, will need to run on local hardware.

“I have tweets about that in 2024 saying small and specialized models are the future and it is going to be very user by per use case per workflow for businesses”

▶ watch at 18:09

“continual learning is not being talked about by the frontier model uh as much it's coming though it will happen and it needs to be running on local hardware for it to happen”

▶ watch at 31:37
05Vision's specialization lesson, and the real adoption barrier

Roboflow's CEO says vision engineering was forced toward specialized, faster learners earlier than language because it typically runs under tight compute constraints (robots, low connectivity, on-device), and he warns that naively fine-tuning a large open-vocabulary model like SAM 3 loses the very capability that makes it useful; distilling to a smaller task-specific model is often better. Separately, a panelist argues the main barrier to mainstream adoption isn't model capability but usability, comparing the bar to how simple opening Cursor already feels.

“I need this model to run in a limited context um and I'm not going to prioritize full world scale generalizability instead I'm going to have specialized in domain context work”

“if you know you're distilling down to a specific fixed class list then you can actually drop the large expensive autoenccoder portion of SAM and use a specific maybe like DTOR or more specialized model”

Commentary (ours)

The DGX Spark story is a useful data point against the idea that local inference is capability-limited: the gains came entirely from applying existing data-center tuning to new hardware, which suggests a lot of local/edge headroom is still just unclaimed engineering effort rather than a fundamental compute ceiling (ours).
