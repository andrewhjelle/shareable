01What the project studies

The presentation is a student microeconomics project (course code ECO162) that uses France's economy and L'Oreal shampoo as a case study. It cites L'Oreal's founding by a French chemist who developed an early hair-color formula, and references L'Oreal's real-world revenue growing from roughly 25.84 billion in 2016 to 44.05 billion in 2025 as evidence of steady demand.

“Eugis Cheller, a French chemist who developed the first safe hair color formula.”

02Why the students say shampoo demand is inelastic

The students argue L'Oreal shampoo behaves as an inelastic good: consumers keep buying it even at higher prices because it's a daily-routine staple rather than a discretionary purchase. Using a hypothetical worked example with invented price points, they calculate a price elasticity of demand of 0.13, which they interpret as strongly inelastic.

“When consumer have higher income, they are more likely to spend money on premium or high quality product.”

▶ watch at 02:03

“Even if the price increase, customers still continue to buy this product because they need them for their daily routine.”

▶ watch at 06:11
03Where the students see demand risk

The project also names where demand could soften: it cites consumer income as a demand driver, noting that lower income during COVID-19 pushed some shoppers toward cheaper shampoo, and separately warns that inflation can push price-sensitive consumers to trade down from L'Oreal to more affordable brands.

“Based on the calculation the PE value is 0.13. Since the elasticity is more than zero but less than one the demand is inelastic.”

▶ watch at 05:09

“during period of inflation, some consumers wish to more affordable shampoo brand instead of exclusive one like L'Oreal to reduce their spending.”
