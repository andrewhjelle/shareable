01The gender gap in beauty-industry leadership

The moderator opened with statistics: as of June 2025 only 11% of Fortune 500 CEOs were women, and most of those had been promoted internally rather than hired externally, which she connected to mentorship access. Within beauty specifically, she cited research showing women hold only 15% of CEO roles at the top 20 beauty manufacturers despite making up 69% of the industry's workforce.

“of the Fortune 500 companies, there are only 11% of CEO positions that are held by women. That's America's list largest list of public companies.”

02What Parlux's president looks for in early talent

Asked what she looks for in early-career hires, Lori Singer named curiosity, passion, and initiative over polished resumes, citing Dara Portoi's internship performance as the example that set the model.

“So what I look for in early talent and first thing I look for is curiosity. I want to see people who ask questions who are really hungry to learn, people who are passionate”

▶ watch at 14:11
03A career setback and a resulting mentorship philosophy

Singer described being passed over for a VP promotion she believed she was the most qualified candidate for, an experience that led her to seek feedback from peers and eventually earn the role a year later. She traced her present-day emphasis on mentorship back to an early boss at Revlon who took her under her wing despite several organizational layers separating them.

“it was heartbreaking and I was so disappointed and I didn't understand why. I thought I was the most qualified person for the job.”

▶ watch at 20:29

“So mentorship is so important to me because of all the people who have helped shape me along the way. I'm very fortunate that I've worked for people who have taken me under their wing”

▶ watch at 22:33
04Case study: rescuing a stalled fragrance campaign

Singer described briefing a creative agency to blend early-2000s and present-day imagery of a celebrity fragrance client into one ad campaign; the agency's proposals failed three times. Rather than continuing to iterate with the agency, a team member proposed dropping it entirely and having the shoot's own photographer originate the concept, a pivot Singer initially resisted before agreeing to it. The resulting campaign became one of the brand's most successful.

“we had briefed a creative agency to create the ad campaign for iconic. And the brief to them was to blend iconic Paris from the early 2000s, right?”

▶ watch at 42:22

“it ended up being one of our most successful campaigns they were right so once again I was wrong they were right and that's what's important”

▶ watch at 43:24
05Describing a celebrity collaboration process

Singer separately described a remote creative session with a musician client during the pandemic, in which the artist used her phone to walk Singer through her personal fragrance-bottle collection, which shaped brand direction. She also described her own approach to work-life balance as 'integration' rather than balance.

“Billy had her phone and she ended up taking her phone and like zooming me around her room and I saw her artwork and her posters and her bed.”

▶ watch at 37:12

“So I think for me it's about work life integration, not balance. I balance like a seesaw. It's like one ends up the other end is up.”

Commentary (ours)

(ours) The campaign rescue and the internal-promotion mentorship stat point the same direction: Parlux's stated preference is for the person closest to the work (the photographer, the internally promoted manager) to make the call, over an external specialist or hire, which tracks with Singer's own account of being passed over for an outside candidate before eventually getting the role.
