The Era of Compound Engineering

SpeakerKieran Klaassen · Every/Cora Watchyoutu.be/_ehJyfHg1Vk
Agentic engineeringMemoryCoding agents

If you only read one thing

  • Kieran runs Kora, a production AI email client, largely alone; he says he hasn't written a line of code this year and started the current rebuild in January.
  • His loop (brainstorm, plan, work, review, polish, compound) puts 50% of effort into teaching the system after each task, on the claim that one engineer with a compounding system beats full teams using AI without it.
  • His compound engineering plugin is used by hundreds of thousands of people daily; his bet is that implementation keeps getting cheaper while judgment does not.
01One engineer, no code written this year

Kieran says he hasn't written a single line of code this year yet ships and is proud of Kora, an agent-native AI email client used by thousands of people. He built the current version-two rebuild essentially alone, starting in January, with some design and infrastructure support but full personal ownership of the product.

“I haven't written a single line of code this year.”

02The loop: brainstorm, plan, work, review, polish, compound

Kieran describes a repeating loop he calls the human-AI sandwich: humans provide judgment at the brainstorming start and the raise-the-bar polish at the end, with AI doing the work in between. He claims one engineer running this compounding loop beats full teams that use AI without a system for capturing what was learned.

“I see that one engineer with a compounding system just beats teams like full teams that use AI that don't.”

▶ watch at 06:15

“But 50% of the time should go to um teaching the system for anything that it did wrong.”

▶ watch at 07:49
03Why extraction beats repetition

His core rule is to spend roughly half of every task teaching the system anything it got wrong, not just shipping the feature, storing that knowledge as solution documents in his repository rather than in chat history. He argues this is more token-efficient long term because the system already has the right answers embedded, cutting the need for review or repeated research.

“make sure to extract never repeat if you see yourself repeating yourself make sure to extract it somehow”

▶ watch at 16:37

“in my research it's actually more token efficient because if you have the right answers and the right solutions already within the token you don't need to do review”

▶ watch at 08:19
04Adoption

Kieran built his compound engineering plugin while building Kora and shared it; it now runs in multiple coding tools (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and others) and is used by hundreds of thousands of people daily.

“now hundreds of thousands of people use it daily.”

▶ watch at 09:23
05The bet: implementation cheapens, judgment doesn't

Kieran's closing framing is that implementation is only getting cheaper while judgment is not, so future systems need access to accumulated judgment and taste to keep delivering leverage, which is why he treats the compounding-knowledge step as the real bottleneck to invest in.

“the bet is implementation is only getting cheaper and judgment is not”

▶ watch at 18:42

Commentary (ours)

The team-beating claim (c8) is presented without a benchmark or comparison group, so it reads as Kieran's own operating conviction rather than a measured result; useful as a design principle to test rather than a proven outcome.
