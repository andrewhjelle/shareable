01The mismatch: RL wants one cluster, capacity is scattered

Jiang contrasts the 'cathedral' shape of a trainer cluster -- one region, fast interconnect, many GPUs -- with the 'bro' shape of actually available compute, which is scattered across different providers, regions, prices, and availability. The default RL loop forces rollout into the same tightly coupled, RDMA-connected cluster as the trainer, so rollout inherits the hardest-to-get capacity constraint even though it doesn't need to.

02The key insight: rollout doesn't need all-reduce, only the trainer does

Training is a tightly coupled job requiring collective all-reduce and model-parallel communication on fast fabric, but rollout is a fleet of independent serving jobs generating trajectories with no cross-job collective communication. That means the trainer must stay in a single cluster, but the rollout serving fleet -- the 'movable unit' -- can be spread across regions as long as it can receive policy weight versions and send back trajectories.

“cross rollout jobs there's no global or reduce”

03Why weight changes are sparse: Adam's push vs BF16's floor

Shipping a full checkpoint (hundreds of gigabytes for a Kimi-scale MoE) every time rollout needs a new policy version is too slow for near-real-time weight updates. Jiang's team found that Adam's per-parameter update is typically far smaller than the rounding resolution of the BF16 format the rollout engine actually serves, so from the rollout engine's point of view, the served weight value doesn't change most of the time, even though the underlying dense gradients and master-weight updates are non-zero. Measured across model families, about 99% of served weights are bit-identical from one step to the next, and an internal run on a model served in FP8 showed only about 0.15% of weights changing in an early high-learning-rate step, dropping further as training stabilized.

04Result: the Stitch protocol and payload size

Because the change is sparse and bit-level reconstructable, Modal ships a lossless diff (changed positions plus replacement bits) instead of a full checkpoint, shrinking the sync payload from hundreds of gigabytes to hundreds of megabytes. In the resulting Stitch protocol, the trainer publishes each immutable policy version to a shared bulletin board after its optimizer step, and rollout engines outside the training cluster pull the version they need independently, without requiring RDMA connectivity to the trainer.
