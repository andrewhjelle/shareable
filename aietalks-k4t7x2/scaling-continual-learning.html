Scaling up Continual Learning

SpeakerRonak Malde · Trajectory Watchyoutu.be/zL1kLftVTlo
Continual learningPost-trainingAgentic engineering

If you only read one thing

  • Trajectory's on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) replaces GRPO's parallel rollouts and sequence-level reward with a single on-policy rollout matched token-by-token to a teacher's full vocabulary distribution (c3).
  • Scaled to 120B-1T parameter models with 50-100+ tool calls, OPSD surpasses GRPO's Sonnet-level ceiling on LiveCodeBench but introduces divergence collapse (hedging into words like 'wait'/'maybe') and hint leakage (c4, c6, c7).
  • The fixes are step-level KL-divergence weighting per token and residual/partial-hint guidance plus LLM-filtered hints to stop the model from shortcutting to a known answer (c8).
01Why current post-training algorithms fall short

Malde traces the evolution from SFT to DPO/RLHF to GRPO, arguing each stage trades away one of four desired properties: online task distribution, on-policy sampling, single-rollout parallelism, and per-token reward. GRPO gets on-policy sampling and strong results but requires heavy parallel-rollout infrastructure and collapses feedback into one scalar per trajectory, which he compares to a teacher grading an entire essay with a single score.

02On-policy self-distillation (OPSD)

OPSD starts from standard distillation (student fits a teacher's log-probs on a fixed dataset), swaps in the student's own on-policy rollout as the training data, and, when no larger teacher model is available, gives the same model a 'hint' (privileged information such as a golden solution) in the prompt so its own hinted log-probs become the teacher target for the un-hinted student.

“you take what's called this hint, put it into the beginning of the prompt, and now you match the log props of the student without that hint to the teacher with that hint”

▶ watch at 08:43
03Scaling breaks: divergence collapse and hint leakage

At 120B+ parameters with 50-100+ tool calls, eval accuracy and run-to-run variance spike and tool-call formatting breaks down. On long trajectories the teacher repeatedly tries to course-correct a diverging student, pushing the model toward hedging tokens like 'wait' and 'maybe.' Separately, hints leak: if a hint states an answer outright, the model's rollout skips reasoning and jumps straight to restating that answer, a shortcut that would never occur in production.

04Fixes: step-level weighting and residual guidance

For divergence collapse, Trajectory computes step-level KL divergence between student and teacher and uses it as a per-token weighting factor rather than a flat KL penalty, so only the steps that actually diverge get corrected. For hint leakage, they use an LLM to filter hints down to what a model should reasonably infer, and a 'residual guidance' technique that blends a partial-hint teacher with a full-hint teacher so the student isn't pushed fully out of its own distribution.

“not just like normal KL where we use that as a KL penalty, but instead we're actually multiplying the token weight uh of every single step based on this divergence property”

▶ watch at 14:31
05Results and what's next

With these fixes OPSD scaled to a 12B model on Mercor Apex agents requiring 100+ tool calls and surpassed RL results there, while also collapsing the number of tokens needed to solve hard problems compared to RL's tendency to think longer. Malde frames continual learning across production traces as the field's next major unlock for 2026-2027, while acknowledging in Q&A that the field is still in a 'pseudo continual learning' phase of batched offline updates.
