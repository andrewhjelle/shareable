The Future Is Domain-Specific Agents

SpeakerJustin Schroeder · Standard Agents Watchyoutu.be/spNAUEgq_A8 · 30 min SourceRecording of this talk at AI Engineer (2026), published on YouTube by the conference. Slides below are frames from that recording, reproduced for reference and study; rights remain with the speaker and the conference. Quotes are verbatim from the talk transcript with the time they were said.
takeAgentic engineeringVertical AIInfra & serving

Argues for composing many small, permission-limited domain-specific agents (coordinator + sub-agents talking in plain English) instead of inheriting more skills/MCP servers/context onto one large agent

If you only read one thing

  • Skills and MCP are both forms of 'inheritance' -- stacking more context onto one agent -- which works until it doesn't; the alternative is 'composition': many small domain-specific agents, each with its own system prompt, tools, and message history, coordinated via a top-level agent that talks to them in plain English.
  • Domain-specific agents are far more token-efficient (regularly over 80% token efficiency claimed) and dramatically cheaper because narrow tasks can run on small/non-frontier models -- DeepSeek V4 Flash is cited as 137x cheaper than [Claude] Fable 5 per task.
  • Prediction: domain-specific agents and frameworks around them will see a dramatic uptick in the second half of 2026, with 2027 becoming 'the year of multi-agent orchestration,' partly because token costs adjusted for IQ are up 29% and unadjusted up 76% so far in 2026.

This traces the speaker's case that skill/MCP inheritance breaks down at scale, motivating a composition model of small coordinated agents that he claims are cheaper and more token-efficient (ours).

Defining agents and why everyone is building custom ones

  • The speaker defines an agent as 'deterministic software that harness the non-deterministic results produced by models in pursuit of some desired objective,' and treats 'agent' and 'harness' as effectively interchangeable.
  • He argues businesses -- from Fortune 500s to independent insurance brokers -- are building custom agents because they want their own data properly integrated into AI, not because generic chat assistants aren't available.

“it comes down to integration. Businesses want their data properly integrated into AI. They They believe, and are probably right, that if they appropriately leverage AI, they're going to have these dramatic gains”

02Custom agents are hard, not portable, not composable
Slide at 06:20 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Custom agents are hard, not portable, not composable

  • He lists concrete failure points: orchestrating the agentic loop, provider abstractions, durable execution/fault recovery, telemetry at scale, and portability -- an agent built and working on one machine often fails on another's environment/runtime, and a working agent for one use case is hard to reuse for another.

“if I try to pass it off to somebody else, there's a very high likelihood that between all of the environment variable configurations and and system requirements and and runtimes, there's a good chance it's not going to run on that person's machine”

said at 06:42
03Skills and MCP are inheritance, and inheritance breaks down
Slide at 12:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 1 more from this section below

Skills and MCP are inheritance, and inheritance breaks down

  • He frames the agent stack (model, system prompt, tools, skills, MCP, messages) as almost entirely context, and argues that adding skills or MCP servers to one agent is a form of software 'inheritance' -- it works, but there are diminishing returns as the number of skills grows, citing the old software principle 'composition over inheritance.'

“Eventually, inheritance starts to break down. Imagine, like, you know, okay, I've got five skills on uh ChatGPT or on or on Claude, excuse me. And uh that works pretty well. Now, what if I have 100 skills?”

said at 12:28
04Composition: small domain-specific agents coordinated in English
Slide at 14:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

Composition: small domain-specific agents coordinated in English

  • His proposed alternative is composition: instead of one agent inheriting a Figma skill, a small standalone agent with a system prompt written specifically for Figma, with only the tools it needs and a minimal message history, sits alongside similarly small Gmail/travel/spreadsheet agents, all coordinated by a top-level agent that communicates with them in plain English, similar to how Apollo mission control was staffed by narrow human specialists rather than one generalist.

“each of them is a separate isolated agent, a full agent. Not just a little server with tools on it. It's a full agent with its own message history, its own agentic loop”

said at 14:01
05Claimed benefits: token efficiency, cost, permission scoping, scaling
Slide at 16:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 4 more from this section below

Claimed benefits: token efficiency, cost, permission scoping, scaling

  • He claims domain-specific agents regularly exceed 80% token efficiency versus a single large agent, because a sub-agent's context only needs its own system message, tools, and the specific incoming request rather than the full conversation history.
  • He cites DeepSeek V4 Flash as 137x cheaper than Fable 5 per task, and argues narrow agents can be permission-scoped tighter than a general coding agent, plus scale more easily since each is a small independent execution environment.

“they are far more token efficient. Far more token efficient. We regularly see over 80% token efficiency for any given task”

said at 16:45

“the cost difference is mind-boggling. It is 137 times cheaper than Fable per task”

said at 18:10
06Prediction: uptick in domain-specific agents through 2026-2027
Slide at 20:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Prediction: uptick in domain-specific agents through 2026-2027

  • He predicts a dramatic uptick in domain-specific agent talk and frameworks through the rest of 2026, with 2027 becoming the year of 'multi-agent orchestration,' citing Vercel's Eve framework as early validation and rising token costs (up 29% adjusted for IQ, 76% unadjusted, so far in 2026) as a driver toward efficiency-focused narrow agents.

“we are going to see a dramatic uptick in people talking about building domain-specific agents, frameworks around them”

said at 20:50

“They're up 29% when you adjust for IQ just this year, halfway through the year, we're already up 30%”

Commentary (ours)

The 137x DeepSeek-vs-Fable figure is presented without benchmark methodology (no task type, no accuracy floor) -- it should be treated as a directional cost-ratio claim rather than a verified number until a source study is found (ours).
