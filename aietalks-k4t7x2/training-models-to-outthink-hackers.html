A general-world-model benchmark models mostly fail

  • Wolf introduces a benchmark, comparable in spirit to ARC-AGI-3, where models must understand what changes in a small interactive game when the agent takes an action -- essentially building a dynamic model of the world on the fly.
  • Current models score only 1-2% success, which Wolf attributes to models being unable to build and act on such a dynamic world model, not to the task being conceptually hard for humans.

02Why access control specifically
Slide at 08:40 · click any slide to enlarge

Why access control specifically

  • Rolls explains Arithmetic's benchmark, Masov, focuses narrowly on access-control vulnerabilities (IDOR-class) because trying to cover all of cybersecurity in one benchmark doesn't work.
  • Access control is the first foothold in most attacks and, per OWASP, the number-one vulnerability category, underlying a roughly $30 billion industry; these are logic-based system flaws rather than simple code bugs.

“it actually leads to being the number one on the OAS list and has created this sort of $30 billion industry”

03How the benchmark is built and graded
Slide at 11:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 2 more from this section below

How the benchmark is built and graded

  • Masov's tasks are built from real zero-day vulnerabilities Arithmetic's own researchers found and submitted for verification, assembled into blackbox environments so models don't see the code or know the exploit.
  • A worked example shows a real submitted zero-day in a Keycloak/vault/broker chain, where an admin check validates by name in one place and by ID in another, allowing privilege escalation by renaming.
  • Every step of the exploitation chain has a deterministic grader, allowing partial-credit measurement of how far a model got.

Current model performance is narrow and slow

  • On the hardest tasks in the live demo, there was only one solve at k=1, and at k=5 only GPT-5.5 among public models solved it; other models captured most of the discovery-phase information but never made the final exploitation leap.
  • In one recorded case a model reasoned continuously for over three hours without solving the task.

05The bet: models as the new defensive stack
Slide at 13:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 5 more from this section below

The bet: models as the new defensive stack

  • The speakers argue that as the economics of cyber offense shift in attackers' favor, the only way to keep pace is to make models themselves capable enough to power defense, following the trajectory coding saw over roughly the past year.
  • They warn against relying on just two labs to solve this and argue for open-source models, specialized fast inference, and post-training data as necessary parts of the response.

“if every model in the world could get really really really good at doing this and very fast, that should give a lasting defense uh and capability to the defenders”

Commentary (ours)

The benchmark's difficulty and realism rest entirely on Arithmetic's own in-house zero-day discoveries, which haven't been independently audited or reproduced outside the company, and the $30 billion IDOR-industry figure is asserted without a cited source (ours).
