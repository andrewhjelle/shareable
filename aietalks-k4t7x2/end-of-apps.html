The End of Apps

SpeakerKitze · Sizzy.co Watchyoutu.be/4fntwuOoedA
Product & UXAgentic engineeringMemory

If you only read one thing

  • Packaged agent platforms (Open Claw and similar) are unreliable exactly where it matters most -- cron jobs and multi-agent handoffs -- and Discord/Telegram were never built to run a life OS (c3, c4, c7).
  • Instead of an agent memory system, the speaker's own tool nests topics so a child topic (e.g., 'Benji customer support') automatically inherits its parent topics' descriptions as context (c5, c8).
  • The speaker predicts the role of AI inverts: instead of humans always prompting, an AI that has ingested notifications, emails, and to-dos will prompt the human with the next task, and most consumer apps disappear (c2, c6).
01From ChatGPT plugins to 'apps are over'

The speaker recounts declaring to his wife that ChatGPT plugins meant the end of all apps and SaaS, and that his own long-running personal productivity app, Benji, was pointless -- a call he says he has repeated at every subsequent model release over three years.

“I called my wife and I was like, "Honey, it's over. It's over for all the apps, for all SaaS”

02Packaged agents break at cron jobs and multi-agent handoff

Despite months of daily use, the speaker describes Open Claw and similar packaged agent setups as unreliable specifically at cron jobs and multi-agent coordination, with agents forgetting context between messages. He also argues Discord and Telegram, while pressed into service as agent interfaces, were never designed for managing a life OS, and that talking to a heavily-guardrailed model feels flat compared to earlier, less-restricted versions.

“it was and kind of is for me unreliable where it matters most, which is like cron jobs, multi-agents, the agents talking”

“Discord and Telegram were not meant for Life OS. We're just molding them into something, but they'll never be the right UI for you to manage your life fully”

03Nested topics instead of agent memory

The speaker says he does not believe current agent 'memory' systems actually solve memory. His own tool instead nests topics (e.g., work, then Benji, then Benji customer support) so that a child topic's first prompt automatically injects the description of all its parent topics, rather than pulling from a separate memory store. He also argues a single one-on-one chat with one generalist agent is worse than specialized agents scoped to specific parts of a person's life.

“I don't believe in memory of agents. Like people are like, "Oh, we finally saw Mila Yov which solved memory." I'm like, "No, absolutely she didn't solve memory”

▶ watch at 15:31

“I think that one-on-one chat with one agent sucks because if you think about delegating in your life, if you have like business and personal and family and blah blah blah”

▶ watch at 09:18
04Prediction: AI starts prompting you

Looking forward, the speaker predicts the direction of prompting will invert: instead of a person always initiating, an AI that has ingested a person's notifications, emails, and to-dos will surface the next task and ask questions, while the person mostly makes decisions. He predicts most consumer apps become unnecessary as a result, replaced by on-the-fly generated interfaces.

“the role of AI is going to inverse. So the way we prompt AI right now, I think it's going to inverse and the fully productive people will be the one who delegate 99% of the stuff”

“That's 100% true because I think where we're going, we're actually not going to need most consumer apps”
