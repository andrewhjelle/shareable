If you only read one thing

  • Meurer's thesis: 'forward deployed engineering' has been used to describe so many different jobs over time — DevOps, data integration, custom solutioning, platform enablement — that as a single term it doesn't really exist (c1).
  • At Palantir, the role started as literal on-call DevOps (her own onboarding project was deploying software to an EC2 instance) before evolving into data integration and later platform enablement work (c2).
  • She predicts the confusion is now spreading rather than resolving: Google and OpenAI have both recently built out dedicated FDE-style hiring efforts, and today's job postings combine incompatible skill sets (staff-level engineering, years of direct sales, solutions architecture) into one role (c7, c8).

Thesis: the term has no coherent definition

  • Meurer, who worked as an FDE at Palantir from 2016-2021 and now leads agent engineering at Sierra, states plainly that forward deployed engineering 'doesn't exist' as a single coherent job — it has been stretched to cover too many different functions to mean anything specific anymore, even as it remains one of the most sought-after titles in AI hiring.

“the dirty secret of forward deployed engineering, I won't make you wait until the end, is it doesn't exist.”

Origins at Palantir: DevOps first

  • Meurer traces the role's history from 2008 Palantir job postings that emphasized being physically 'forward deployed' with customers, largely because deployments were on-prem.
  • She says platform stability made up the bulk of the early job, illustrating this with her own onboarding project: deploying Palantir's software onto an EC2 instance.

“my onboarding project at Palantir was actually deploy the software on an EC2 instance.”

03Today's hiring bar is incoherent

Today's hiring bar is incoherent

  • Meurer says the role's job-to-be-done grew over each era rather than being replaced (DevOps, then data integration, then custom solutioning, then platform enablement) and that by 2026 companies like Google (GCP customer engineers) and OpenAI have built dedicated units around it.
  • She argues a composite of current FDE job postings would demand contradictory experience levels — years of staff engineering plus years of direct sales plus solutions architecture — in a single hire.

“Google recently announced with GCP an effort to hire customer engineers or FDEs. Open AI created a new unit with a massive investment to aid the corporate AI push.”

04Convergence: product engineering becomes forward deployed
Slide at 13:40 · click any slide to enlarge

Convergence: product engineering becomes forward deployed

  • Meurer argues the boundary is now dissolving from the other direction too: at Sierra, product engineering itself is becoming more client-facing, and she recommends that both good product engineers and good FDEs should be thinking about the product and the customer together rather than treating them as separate lanes.

“So product engineering is also becoming more client-facing. And so if you're a good product engineer, if you're a good forward deployed engineer, you should be thinking about the product and the customer both together.”

05Outcome-based pricing needs FDE to guarantee the outcome
Slide at 15:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 1 more from this section below

Outcome-based pricing needs FDE to guarantee the outcome

  • Meurer connects the FDE conversation to how software is priced, describing a shift from seat-based to usage-based to outcome-based pricing.
  • She says Sierra has always priced on the value software delivers rather than seats, and predicts most of the market will move toward outcome-based pricing — which she argues is exactly the function forward deployed engineering exists to guarantee, closing with the line that FDE 'is dead, and long live forward deployed engineering.'

“we've always been focused on this outcome-based pricing model. We think that you should pay a software platform for the value that that software delivers.”
