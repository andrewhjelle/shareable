From single calls to ReAct loops to workflow graphs

  • AI systems evolved from a single prompt-and-response, to a fixed retrieval chain, to the ReAct pattern of models reasoning and acting in a loop.
  • That loop was exciting but the models of 2023-24 weren't robust enough at tool calling and long-context reasoning, so teams baked orchestration and planning logic directly into workflow graphs or state machines to regain reliability, trading flexibility for predictability, until the graphs' own complexity created new failure surfaces.

03Back to loops, with new reliability metrics
Slide at 13:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 1 more from this section below

Back to loops, with new reliability metrics

  • As tool calling and planning got reliable again in mid-to-late 2025, teams moved back toward open agent loops.
  • But run-to-run trajectory variance is now high, so evals shifted from single-answer scoring to distributional metrics: pass@k (did it succeed at least once across k runs, a capability measure) and pass^k (did it succeed every time across k runs, a reliability measure).

Closing the loop: harvesting production failures

  • Bhatawdekar recommends treating evals as a flywheel, continually harvesting failures from production data to keep eval sets reflective of real-world use.
  • Braintrust's "topics" feature runs cluster analysis on production data specifically to surface new failure categories teams hadn't anticipated and hadn't built guardrails or evals for.

Commentary (ours)
