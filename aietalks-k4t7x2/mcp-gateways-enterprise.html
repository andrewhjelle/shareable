Gateways are All You Need

SpeakerKaran Sampath · Anthropic Watchyoutu.be/CD6R4Wf3jnY · 17 min SourceRecording of this talk at AI Engineer (2026), published on YouTube by the conference. Slides below are frames from that recording, reproduced for reference and study; rights remain with the speaker and the conference. patternMCP & protocolsSecurityInfra & serving

Names a concrete architecture pattern -- a gateway as an enterprise's single MCP root of trust -- for deciding how to let internal teams stand up their own MCP servers without funneling each one through security review

If you only read one thing

  • Anthropic's forward-deployed engineering view is that enterprises hit three unsolved problems with MCP -- observability, access control, and security -- even as the official registry grows with 'over thousands of servers.'
  • Their proposed fix is a gateway: a middle layer between MCP servers and clients that becomes the enterprise's single root of trust, so any team (even legal) can build and ship its own MCP server without repeated security review.
  • The pitch extends to scaling from tens of MCP servers to 'hundreds of thousands of agents' and giving agents their own novel identity definitions.

01Three problems enterprises hit with MCP

Three problems enterprises hit with MCP

  • Despite an official MCP registry with thousands of servers and growing, enterprises struggle with what they see as table-stakes needs: observability (who's using which tools, what's failing), access control (scoping servers and tools to the right groups), and security (verifying servers are safe and that untrusted remote clients can't reach private data).

“there is an official registry today which contains over thousands of servers, and this is growing rapidly over the last year”

said at 00:31

“Enterprises struggle to deal with what they believe to be table stakes. Things like observability”

said at 01:33

“finally, security. And I like to think of the three of these is almost like a three-headed hydra in some ways, right?”

said at 02:35
02Why a registry alone doesn't solve it
Slide at 03:20 · click any slide to enlarge · 4 more from this section below

Why a registry alone doesn't solve it

  • The speaker argues that problems like authentication, access control, observability, and credential management were solvable in prior API paradigms but don't yet have a good MCP-native solution, leaving individual teams unable to get their MCP servers deployed even when they build them.

“These are all really hard problems that you've kind of solved in previous paradigms with APIs, but we really don't have a good way of solving it uh at the moment”

said at 03:06
03The gateway pitch: one root of trust
Slide at 05:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 7 more from this section below

The gateway pitch: one root of trust

  • The core recommendation is that security teams establish a root of trust by 'blessing' one platform.
  • A gateway is defined as a middleman layer sitting between potentially hundreds of MCP servers and any MCP client, so the client only ever talks to the gateway.

“The really important thing for security teams and enterprises that want to allow this to be decentralized is they need to establish a root of trust”

said at 05:40

“a gateway, as a black box definition of it, is simply a middleman or sort of middle layer in between your MCP servers, and they can be numerous into the hundreds, and any MCP client”

said at 06:43
04What a gateway buys a team
Slide at 10:30 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

What a gateway buys a team

  • With a gateway handling auth, access control, and connectivity, individual teams -- including non-engineering teams like legal -- only need to build their MCP server's business logic and can adhere to enterprise-defined standard primitives without owning security infrastructure.
  • The speaker also expects agents will need novel, enterprise-scoped identity definitions going into the next year.

“So, any new MCP server that you want to build kind of has to adhere to primitives that are that are within your enterprise and how your enterprise wants to go about it”

said at 13:57

“This is something we think is going to be really important into the coming year, where we think agents are going to require newer and novel definitions of identity”

said at 10:19
05Scaling and the summary takeaway
Slide at 15:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Scaling and the summary takeaway

  • Scaling dozens of MCP servers to serve hundreds of thousands of agents is described as a hard surface to maintain without a gateway routing requests intelligently.
  • The talk closes on gateways as the mechanism for building that root of trust at enterprise scale.

“if you have 40 MCP servers that need to scale to from tens to hundreds to thousands to hundreds of thousands of agents, that's a really hard surface to maintain”

said at 14:28

“we really think gateways for secured connections allow you to build that root of trust”

said at 17:04

Commentary (ours)

This is an Anthropic vendor talk pitching a pattern Anthropic sells services around, so the specific claim that gateways are the best (or only) fix should be read as their commercial framing rather than a neutral survey of alternatives (ours).
