01FDE is a product strategy, not a role

The speaker frames the entire talk around a quote from Palantir's current CTO: the foundational truth of forward deployed engineering is that it is not a job title but a mechanism for discovering what a product should be. He traces this back to Palantir's early, isolated build of a data-retention product (Phoenix) that failed on real bank data, arguing the root cause was building without being embedded with a customer.

“the first fundamental truth of FTE is that this is not a role this is a product strategy”

02Detect the real problem, ship the real thing

A customer's 47-page requirements doc for a custom BI dashboard was scoped for three months of dev work; going on site and asking what the dispatcher actually does each morning reduced the real need to a Slack alert, built in four hours. The lesson stated is that customers describe solutions, not problems, and the FTE's job is to define the actual problem before building anything.

“The first move that we learned from forward deployed perspective is detect the real problem and ship the real thing.”

▶ watch at 05:39
03Actions speak louder than words

An engineer resisted migrating CSV exports to Parquet for a year despite clear pipeline benefits; going on site revealed she was manually opening CSVs in a spreadsheet-style viewer for spot checks, something Parquet's format didn't support. Building her a Parquet viewer overnight got the migration approved within two days and reduced pipeline execution time from about 17 hours to about two.

“you are effectively an observer of what happens on site. An action speaks significantly louder than words.”

“the pipeline execution time went from 17 hours to about two”

04Own the vocabulary

Different teams (sales, ops, finance, dev) define the same customer entity differently, causing integration breakage and data-quality issues. The speaker argues that codifying a shared ontology of nouns and verbs is a deliberate FTE responsibility, and that once users adopt a product, they also adopt its language.

“users don't just adopt your product, they actually adopt your language”

▶ watch at 13:56
05Ship fast has a cost

A quick Groovy script written as a temporary fix ended up running in production for years at a ~100,000-person customer. The stated rule is that any hack that solves a real problem will go into production and stay there indefinitely, so FTEs should build with the expectation that whatever they ship will run for roughly 18 months.

“The most dangerous words in forward deployed engineering or engineering is this is just temporary.”
