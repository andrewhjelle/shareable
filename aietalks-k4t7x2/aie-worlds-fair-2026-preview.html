Scale and curation approach

  • Swix says World's Fair 2026 is larger than all previous AI Engineer events combined by attendee count, framing the event as a curated 'buffet' where one ticket covers all tracks.
  • Roughly 50% of topics are returning evergreen tracks and 50% are new topics judged newly relevant.

“it's bigger than all the previous AIEs. I almost wanted to say it's all previous AIEs combined, but that's not exactly true cuz if you do the math, we actually we're we're a little bit over that”

said at 01:01

“Our top tracks are like auto research that didn't exist last last year. We've split out um the sort of GPUs track into inference and post-training and pre-training”

said at 02:33
02Expo and academic-conference positioning
Slide at 04:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Expo and academic-conference positioning

  • He describes the Expo as roughly 4x bigger than 2025's and positions AI Engineer as aiming to be the industry counterpart to academic conferences like ICML and NeurIPS, including a partnership with ACM on a co-located conference on AI and Agentic Systems and a call-for-posters format extended to tweets/hot takes, not just papers.

“Compared to last year, this is how it was last year for 2025. It is roughly 4x bigger”

said at 04:35
03Leadership track: token billionaires and daily themes
Slide at 09:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Leadership track: token billionaires and daily themes

  • The leadership track now occupies an entire floor (about 50% of breakout space) and introduces a 'token billionaire' lounge for attendees/orgs spending on the order of a billion tokens a month (some spend 10 trillion/month), plus daily thematic focuses (token maxing, AI factories) replacing last year's undifferentiated leader track, alongside off-the-record networking sessions with McKinsey.

“if you spend something on the order of uh a billion tokens a month, that's actually a really low bar for some of these people. Some of them spend 10 trillion a month”

said at 09:57
04Betting on verticals, with finance as the flagship
Slide at 13:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Betting on verticals, with finance as the flagship

  • For 2026 the event organizes around four deployed verticals -- agentic commerce, healthcare, finance, and GTM -- replacing last year's broader horizontal tracks like design engineering and product management.
  • AI in finance is called out as the biggest bet, tied to a newly pre-announced, finance-focused AI Engineer New York conference, the third New York event.

“we are also going to pre-announce AI engineer New York. Um this is our third New York conference now, but our first entirely focused on finance”

said at 13:00
05Side events and new-attendee orientation
Slide at 14:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 5 more from this section below

Side events and new-attendee orientation

  • The event lists 41 side events as of recording, including a World Cup watch party and a new 'NEO' (new engineer orientation) session the night before the main day for solo or new attendees, with about 300 people signed up already and an expectation of growing to 1,000.

“So far we have 300 people signed up. I think this is going to go to 1,000 people”

said at 15:05

Commentary (ours)

The pivot to vertical tracks (finance, healthcare, agentic commerce, GTM) with finance singled out as the lead bet is a reasonable industry-adoption signal to track against Andrew's own AI-PM/client deployment interest, though it's the organizer's own promotional read on where deployment is headed rather than independently verified data (ours).
