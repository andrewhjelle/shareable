Data Quality Is the Compute Multiplier

SpeakerAri Morcos · DatologyAI Watchyoutu.be/_PdK6x7PQNM
Data curationPost-training

If you only read one thing

  • Curation alone (no new tokens) moved a VLM about 14 absolute points past the public Pareto frontier and matched a larger model with 145x less training compute. (c5)
  • Domain mid-training on curated data made a later post-training pass on the same model almost 3x more effective, without extra post-training data. (c7, c8)
  • A fully open, non-proprietary 17-trillion-token model competitive with the open frontier was built for under $20 million total, including compute and salaries. (c6)
01Compute is getting scarcer, not cheaper

Morcos opens with evidence that compute access is tightening: H100 prices reversed their multi-year decline and are up roughly 40% off their lows, reasoning models use 8x the tokens of non-reasoning models with a projected further 5x next year, and Google capped Meta's Gemini usage over inference constraints. He frames data quality as the lever available when compute itself becomes the constraint.

02Data quality as a compute multiplier

The core framing: better data steepens the performance-vs-compute curve, so the same compute budget yields performance equivalent to a much larger one. Morcos stresses there is no single universal dataset — quality is defined relative to the target task/domain, and datasets must be diverse or models become brittle to phrasing changes.

“data quality is a compute multiplier because what it does is it it makes the learning curve steeper”

▶ watch at 02:07

“there's no one golden data set to rule them all that's good for everything no matter what you want to do”

▶ watch at 03:08
03Curation results on vision-language models

Applying DatologyAI's curation pipeline (clean, curate, create, compose) to the Mammoth dataset produced roughly a 14-point absolute improvement on benchmark error over the uncurated input, pushing past the public VLM Pareto frontier and matching a comparable model's performance while using about 145x less training compute.

“you're able to get around a 14 absolute percentage point improvement um holding everything else constant just through better data alone”

▶ watch at 07:49
04Synthetic rephrasing avoids model collapse

DatologyAI's synthetic-data approach rewrites source documents into many templated formats (e.g., true/false questions) rather than generating new content from a model's own knowledge. Because the informational content still comes from the source document, Morcos says this sidesteps the model-collapse risk associated with training on model-generated data, while boosting diversity.

“because all the information is coming from the document on the left, you don't have any issue with model collapse”

▶ watch at 12:59
05Customer cases: legal mid-training and a $20M frontier-competitive model

For Thomson Reuters, mid-training an open-source model on a mix of curated legal and general data lifted legal-benchmark performance by about 5 percentage points using less than 1% of the pretraining compute budget, without losing general capability. When the same team's existing post-training harness was applied to this mid-trained model instead of the default instruction-tuned one, the measured gain from post-training almost tripled. Separately, RC's Trendy Large model — 17 trillion tokens of curated public data, no proprietary or closed-model data — reached open-frontier-competitive performance for under $20 million total spend.

“legal capabilities go up about five percentage points um as measured by uh legal bench after you do this 100 billion mid-training which was less than 1% of the pre-training budget”

Commentary (ours)

The headline multiplier numbers (145x, 50x, 3x) are all DatologyAI's own benchmarking of its own product, so they should be read as vendor-reported upper bounds rather than independently replicated results (ours) — the underlying mechanism (curation reduces redundant/low-signal tokens) is well-established in the literature Morcos cites, but the specific magnitudes are a sales case study, not a peer-reviewed comparison.
