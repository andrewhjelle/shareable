01The problem: every agent rewires its own data access
Slide at 03:20 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

The problem: every agent rewires its own data access

  • Eifrem describes teams at large organizations independently discovering, from scratch, where the data for each new agent lives across many databases, warehouses, and buckets.
  • He argues this duplicated wiring violates the DRY (don't repeat yourself) principle of software engineering, since a change to a data source has to be manually propagated across every agent that uses it.
  • He also addresses the common proposed fix of markdown-based 'skills' files, saying they are part of the solution but not sufficient on their own.

“It also violates one of the core principles of software engineering, the DRY principle, don't repeat yourself.”

said at 02:37

“And the summary is it is part of the solution, but it is not the solution.”

The proposed pattern: thin agents on a shared substrate

  • Based on work with a Fortune 20 bank, a large Bay Area tech platform company, and a fintech company, Eifrem says the emerging pattern for operating at scale is 'thin agents on a smarter shared substrate' rather than agents that each carry their own data-wiring logic.

“the pattern that is emerging is that in order to do things at scale, we need thin agents on a smarter shared substrate”

03Three pillars: business ontology, technical ontology, execution traces
Slide at 05:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 7 more from this section below

Three pillars: business ontology, technical ontology, execution traces

  • The substrate has three parts.
  • A business-facing ontology names the key concepts in the organization (customers, accounts, transactions) in terms understandable to people, not raw column names.
  • A technical ontology is metadata about every data source and asset in the enterprise (databases, warehouses, buckets) plus a mapping from business concepts to the systems of record that hold them.
  • A third pillar records runtime execution traces: what an agent tried against the graph and what happened.

“What are the key concepts in your organization? In our banking example, customers, accounts, um debit cards, checks, transactions, and how do they all relate?”

said at 04:44

“The second pillar is a technical ontology. This is all the metadata of all the data sources and data assets in your enterprise ecosystem.”

said at 05:14

“the third pillar is the run time signals out of your agents”

said at 05:45
04Self-learning across agents
Slide at 07:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Self-learning across agents

  • Eifrem says the three pillars together are meant to solve four problems he outlined earlier: data discovery, trust/versioning, duplicated wiring, and lack of learning.
  • He states the intended result is that an individual agent becomes smarter over time using its own execution traces, and that learning is shared across different agents, not confined to one agent instance.

“Three pillars of the ontology-based semantic layer, a business ontology, a technical ontology, the execution traces taken together, they solve all four of the problems.”

said at 07:51

“my agent that wakes up tomorrow is slightly smarter than it was today. And not just self-learning on an individual agent, but across agents as well.”

said at 08:22

Commentary (ours)

This is a vendor keynote pitching a pattern that maps closely onto Neo4j's own graph-database product, so the claim that the three-pillar ontology layer solves all four stated problems should be read as a sales pitch backed by a few named enterprise deployments rather than independently validated results (ours).

The underlying problem described, duplicated, ungoverned data wiring repeated across many agents, is a commonly reported pain point independent of the vendor, which is likely why it lands even without independent verification of the fix.
