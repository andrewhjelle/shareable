Lays out the newly released Gemma 4 family's sizes (E2B/E4B mobile, 26B MoE, 31B dense) and the license change to Apache 2

If you only read one thing

  • Gemma 4 ships four sizes -- E2B/E4B for mobile (memory-mapped so only 2B/4B params need GPU/device memory), a 26B mixture-of-experts model, and a 31B dense model -- and Gus (Google) reports both larger models rank 4th/7th on LM Marena among open models despite being 2-20x smaller than top competitors.
  • Gemma models moved from a custom Gemma license to Apache 2.0, which Gus frames as removing the ~18-month legal procurement delay that a custom license created for sovereign/enterprise adopters; named users include Ukraine and Gemma variants fine-tuned for Bulgarian and Portuguese.
  • Ian (Google) frames on-device/owned-model costs as shifting from per-token pricing to energy/hardware utilization, and recommends starting by dropping an open model into existing workflows, then bolstering task-specific evals rather than relying on general benchmarks.
▣The system in one picture (ours, from the slides and the transcript)

The size/benchmark/cost claims and the license change form one adoption argument: smaller models that rank well and run cheap make the Apache 2.0 switch matter more for sovereignty use cases (ours). We have not verified the 18-month procurement figure independently (ours).

Gemma 4 model sizes and mobile efficiency

  • Gus described four newly released Gemma 4 sizes: E2B and E4B target mobile/IoT devices, using an 'effective' parameter naming where the model behaves like a 2B or 4B model in GPU/device memory even though it has more total parameters, because some layers (token mappings) can live in other memory.
  • The two larger models are a 26B mixture-of-experts model (using roughly 4B-equivalent compute per query) and a 31B dense model.

Benchmark standing and cost per size

  • On the LM Marena leaderboard, Gus said both larger Gemma models rank around 4th and 7th among open models despite being 2-20x smaller than many top-20/30 competitors, which he frames as a disproportionate amount of intelligence per parameter.
  • He also cited a hardware cost comparison: the 31B dense model runs on one GPU, while competitor models of comparable capability need roughly 200GB of memory, or about four to five GPUs.

03License change to Apache 2.0 and sovereignty framing
Slide at 11:30 · click any slide to enlarge

License change to Apache 2.0 and sovereignty framing

  • Gus explained Gemma moved from a custom Gemma license to Apache 2.0 for Gemma 4 going forward, arguing custom licenses previously triggered long legal procurement reviews (he cited roughly 18 months) that made it hard for sovereign institutions to adopt the model.
  • He defined sovereignty as owning the model, being able to adapt it to your use case, and not being subject to loss of service or access being revoked, citing Ukraine's use of Gemma and fine-tuned variants for Bulgarian and Portuguese as examples.

“it's pretty hard for sovereign institutions to adopt this kind of thing. That's why we move to a pass 2.0 you for for Gemma 4 and going forward”

said at 07:42

“sovereignty means in terms of you own the model and you can adapt your use cases and you you are not susceptible to I don't know loss of service”

said at 07:11
04Cost shifts from tokens to energy/hardware, and task scope
Slide at 14:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 4 more from this section below

Cost shifts from tokens to energy/hardware, and task scope

  • Ian said that owning and running models locally shifts the cost conversation from per-token API pricing to energy and hardware utilization costs, which changes how teams decide when a task should run in real time versus as an offline/background job.
  • He also cautioned that these smaller open models are well-suited to specific, modular tasks like refactoring or targeted code generation, but not to something like a full systems architecture redesign.

“we're not paying for uh the price of these agents or models within tokens, we're actually paying for them in terms of energy costs”

said at 13:54

“I wouldn't get this model to do like a you know a full systems architecture and redesign of your application right it's not kind of for that”

said at 10:48
05Recommended path to evaluate open models
Slide at 18:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 1 more from this section below

Recommended path to evaluate open models

  • Ian recommended starting by dropping an open model into existing workflows to see what it handles well, then building out task-specific evaluation suites rather than relying solely on general benchmarks, since real performance depends on the specific task, not general capability scores.

“the first thing we recommend you do is to drop it into existing workflows that you have to then see what the model can handle”

said at 18:36

Commentary (ours)

The 18-month procurement-delay figure and the 'disproportionate intelligence per parameter' framing are both Google's own talking points for a same-day product launch -- worth checking against independent benchmarks and actual enterprise adoption timelines once Gemma 4 has been in the wild longer (ours).
