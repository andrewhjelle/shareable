Why this talk is a blueprint, not a take (ours)

Scope: The Gates Foundation's Strategic Intelligence Platform (SIP): a knowledge graph modeling internal operational processes (funding, management, people, documents) exposed to agents via MCP, running in production for about 4,000 employees.

A curated, governed knowledge graph that stitches together previously siloed systems of record into multiple aligned hierarchies, queryable by agents through a forked MCP server. A structured eval pipeline compares agent answers against direct graph queries with an LLM judge, feeding failures back into data model and schema fixes.

Prerequisites the talk implies:

  • Multiple siloed systems of record (structured and unstructured) covering a real, multi-year operational history worth modeling
  • Data owners willing to be engaged repeatedly to explain field meanings, join logic, and how questions have historically been answered
  • A governance process capable of masking PII, classifying sensitive data, and setting per-user entitlements before ingestion
  • Engineering capacity to fork and modify an off-the-shelf MCP server rather than use it unmodified
  • A held-out set of reference questions built with data owners to drive the eval pipeline

What the talk does not say:

  • No team size or build timeline given for constructing SIP
  • No cost figures for the data lakehouse, graph database, or ongoing maintenance
  • The exact pass@1 and stability percentages appear only on a results slide, not spoken in the talk itself, so the specific eval numbers are asserted by the slide but not verbally quantified
  • No detail on how many source systems still remain outside the graph or the timeline for the federated graph and workflow-agent expansion described as coming soon

If you were to replicate one thing first: Pick one high-friction internal process, engage the people who currently answer questions about it by hand, and model just their tacit rules and terminology as a small graph before trying to connect multiple systems of record.
