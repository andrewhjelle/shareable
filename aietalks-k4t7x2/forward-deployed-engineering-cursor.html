Brunet gives a concrete fit-matrix for when a forward-deployed engineering (FDE) team is worth the cost versus when it's disguised staff augmentation

If you only read one thing

  • FDE fits a 2x2 of customer digital maturity vs. product customization; it's most valuable for mature customers on highly customizable products, where FDEs act as advisers rather than implementers.
  • The clearest red flag for a bad FDE engagement is a customer asking for headcount because they're understaffed; Cursor scopes engagements directionally (phase 1, phase 2) rather than open-ended.
  • Cursor hires FDEs as 5+ years experienced engineers with customer-facing aptitude, not early-career hires, and measures success against revenue, cost, or risk.

Where an FDE team is worth the cost

  • Brunet maps customers on digital maturity vs. product customization.
  • Low-maturity, low-customization customers should get self-service SaaS deployment; high-maturity, highly-customizable accounts are where FDE adds the most value, acting as advisers who accelerate adoption rather than doing implementation work for the customer.

“you're going to have crazy adoption, right? And I would say a lot of it should be your FDE team should be acting as advisers”

02Avoiding staff augmentation in disguise
Slide at 01:10 · click any slide to enlarge

Avoiding staff augmentation in disguise

  • She warns that FDE engagements can slide into disguised staff augmentation and treats any 'we're understaffed' request as a warning sign to scope more carefully.
  • Her fix is to keep scope directional (defined phases with clear automation targets) rather than open-ended headcount, and to insist the customer names a working team and stays involved at every step.

“This is where you have to be really careful that you end up uh not doing staff augmentation.”

said at 09:25

“keep the scope directional. We're going to do phase one and two. We're going to automate these things.”

03Say no, and measure ROI in three buckets
Slide at 20:00 · click any slide to enlarge

Say no, and measure ROI in three buckets

  • Brunet recommends being willing to tell a customer the product isn't the right fit for their use case, arguing this builds credibility that pays off on future asks.
  • She frames all engagement value in three measurable buckets: increasing revenue, decreasing costs, or mitigating risk.

“you build credibility by being very honest about where our applications and our products and our platform are the right tools and where they're not.”

said at 15:40

“It's always three things, super simple. Am I increasing revenue? Am I decreasing costs? Or am I mitigating risks? That's it.”

said at 19:25

Hiring and staffing the team

  • Cursor's FDE hires are 5+ years experienced engineers with customer-facing aptitude, not early-career hires, reflecting the technical depth customers expect.
  • Brunet frames the FDE role as a 2026 breakout function and closes with a blunt hiring maxim about talent tiers.

“we hire 5 plus years software engineers. We don't hire out of school. We don't hire early career professionals at this moment.”
