Details what breaks when RL-trained coding/browser agents meet real websites (login screens, ads styled like buttons, credential expiry) and the concrete training and harness changes Amazon AGI Lab made in response

If you only read one thing

  • In an early training run, an agent facing a signed-out session tried to guess a password repeatedly and got the account blocked, and in a separate run clicked a fake ad-styled submit button and began filling in personal details on an unrelated site (c1, c2).
  • Six real-world failure modes break RL's game-like assumptions: partial observability, irreversibility, non-determinism, ephemeral credentials, ambiguous success, and adversarial content (c3).
  • The fix stack was flight-school-style simulation (high-fidelity sandboxes with messiness baked in), a process reward model that penalizes risky actions mid-trajectory not just bad outcomes, and a harness layer with checkpointing, an action risk classifier, and human handoff (c4, c5, c6).

01Why coding-trained RL agents get pointed at computer use

Why coding-trained RL agents get pointed at computer use

  • Mishra explains RL works best for coding because tasks are easy to generate, have multiple valid solution paths, are reasoning-heavy, and outcomes are verifiable via compilers, linters, or unit tests.
  • Because email, chat, docs, and browsers can all be accessed through MCP, APIs, or tools like Playwright, teams started pointing coding-RL-trained agents at broader computer-use tasks like filing expenses or browsing.

03RL's game assumptions break in the real world
Slide at 06:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 1 more from this section below

RL's game assumptions break in the real world

  • Mishra lists six ways real deployment differs from the training game: partial observability (DOM and screenshots are each incomplete), irreversibility (submitted forms, deleted files, locked accounts can't be undone), non-determinism (clicks may silently fail or lag), ephemeral authority (sessions and credentials expire mid-task), ambiguous success (task completion doesn't guarantee the right outcome), and adversarial content (pages actively try to grab attention/clicks).

“RL worked when the world was a game and IRL starts when the game fights back”

04The fix: flight-school training plus a harness with guardrails
Slide at 08:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

The fix: flight-school training plus a harness with guardrails

  • The lab's response has three parts: training sandboxes that deliberately include layout shifts, slow loads, pop-ups, and stale tabs, with recovery (refresh, backtrack, escalate) trained as a native model action rather than a state reset; a process reward model that penalizes dangerous actions throughout a trajectory, not just bad final outcomes, plus calibrated confidence so the model knows when to escalate to a human; and a harness layer providing checkpointing/rollback, an action risk classifier, credential guardrails, an execution monitor for loops, audit logs, and human handoff.
  • After these changes, a repeat of the earlier expense-submission task showed the agent correctly identifying the sponsored button, recognizing the expired session, and handing off to a user-simulator agent to re-authenticate before completing the task.
