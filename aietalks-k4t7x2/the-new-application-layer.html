The New Application Layer

SpeakerMalte Ubl · Vercel Watchyoutu.be/XKup1pj-34M
Agentic engineeringProduct & UXInfra & serving

If you only read one thing

  • Vercel frames agents as a new, economically-viable category of software and recommends starting with low-risk archetypes: 24/7 service agents, compressed-research agents, and agents that surface information already scattered across a company.
  • Two internal examples quantify the payoff: a contact-sales triage agent resolves about 75% of inbound messages as support issues, and an in-house support agent reaches a 90% deflection rate.
  • On vercel.com, over 60% of page views in the last 7 days came from AI agents rather than humans, and Ubl warns most popular agent harnesses wrongly run in the same place as the code they generate.
01Agents make previously uneconomical software worth building

Ubl argues agents are a new kind of software because they make automation economically viable for business logic that was too expensive to hand-code before. He frames this as more software getting built overall, with more companies choosing to build in-house ('make') rather than buy SaaS, which in turn increases rather than decreases demand for engineers as the cost of making software falls.

“One of our main thesis is that agents are a new kind of software.”

02Low-risk agent archetypes already shipping

Ubl lists archetypes he sees working today without major process changes: 24/7 service agents, 'compressed research' agents that automate only the research step of an existing human-decision workflow, and agents that surface information that already exists but is practically unusable (issue trackers, chat logs, meeting recordings). He gives two Vercel production examples with numbers: a contact-sales agent and an in-house support agent.

“I hear about 75% of the time that agent says, Well, actually, they just wanted support.”

▶ watch at 09:22

“we actually did build our own in-house support agent, and it has what's called a 90% deflection rate”

03Software is increasingly built and used by agents, not humans

Ubl cites internal Vercel traffic data showing agents, not humans, are now the majority visitor to vercel.com, and describes a broader shift from UI-driven dashboards to CLI/API usage as the primary interface for agents.

“over 60% of page views on vercel.com were AI agents”

▶ watch at 12:59
04A named architecture mistake in current agent harnesses

Ubl warns that most popular agent harnesses combine where the harness runs with where the code it generates runs, calling this fundamentally the wrong architecture, and notes Anthropic's newest agent product separates the two.

“I think almost all currently popular agent harnesses have fundamentally the wrong architecture.”

▶ watch at 15:02
05Model layer commoditizing, application layer capturing value

Ubl contrasts two futures: one where model labs capture all the value and engineers become forward-deployed staff for whichever lab wins, versus one where model companies commoditize on price and the application layer captures the value. He argues the second is what's actually happening, citing Europe-built projects (Vercel's AI SDK, an Austria-made coding agent, and Open Claw) as evidence of application-layer leadership independent of who wins on models.

“I do see kind of two big futures ahead of us. One is where the big model labs win. In that world, AI will stay very expensive.”

▶ watch at 17:03

“it appears to me that Europe, against all odds, is taking actually a leadership role in AI engineering.”

Commentary (ours)

The 75%/90% deflection figures are single-company anecdotes from Vercel's own support and sales pipelines, not benchmarked against other vendors' agent-service numbers, so treat them as an existence proof of the archetype rather than a typical rate (ours).
