Contact Center Voice AI: Low-Latency Intelligence Extraction from Messy Audio Streams

SpeakerDippu Singh · Fujitsu North America Watchyoutu.be/IEF842ZEU5A · 22 min SourceRecording of this talk at AI Engineer (2026), published on YouTube by the conference. Quotes are verbatim from the talk transcript with the time they were said.

Fujitsu ships a four-stage voice pipeline (channel-separated capture with PII masking, STT at a 90% accuracy floor, orchestrated GenAI summarization, CRM sync with human verification) that cut after-call work from 6.3 to 3.1 minutes.

If you only read one thing

  • Average after-call work time was 6.3 minutes against a 6.5-minute average call, a roughly 1:1 ratio that AI-assisted summarization cut to 3.1 minutes (c2, c3).
  • The pipeline requires STT accuracy above 90%, stereo channel separation of agent vs. customer audio, and PII masking before audio ever reaches the LLM (c4, c5, c6).
  • Prompting the LLM directly for a call summary produced a messy narrative paragraph, so the team switched to few-shot templates that force separate bullet lists (c7).

This traces the four stages the speaker names end to end, from telephony capture through PII masking and a 90% STT floor to a few-shot summarization step and a human-verified CRM write; the roadmap items (coaching, staffing, abuse detection) are left off since they are not yet built (ours, from the transcript).

Why this talk is a blueprint, not a take (ours)

Scope: Fujitsu's four-stage voice pipeline for contact-center after-call work, from telephony capture through CRM sync with human verification.

A pipeline that separates agent and customer audio channels, masks PII before it reaches the LLM, holds speech-to-text to a stated above-90% accuracy floor, and uses few-shot templates plus a reasoning and trust layer to turn a call into a structured, CRM-ready summary. The reported result is after-call work time cut from 6.3 to 3.1 minutes, with a human operator still confirming the auto-populated summary before it commits.

Prerequisites the talk implies:

  • A telephony system that exposes raw, high-fidelity, stereo (agent/customer separated) audio
  • A speech-to-text engine tunable with domain-specific dictionaries and inverse text normalization
  • An orchestration layer capable of few-shot prompting plus a separate reasoning and trust (hallucination-check) step
  • CRM or API endpoints that accept schema-mapped JSON fields
  • An operator workflow that keeps a human verification and confirm step in place

What the talk does not say:

  • Team size or roles required to build and run the pipeline
  • Full cost figures beyond the general mention of token cost on long transcripts
  • Accuracy or failure rate of the AI-generated summaries themselves, as opposed to STT accuracy
  • How the near-50% ACW reduction figure was measured, including sample size
  • End-to-end latency numbers for the pipeline
  • What happens operationally when the hallucination check flags a summary

If you were to replicate one thing first: Start by instrumenting channel-separated audio capture with PII masking before any transcript reaches an LLM, since every downstream stage in this pipeline depends on that ordering.

01The problem: after-call work eats as much time as the call

The problem: after-call work eats as much time as the call

  • The speaker frames contact-center attrition as a negative spiral: over half of contact centers name hiring, training, and productivity as their top barriers, and high stress is the leading reason operators leave.
  • Baseline data showed an average 6.5-minute call followed by an average 6.3 minutes of manual post-processing (typing notes, summarizing, selecting disposition codes), close to a 1:1 ratio between talking and paperwork.

02Voice capture: separate channels, mask PII, before anything reaches the LLM

Voice capture: separate channels, mask PII, before anything reaches the LLM

  • The first pipeline stage taps the telephony system and applies noise filtering and level normalization.
  • It splits stereo audio so the agent and customer are on separate channels, since a mixed mono track makes it hard for the AI to attribute who said what.
  • A buffer-management and early-stage PII-masking step is applied before the audio content reaches the LLM's memory.

03STT accuracy floor and a summarization prompt fix

STT accuracy floor and a summarization prompt fix

  • The team set a requirement that speech-to-text accuracy stay above 90%, using acoustic modeling, domain-specific dictionaries, and inverse text normalization for things like spoken dollar amounts.
  • On the generative AI core, directly asking an LLM to summarize a call produced an unstructured narrative paragraph, so the team moved to few-shot prompt templates that force the output into separate bullet lists for customer inquiry and operator action.

04Measured outcome and the token-cost tradeoff

Measured outcome and the token-cost tradeoff

  • After deployment, after-call work time dropped from 6.3 to 3.1 minutes, described as almost a 50% reduction, with a human verification step retained (operator confirms an auto-populated summary rather than the AI writing it unsupervised).
  • The speaker names token cost on long transcripts as an ongoing constraint, since running complex LLM reasoning on roughly 20-minute transcripts adds up during scaling.
