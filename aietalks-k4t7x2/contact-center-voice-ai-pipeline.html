01The problem: after-call work eats as much time as the call

The speaker frames contact-center attrition as a negative spiral: over half of contact centers name hiring, training, and productivity as their top barriers, and high stress is the leading reason operators leave. Baseline data showed an average 6.5-minute call followed by an average 6.3 minutes of manual post-processing (typing notes, summarizing, selecting disposition codes), close to a 1:1 ratio between talking and paperwork.

02Voice capture: separate channels, mask PII, before anything reaches the LLM

The first pipeline stage taps the telephony system and applies noise filtering and level normalization. It splits stereo audio so the agent and customer are on separate channels, since a mixed mono track makes it hard for the AI to attribute who said what. A buffer-management and early-stage PII-masking step is applied before the audio content reaches the LLM's memory.

“we absolutely must spit split the, uh, stereo audio to isolate the agent on one channel, say, on the very left, and the customer on the other side”

“we utilize buffer management and early-stage PII masking technique uh, so that the sensitive data it never hits the LLM memory banks”

03STT accuracy floor and a summarization prompt fix

The team set a requirement that speech-to-text accuracy stay above 90%, using acoustic modeling, domain-specific dictionaries, and inverse text normalization for things like spoken dollar amounts. On the generative AI core, directly asking an LLM to summarize a call produced an unstructured narrative paragraph, so the team moved to few-shot prompt templates that force the output into separate bullet lists for customer inquiry and operator action.

“we found that the speech-to-text, the STT um accuracy must be above 90%.”

▶ watch at 10:06

“if if you just ask an LLM to summarize a call, it outputs a messy narrative paragraph. So instead, we use few-shot libraries to instruct the LLM to output separate bullet points.”

▶ watch at 11:40
04Measured outcome and the token-cost tradeoff

After deployment, after-call work time dropped from 6.3 to 3.1 minutes, described as almost a 50% reduction, with a human verification step retained (operator confirms an auto-populated summary rather than the AI writing it unsupervised). The speaker names token cost on long transcripts as an ongoing constraint, since running complex LLM reasoning on roughly 20-minute transcripts adds up during scaling.

“after average after-call work was 6.3 minutes. Uh powered by our AI workflow, that dropped to like 3.1 minutes, which is like almost 50% reduction in the processing time.”

▶ watch at 15:52

“initial consumption of the API tokens, especially running complex LLM reasonings on long 20-minute uh transcripts can be costly.”

05Roadmap: operator protection as an explicit design goal

The stated roadmap includes explainable-AI coaching feedback for operators, predictive staffing from categorized intent data, and a phase the speaker calls the most important for human well-being: low-latency sentiment and acoustic analysis to detect when a customer becomes abusive, aimed at protecting agents from verbal abuse.

“The phase three is perhaps the most important for human well-being combating the customer harassment uh contact center agents they mostly face an increasingly amount of verbal abuse.”
