How Web Data Infrastructure Powers the Next Generation of AI

01From one feature request to a product suite

A sales team returned from San Francisco with a client request for a video API for AI training: two weeks to build it, at a scale of at least 5 petabytes per month. That single request expanded through successive client asks (transcripts, then subtitles, then search, then metadata) into Oxylabs' full video API product line.

“What's the What's the scale? At least 5 petabytes per month.”

02Chasing sub-second search

A 2024 client request for sub-second SERP delivery, against a traditional scraper baseline of about 4 seconds, produced a working fast search API in under two weeks at roughly 650ms P90. The product line has since matured into a search API that delivers results directly into AI workflows at 550 milliseconds average latency.

“In less than 2 weeks, we got around 650 milliseconds P90.”

▶ watch at 09:23

“today we have fast search API that delivers results and fresh data directly into AI workflows with 550 milliseconds”

▶ watch at 11:30
03Scaling request volume

Daily traffic on the fast search product grew from 400 million to almost 6 billion requests. Separately, demand forced the web-unblocker product to scale from roughly 10,000 to 60,000 requests per second in under two months; the hardest bottleneck wasn't a dramatic outage but a wall hit during organic-traffic load testing at around 20,000 requests per second.

“our scale move from 400 million daily requests to almost 6 billion daily requests”

“we were working our way around 10,000 requests per second. Demand has forced us to scale to 60,000 requests per second and in less than 2 months.”

▶ watch at 12:36

“during one of those load tests, we hit the wall around 20,000 requests per second.”

▶ watch at 14:09
04What's next: project 150

Internally, the 60,000 requests/second scaling effort was called "project 60." It's already becoming "project 150," with the team now working toward roughly 100,000 requests per second, illustrating that each scale target is followed immediately by the next one.

“internally, we call this project 60 because we had to scale up to 60,000 requests per second. Now, it's already becoming project 150.”

▶ watch at 15:43
05Infrastructure over models

Žemaitytė closes by arguing that further progress in AI will depend more on the infrastructure connecting models to live external data than on model improvements themselves.

“the next generation of AI will not be powered by better models. It will be powered by better infrastructure around it.”
