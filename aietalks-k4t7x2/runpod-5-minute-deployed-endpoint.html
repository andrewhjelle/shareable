Under 5 minutes to a deployed LLM endpoint

It is a product demo and company narrative (origin story, scale, live deploy) rather than a full end-to-end architecture or one technique explained in depth.

If you only read one thing

  • RunPod started from founders' basement GPU rigs left over from failed crypto mining in 2022, and now reports 500,000+ developers, 30+ data centers, and $120M in annual recurring revenue.
  • Serverless auto-scales workers so teams don't have to pre-provision GPU capacity, and billing is per-second only while a worker is actively handling a request.
  • A live demo deploying a vetted vLLM hub listing took under 5 minutes end-to-end, with the first request queued about 41 seconds (cold start/model download) and roughly 1.5 seconds of execution time thereafter.

03Product surface: pods, serverless, clusters, hub
Slide at 05:30 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Product surface: pods, serverless, clusters, hub

  • RunPod offers always-on container 'pods,' auto-scaling 'serverless' for bursty or batch workloads, multi-node 'clusters' with high-speed networking for training, and a 'hub' of pre-configured, pre-vetted AI repos that can be forked, starred, and deployed.
  • Serverless is pitched for real-time inference because teams don't need to pre-provision compute, can cap spend, and can configure some workers to stay always-on.

“it's kind of like our central repository for AI repos. Um these are already preconfigured, pre-vetted”

said at 04:44

“why teams use it is mostly because they don't need to um preempt and figure out how much compute they need ahead of time”

04Live demo: hub listing to endpoint
Slide at 09:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 4 more from this section below

Live demo: hub listing to endpoint

  • The speaker deployed an LLM from a hub listing (a pre-configured vLLM Dockerfile), configured max model length and worker limits, and hit the resulting HTTP endpoint.
  • Pricing showed as a fraction of a cent per second, charged only while a worker is running a request; the first request sat in queue about 41 seconds due to cold-start/model-download, with roughly 1.5 seconds of execution time, and the whole process took under 5 minutes.

“this is only going to be charged for a while the worker is actually running and handling a request”

said at 09:25

“I got a request back and it sat in the queue for about 41 seconds”

said at 12:03

“execution time only about 1 and 1/2 seconds, so yeah, that was probably less than 5 minutes to get started and get something deployed”

said at 12:03

Commentary (ours)

The 41-second cold start on first request versus ~1.5s execution afterward is the concrete number to press on with any serverless GPU vendor -- worth asking how RunPod's always-on worker option changes that tradeoff for latency-sensitive production traffic (ours).
