What Lies Beneath the API

SpeakerBenjamin Cowen · Modal Watchyoutu.be/HvZXAOZ3iv8
Post-trainingInfra & servingAgentic engineering

01Frontier API vs full custom training is a spectrum

The speaker frames AI application development as a spectrum: frontier APIs on one end let people build fast but can't be customized beyond prompt engineering, while full custom training on the other end requires isolating infrastructure and often pulls AI engineers or scientists into infrastructure work. As companies mature and specialize, more are turning to fine-tuning to get better performance and lower cost than the frontier API provides.

“as companies mature and their products mature and specialize, we're seeing more and more turn to fine-tuning to get in increased performance, better costs, and so forth”

02Companies are already winning with fine-tuned models

The talk cites Intercom beating their frontier API at 1/5 the cost after fine-tuning, and quotes a customer, Decagon, framing the reason: frontier labs optimize their models to win on everything, whereas a company fine-tuning its own model optimizes to win at its specific business logic.

“Intercom is beating their frontier API at 1/5 the cost”

▶ watch at 04:10

“don't have the exact same goal as you, right? They want their models to win on everything possible. And um we want our models to win at our business logic, right?”

03Signals that it's time to fine-tune

Proposed readiness signals include paying more for API calls than your customers pay you (after already trying prompt-engineering tricks like 'caveman mode' to cut tokens), plateauing evals, and latency/throughput requirements the API can't meet. But the speaker warns that without collected data and mature evals, garbage-in-garbage-out applies and it's not yet time to train.

“if you've uh moved to caveman mode and you're still paying more for your API than your customers are paying you, that might be a signal that your economics aren't scaling, right?”

▶ watch at 06:15

“there's a decades-old adage in training that if you have garbage data, it's garbage in, garbage out. So if you haven't been collecting data and you don't have mature evals, it's probably not time to train”

▶ watch at 06:47
04If you've built a product, you likely already have what's needed to train

The speaker's main takeaway is that a product with evaluation and data collection already in place has touched most of what's needed to train a custom model, and a product with an agent harness already has what's needed to train a model via reinforcement learning to perform that service.

“if you've built an agent harness, then you have what you need to have a new model, you know, learn through reinforcement learning how to provide your service”

▶ watch at 07:17
05Training infrastructure has gotten much easier and cheaper

Open-source training libraries now let teams write supervised fine-tuning in about 300 lines of Python without managing a dedicated cluster, and serverless compute allows fast fan-out for hyperparameter tuning or RL rollouts, including one customer scaling to 50,000-100,000 sandboxes for RL. The speaker's overall prediction is not to train immediately, but to start preparing now since the decision point may arrive in 6 months to a year rather than 10 years out.

“You can do supervised fine-tuning in 300 lines of Python”

Commentary (ours)

The framing implicitly favors Modal's own serverless training/serving product, so the ease-of-fine-tuning claims (300 lines of Python, no infra team needed) are worth weighing against the fact that data curation and eval maturity -- the harder, non-infra part of the problem -- are treated as a precondition rather than something the platform solves (ours).
