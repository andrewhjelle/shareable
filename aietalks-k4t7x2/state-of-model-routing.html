The State of Model Routing

SpeakerWalden Yan, Carter Abdallah, Tuhin & Alex Atallah · Cognition, NVIDIA, OpenRouter Watchyoutu.be/QHBjufYK8TA · 48 min SourceRecording of this talk at AI Engineer (2026), published on YouTube by the conference. takeAgentic engineeringInfra & servingContext engineering

Informs the architecture decision for teams building multi-model agent systems: whether routing simple tasks to cheap small models actually saves money (it can backfire badly on out-of-domain tasks), and how to preserve KV-cache savings ...

If you only read one thing

  • Cognition's Devin Fusion cuts the cost of frontier-level intelligence by 40% by keeping a smart model in charge of planning while delegating implementation to cheaper models, but naive task-based routing to small models is 'extremely fragile' since task complexity shifts mid-session. (c1,c2)
  • Routing to the wrong side of the in-domain/out-of-domain line can make small models more expensive, not less: on terminal-bench, Opus scored roughly 3x better than Haiku at 1/10 the cost, despite Haiku's lower per-token price. (c3)
  • Compaction forces a cache miss and can make input tokens 10x more expensive, so the panel recommends compacting for intelligence reasons rather than cost savings, and generally staying under 200K tokens of context. (c5,c6)

Devin Fusion: cutting cost without losing frontier planning

  • Cognition co-founder Walden Yan describes Devin Fusion as reducing the cost of frontier-level (Fable) intelligence by 40% by having the frontier model handle planning and hard decisions while delegating implementation to cheaper models, sometimes running several sub-agents in parallel to explore more thoroughly than a single model would.

“we're reducing the cost of Fable level intelligence by 40%.”

02Why naive routing breaks on agentic tasks
Slide at 09:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

Why naive routing breaks on agentic tasks

  • Yan warns that routing based on a task's apparent type is fragile once a session becomes agentic, because task complexity shifts over the course of a single conversation — a coding agent might move from explaining a codebase to implementing a feature to debugging a live test failure within minutes.

“this kind of like naive like initial routing to based on the task type is extremely fragile, especially the more agentic the task you you work on is.”

said at 09:15
03Small models can cost more, not less, out of domain
Slide at 14:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

Small models can cost more, not less, out of domain

  • NVIDIA's Tuhin argues model choice should track whether a task is in-distribution for that model's training data: on terminal-bench, Opus outperformed Haiku by roughly 3x at 1/10 the total cost, because Haiku's cheaper per-token price was offset by far more tool calls and thrashing on unfamiliar tasks.

“if you run terminal bench on Opus and Haiku, like Opus will do about three times better at 1/10 the cost of Haiku, even though Haiku's significantly cheaper per token.”

said at 15:25
04Preserving cache across delegated work
Slide at 18:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Preserving cache across delegated work

  • Rather than using disposable sub-agents, Devin Fusion routes most context to a single persistent 'sidekick' sub-agent so the main model doesn't have to re-provide earlier context, keeping it inside the far cheaper cached-token pricing tier.

“we don't use sub agents. We use what we call a sidekick, which is um, one sub agent that continually has a running context.”

said at 18:29
05Compaction's real cost, and OpenRouter's routing surge
Slide at 27:30 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Compaction's real cost, and OpenRouter's routing surge

  • The panel notes that compacting context forces a cache miss, making the freshly-sent tokens far more expensive, so compaction should be used for intelligence reasons rather than cost savings, and models generally shouldn't be pushed past 200K tokens of context.
  • Separately, Alex Atallah traces OpenRouter's routing product taking off to open claw's practice of sending a heartbeat ping every 10 minutes, which silently burned tokens on expensive default models; OpenRouter's public data now shows Opus as the top model by dollar spent on classification tasks.

“when you compact, you're taking a cache miss. So, you're actually then now like paying 10 times as much for the for those input tokens if you didn't compact.”

“I would like never recommend using like these models past like 200K tokens, under 100K if you can.”
