Judge the Judge: Building LLM Evaluators That Actually Work with GEPA

01The dataset: Tau-Bench airline agent

The worked example evaluates a customer-support airline agent from Tau-Bench, using 599 conversation traces with annotations, fairly balanced between compliant and non-compliant outcomes and generated across multiple models and trials. The data is split by task (not randomly) into 480 training traces and 112 validation traces so there's no overlap between the two sets.

02Baseline judge and GEPA's measured gains

Before optimization, a seed LLM-as-judge prompt (deliberately biased to assume compliance by default) scores 61% accuracy on the validation set. After running GEPA optimization against the human annotations, validation accuracy improved by about 14 percentage points and training accuracy by nine points, and per-task coverage across the sampled candidate pool (the Pareto frontier) reached 100%.

“we run this on the validation set like this uh initial uh LLM as a judge uh we basically find 61% accuracy”

03Cost and model-size tradeoffs

He warns that even these small-scale optimization experiments cost roughly $200-300 in tokens because the trajectories being judged are long. He also found that substituting a smaller or older model (transcribed as 'GPD40') for both the refiner and the judge was a complete failure at this task.

“even these small experiments I've done I think they cost like $2 $300 uh in tokens especially since uh the trajectories are long”

▶ watch at 38:23

“using smaller or older model like using GPD40 uh for both the refiner and the LLM as a judge and that was a complete failure like uh smaller models really are very bad at least in this example”

▶ watch at 34:36
04Design lesson: narrow, use-case-specific judges

Rather than a single general-purpose judge, he recommends deriving metrics from error analysis on real traces and building a separate, narrow LLM-as-judge per discovered error type (e.g. policy adherence and response style were two of the four categories he found).

“I discovered kind of these four error types while going through these uh traces. So there is sometimes issues with policy adherance uh sometimes issue with the response style”

Commentary (ours)

(ours) The finding that the seed judge should default to assuming compliance rather than starting neutral (c4's 61%-accuracy, heavily compliance-biased baseline) is a specific, actionable prompt-design detail that likely generalizes beyond this one dataset.
