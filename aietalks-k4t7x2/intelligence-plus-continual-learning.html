01Why coding agents succeed where generalist agents don't

Why coding agents succeed where generalist agents don't

  • Su notes language agents found their first mass market in coding, illustrated by Anthropic's revenue growing 400x to roughly $40B annualized in under two years, largely on coding-related productivity.
  • He attributes this to code already being a language-native, symbolically structured world with built-in verifiable rewards (tests, compilers), a privilege most other domains lack.

“In just under 2 years, their revenue has grown 400 times uh to uh 40 billion.”

said at 03:07
02A modern Moravec's paradox
Slide at 04:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 1 more from this section below

A modern Moravec's paradox

  • Su frames current agent limitations as a modern version of Moravec's paradox: models excel at symbolic reasoning tasks like coding and math but struggle with everyday digital work, because that work spans millions of idiosyncratic 'microworlds' (each company, domain, and software configuration different) that no single static model representation can capture.

“Moravec's paradox essentially states that uh uh for AI, uh hard things are easy, easy things are hard.”

said at 04:39
03Defining intelligence versus expertise
Slide at 06:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 1 more from this section below

Defining intelligence versus expertise

  • Su offers working definitions: intelligence is the capacity to reason through unfamiliar problems from available context, largely independent episode to episode; expertise is accumulated, situated competence, the ability to act reliably, efficiently, and with judgment in a specific domain, built from pattern recognition, conditional rules, and knowing when to stop.

“it's the capacity to reason through unfamiliar problems from available context.”

said at 06:14

“But expertise is different. Expertise is really accumulated and situated competence.”

said at 06:47
04Continual learning sets the slope between the two
Slide at 19:10 · click any slide to enlarge

Continual learning sets the slope between the two

  • Plotting intelligence against expertise as roughly orthogonal axes, Su argues that scaling raw intelligence alone, without continual learning, produces 'the world's smartest novice': capable of attacking any problem but never accumulating expertise, brute-forcing every time.
  • He predicts a stronger goal is continual learning strong enough to deliver 'unbounded expertise from bounded intelligence,' where expertise keeps growing even without a stronger base model.

“what we will get is what I call the world's smartest novice”

said at 13:04

“continual learning will bring us like unbounded expertise once we have like a reasonable level of intelligence.”

said at 14:37
05Call to action: scale expertise, not just intelligence
Slide at 19:10 · click any slide to enlarge

Call to action: scale expertise, not just intelligence

  • Su closes by arguing that intelligence is already becoming abundant while expertise remains scarce, and calls for the field to start scaling expertise as the next dimension, alongside enabling each company to build its own human-AI learning loop.

“let's start scaling expertise. This will be a new dimension for us to scale because intelligence is already becoming abundance.”

said at 17:44
