01Oliver's scale and feedback loop

Oliver is a former traditional ad agency now operating almost fully on generative AI, with 3,000 staff across 46 countries generating around 4,000 assets a day for more than 200 brands. Because Oliver puts real media spend (from $20,000 to a few million) behind these AI-generated assets, they get performance data in the wild that feeds a much faster iteration loop than typical gen-AI content agencies.

02LLMs are a closed box, not a learning system

McLean argues LLMs still don't understand the data they're presented with despite appearing advanced, citing limitations like poor data efficiency relative to humans and the inability to continuously learn without forgetting. He frames most agent tooling, and even how models themselves are trained, as band-aids around these constraints: temporary, superficial fixes that mask symptoms rather than solve the underlying problem.

“no matter how advanced LLMs seem, today's large language don't actually understand the data they're presented with. And we know this cuz they've still got several clear limitations”

“most of our tools are band-aids and you could argue even the way the models themselves are being trained is a form of band-aid, right”

03Context is a soft constraint, and it will never be enough

McLean says most recent agentic capability gains come from increased context windows enabling longer-running tasks (storing history of actions, tool outputs, goals and plans), but argues total world knowledge doubles roughly every 12 hours so context windows will never be sufficient. He treats context more as a soft constraint than a hard guardrail, and says the practical challenge has shifted from getting enough context in to filtering noise out, including keeping promotional/SEO-optimized content from crowding out genuine consumer information.

“most recent advances in agentic capabilities have been largely due to increased context window. This is especially true with like long-running agents because the longer context windows allows them to do longer running tasks”

▶ watch at 06:42

“the total knowledge in the world keeps doubling about every 12 hours, so we're always going to want more”

▶ watch at 07:43
04Constraints produce creativity

McLean advocates deliberately self-imposing constraints -- asking how little context window you can use and still complete a task -- rather than defaulting to maximal context. He illustrates with his own CV: a complex custom application underperformed a version built in plain HTML by roughly 10x, and argues models are naturally verbose and tend toward unnecessarily complex solutions unless reined in.

“it was I was pretty uh blown away by this. So that was over a 10x improvement. I'd say it's probably a 100x improvement”

▶ watch at 11:51

Commentary (ours)

The 'context as soft constraint, not hard constraint' framing is a useful reversal of the usual context-engineering advice to just add more retrieval -- McLean is arguing the harder skill is subtraction, which tracks with his separate point about noise/SEO pollution in tool outputs (ours).
