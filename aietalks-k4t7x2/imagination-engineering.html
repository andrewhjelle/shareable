Imagination Engineering: "Live in the future and then build what's missing."

SpeakerEve · Y Combinator Watchyoutu.be/Z2Erdirpudo
Generative mediaProduct & UXMemory

If you only read one thing

  • Built a personal website from a week of Slack brain-dumps using one Opus 4.8 prompt two days before the talk, then built a second demo tool ("Shape of Minds") in a single morning session at a coffee shop.
  • Maintains agent-facing artifacts alongside the human-facing page: a voice.md distilled from her own tweets and writing, and a glossary.md she says is far more efficient for an agent to read than the live page itself.
  • Frames the exercise as "imagination engineering": as one-shotting gets easy, the bottleneck becomes generating ideas worth building, illustrated by the borrowed line "live in the future and then build what's missing."
01A week of Slack brain-dumps became a personal site in one prompt

She describes creating a private Slack channel to brain-dump her stream of consciousness for a week, then asking Opus 4.8 to build a website encapsulating everything she'd written, two days before the talk. The resulting site pulled in her favorite quotes, projects, tools, and books, including customizable backgrounds and design sliders.

“2 days ago, I asked Opus 4.8, I wish it was Fable, but it was Opus 4.8 on Saturday to make a website that encapsulate all the thoughts that I had in the last week.”

02The organizing idea: live in the future, build what's missing

She cites a Paul Graham line as her guiding prompt and pairs it with a Steve Jobs/Garry Tan riff recasting computers from "bicycle for the mind" to "rocket ship for the mind" now that models are this capable.

“quote that says live in the future and then we'll build what's missing”

▶ watch at 05:14

“computers are the bicycle of their minds and Gary always says that with Fable and these insanely capable models it's like a rocket mind like a rocket ship for the minds”

▶ watch at 05:14
03A same-morning tool comparing history's "spikiest minds"

In a separate session that same morning, starting at 10am at a coffee shop with Opus 4.8, she built a tool called "Shape of Minds" comparing historical figures across dimensions like obsessions, routines, and worldview. She read out unreviewed AI-generated "highlights" from the tool live during the talk, including that naps were common among the figures studied and that many ate little.

“this morning started at 10:00 a.m. at my favorite coffee shop in the marina uh with Opus 4.8 because Fable was not out at this point”

▶ watch at 08:51

“naps naps were very popular among the best minds. Um they barely ate.”

▶ watch at 10:54
04Agent-legible infrastructure alongside the human-facing page

Beyond the visible site, she had the model generate a voice.md distilled from her own tweets and public writing to keep her tone consistent, and a separate glossary.md describing the site's content, which she says is far more efficient for an agent to read than parsing the live page.

“I asked it to create a voice on MD which um it picked up on all the writings that it could find on the internet that was written by me including my tweets and everything”

▶ watch at 07:49

“easily readable by the agents and it's a lot more efficient than having the agent look at this page, obviously.”

▶ watch at 11:56

Commentary (ours)

The "Shape of Minds" highlights (naps, low food intake, childlessness among great thinkers) were read live from output generated about 20 minutes before the talk and were explicitly unreviewed by the speaker; treat them as an unverified demo output, not a vetted finding (ours).
