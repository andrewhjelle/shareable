If you only read one thing

  • Built a personal website from a week of Slack brain-dumps using one Opus 4.8 prompt two days before the talk, then built a second demo tool ("Shape of Minds") in a single morning session at a coffee shop.
  • Maintains agent-facing artifacts alongside the human-facing page: a voice.md distilled from her own tweets and writing, and a glossary.md she says is far more efficient for an agent to read than the live page itself.
  • Frames the exercise as "imagination engineering": as one-shotting gets easy, the bottleneck becomes generating ideas worth building, illustrated by the borrowed line "live in the future and then build what's missing."

A week of Slack brain-dumps became a personal site in one prompt

  • She describes creating a private Slack channel to brain-dump her stream of consciousness for a week, then asking Opus 4.8 to build a website encapsulating everything she'd written, two days before the talk.
  • The resulting site pulled in her favorite quotes, projects, tools, and books, including customizable backgrounds and design sliders.

A same-morning tool comparing history's "spikiest minds"

  • In a separate session that same morning, starting at 10am at a coffee shop with Opus 4.8, she built a tool called "Shape of Minds" comparing historical figures across dimensions like obsessions, routines, and worldview.
  • She read out unreviewed AI-generated "highlights" from the tool live during the talk, including that naps were common among the figures studied and that many ate little.
