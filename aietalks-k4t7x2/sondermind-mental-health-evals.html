Evals-Driven Development for a Mental Health AI Coach

SpeakerAkele Reed & Dave Revere · SonderMind Watchyoutu.be/O72p-rBb2bA

If you only read one thing

  • SonderMind built separate input and output LLM-as-judge guardrails around its Sonder core model specifically because separating them is more robust and harder to jailbreak than folding safety into one pass, even at added latency/cost (c2, c3).

01The gap general-purpose LLMs leave in mental health

SonderMind cites an American Psychological Association survey finding that 77% of psychologists said their patients are using AI for some kind of mental health support, while general-purpose LLMs are not built for mental health care. Sonder, SonderMind's clinically grounded AI coach, is positioned to support people between therapy sessions or not yet ready for therapy, and to act as a front door into SonderMind's human provider network when a human is the right next step.

02Input and output guardrails sandwich the core model

Sonder's architecture places separate input guardrails, which screen incoming user messages for whether intervention is needed before the core model responds, and output guardrails, which monitor the AI's responses and the conversation as a whole for clinical safety risk. SonderMind deliberately implemented these guardrails as separate LLM-as-judge calls rather than folding them into the main response generation, judging that separation makes them more robust and harder to circumvent through prompt engineering, despite the added latency and cost.

03Correct triggers, not more triggers

SonderMind explicitly optimized for correct guardrail triggers rather than more frequent ones, because inappropriately guardrailing someone in a vulnerable moment can feel like a door slam to the face and increase isolation. For a message indicating present-tense danger (a user hiding from a drunk, threatening husband), Sonder surfaces local resources and disengages from the conversation rather than continuing to chat.

“when you inappropriately guardrail on somebody, then that can often feel like a door slam to the face and make that person feel more isolated”

04Clinician annotations become typed evals that gate releases

The talk uses the example of an indirect, coded risk message ('I packed a box today, just one to feel what it would be like to be gone') to argue that pattern-matching, verbose prompt rules, or a generic moderation API would miss the clinical nuance. Instead, a flagged conversation is traced and handed to a licensed clinician to annotate; that annotation is converted into a typed eval with an expected result and expected observation, and every subsequent prompt, model, or guardrail change is scored against it.

05Open-sourcing the guardrail scenario datasets

SonderMind's calibration approach rests on the clinical team owning the definition of 'good' rather than engineer intuition, with labeled scenarios turned into evals that gate releases based on concrete questions (did the right category fire, at the right point, catching the right issue type). The team open-sourced 200 clinically reviewed input guardrail scenarios and 100 output guardrail scenarios as a shared baseline for others building similar systems, while noting this isn't a replacement for building one's own learning loop.

Commentary (ours)

(ours) In Q&A, the speakers noted they had to disable the underlying frontier models' built-in safety guardrails on day one because those over-filtered their mental-health test scenarios entirely, which is a useful data point for anyone building sensitive-domain products on top of general-purpose model APIs rather than something covered in the main talk content.
