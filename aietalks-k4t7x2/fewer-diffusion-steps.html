You Might Not Need 50 Diffusion Steps

SpeakerZiv Ilan · Nvidia Watchyoutu.be/gHs5ZiY80PM
Generative mediaInfra & serving

If you only read one thing

  • Three incremental optimization layers apply to diffusion models: quantization (easiest, smaller memory/compute footprint), caching (skip recomputation between similar denoising steps), and step distillation (reduce 50 steps to as few as 1-8, biggest payoff).
  • Step distillation, not parameter-count reduction, is described as the only current path to real-time generation, demonstrated live at GTC on a single Blackwell B200 GPU.
  • Distillation requires post-training compute and data considerations (general open-source data works for general use cases; domain-specific use cases need domain-specific fine-tuning data), but doesn't require top-tier compute like GB200.
01Diffusion models need latency work before enterprise use

The speaker frames diffusion image/video models (denoising over 20-50 iterative steps) as maturing fast in quality (Flux 2, LTX 2, Nano Banana) but not yet fast, mature, or scalable enough for developer/enterprise contexts, and says the field is borrowing optimization concepts already proven in the LLM/VLM ecosystem.

“this ecosystem is is not as mature as the auto regressive LLM VLM ecosystem”

▶ watch at 02:05
02Quantization: the easy, lower-impact layer

Post-training quantization is described as simpler than quantization-aware training but harder to apply cleanly to diffusion models than to LLMs because diffusion models are more attention-heavy, making quantization's impact on quality less predictable, though still worthwhile on modern hardware. Nvidia's work with Black Forest Labs on Flux 2 used dynamic quantization, and pre-quantized checkpoints are available on Hugging Face via the open-source TRT-LLM visual-gen repo.

“this type of models are more attention heavy, which means that the impact of doing quantization is not as impactful as the LLMs VLMs”

▶ watch at 04:09
03Caching: skipping recomputation between similar steps

Unlike KV caching in autoregressive LLMs, diffusion caching (e.g., T-cache) compares denoising steps and skips recomputation when the change between steps is minimal, either across the whole latent space or, in more modern chunk-based approaches, for isolated regions of a frame. Done incorrectly, caching can meaningfully degrade image/video quality, so thresholds must be tuned and tested.

“Caching is something that if you don't do the right way, can have quite a significant impact on the quality of the image”

▶ watch at 08:47
04Step distillation: the biggest payoff

Distillation in diffusion targets step count, not parameter count: a student model is trained to match a teacher's output using far fewer denoising steps (4, 8, or even one-shot instead of 50), which can yield 10x to 200x performance improvement and is described as the only realistic route to real-time generation today. The two main approaches are trajectory-based (student follows the teacher's exact denoising path) and distribution-based (student only matches the final output distribution), with distribution-based currently considered the more common, better-quality technique; recent work combines both.

“the goal is not to get to a smaller model, okay? You'll still have the same number of parameters. This is more about step distillation”

“it's something that can give you 10x, 200x improvement in performance”

05Real-time demo and open-source tooling

Nvidia demonstrated real-time video generation at the recent GTC conference in San Jose using two distillation techniques on a single Blackwell B200 GPU. FastGen, an open-source Nvidia research repo, structures the post-training/scale-sharding process for large (20-40B+ parameter, trending toward hundreds of billions) video diffusion models, and supports fine-tuning on open-source data by default or custom data for domain-specific quality needs.

“there's some demo we did in the last GTC conference a couple of weeks ago in in San Jose uh with two different um distillation techniques”
