Harness Engineering: How to Build Software When Humans Steer, Agents Execute

01Code becomes free, engineering becomes systems design

The speaker frames GPT-5.2 (late 2025) as the point models became capable of doing the full job of a software engineer, which he says makes implementation no longer the scarce resource. In this framing, hiring more effective engineering capacity is constrained only by GPU capacity and token budget, and the human role shifts toward systems design and delegation.

“Hiring the hands on the keyboards as part of our teams is only constrained by GPU capacity and token budgets”

“the magic moment was GPT 5.2, which when it came out was able to do the full job of a software engineer”

02Prompts and guardrails replace the code artifact itself

Because implementation is delegated to agents, he argues the durable artifact of engineering work is the prompt and guardrails that produced the code, not the code. Teams should explicitly instruct agents not to produce slop and back that instruction with enforced guardrails rather than relying on humans catching bad output after the fact.

“The important thing is not the code, but the prompt and the guardrails that got you there”

▶ watch at 06:52

“You can just simply say do not produce slop. Don't accept slop, you won't get slop in your code base”

▶ watch at 09:26
03Structuring repos to be context-efficient for agents

Alongside lints, the team writes tests that constrain source structure itself -- for example, capping files at 350 lines -- to keep the codebase easier for the model to hold in context and to make agent output more predictable.

“we can write a test that limits the fact that files are no longer than 350 lines”

▶ watch at 13:37
04Depending on labs' harness-specific post-training

He describes labs as post-training models specifically within the context of the harness they are primarily deployed in (citing the apply-patch tool and bash-invocation semantics as examples), and argues this gives leverage to teams that build on first-party harnesses like Codex rather than reinventing harness mechanics themselves.

“the labs are not just post-training the models, but post-training the models in the context of the harness in which they are primarily deployed in”

▶ watch at 27:08
05Scaling PR throughput past the human review bottleneck

At three to five PRs per engineer per day, even a three-person team hit severe merge conflicts from concurrent work on the same code, with long-open PRs waiting on human review as the root cause. The team's fix was a weekly 'garbage collection day' dedicated to turning each week's recurring review feedback into categorical, automatically enforced guardrails.

“with each engineer producing three to five PRs per day, even on a team of three, merge conflicts were super miserable”

▶ watch at 37:04

“we called it garbage collection day, where our entire job was to take every bit of slop we had observed over the course of the week”
