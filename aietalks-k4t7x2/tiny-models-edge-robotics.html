patternLocal modelsInfra & serving

It goes deep on one technique, deploying and fine-tuning tiny models for edge hardware, rather than a full multi-system architecture.

If you only read one thing

  • DRAM cost is a hard constraint on edge deployment: the cost of a comparable Raspberry Pi with 36 gigabytes has gone up roughly 2.5x since launch, and some phone makers are shipping less DRAM this year than previously (c1).
  • A quantized 2B-parameter Gemma model reasons on par with a much larger Gemma 3 model from about a year earlier, running at 7.6 tokens/sec decode on a Raspberry Pi and up to 31 tokens/sec decode on a Qualcomm IoT NPU (c2, c4, c5).
  • Fine-tuning a small model (starting from Gemma 3 270M / Function Gemma) with 10,000 to 10 million synthetically generated samples was enough to reach over 86% reliability calling 10 different mobile functions from arbitrary free text input, and pushed Raspberry Pi decode speed to 45 tokens/sec (c6, c7, c8).

DRAM cost is the binding constraint on edge AI

  • The talk names DRAM cost, not compute, as the significant new constraint on deploying AI to edge devices, noting some mobile phone manufacturers are shipping less DRAM this year than previously.
  • As a concrete data point, the cost of a comparable Raspberry Pi with 36 gigabytes has risen by roughly a factor of 2.5x since launch, which forces heavy investment in quantization and picking the smallest model that can do the job.

Small models (1-4B parameters) are already capable with zero-shot prompting

  • Google's 2B-parameter Gemma model (E2B) reasons on par with a much larger Gemma 3 model from roughly a year earlier, using zero-shot prompting to get good results for a given feature without fine-tuning.
  • Aggressive quantization (a mix of 2-bit, 4-bit, and 8-bit, plus per-layer embeddings) gets the effective footprint down to about 2.9 bits per weight, which is the basis for the team's roughly 4GB+ RAM rule of thumb for deploying this class of model.

“It's certainly like on par like with a Gemma 3 much larger model from kind of 12 months ago”

“getting it down to like I know like 2.9 bits per weight if you look at the actual weights we need to hold in memory”

Throughput varies enormously by device class

  • Running the same 2B model on a Raspberry Pi produces about 7.6 tokens per second decode without speculative decoding (roughly 2x faster with it turned on), while a Jetson Orin Nano reaches up to about 24 tokens/sec, and a Qualcomm IoT board with an NPU reaches close to 4,000 tokens/sec prefill and about 31 tokens/sec decode, enough for near real-time multi-frame image applications.

04Fine-tuning tiny models pushes both speed and reliability further
Slide at 14:30 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Fine-tuning tiny models pushes both speed and reliability further

  • For sub-500M-parameter tiny models, the recommended playbook is to start from a small base (as small as Gemma 3 270M or Function Gemma) and fine-tune on synthetically generated data, which the team found reaches high reliability in the range of 10,000 to 10 million samples.
  • A resulting fine-tuned mobile-actions model can call 10 different device functions from arbitrary free-text input at over 86% reliability, and runs the base model at 45 tokens/sec decode on a Raspberry Pi versus mid-single-digit tokens/sec unfine-tuned.

“This model knows about 10 different output functions and can call them at over 86% uh reliability from a given arbitrary text input”

05Production example: fully offline voice dictation

Production example: fully offline voice dictation

  • A shipped iOS voice dictation app runs entirely on-device, using two fine-tuned tiny Gemma models, one for ASR and one to clean up filler words and personalize toward the user's names and vocabulary, replacing what would otherwise be a subscription-gated server-side feature.

“there's an ASR engine and a text policing engine. And both of these are fine-tuned versions of tiny Gemma models”

said at 18:13

Commentary (ours)

(ours) The consistent pattern across the throughput numbers (Raspberry Pi vs. Jetson vs. Qualcomm NPU) is that hardware acceleration, not model size alone, drives most of the usability gap for real-time interaction, which is worth separating from the fine-tuning reliability gains when deciding where to invest engineering effort for a given device target.
