Same task, different models

  • During the original 6-month refactor of a 10,000+ page medical-claims processing pipeline (more than 10 repos merged into one), giving O3 a code-migration task took 3 hours of back-and-forth and produced 10 major mistakes, requiring manual intervention.
  • Re-running the same benchmark later on Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.8 showed both models were substantially faster: Sonnet 4.6 solved it with one additional iteration, and Opus 4.8 one-shot it.

02Reading the task-length/accuracy curve correctly
Slide at 04:50 · click any slide to enlarge

Reading the task-length/accuracy curve correctly

  • Referencing model capability meters (task length a model can complete vs. success rate), the speaker argues the commonly cited 50% accuracy line is misleading; models are not reliably completing tasks that would take a human 18+ hours.
  • He recommends planning around the 90-99% accuracy threshold instead, since kicking off an hour-long agent run with only a 50% chance of success risks wasting that hour and the attention spent constructing the plan.

“we're no longer claiming that models can accomplish tasks that would take a human 18+ hours. Now, I actually think it's much better to measure the accuracy at 90% or 99%”

said at 08:09

“if you're kicking off a process that is going to take an hour and it has a 50% chance of completing, there's a very high chance you just wasted that hour and you could have been doing something different”

said at 08:39
03Can a modern model just zero-shot the refactor?
Slide at 17:40 · click any slide to enlarge

Can a modern model just zero-shot the refactor?

  • Testing whether a current frontier model could zero-shot the whole migration, the speaker gave GPT-5.5 (extra-high reasoning) the refactor goal directly.
  • It completed in 10 minutes 22 seconds and wrote only 2,000 lines of code, which turned out to be scaffolding rather than a real implementation of the underlying models, indicating models still cannot reliably self-validate and zero-shot a substantial refactor.

“I ran this experiment with GPT 5.5 extra high, and I gave it this goal, giving some of the names of the repositories with the underlying models and other components, and it completed its goal in in 10 minutes and 22 seconds”

said at 11:42
04Was the refactor worth it?
Slide at 17:40 · click any slide to enlarge

Was the refactor worth it?

  • The speaker concludes the refactor was worthwhile: it reduced pipeline processing time and cost, enabled support for larger files, and let the team ship features in under a week that previously took multiple months.
  • In Q&A, he notes the refactor hit 15 of 17 originally gathered requirements, and that PR review stayed fully human throughout, since the small team was using PRs partly to build shared context on the new codebase.

“we were able to actually reduce the amount of time the pipeline took. We were able to reduce the costs. We could support larger files, and now we can ship features that would take multiple months in under a week.”

said at 13:44

“I think we got uh 15 out of 17 requirements right when we were going ahead with the refactor”

said at 15:59

Commentary (ours)

(ours) The model-vs-model comparison (O3 vs Sonnet 4.6 vs Opus 4.8 on the same task) is the most concrete, checkable claim in the talk; the GPT-5.5 zero-shot result is a single anecdote on one specific migration task and shouldn't be generalized to "models can't zero-shot refactors" broadly.
