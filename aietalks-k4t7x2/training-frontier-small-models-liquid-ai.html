03Doom looping is a small-model problem, fixed via DPO then RL

Doom looping is a small-model problem, fixed via DPO then RL

  • Doom looping -- a model repeating the same sequence of words indefinitely -- is described as especially likely with small models, reasoning models, and complex tasks combined.
  • Liquid's fix has two stages: during preference alignment, five temperature-sampled rollouts plus one temperature-zero rollout are scored by an LLM jury, training the model via DPO to reject doom-looping outputs; then reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards plus an n-gram repetition penalty further suppresses it.
  • On LFM 2.5 1.2B thinking, the doom-loop ratio was about 15-16% after pre-training, barely moved after SFT, dropped substantially after DPO, and was almost eliminated after RL -- compared to over 50% doom loops for Qwen 3.5 0.8B in reasoning mode.

04Pairing small models with tools compensates for low knowledge capacity

Pairing small models with tools compensates for low knowledge capacity

  • Because small models are memory-bound, they have low knowledge capacity and hallucinate more; the speaker recommends giving them web search tools so they can look up facts rather than rely on stored knowledge, arguing tiny models are already well suited to agentic tasks if given reliable tool access.

“If you're memory bound, it means that you have low knowledge capacity. If you have no low knowledge capacity, it means that you're going to hallucinate a lot.”

said at 15:33

Commentary (ours)

The doom-loop comparison to Qwen 3.5 0.8B is framed as evidence that treating small models as scaled-down copies of large ones (rather than architecting for their unique constraints) leaves this failure mode unaddressed -- worth checking whether other edge-model vendors report doom-loop rates at all before assuming the problem is solved industry-wide (ours).
