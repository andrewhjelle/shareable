The biggest challenge in your stack? Evals, Evals, Evals - 2026 State of AI Engineering results

SpeakerBarr Yaron · Amplify Partners Watchyoutu.be/RGe6EjucbzI
Evals & benchmarksAgentic engineering

If you only read one thing

  • 95% of the 1,048 surveyed AI engineers report using agents (roughly double last year), and among agent users, write-access rose from 52% to 89% (c1, c5).
  • 94% still use closed models and only 45% use open-weight models, mostly as an augmentation rather than a replacement, while cost now regularly constrains AI usage for 40% of respondents (c4, c9).
  • Evals remain the number one cited stack challenge every year the survey has run, with 96% of respondents reporting some kind of stack problem (c6).
01Survey scale and composition

The 2026 survey drew 1,048 respondents, described as a large and experienced but AI-newer population: engineers with over 10 years of software experience mostly have three years or less of AI experience, while the newest engineers entering the field have nearly as much AI experience as 10-year software veterans.

“We had 1,048 respondents this year, which is a lot of AI engineers.”

▶ watch at 02:12
02Modality adoption: audio's intent, image's leap

Audio has the highest 'intent to adopt' among modalities not yet in use, with 56% of non-adopters saying they plan to add it, up from 37% last year. Image generation showed the largest actual year-over-year jump, with respondents using generative AI for images doubling from 18% to 36%, attributed to model releases like Nano Banana and ChatGPT Images 2.0.

“a whopping 56% say they plan to adopt it in the AI applications they build”

“The share of respondents using generative AI for images and feeling really good about it doubled from 18% last year to 36% this year.”

03Model mix and cost as a constraint

94% of respondents use closed models in production versus 45% using open-weight models, and most open-weight users also use closed models, suggesting augmentation rather than replacement. Cost has become a first-class engineering constraint: 40% say cost regularly shapes how ambitiously they use AI, and another 36% say it sometimes does.

“94% use closed models. 45% are using openweight models.”

▶ watch at 05:50

“40% of respondents say that cost regularly shapes how ambitiously they use AI and another 36% say that it sometimes does.”

▶ watch at 08:25
04Agents gained write access, not more control

95% of respondents say they're using agents this year, roughly double the prior year, and among agent-building teams, the share whose agents can write data jumped from 52% to 89%. The survey frames this as agents moving from reading and drafting to taking real actions inside systems, while the top controls in use (human-in-the-loop approval, permission gating) remain the same blunt instruments as before.

“This year, 95%, this seems high to me, 95% say they're using agents, roughly double last year.”

▶ watch at 09:56
05Evals stay the top stack problem, prompts stay in-house

Evaluating AI outputs is again the single most-cited stack challenge, though the margin over other challenges is narrowing; 96% of respondents report some kind of problem with their stack overall. Looking at build-versus-buy across eight stack layers, inference/model serving is bought the most, while 61% build prompt management themselves, with product-logic-adjacent layers generally staying in-house.

“96% of the people in the survey in this room have a problem with the stack”

▶ watch at 12:01

“prompt management is the opposite. 61% build it themselves.”

▶ watch at 13:04
06Five-year bets

Asked about a five-year horizon, 67% of respondents expect a leading AI lab will publicly declare AGI (the question was about the announcement, not the underlying achievement), while only 9% bet that Transformers will still be state-of-the-art architecture in five years.

“67% expect a leading lab will declare AGI in the next five years.”

▶ watch at 17:14

Commentary (ours)

(ours) The AGI figure is explicitly about a press-release event rather than a technical milestone, which the speaker flags directly; worth citing that framing whenever the 67% number gets repeated, since it easily gets misquoted as a capability prediction.
