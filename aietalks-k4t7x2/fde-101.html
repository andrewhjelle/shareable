Forward Deployed Engineering 101

SpeakerKevin Bai · Anthropic Watchyoutu.be/KwhgfwOSToQ
Vertical AIProduct & UX

If you only read one thing

  • FDE only makes sense when you must sell something technically complex to a buyer who isn't technical themselves (c2).
  • Without a shared platform of primitives to build on, an "FDE function" where every engineer builds from scratch is actually just a dev shop (c3, c4).
  • Predicts AI is making nearly every software platform agentic and customizable, which pushes more companies into needing an FDE-like function even outside Palantir's original niche (c6).
01When you actually need FDE

The speaker frames the decision as a 2x2 of what's being sold (simple vs. technically complex) against who's buying (technical vs. non-technical). FDE, in his framing, is only necessary in the specific quadrant where a highly technical platform is being sold to a non-technical buyer; outside that quadrant, DevRel or a standard sales-led motion fits better.

“You only need FTE if you are in this weird unique situation of Palunteer where you are having to sell something”

▶ watch at 04:39

“a FTE is nothing more than a customerfacing software engineer”

▶ watch at 16:41
02The ACV case for the model

He cites average contract value (ACV) figures for public SaaS companies as evidence the model works: Palantir leads at around $4 million, followed by ServiceNow near $1.2 million and Workday around $600K, with no other public SaaS company reaching even half a million in ACV.

“Balance is first at 4 million uh last I checked. Next biggest is Service Now at 1.2.”

▶ watch at 06:42
03Platform or dev shop

He states that an FDE function only works if engineers assemble solutions on top of a shared platform of primitives; if every engineer builds custom software from scratch for each customer, the organization has a dev shop, not an FDE function, and will be buried by maintenance costs.

“if they are not building on top of a platform with some number of shared primitives you are in for a very bad time”

▶ watch at 10:47

“If you were to implement an FTE function where each FTE is building entirely from scratch, my friends, you do not have an FTE function. You have a dev shop.”

▶ watch at 08:14
04FDE as design partnership at scale

He describes FDE as taking the early-stage-startup practice of a close design partnership and scaling that same close-collaboration model up to enterprise customers, which he frames as Palantir's original bet.

“FDE is basically taking this concept of a design partnership and scaling it up into enterprise.”

▶ watch at 07:43
05Why this applies more broadly now

He argues that AI's effect on the industry isn't that people suddenly recognized Palantir's motion was smart, but that AI has made most software platforms agentic and therefore customizable, meaning more companies now face the same non-technical-buyer, complex-platform mismatch that originally drove Palantir to FDE.

“now nearly every platform is agentic. And that means nearly every platform is customizable.”

▶ watch at 12:20

Commentary (ours)
