The distillation spectrum: offline to fully online

  • Denton frames continual learning as a spectrum.
  • On one end, offline distillation works from a single static batch of production traces.
  • In the middle, a daily batch of traces gets processed.
  • On the far end is a fully online flywheel where a model serves production traffic, a rollout becomes a trace, the system learns from that trace, updates the model, and serves the next request.
  • Most enterprises today sit on the offline end; Applied Compute's stated goal is to provide value across the whole spectrum.

A second axis: where the teaching hint comes from

  • Orthogonal to online/offline traces is online/offline hinting.
  • Offline hints come from static priors, known rubrics, or a general behavior to fix, such as a customer support agent that gives refunds too readily.
  • Online hints are constructed dynamically from the specific online rollout that just happened, rather than applied uniformly.
  • Crossing these two axes produces a 2x2 grid of distillation types; Applied Compute focuses its work on two quadrants: offline-hint/offline-trace and online-hint/online-trace.

Offline case: getting a model to submit earlier on SWE-bench

  • Working with a Qwen 3.5 thinking model on SWE-bench, the team found it was taking up to 80 turns to submit an answer, and tried to get it to call a submit tool before turn 40 without hurting the underlying task pass rate.
  • Using an offline hint applied to offline production traces, the task-complete call rate rose from about 22% to 60% while the test pass rate held roughly constant (it went up slightly).

04Online case: fixing hyperlink formatting without breaking coding ability

Online case: fixing hyperlink formatting without breaking coding ability

  • For a customer-specific hyperlink formatting requirement, direct approaches (adding a reward for the format, or SFT on correctly-formatted traces) degraded overall coding-agent performance because the format was far out of distribution.
  • Instead, the team did a rollout, then injected a hint specific to that rollout telling the model how it had formatted the link and how to format it next time.
  • This online-hint/online-trace approach lifted correct formatting from about 15% to around 80%, a bigger gain than applying the same hint uniformly to every offline rollout.

“the percentage of correct hyperlink formatting jumped drastically from about, I guess, 15% all the way up to around 80%”

said at 15:28
05Tactics: per-step hinting and relevance-mask distillation
Slide at 18:40 · click any slide to enlarge

Tactics: per-step hinting and relevance-mask distillation

  • Two practical findings: injecting the hint at the specific step in the rollout where it's relevant (via a judge that picks the injection point) works much better than injecting at the start, since the learning signal decays the further you get from the hint.
  • Second, 'relevance mask self-distillation' uses an LLM judge to select which specific tokens to learn from the teacher model, since the teacher often has stylistic preferences on filler/connector words that aren't the actual target behavior; this improves learning of out-of-distribution behavior while avoiding broader catastrophic degradation.

Commentary (ours)

The framing avoids requiring a 'golden answer' dataset, which is the practical blocker most teams hit with distillation (ours). The SWE-bench and hyperlink examples are single-customer case studies rather than general benchmarks, so the specific percentages are best read as evidence the mechanism works, not as expected gains for other tasks.
