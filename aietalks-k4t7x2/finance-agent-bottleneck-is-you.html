If you only read one thing

  • Demos fail in production because agents haven't seen the data pilots surface; the fix isn't swapping models or frameworks but compressing the developer's own iteration loop (c1, c2).
  • Concretely: git worktrees isolate parallel sub-agents (the speaker cites 50 active work trees on 48GB of RAM), with humans needed only at task intake and final validation of a 9-step bug-fix pipeline (c3, c4, c5).
  • In a regulated finance setting, agent-reviewed-by-agent work creates an accountability gap; the proposed fix is a self-improving harness where the human stays a verifier, not the throughput ceiling (c6, c7, c8).

01The demo-to-production gap

The demo-to-production gap

  • The speaker argues that AI finance demos are easy to build and impressive to show, but fail once promoted to pilots because the agent has never seen the customer's actual future data, producing high rates of production bugs.

“once these demos are promoted to pilots and you start onboarding new customers, the agent has never seen these future data”

said at 01:33
02Dev loop velocity, not model swaps, is the lever
Slide at 13:20 · click any slide to enlarge

Dev loop velocity, not model swaps, is the lever

  • Since better models and faster chips arrive on a multi-month lag outside a team's control, the talk argues the controllable variable for fixing production bugs in real time is the developer's own iteration loop, which needs to be automated and sped up continuously.

“The answer is your dev loop velocity. The model capability increases very exponentially.”

said at 02:37
03Sub-agents, worktrees, and where humans stay in the loop
Slide at 13:20 · click any slide to enlarge

Sub-agents, worktrees, and where humans stay in the loop

  • The recommended pattern is isolated git worktrees per sub-agent so parallel tasks don't collide, with a concrete example of 50 active work trees running on a MacBook with 48GB of RAM.
  • Across a nine-step bug-fix pipeline (from parsing a QA ticket to shipping to a stage environment), the speaker argues a human is only needed at step 1 and step 9 — task intake and final validation.

“get work trees are your best friend. So, think of work trees as isolated folders.”

“with 48 GB of RAM on your MacBook, you can have 50 active work trees. That is 50 active sub-agents working independently on”

said at 04:42

“the human is only required at steps 1 and 9 because the in-between steps, the agent can do a lot better work”

said at 06:16
04Accountability in a regulated, agent-reviewed pipeline
Slide at 13:20 · click any slide to enlarge

Accountability in a regulated, agent-reviewed pipeline

  • Because Auditoria's agents operate in finance, where a human auditor and SOC-compliance controller normally sign off on changes, the talk raises accountability as unresolved when agents review agents' work: if something breaks in production, there's no clear party to hold responsible.

“If something goes wrong in production, you can't say Cloud is doing this. Something is wrong.”

said at 08:52
05Removing yourself from the loop
Slide at 13:20 · click any slide to enlarge

Removing yourself from the loop

  • The talk's second half describes iterating the dev harness itself: having the agent analyze its own process bottlenecks after each batch of bug fixes and remove them over roughly a month, until a single sentence can trigger the whole pipeline.
  • The recommended end state keeps a human as verifier of agent output, not as the ceiling on how much work can get done.

“you just tell the agent to analyze all the bottlenecks in this process. Make a list of them. And somehow slowly keep removing these bottlenecks every day.”

said at 09:54

“Always have the human as a verifier, but not the throughput ceiling because human attention is very limited.”

said at 13:00

Commentary (ours)

(ours) The accountability claim (c6) is the sharpest point in the talk and the least resolved — the speaker raises the agent-reviewing-agent gap as a real problem in a SOC-compliance context but doesn't describe how Auditoria itself closes it, beyond keeping a human at intake and final validation.
