How to Leverage Domain Expertise

This talk gives a concrete framework (oracle, evaluator, architect) for deciding where to place a domain expert as an AI product scales, illustrated with named companies (Granola, Tandem, Anterior) and specific hiring/role details

If you only read one thing

  • Chris Lovejoy argues winning in vertical AI is an organizational problem rather than a model-quality problem, citing Gartner's finding that about 50% of generative AI projects were abandoned last year.
  • He frames three ways to bake in domain expertise: oracle (the expert directly edits outputs, as at Granola), evaluator (the expert defines quality metrics), and architect (the expert designs a self-improving system, the direction Anterior evolved toward).
  • His organizational advice is to name one principal domain expert accountable for AI quality; at one company, splitting that ownership between two senior clinicians led to slow progress, and both left within 12-18 months.

01Why vertical AI is an org problem, not a model problem

Why vertical AI is an org problem, not a model problem

  • Lovejoy states his belief that winning in vertical AI is fundamentally an organizational problem rather than a matter of picking the best model.
  • He cites a Gartner figure that about 50% of generative AI projects were abandoned last year, and attributes much of that to companies building AI systems without a deep understanding of the workflows they're automating.

02Three models: oracle, evaluator, architect

Three models: oracle, evaluator, architect

  • He lays out three ways a domain expert can be embedded in an organization.
  • As an oracle, the expert directly edits AI outputs and prompts themselves.
  • As an evaluator, the expert instead defines how quality is measured and feeds that data to engineers.
  • As an architect, the expert designs a system that improves itself automatically from usage, with less human-in-the-loop involvement.

03Case studies: Granola and Anterior

Case studies: Granola and Anterior

  • At Granola, an AI meeting-notes company that recently passed a billion-dollar valuation, the first employee has remained the primary oracle for AI quality because there's no single objectively correct meeting note.
  • At Anterior, a prior-authorization company where Lovejoy was first technical employee, the domain-expert role progressed from oracle to evaluator to architect as the AI's outputs became a clearly measurable correct/incorrect decision requiring an adaptive system.

04How to leverage the domain expert you hire

How to leverage the domain expert you hire

  • Lovejoy recommends naming a single principal domain expert who is accountable for AI quality, rather than splitting responsibility across a committee.
  • He describes a failure mode at one company where two senior clinicians shared the role without clear ownership, progress on the AI-quality system was slow, and both left the company within 12-18 months.

Commentary (ours)

This is one practitioner's self-reported framework built from a handful of companies he's worked at, not an independently validated study; the Gartner abandonment statistic and the Granola/Anterior details are secondhand and undated in the talk itself.
