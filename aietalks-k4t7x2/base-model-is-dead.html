The Base Model Is Dead

SpeakerVarun Singh · Arcee AI Watchyoutu.be/xbPriQWXtWM
Post-trainingData curation

If you only read one thing

  • Web text's share of pretraining data has fallen from about 85% (GPT-3) to about 15% in MAI Thinking 1, with labs split on whether to replace it with synthetic data or stay web-text-only (c2, c5).
  • Skipping post-training-style data in early pretraining creates MOE load-balancing imbalances; MAI Thinking 1 had to compensate by cranking the load-balancing coefficient during SFT (c3, c6).
  • The framing shift: base models are being reconceived as an atomic-skills prior for RL rather than a repository of world knowledge, alongside compute allocation moving toward RL (c7, c4, c1).
01Base models used to be defined by web-scale text

Historically the base model, trained mostly on Common Crawl, WebText-2, books, and Wikipedia, determined how good a model could get, consuming the bulk of the compute budget while post-training mainly taught it to use that knowledge in a chat format. Web text made up roughly 85% of GPT-3's training mix; by MAI Thinking 1 that share is down to about 15%, with code and STEM data taking a larger share as models gain agentic use cases.

“web text, which used to make up like up to 85% of the train data in GPT uh 3, is now all the way down at 15%”

▶ watch at 06:42
02Reasoning models made the choice of pretraining prior urgent

The release of OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek's R1 turned RL from a minor post-training step into a phase that can dramatically change model performance, and models built with RL later learned to interact with software environments end to end. That shift raises the question of whether a standard base model is still the best starting point for this large-scale RL phase.

“is your standard base model still uh, what the best uh, what will be the best um, prior for the for the this large-scale reinforcement learning phase”

▶ watch at 05:10
03Labs are split on pulling post-training data back into pretraining

MAI Thinking 1 explicitly avoids any synthetic or other-model-generated data, while NeMoTron 3 Ultra leans heavily into SFT-labeled synthetic data pulled back into the pretraining mix so the model learns conversational and task shapes from the start. Contrary to fears that synthetic data causes model collapse, large-scale examples (Arcee's own Trinity Lodge via rephrasing, and Kimi K2) show it working at scale, and mid-training is emerging as a related technique that exposes the model to post-training-style, longer-context distributions before RL.

“there's a lot of uh talk around synthetic data that, you know, blindly tossing it into a model can cause the model to collapse and uh and performance to tank”

▶ watch at 09:52

“mid-training, uh which is exposing the model to the distribution that it would see during post-training in RL and at a longer context”

▶ watch at 12:25
04Skipping this exposure breaks MOE load balancing

Without post-training-style data early in pretraining, an MOE model's post-training data distribution can differ sharply from what it saw in pretraining, causing load-balancing imbalances across experts. MAI Thinking 1 hit this problem and compensated by increasing the load-balancing coefficient during SFT rather than fixing the distribution mismatch upstream.

“MAI overcame it by uh really cranking up the load balancing coefficient during the SFT stages”

▶ watch at 11:55
05Reframing the base model as an RL prior, with compute shifting to match

The talk's closing argument is that base models need exposure to the atomic skills RL will later compose, rather than exposure to the entirety of human knowledge, and that this shift is already visible in compute allocation: Xiaomi's Mimo Labs reports roughly equal pretraining and post-training compute, and Composer 2.5 pushed RL compute even further past what the model saw in supervised learning.

“the base model needs to have some exposure to like uh like the atomic skills that it would need to compose during RL”

▶ watch at 14:29

“pre-training and post-training in the final model have a roughly equal compute allocation”

▶ watch at 13:58

“base models have kind of moved from general, uh, human knowledge and world priors to reasoning and agentic behavior priors”

▶ watch at 16:32

Commentary (ours)

Singh frames this as a clear trend, but his own slide contrasts MAI Thinking 1 (no synthetic data) against NeMoTron 3 Ultra (heavy synthetic data) as opposite strategies both currently in production, so the field hasn't converged on one recipe (ours).
