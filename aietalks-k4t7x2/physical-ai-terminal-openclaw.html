If you only read one thing

  • Built a handheld terminal with a dual OLED-plus-e-paper display to talk to a local Open Claw/LLM backend, because e-paper alone is too slow for live dynamic text.
  • The backend serves an open-source 120-billion-parameter model via TensorRT behind an OpenAI-style proxy, since not all open models match the OpenAI API format.
  • A power-regulator failure killed the OLED and other fragile parts (weeks to get replacements), and the project's most-liked feature turned out to be a text-based RPG mode with 4 worlds and 16 classes.

Why two displays: fast OLED plus slow-but-persistent e-paper

  • The device pairs a small, fast, single-color OLED display for dynamically updating live text with a bistable electronic-paper display, because e-paper alone is well suited to reading but too slow to render text dynamically as it streams.

“I realized electronic paper can be a bit slow. Of course, it's perfect for reading, but if you just want to provide dynamically text to it, you proba- probably need uh faster display”

Backend: a locally served 120B model behind an OpenAI-style proxy

  • The demonstrated backend connects the terminal to an open-source, 120-billion-parameter model, served using TensorRT for performance.
  • Because not all open-source models match the OpenAI API's request/response style, the backend exposes an OpenAI-style proxy layer in front of the served model.

“I just want to demonstrate you um the device connected to an open-source GPT 120 billion parameters model”

“I use their TensorRT serving system. And I just exposed OpenAI style to uh also like LLM proxy because I just hit a lot of walls with uh other open source models”

03Firmware constraints on the microcontroller

Firmware constraints on the microcontroller

  • To keep the terminal responsive on an ESP32 dual-core microcontroller, the firmware uses fixed static buffers and renders pre-generated one-bit images stored in pre-allocated memory, with no markdown-rendering engine and no dynamic memory allocation (malloc) on the MCU side.

“you can just have fixed static buffers and render one-bit images stored in the in the memory. That means the pages live in a pre-allocated memory. There's a no markdown engine and no malloc on the MCU side”

said at 06:48

Hardware lessons: a regulator failure and other build problems

  • Field notes from the build include a software I2C wiring issue, a silent GPIO 13 failure that required moving to a different port, cheap encoder hardware causing rotational noise that needed added pull-ups and capacitors, and a power-regulator problem that killed the OLED display and other fragile parts, costing significant time waiting weeks for replacement parts.

“the regulator uh kills the LED and and fragile parts of the display and cost me a lot of time and and getting the replacement parts from the market took so long”

said at 11:01
05The RPG mode, and what's next
Slide at 16:20 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

The RPG mode, and what's next

  • An unplanned feature -- a text-based RPG mode with four generated worlds (cyberpunk, fantasy, deep-space, among others) and 16 character classes, built using the same one-bit rendering pipeline -- became a favorite part of the project.
  • The speaker notes current small models still can't run well on tiny MCUs and has filed a provisional patent, with an eye toward a possible future commercial device.

“Four modes. For worlds, for gaming. In general, 16 classes. That means you can generate RPG worlds inside of it”

“I also decided to build a the provisional patent here in that field and maybe it will bring some commercial device in the future to a to a market”

said at 15:44

“Keep the model of the metal because it's really heavy. That means for now we don't have such a models which runs really on the super tiny MCUs”
