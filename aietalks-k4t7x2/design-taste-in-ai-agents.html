Argues for two concrete workflow changes for anyone shipping AI-generated UI: maintain a written anti-pattern/theme reference (his Hallmark skill) instead of relying on default model output, and use a cheaper, faster open-source model fo...

If you only read one thing

  • The speaker attributes his apps' user growth mainly to design and UX rather than features, and argues a small (10 to 20%) extra polish pass on the UI is a meaningful edge because AI-generated apps are recognizable as "slop" within seconds.
  • His Hallmark skill codifies specific AI-slop visual tells (purple gradient backgrounds, italic headers, all-caps letter-spaced pill labels, gradient logos) as things to avoid, and feeds the model curated visual themes as reference material.
  • In a blind side-by-side at the talk, the audience largely could not distinguish a landing page generated by open-source GLM 5.2 from one generated by Opus, which cost five times as much and was slower -- his takeaway is to use cheaper, faster models for iteration and always supply reference screenshots.

03Hallmark: codifying anti-patterns and feeding curated themes
Slide at 03:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Hallmark: codifying anti-patterns and feeding curated themes

  • He built a design skill called Hallmark that encodes these slop patterns as instructions for the model to avoid (for example, no purple gradients, no italic titles) and separately feeds the model a set of curated visual themes as inspiration when generating a page.
  • He says giving models strong inspiration is a theme throughout the talk because it consistently improves output.

“Hallmark basically takes all of these uh AI slop patterns uh and uh codifies them uh and and tells AI models like hey like don't don't use these you know don't do a purple gradient”

said at 04:09

“if you give AI models really really good inspiration, they tend to perform uh very very well”

said at 04:41
04Cheaper models for iteration: GLM 5.2 vs. Opus

Cheaper models for iteration: GLM 5.2 vs. Opus

  • For app iteration after an initial build, he says he now favors smaller, faster open-source models like GLM 5.2 over frontier models.
  • In a blind comparison, one landing page generated by GLM 5.2 was hard to distinguish from one generated by Opus, while the Opus version cost five times as much and was slower.

“the one on the right, you know, cost five times as much and and was a lot slower as well”

“a lot of people I think undervalue the importance of using uh smaller faster models for these app iteration uh cycles”

05Practical prompting takeaways
Slide at 10:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

Practical prompting takeaways

  • His top recommendation is giving agents reference screenshots and inspiration images pulled from a personal collection of liked designs.
  • He also recommends longer, more specific prompts -- including multi-minute voice notes -- over short ones, and breaking large builds into one or two features per prompt.

“this is maybe the most important one. If you take one thing away from this talk, please give your agents references and screenshots.”

“longer and more specific prompts are always better. Um these days I I will just record voice notes.”

Commentary (ours)

The GLM 5.2 vs. Opus comparison is a single informal audience poll on two landing pages, not a controlled eval, so the "almost indistinguishable" claim should be read as anecdotal support for using cheaper models on iteration tasks specifically, not as a general claim that GLM 5.2 matches Opus on design quality (ours).
