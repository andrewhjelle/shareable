Lays out a concrete offline/online loop (spec, build, eval, ship, monitor, diagnose, optimize) for using agents to build and maintain other agents, and argues human review of traces is the bottleneck once an org runs more than a handful ...

If you only read one thing

  • The agent-building lifecycle has an offline loop (spec, build, eval) and an online loop (monitor, diagnose, optimize) that should both run agentically, not manually, once you have more than a few agents.
  • Eval suites should favor binary pass/fail criteria over LLM-judge scores because scores without a well-defined rubric don't tell you what to fix.
  • Reading all production traces doesn't scale; representative sampling plus learned, code-checkable failure indicators makes diagnosis tractable at high trace volume.

01Two loops: building and running agents
Slide at 02:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

Two loops: building and running agents

  • The speakers describe an offline loop (build, test, evaluate, improve an agent before shipping) and an online loop (monitor production traces, diagnose failures, feed fixes back into optimization).
  • They argue that running this cycle manually doesn't scale once an organization has more than a handful of agents, because human review becomes the bottleneck.

“can't scale especially if in your organization you're not planning to roll out hundreds of agents etc”

“doing this agentically is the key to increasing the throughput because then you can fit many more cycles into the same time window”

02Spec-driven development as the starting artifact
Slide at 08:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 1 more from this section below

Spec-driven development as the starting artifact

  • Before building, the process starts with a spec capturing the agent's context requirements, integrations/tools, responsibilities, and constraints.
  • The spec is kept isolated from implementation so a team can retarget a different agent framework or harness later without rewriting the spec.

“spec is isolated from the implementation details. So once you decide to you can pick any target”

said at 09:02
03Eval-driven development replaces ad hoc review
Slide at 11:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

Eval-driven development replaces ad hoc review

  • After build, teams define an eval suite (metrics/criteria plus datasets) rather than trying to pre-guess every case; the complete suite is described as a product of ongoing discovery from user feedback and production failures.
  • The speakers recommend binary evals over score-based LLM-judge evals because binary results give a clear call to action, and warn that LLM-judge scoring needs calibration to control run-to-run variance.

“the real and the complete eval suite is a product of discovery. What that means is over time, from user feedback, from production failures”

said at 12:45

“using uh, binary type of evals or criteria, is preferred because there you have a kind of a call to action”

said at 17:25

“your LLM as a judge solution should be calibrated so that you don't have the, uh, scoring noise between judges”

said at 17:56
04Diagnosis at scale without reading every trace
Slide at 20:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Diagnosis at scale without reading every trace

  • In production, the diagnose stage groups failures by root cause and builds up code-checkable indicators per failure mode over time, because reading all traces at high volume can cost more than the agent's execution itself.
  • The speakers describe using representative sampling and learned indicators instead of full trace review.

“if you have let's say millions of agent traces and to read all of these uh it actually costs more than the execution itself”

said at 21:05

“try to uh pick a representative sample from your um whole traces by you know intelligent segmentation strategies”

said at 21:37
05Mutagent's product: orchestrator plus evaluator and diagnostics agents
Slide at 25:00 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Mutagent's product: orchestrator plus evaluator and diagnostics agents

  • Mutagent's current product runs two agents in research preview -- an evaluator agent that builds eval sets, and a diagnostics agent that analyzes production traces and outputs an HTML artifact with failure modes, root-cause explanations, and proposed remedies -- connected through an orchestrator that runs inside a coding environment like Claude Code.

“both of these agents um are connected through an orchestrator, which runs in your coding environment”

Commentary (ours)

The claim that binary evals beat score-based LLM-judge evals for actionability is a useful operating rule, but it trades away nuance for cases where partial credit genuinely matters -- worth testing whether it holds for agents with graded rather than pass/fail success criteria (ours).
