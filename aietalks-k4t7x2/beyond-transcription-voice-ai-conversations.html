If you only read one thing

  • In a live demo on a two-person phone call, pyannoteAI's community-1 diarization model scored 5% DER and its precision-2 model scored 3% DER (c3, c4).
  • State-of-the-art diarization varies hugely by scenario: about 8% DER on clean telephone speech versus roughly 41% DER in a noisy, multi-speaker restaurant setting (c5, c6).
  • Speech-to-text models trained on single-speaker audio degrade sharply on multi-speaker recordings -- NVIDIA Parakeet's reported 11.4% word error rate became 26% on the same AMI meeting dataset using a table microphone instead of a headset (c7).

Benchmarking diarization live: 5% down to 3% DER

04Why speaker-attributed transcription is hard
Slide at 17:50 · click any slide to enlarge · 8 more from this section below

Why speaker-attributed transcription is hard

  • Most STT models are trained primarily on single-speaker audio and fail badly on multi-speaker recordings with overlap and speaker change; NVIDIA Parakeet's published 11.4% word error rate on the AMI meeting dataset (recorded via headset mic) rose to 26% when the same model was applied to the same dataset's distant table-microphone recordings.
  • A second source of error is reconciling mismatched timestamps between the separately-run diarization and STT models, since STT handles overlapping speech poorly and the two systems' detected speech regions don't always agree.

“When we apply the very same model on the same AMI um on on our side, we we get 26%”

said at 17:41

“STT does not transcribe overlapping speech well because the timestamps disagree between STT and diarization, and because sometimes diarization will detect speech that the transcription will not transcribe”

said at 19:15
05How pyannoteAI resolves overlap conflicts
Slide at 23:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 2 more from this section below

How pyannoteAI resolves overlap conflicts

  • PyannoteAI's approach to reconciling diarization and transcription timestamps is described as proprietary, but part of it -- available in the open-source community-1 model as 'exclusive diarization' -- selects the single more-likely speaker of an overlapping pair to simplify which speaker a given transcribed word gets assigned to.

“there's already part of this trick that is available in the community one model, which we call exclusive diarization. Basically, what we do is that we find a way when there is overlap”

said at 23:52

Commentary (ours)

The 8% vs 41% DER gap between clean telephone audio and a noisy multi-speaker room is a useful sanity check against any vendor's single headline accuracy number -- any voice-AI accuracy claim that doesn't specify the acoustic scenario (mic distance, number of speakers, background noise) is likely citing the favorable end of that range (ours).
