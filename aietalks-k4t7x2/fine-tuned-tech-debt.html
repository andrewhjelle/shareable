Your Fine-Tuned Model Is Tech Debt: A 50x ROI House of Cards

SpeakerDan Bjornn · Lease End Watchyoutu.be/4loPnxvWWhg
Post-trainingVertical AIContext engineering

If you only read one thing

  • Fine-tuning delivered $12 million of revenue at 50x ROI in year one, but locked the team into one model and a week-long fix cycle they called the calcification tax.
  • Replacing fine-tuning with editable skills and system prompts cut the fix cycle from about a week to under an hour and beat the fine-tuned model on accuracy.
  • Recommendation: fine-tune only when you literally cannot call a frontier model, and even then the decision still has to beat the tax.
01The fine-tuning bet and its early payoff

Lease End built an LLM-based texting application in late 2024 to help customers finish lease buyouts, replacing a RAG-based intent classifier with a fine-tuned model for better accuracy, lower cost/latency, and vendor independence. Within its first year the application helped bring in $12 million of revenue at a 50x ROI.

“Within a year, this application had helped us bring in $12 million of revenue at a 50x ROI.”

02The calcification tax

Despite the revenue, the system quietly accumulated debt: fixing one misclassification often caused regressions elsewhere, turning fixes into a whack-a-mole process, and the team grew more locked into a single rigid model and architecture the longer they relied on it.

“the more we use the model, the more rigid everything became.”

▶ watch at 09:19

“we fixed the problem that we were just trying to solve, but then we caused regressions in other things.”

▶ watch at 07:16
03The weeklong retrain cycle

Each fix required gathering and validating new examples, running the fine-tuning job (itself about an hour), and then iterating because the first pass rarely solved the problem without breaking something else. The whole discover-to-deploy cycle took about a week, so the team triaged bugs by frequency and customer impact before deciding whether a retrain was worth it.

“we fixed the problem that we were just trying to solve, but then we caused regressions in other things.”

▶ watch at 07:16

“this whole process took about a week to gather the data, label everything, go through the fine-tuning process, and iterate, and then deploy.”

▶ watch at 07:47
04The rebuild: skills instead of retraining

The team migrated the workflow-based, fine-tuned system to a series of skills, tools, and resources an agentic framework could load, inspired by noticing they never needed to change models for Claude Code coding tasks, only the context. This also removed the earlier assumption that fine-tuning bought them freedom to switch model providers, since in practice provider- and version-specific training data made switching too costly.

“within providers, there's nuance between one model version to another, and so that changes the the training data that you need to provide it.”

▶ watch at 09:49
05Verdict: costs, accuracy, and when fine-tuning still makes sense

After the rebuild, the fix cycle dropped from about a week to under an hour, and accuracy beat the fine-tuned model even though per-message API costs rose from using better models; total cost still fell because far less time went into keeping fine-tuning running. Dan's guidance: fine-tune only when you literally cannot call a frontier model, and even then the decision still has to outweigh the ongoing tax.

“This whole process from discovering a problem to deploying the fix, we reduced down to less than an hour.”

▶ watch at 12:57

“while it cost us a little more per message, the total cost went down because we were spending far less time trying to keep it up and running”

Commentary (ours)

The comparison is persuasive but comes from a single narrow classification task (six intent buckets); teams with genuinely different latency/cost constraints or offline/privacy requirements may still land on fine-tuning, which Dan flags as one of the legitimate exceptions.
