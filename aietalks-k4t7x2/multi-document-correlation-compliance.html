Blueprint · full operating modelVertical AIEvals & benchmarks

A three-part framework combining entity-correlation graphs, probabilistic risk scoring, and cross-jurisdictional normalization, benchmarked on 3M records with 91% precision and 40% audit-effort reduction.

If you only read one thing

  • A framework combining entity-correlation graphs, probabilistic risk scoring, and cross-jurisdictional normalization is built to catch fraud patterns that only appear when documents are connected, not within any single document.
  • Tested on approximately 3 million financial records across 4 jurisdictions over 5 years, it reported 91% precision and 87% recall (F1 0.89).
  • It reduced false positives by 76% and manual audit effort by approximately 40% versus traditional rule-based, document-level review.

Why this talk is a blueprint, not a take (ours)

Scope: A three-component correlation, risk-scoring, and normalization pipeline for detecting cross-document fraud and compliance risk in enterprise payroll, tax, procurement, and financial records.

A graph-based entity correlation engine that links records across source systems, an adaptive probabilistic risk model that scores the connected data and learns from audit outcomes, and a normalization layer that makes risk scoring consistent across jurisdictions. On the evaluation dataset this reported 91% precision, 87% recall, F1 0.89, a 76% cut in false positives, and about 40% less manual audit effort versus the document-level baseline.

Prerequisites the talk implies:

  • Integration hooks into existing ERP, payroll, procurement, and tax platforms so records can be pulled into the correlation graph
  • Jurisdiction-specific configuration of currency, tax structure, and reporting-standard rules for every regulatory environment covered
  • An audit workflow that captures confirmed-fraud and cleared-case outcomes, since the risk model is recalibrated from that feedback
  • Enough historical volume to build reliable entity links and risk baselines (the evaluation used 3 million records over 5 years)

What the talk does not say:

  • No baseline methodology is described for the rule-based comparator used to claim the 76% false-positive reduction
  • No team size, build timeline, compute cost, or ongoing maintenance cost for running the graph and risk model
  • No description of how thresholds are set or audited to avoid the probabilistic model drifting toward false negatives
  • No named client, industry vertical, or independent replication of the reported numbers

If you were to replicate one thing first: Stand up the entity-correlation graph over two already-integrated systems (for example payroll and procurement) before adding jurisdiction normalization or the risk model, so cross-document linking can be validated on its own.

The problem: fraud hides between documents, not within them

  • Traditional compliance systems validate each document independently -- a payroll register against payroll rules, an invoice against procurement policy, a tax filing against tax regulation -- and consider a transaction compliant if each document passes on its own.
  • The talk argues sophisticated fraud rarely shows up as an error in a single document; it emerges only when records are cross-referenced, which rule-based and document-level NLP systems aren't built to do.
Where fraud actually shows up
Document-by-document checks
  • Payroll register checked against payroll rules
  • Invoice checked against procurement policy
  • Tax filing checked against tax regulation
  • Compliant if each document passes alone
Cross-document fraud
  • Emerges only when records are cross-referenced
  • Rule-based and document-level NLP can't see it
The talk states document-level systems can't see relationships across documents; splitting that contrast into two columns is our layout (ours).

“Traditional rule-based and document-level NLP system are designed to validate individual records, but they are not built to understand relationship across the documents”
