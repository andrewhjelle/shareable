Why Off-the-Shelf AI Doesn't Understand Money

SpeakerUdi Menkes · Intuit Watchyoutu.be/Owb8g3yDyzo · 19 min SourceRecording of this talk at AI Engineer (2026), published on YouTube by the conference. This talk gives concrete evidence for whether a frontier LLM with full business context is safe to use for financial recommendations, versus a model trained on verified outcome data

If you only read one thing

  • Frontier LLMs gave confidently wrong, risky financial advice on real Intuit business cases (grow via a second property while cash-negative; raise prices 15-20% on a customer worth 70% of revenue).
  • Across a 100,000-business study, over half of frontier-model advice reduced to 'acquire new customers' or 'raise revenue,' and a separate Princeton study found frontier models drove simulated companies bankrupt, losing to a simple rules-based system.
  • Intuit's fix: train a midsize model on millions of real state-action-outcome trajectories from its own financial data, which then outperformed frontier models on the same task.

Risky advice from frontier models

  • Menkes shows two real examples from an Intuit study of small businesses: a cash-negative new landlord whom a frontier model told to acquire a second rental property, and an egg supplier with one vendor covering 80% of cost and one customer covering 70% of revenue, whom a frontier model told to raise prices 15-20%.

02The fluent bluff
Slide at 07:40 · click any slide to enlarge

The fluent bluff

  • Menkes names this pattern the fluent bluff: a generic, fluent, confident answer that frontier LLMs give about money because they learned from blogs, books, and advice columns about what people wrote, not from what actually happened to real businesses.

“The fluent bluff is a generic fluent and confident answer that frontier LLMs can give you around money because of what they learned on the internet, blogs, books, advice columns, what people wrote about money, but not based on what actually happened.”

03How often generic advice fails at scale
Slide at 11:10 · click any slide to enlarge · 1 more from this section below

How often generic advice fails at scale

  • In Intuit's own research across 100,000 businesses, 40% of the time the essence of frontier-model advice was to acquire a new customer, and another 14% was to increase revenue from the product, meaning more than half of all advice collapsed into those two moves.
  • A separately cited Princeton study gave models a harness, tools, and $1 million each to run a simulated company for up to 500 days; most models drove the company bankrupt before reaching that mark, and a simple rules-based baseline outperformed nearly all of them.

“40% of the time the essence of the advice that the frontier models were giving was acquire a new customer which which is you know everyone would wish they could do that and 14 more additional percent were increase basically the revenue from your product.”

said at 05:51

“Each model got a million dollars to start with and guess what happened? Most of the models drove the company bankrupt and it didn't even take 500 days.”

said at 06:54

“they also ran a simple rules-based system and that rules-based system outbeat almost all of the models.”

said at 07:26
04Grounding in verified outcomes wins
Slide at 15:40 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

Grounding in verified outcomes wins

  • Menkes recommends teams look in their own data for entities with comparable situations and verifiable outcomes, then ground AI in that.
  • Intuit did this by building millions of state-action-outcome vectors from QuickBooks, TurboTax, Credit Karma, and Mailchimp data and training on them; the resulting midsize model, though cheaper than frontier models, outperformed them on financial recommendations because of that outcome grounding rather than model size.

“find where you can see a lot of different situations across entities that you have in your data. In our case, it's businesses what they did and verify the outcomes and how things turned out.”

said at 12:35

“we were able with a midsize cheaper model to outperform the frontier models because of the grounding that I just showed you.”

said at 15:09
