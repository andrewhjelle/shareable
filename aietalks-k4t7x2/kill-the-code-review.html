AI reviewing AI, nobody reading

  • Teams already run AI code review tools in the PR UI, but the human in the loop is often just skimming and merging once the AI signs off.
  • Jain calls this a misconfiguration: when AI reviews and nobody reads, the process has been set up wrong.

“when AI reviews and nobody reads, we have configured the wrong thing”

03Why spec-driven development falls short

Why spec-driven development falls short

  • Spec-first workflows (write the spec, hand it to an agent, verify the output) repeat the 1970s waterfall model's flaw: no feedback loop.
  • Issues surface only during implementation, the spec never gets updated, and because LLMs aren't deterministic, the code still diverges from what was specified.

“spectrum development is a great uh methodology, but it falls short in day-to-day software development.”

said at 05:12
04Building an AI slop registry
Slide at 01:10 · click any slide to enlarge

Building an AI slop registry

  • Recurring review comments should be codified into a registry that automatically flags the same issue in future PRs instead of requiring a human to write the comment again.
  • Jain's homework for the audience: mine your last 1,000 review comments and build a registry from the ones that repeat.

“Every recurring comment is now a guardrail that you don't have to review again.”

said at 07:49

“Go home and mine your last 1,000 review comments and build out a AI slot register for the things which are repeatable.”

said at 14:12
05New review surface: intent, not diff
Slide at 15:30 · click any slide to enlarge · 3 more from this section below

New review surface: intent, not diff

  • In the system Jain's team is building, session interactions with the coding agent become acceptance criteria, which combine with the slop registry to generate a test plan; a verification system runs that plan and produces evidence.
  • Reviewers then evaluate that evidence and the architectural decisions, not the line-by-line diff.

“now reviewers are reviewing the intent, not the diff.”

said at 13:08

Commentary (ours)

The shift from diff-review to intent-review assumes the captured session (prompts, acceptance criteria) is a reliable record of what was actually decided; whether that holds under real engineering time pressure, versus decisions made verbally or async, is the open question the talk doesn't fully address (ours).
