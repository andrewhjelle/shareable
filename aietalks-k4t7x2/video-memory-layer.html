Video Has No Memory. Here's How We Built One.

01Why treating video as frames or transcript loses the meaning

Le's starting claim is that most video AI systems lack memory in a system sense: they process each query from scratch rather than preserving structure across a corpus. He argues the common mental model, treating video as a stack of images or an attached transcript, is a useful approximation for narrow tasks but discards continuity, since meaning in video comes from the interplay of space, time, modalities, and sequence. Forcing video into a text-token sequence (sampling frames, extracting transcripts, dumping into a prompt) loses the spatiotemporal relationships that define an event.

02The building block: reason over years of footage without reprocessing

Le frames the goal as a system that reasons over years of footage without reprocessing the whole archive on every query, which requires reusable representations built once and retrieved many times rather than recomputed per request.

“a real video system should be able to reason over years of footage without reprocessing the whole archive every time”

▶ watch at 06:45
03The context graph and five principles for building it

TwelveLabs' proposed structure is a context graph: a durable, queryable representation connecting video moments, entities, appearances, relationships, time spans, metadata, and corpus-level context. Le lays out five principles for building it, including ingesting once and reasoning many times, and grounding every claim back to a specific timestamp in the source video so answers can cite where a scene occurred.

04Demoed on 67 World Cup videos and framed as infrastructure, not an app

Le demonstrated the approach, branded Jockey, by ingesting 67 videos from the 2022 World Cup and asking multi-step questions (find near-misses and explain why each wasn't a goal, track a specific player across the corpus), producing structured, cited answers rather than single retrieved clips. He positions the underlying product explicitly as cognition infrastructure with a knowledge layer and harnesses, not an application, editing platform, or compliance product itself.
