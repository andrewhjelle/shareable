04Month by month — in their words

Narrative (ours, hedged) with the evidence it leans on, then three talks per month chosen mechanically: the most-viewed talks whose strongest claim is a prediction or a warning. Quotes are verbatim and time-linked.

The arc (ours)

Across the five months these talks are batched by conference edition on the AI Engineer YouTube channel, so themes track publishing cadence more than the moment of insight. The six-month window starts February 20 but the channel's first in-window uploads land in April, and August is partial through the 21st.

In April the room is still arguing about protocol shape: MCP, identity, and what quality means when software is cheap. May shifts hard into agentic-engineering, with speakers naming harnesses and long-running missions. June refines that same conversation toward efficiency and composition. July, at 172 talks the largest batch, folds evals, governance, and shrinking human oversight into the agent story.

August's partial slice turns to what sits underneath: local models, continual learning, agent-generated data, and computer-use for the web.

Aug 2026

71 talks · 595 claims · top themes: Agentic engineering (37), Evals & benchmarks (19), Infra & serving (17)

August: local models, continual learning, and agents that use the web

August's 71 talks run through the 21st. On a panel with Vincent Weisser of Prime Intellect and Lucas Atkins of Arcee AI, Chris Alexiuk calls the coming year the "most consequential year" for open intelligence and predicts most day-to-day AI tasks will not need an API call within a year.

Yu Su of NeoCognition argues that a sufficiently strong continual learning algorithm could turn a merely reasonable level of intelligence into unbounded expertise.

Vivek Trivedy of LangChain describes agent-generated data soon eclipsing all human-produced data across shrinking time windows from yearly down to daily, and argues continual learning for agents has to run across three axes together: training data from actions, harness updates, and memory updates. Dhruv Batra of Yutori says computer-use agents, not APIs, will make the long tail of the web machine-accessible.

Extrapolation (ours): the August slice reads less like debate about how to build agents and more like a preview of what those agents will need next.

Jul 2026

172 talks · 1443 claims · top themes: Agentic engineering (116), Evals & benchmarks (56), Coding agents (42)

July: the largest batch, evals and oversight enter the agent story

July delivers 172 talks, 116 tagged agentic-engineering and 56 tagged evals-benchmarks. Geoffrey Litt of Notion observes that the human role in correctness-checking agent output is decreasing as agents get better at self-verification. Addy Osmani of Google Chrome frames the same shift as automation moving the floor of engineering work without reducing the total need for engineers.

Theo Browne, of t3dotgg, retracts his earlier wall claim and says the models are getting better faster than engineers are. Aaron Stanley of dbt Labs brings governance in, noting CISOs handling high-risk AI will soon owe the EU AI Act meaningful evidence of human oversight, and a sandbox diagram will not do.

Emil Eifrem of Neo4j sketches a substrate where an agent wakes up "slightly smarter" and learning is shared across agents, not siloed to one. Extrapolation (ours): July is where the agent story stops being a demo genre and absorbs eval, oversight, and cross-agent memory as first-class concerns.

Jun 2026

72 talks · 610 claims · top themes: Agentic engineering (43), Infra & serving (25), Evals & benchmarks (21)

June: efficiency, composition, and a shift in generative UI

June's 72 talks turn from building agents to spending them well. Vincent Koc of OpenClaw frames the calendar year as a turn from token maxing to token efficiency. Sunil Pai and Matt Carey of Cloudflare show one shape of that efficiency: a code mode for MCP that reaches 2,600 Cloudflare API endpoints in about 1,000 tokens.

Justin Schroeder of Standard Agents predicts a sharp uptick in domain-specific agent building through the rest of the year, with multi-agent orchestration following in 2027. Ruben Casas of Postman argues generative UI will become collaborative and personalized rather than a fixed component set.

Raymond Weitekamp of OpenProse names RLMs, which operate symbolically on an externalized prompt, as the next paradigm of test-time compute. Brendan O'Donoghue of Google DeepMind notes no hosted large model has yet landed text diffusion, blocked by throughput cost. Extrapolation (ours): June is where speakers start asking what to spend agentic capacity on, not just how to build more of it.

May 2026

94 talks · 823 claims · top themes: Agentic engineering (63), Evals & benchmarks (26), Coding agents (26)

May: agent-building takes over, and harnesses get their name

May's 94 talks are where agentic-engineering, at 63 of them, becomes the dominant theme and a named practice. Tejas Kumar of IBM calls 2026 "the year of harnesses" and frames 2027 as the year those harnesses get generated on the fly for each task. Luke Alvoeiro of Factory reports a longest mission of 16 days with the team believing 30 is reachable, treating an agent's unit of work in weeks rather than minutes.

Philipp Schmid of Google DeepMind pushes the implication further: agent software is disposable, expected to be rebuilt many times as better models arrive. Hugo Santos and Madison Faulkner of Namespace and NEA describe an inner loop where external validation is no longer human, other agents review the changes, and one plan can be attempted through multiple concurrent commits.

Extrapolation (ours): May is where the vocabulary of long-running, agent-checked work gets settled.

Apr 2026

50 talks · 449 claims · top themes: Agentic engineering (27), Coding agents (16), Context engineering (15)

April: agent protocols, identity, and quality as the new differentiator

April's 50 talks include the window's earliest MCP and identity work. David Soria Parra of Anthropic previews two MCP moves: shipping skills over MCP so a large server can bundle usage knowledge with its tools, and a cross-app access pattern that lets a single login through a company identity provider carry across MCP servers.

Karan Sampath, also of Anthropic, frames agents as forcing "newer and novel definitions of identity" for enterprises. Around that plumbing conversation, Malte Ubl of Vercel argues the cheaper software gets to make, the more of it teams will want, so demand for engineers rises rather than falls, and Maggie Appleton of GitHub adds that quality becomes the differentiator when software is cheap.

Raia Hadsell of Google DeepMind gestures at a further-out use case for generative world models as places people enter to learn. Extrapolation (ours): April is spent negotiating the surface, not the interior, of an agentic stack.
