Switchboard Design Brief The requirements, the proposed layout, and what the field already figured out · annotated for the Claude Design pass · 2026-08-25 · companion to projects/switchboard-spec.md (full written spec) and the prior-art deep dive (requirements + real screenshots)

0. North star · four principles that settle every tie

Take load off the userAuto by default. The router, the budget math, the model choice, the scheduling: all decided for you, all explainable on demand. You never do quota arithmetic or pick a model unless you want to.
Simple and fastOne calm chat surface. Local answers stream in about a second. Chrome stays thin; anything secondary lives in the drawer, closed.
Multiple windowsSplit view: two chats side by side in one window (v0), pop-out windows later. Different threads, different models, same rules.
Quiet drawer, loud when it mattersDetails (usage, models, queue) sit in a side drawer that stays shut. Approaching capacity (budget, VRAM, context) auto-opens it with the one number that matters.

1. The proposed layout · annotated

1 Mode toggle: AUTO is default and 95% of use; the other two are escape hatches.
2 Mini budget bar with pace marker lives in the strip; click = drawer usage panel.
3 Badge row ABOVE every answer: model · routing reason · latency · left machine or not. The whole trust story in one line (nobody in the field ships this).
4 Fable answers get a distinct cool tint + quota cost. Cloud is always recognizable at a glance.
5 Composer chips: model override and Private toggle. Private disables the cloud lane, visibly.
6 Context meter in the composer (LibreChat lesson: capacity lives in the chat, not settings).
7 Drawer, usage panel: cc-budget grammar (pace marker, arrow, color ladder, reset countdown).
8 Queue entries name their trigger condition (Deadline pattern): "waiting for idle GPU," never bare "pending."
9 Split control: second chat pane side by side (Msty pattern, bounded at two).
10 Private chats carry a lock glyph + model dot in the sidebar; hover shows a preview card (Open WebUI).

2. The drawer's three states · quiet until it matters

The escalation ladder is the design: closed rail → user-opened panels → auto-opened alert with exactly one number and one action. Never a toast storm, never a nag while calm.

3. Prior art · what to copy, what to avoid

COPY LM Studio + Jan: fit-before-download

LM Studio's badge answers "will it run?" at decision time; Jan adds tiers + a Recommended tag. Ours adds the third state the field lacks: "fits when idle" (overnight lane).

COPY cc-budget: the pacing bar

The one genuinely proven Claude-quota visualization. Ours goes further: the same signal FEEDS the router (adaptive allowance with rollover), which no product surveyed does.

COPY Msty: split view · COPY LibreChat: artifacts + context gauge

Msty's window split (one prompt can fan to both panes; every pane labeled) is the "multiple windows" requirement, bounded at two. LibreChat proves the preview pane can carry React/HTML/SVG/Mermaid with fullscreen + export, and that a live context/cost gauge belongs inside the conversation.

AVOID ChatGPT auto-router: hidden routing · AVOID LibreChat: shared right panel

GPT-5's router hid which model answered; it became a trust crisis within days and OpenAI walked it back. Routing may be automatic, its identity may not be invisible: that is the whole case for the badge row. And LibreChat's parameters/artifacts panel collision (hide one, lose both) becomes our hard rule: independent surfaces, independent close controls.

COPY AnythingLLM: isolation as a scope property

Workspace-level model binding (this room is local-only, that one may use cloud) maps to Private chats: privacy is a property of the chat, enforced by the router, not a per-message checkbox you can forget.

COPY OpenRouter: per-request drill-down · Deadline/W&B: named triggers

OpenRouter's dashboard lets any spend number open the individual requests behind it (cost breakdown, provider, fallback). Our usage page inherits that: every day-bar opens its turns. Queue items name their trigger ("waiting for idle GPU") and announce what starts when idle arrives.
