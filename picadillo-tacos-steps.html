            The timeline

              Picadillo filling

              1–1.25 lb ground beef (80/20) · 1 medium white onion · 4 garlic cloves · 2 medium yukon gold potatoes · 1 medium carrot · 2 tbsp tomato paste · 1 (14 oz) can diced tomatoes (or 2 romas) · ½ cup water or beef broth · small handful raisins (optional) · 1 bay leaf
              1. Small dice: 2 yukon golds (~1/3-inch cubes), 1 carrot, 1 white onion. Mince 4 garlic cloves.
              2. Brown 1–1.25 lb ground beef in a wide skillet over medium-high, breaking it up fine. 6–8 min. Drain excess fat, leaving ~1 tbsp behind.
              3. Add the diced onion. Cook 4–5 min until translucent. Add garlic, stir 30 sec.
              4. Add spices: 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp Mexican oregano, pinch (1/8 tsp) cinnamon, 1 bay leaf, 1 tsp ancho chili powder, ½ tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp black pepper. Stir 30 sec to bloom.
              5. Stir in 2 tbsp tomato paste. Cook 1 min until it darkens a shade.
              6. Add the can of diced tomatoes + ½ cup water or beef broth. Stir.
              7. Stir in diced potato + carrot. (Raisins now if using.)
              8. Cover, reduce to a low simmer, cook 15–20 min. Stir once at 10 min. Done when a paring knife slides through the potato with no resistance and the liquid is mostly reduced to a thick sauce.
              9. Fish out the bay leaf. Salt to taste (start 1 tsp, more if needed).

              Table setup

              • Warm corn or flour tortillas (dry skillet 30 sec/side, or wrapped stack in the oven at 300°F for 10 min)
              • Diced avocado or sliced
              • Chopped white onion + cilantro
              • Lime wedges
              • Queso fresco or cotija, crumbled
              • Mexican crema (or sour cream thinned with lime juice)

              Heat at the table (adults only)

              • Chipotles in adobo — mince a spoonful into your own portion
              • Hot sauce (Cholula / Valentina / Tapatío)
              • Sliced fresh serrano or pickled jalapeños

              Kid mild

              The base pot is mild on purpose. Ancho + smoked paprika give the "seasoned Mexican meat" flavor with near-zero heat. Any real heat goes on adult tacos at the table, not in the pot.

              If Zada is picky about visible veg: the potato dice hides in the sauce; the carrot dice can be finer or skipped.
