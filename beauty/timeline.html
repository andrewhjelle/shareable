04Month by month — in their words

Narrative (ours, hedged) with the evidence it leans on, then three talks per month chosen mechanically: the most-viewed talks whose strongest claim is a prediction or a warning. Quotes are verbatim and time-linked.

The arc (ours)

This is a 31-talk first pass (36 candidates, 5 had no captions). Months with 1-4 talks are anecdotes, not trends; the clusters of May and August are earnings seasons. The through-line: small and challenger brands say the paths into large retailers are narrowing, while big houses look for growth in new geographies, new formats, and new categories.

Value and challenger dynamics dominate the mainstream voices; older-brand loyalists and one regulator start pressing back on influencer skincare aimed at younger buyers. AI shows up mostly as a discovery-layer conversation, not a product one.

Tentative (ours): the corpus rhymes with a two-track year, challenger acceleration on top, legacy repositioning underneath, with the shelf itself becoming a smaller share of the story.

Aug 2026

7 talks · 63 claims · top themes: Earnings & financials (6), Prestige & luxury (3), Specialty retail (2)

Earnings season two: e.l.f. raises, Estée diversifies, Sally narrows

Seven talks, mostly earnings. e.l.f. Beauty raised its fiscal 27 net sales growth guidance to 18 to 20%, up from 12 to 14% previously, and said management believes Rhode could become the fastest beauty brand to reach $1 billion in net sales. Estée Lauder guided fiscal 27 organic net sales growth to a range of 3 to 5%, and raised its operating margin outlook to 12.7 to 13.5%.

The Estée Lauder CEO signaled deeper expansion into prestige fragrance beyond the existing luxury portfolio. Sally Beauty narrowed fiscal 2026 consolidated net sales guidance to $3.725 to $3.733 billion, including about 30 basis points of favorable currency impact. Tentative (ours): the print made the challenger versus legacy gap visible in guidance, and Estée's category pivot is the follow-up story to watch.

Jul 2026

1 talks · 6 claims · top themes: Pricing, promo & loyalty (1), Consumer trends (1), Haircare (1)

One student case study, not a trend

Only one talk landed in July, a student ECO162 case study on L'Oreal shampoo pricing, so treat this month as context. The project described L'Oreal's founder as a French chemist who developed the first safe hair-color formula, and argued that higher consumer income lifts spending on premium shampoo.

It also warned that during periods of inflation, some price-sensitive shoppers trade down from L'Oreal to more affordable brands to reduce spending. Tentative (ours): the trade-down concern echoes what earnings-side voices have flagged elsewhere in the corpus, and is worth revisiting when the next earnings window brings direct company voices back in.

Jun 2026

3 talks · 22 claims · top themes: DTC & e-commerce (2), Creators & influencers (1), Social commerce & TikTok (1)

AI discovery, K-beauty then and now, guardrails on kid skincare

Three talks. At the CEW Future Beauty Summit hosted at YouTube, Google framed generative engine optimization as the next discovery layer, citing an eMarketer article that ranks Google/YouTube as the top-referenced platform for GEO and urging brands to work with authentic creators to build LLM credibility.

Google's internal analysis reportedly showed a direct correlation between product design, scent, and texture and consumer loyalty. Maureen Masary of Laura Geller and AS Beauty said leaders should openly normalize visible AI use in day-to-day work.

Beauty & Seoul founder Maree recalled that in 2016, major online beauty retailers were not interested in curating Korean skincare, and today flags PR sampling of actives-adjacent products reaching preteens. Tentative (ours): the discovery layer is moving from search-plus-shelf toward LLM-plus-creator, while skincare guardrails for younger buyers are tightening.

13 talks · 112 claims · top themes: Earnings & financials (5), Skincare & dermo (4), Consumer trends (4)

Earnings season opens, dupes rise, Rhode expands into Europe

Thirteen talks made May the first earnings cluster. e.l.f. Beauty guided fiscal 2027 net sales growth of approximately 12 to 14%, with Q1 organic net sales down high single digits against a busy prior shipping period. Sally Beauty Holdings tightened its fiscal 2026 consolidated net sales guidance to $3.725 to $3.750 billion. On the trend side, e.l.f.

said Rhode is rolling out with Sephora across 19 European countries this fall. At Google's Beauty Futures keynote, Natasha Kizzie cited a forecast that dupes will grow from 4.5% of the market in 2020 to 8.9% by 2030. A skincare influencer panel flagged a new certification requirement, said to originate in China, for professional-style advice.

Tentative (ours): the earnings prints look measured, but the trend voices around them describe a category getting reshaped from outside the store.

Where growth might come from, and where it might not

Four talks bracketed the growth question. At the NEW YOU Beauty Awards panel, Peterson said the industry has entered what she and other leaders call "agentic commerce." Panelist Baoza predicted continued store consolidation in luxury retail and less shelf space for brands.

L'Oreal India CEO Jacques Lebel said the addressable Indian consumer base is projected to reach 450 million by 2030, and framed quick commerce as an Indian invention likely to grow to the size of some of L'Oreal's biggest divisions. Rare Beauty's Elyse Cohen said the fund's nonprofit partners are seeing budgets cut and lack marketing capacity. Yarborough flagged persistent under-investment in R&D for women.

Tentative (ours): the panel voices pointed outside U.S. specialty retail for the next unit of growth.

Mar 2026

3 talks · 24 claims · top themes: Brand building (3), Creators & influencers (2), Consumer trends (1)

Small brands say the doors into big retail are narrowing

Three talks. Rodial founder Maria Hatzistefanis said she lost an Australian retail account within 18 months because her small team could not produce the multi-year business plans the buyer required. On a USC alumni panel, Song called Sephora "notorious for being super cutthroat" on margins for small brands, and said standing out on TikTok Shop has grown very difficult.

At the Forest keynote, Alex Belisle-Springer described feeling structurally uncomfortable in some industry spaces and called on brands to help more people feel they belong. Hatzistefanis also recounted that in 1999, no investor would fund her because she had just been fired from banking and had no beauty experience.

Tentative (ours): early-stage founders framed the retailer relationship as the pinch point, not product.
