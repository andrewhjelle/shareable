01Q3 headline results and raised outlook

Q3 headline results and raised outlook

  • CEO Stephane de La Faverie reported organic sales growth of 2% and 40% EPS growth for the quarter, and the company raised its fiscal 2026 outlook to roughly 3% organic sales growth and 10.7-11% operating margin.
  • Fragrance was called out as the standout category, growing double-digit organically fiscal year-to-date and outperforming the industry, while skin care grew low single digits and hair care stabilized.

02Restructuring: door exits and cost cuts

Restructuring: door exits and cost cuts

  • The company described expanding its restructuring program (PRGP) to include exiting select unproductive doors in department stores and freestanding stores as it shifts toward online channels, a move CFO Akhil Sibal said will affect beauty advisor headcount globally.
  • Total restructuring and other charges are now guided to $1.5-1.7 billion before taxes, up from $1.1 billion in cumulative charges recorded through March 31.

“This include the expansion of the position impacted which largely reflects the anticipated exit of select unproductive doors in department stores and freestanding stores stores channel as we increasingly tap into the high growth potential of online.”

03Two skincare M&A bets

Two skincare M&A bets

  • Estée Lauder is acquiring the remaining shares of Forest Essentials, which the company called the number one prestige skin care brand in India, building on an existing minority stake.
  • Separately, in April the company made a minority investment in 111 Skin, described as positioned for pre- and post-procedure demand, as part of a stated minority-investment strategy that also included Deciem.

“Before I close, I'm thrilled to welcome to the Estée Lauder Companies portfolio the number one prestige skin care brand in India, Forest Essentials.”

04Uneven regional picture

Uneven regional picture

  • North America sales declined low single digits, which management attributed to continued brick-and-mortar pressure including retailer bankruptcies and shop-in-shop closures.
  • Continental Europe's consumer sentiment was described as the most affected in the world outside the Middle East conflict region.
  • By contrast, Hainan travel retail sales grew over 30% in the quarter.

Commentary (ours)

(ours) The door-exit language (unproductive doors, beauty advisor impact) paired with the North American brick-and-mortar decline suggests the physical retail contraction is not just a response to weak traffic but a deliberate reallocation toward the channels management repeatedly named as high-growth (online, Amazon, TikTok Shop, specialty multi); the framing puts the burden of proof on any physical retail partner to demonstrate productivity per door going forward.
