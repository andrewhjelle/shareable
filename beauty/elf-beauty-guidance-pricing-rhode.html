If you only read one thing

01Guidance and the Q1 dip

Guidance and the Q1 dip

  • Management guided fiscal 2027 total net sales up 12-14%, with organic net sales up 4-5%, while flagging a high-single-digit organic decline in fiscal Q1.
  • That Q1 decline is attributed to cycling last year's pull-forward of retailer orders ahead of a July 1 ERP system switch, plus a slower start to spring innovation, rather than a change in underlying demand.

“You're guiding to net sales up 12 to 14% for the year with organic net sales up 4 to 5%”

02Testing price cuts to win back units

Testing price cuts to win back units

  • e.l.f. is testing price cuts on a subset of its range after cutting its skin tints from $18 to $14 and seeing almost a 40% unit lift, even higher in more recent weeks.
  • Management frames this as reinforcing e.l.f.'s value position: its average unit retail is about $7 versus roughly $10 for other mass brands and over $20 for prestige.

“we did a test where we took our skin tints from $18 to $14 and we saw almost a 40% lift”

“our average unit retails are closer to $7 versus about $10 for everyone else and well over $20 for prestige”

said at 07:16
03Rhode's scale and international rollout

Rhode's scale and international rollout

  • Rhode's fiscal 2026 annualized net sales were reported at about $390 million, achieved in under 20% of Sephora's global store footprint.
  • The brand is rolling out with Sephora across 19 European countries this fall, following its North American, UK, and Australia/New Zealand launches.

04Moving manufacturing outside China

Moving manufacturing outside China

  • e.l.f. says production outside China has grown from roughly 1% a few years ago to over 45% today, driven by category expansion (Rhode's production is centered in Italy and South Korea, Naturium mainly in the US) and by moving strategic suppliers into new facilities, including a recent one in Thailand.

“if you back up a few years ago, almost 99% of our production was done in China”

said at 31:02
05Capital priorities: buybacks over big deals

Capital priorities: buybacks over big deals

  • e.l.f. repurchased another $50 million of stock this week against a roughly $500 million authorization, citing a disconnect between the stock price and business fundamentals.
  • On M&A, management says it would only pursue another deal that clears an 'exceptionally high' bar similar to Naturium or Rhode, ruling out a near-term transformative acquisition.
