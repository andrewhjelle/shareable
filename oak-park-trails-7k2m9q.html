Getting outside from Oak Park

Bike trails, forest preserves and day trips, all measured from an Oak Park front porch.

Short answer: not locked in at all

Oak Park's west edge is a continuous strip of Cook County forest preserve along the Des Plaines River, about a mile from the middle of town. From there, three long trails fan out: the Illinois Prairie Path runs 61 miles west through the suburbs to the Fox River, the Des Plaines River Trail runs 28 miles north through the woods, and the Salt Creek Trail runs south to Brookfield Zoo. East, the 606 and the Lakefront Trail are a bike ride or a 25-minute train hop away.

The village itself is flat, gridded, and has marked bike routes on the quiet streets, so kids ride to school and to the pool. Metra and the CTA both take bikes, which makes one-way rides easy. Big-landscape nature is a 90-minute drive (Starved Rock, Indiana Dunes), but the daily and weekend version is genuinely close.

The honest trade: it is less nature than a mountain or lake town. What you get instead is downtown Chicago 20 minutes away by train: the Art Institute, Millennium Park's free summer concerts and Jazz Fest, Maggie Daley Park, the museum campus, all without a car.

Out the door and big rides west

Everything here starts from the front porch. The first is the daily fix; the rest are half-day rides.

Out the door

Thatcher Woods and the Des Plaines River corridor

1 to 2 miles west. A 10-minute ride.

Oak Park's western neighbor, River Forest, ends at a continuous band of Cook County forest preserve along the Des Plaines River. Oak woods, floodplain forest, a pond the kids can fish, dirt trails, and the Trailside Museum (free, live native animals, a good rainy-day stop). This is the everyday nature fix, not a day trip.

  • Unpaved walking trails through Thatcher Woods and GAR Woods
  • Trailside Museum of Natural History at 738 Thatcher Ave, free, open most days
  • Connects north into the Des Plaines River Trail and south to the Prairie Path trailhead

Big ride west

Illinois Prairie Path

Trailhead at 1st Ave in Maywood, about 3 miles from central Oak Park.

This is the answer to 'is there a trail all the way out to the western suburbs.' Yes. The Prairie Path is 61 miles of former rail line, crushed limestone, nearly flat, running west through Elmhurst, Villa Park, Lombard, Glen Ellyn and Wheaton, where it forks to Elgin, Geneva, Batavia and Aurora. It was the first rail-to-trail conversion in the country. You can ride 10 miles to Elmhurst for lunch and come back, or go 40 miles to the Fox River and take the Metra home with the bikes.

  • 61 miles total, flat, mostly off-street, parks and ice cream stops along it
  • Kid-ready: the stretch to Elmhurst has only a handful of road crossings
  • Links to the Great Western Trail and the Fox River Trail at the far end

Big ride north

Des Plaines River Trail

Southern access at North Ave and 1st Ave, about 4 miles from Oak Park.

28 miles of Cook County trail running north along the river through more than a dozen forest preserves, then another 30 or so miles in Lake County. Mostly crushed gravel and dirt, so a hybrid or gravel bike beats a road bike. This one feels like the woods, not a bike highway: deer, herons, long stretches with no traffic noise.

  • 28.4 miles in Cook County, paved and unpaved mix
  • Best for older kids on a hybrid; some muddy stretches after rain
  • Continues into the Lake County section for a full-day ride

Zoo by bike

Salt Creek Trail to Brookfield Zoo

Brookfield Zoo is about 5 miles south; Bemis Woods another 7 west.

A paved 7-mile trail follows Salt Creek from Brookfield Zoo west through Brezina Woods and Bemis Woods to Western Springs, part of a 25-mile greenway that eventually reaches Busse Woods. Riding to the zoo from Oak Park is a real family outing: bike there, see the animals, ride home.

  • 7 miles paved, gentle, forest preserve almost the whole way
  • Brookfield Zoo has bike racks at the gates
  • Bemis Woods has a sledding hill and picnic groves

Into the city

Oak Park sits on the western edge of Chicago. The city's two signature trails are a bike ride or a short train hop away.

City ride

The 606 (Bloomingdale Trail)

West end at Ridgeway Ave, about 5 miles northeast of Oak Park.

A 2.7-mile elevated rail line turned linear park, 17 feet above Humboldt Park, Logan Square, Bucktown and Wicker Park. Ride Augusta or Division east from Oak Park, climb the ramp at Ridgeway, and you are gliding over the city with no intersections. The east end drops you at Ashland, a few blocks from coffee, pizza and the Blue Line. An extension east toward the Chicago River is planned for 2027.

  • 2.7 miles, no cross traffic, playgrounds and small parks at the ramps
  • Kids love it; it is basically a bike bridge across four neighborhoods
  • Combine with the Blue Line at Damen to get home without riding back

City ride

Chicago Lakefront Trail

About 9 miles east by bike, or 25 minutes on the Green Line with bikes aboard.

18.5 miles along Lake Michigan from the South Shore to Edgewater, now with separated bike and pedestrian lanes on most of it. Beaches, harbors, the museum campus, Lincoln Park Zoo (free). From Oak Park you ride straight east on Washington or Lake, or put the bikes on the Green Line and start at the lake.

  • 18.5 miles, separated bike lane, beaches every couple of miles
  • CTA trains allow bikes outside rush hour; the Green Line runs through Oak Park
  • Lincoln Park Zoo and North Avenue Beach are the kid magnets

Downtown in 20 minutes

The other half of the trade. Less raw nature than a mountain town, but the Art Institute, Millennium Park and the best playground in the Midwest are a short train ride, no car needed.

20 to 25 minutes

Green Line and Metra to the Loop

Four CTA Green Line stops and two Metra stations inside the village.

This is the part that makes Oak Park work: downtown Chicago is a 20-minute train ride, no parking, kids ride free under 7 and cheap after. The Green Line runs every 10 minutes and drops you at Millennium Park; Metra UP-West is faster and quieter and lands at Ogilvie. Both take bikes. The Blue Line at the north edge of town runs to O'Hare.

  • Green Line: Harlem, Oak Park, Ridgeland, Austin stops, straight into the Loop
  • Metra UP-West: Oak Park and River Forest stations, about 20 minutes to Ogilvie
  • Blue Line at Harlem or Austin goes the other way to O'Hare in 25 minutes

Free, all summer

Millennium Park and Jazz Fest

One block from the Green Line's Washington/Wabash stop.

The Bean, Crown Fountain (kids run through it in summer), the Lurie Garden, and the Pritzker Pavilion, which hosts free concerts most summer nights. The Chicago Jazz Festival takes over the park on Labor Day weekend; Blues Fest in June, Gospel Fest, the Grant Park Music Festival's free classical concerts all summer. Bring a blanket and dinner.

  • Free concerts at the Pritzker Pavilion, June through August
  • Crown Fountain and the Bean are the two things every visiting kid asks to go back to
  • Jazz Fest is Labor Day weekend every year

Best playground in the Midwest

Maggie Daley Park

Across the bridge from Millennium Park, over the road.

A 20-acre park built over a parking garage: the Play Garden with its three-story slides and climbing towers for the 8 to 12 set, a separate toddler area, two rock-climbing walls, mini golf, and a skating ribbon that winds through the trees in winter. It is the answer to 'what do we do with the kids downtown' and it is free.

  • Play Garden is free; climbing walls and ice skating cost a little
  • The Skating Ribbon runs roughly November through early March
  • Walk across the BP Bridge from the Pritzker Pavilion lawn

Rainy days and winter

Art Institute and the museum campus

Art Institute is next to Millennium Park; the Field, Shedd and Adler are a 15-minute walk south.

The Art Institute is one of the great museums of the world, and Illinois residents with kids get in on discounted or free days. Thorne Miniature Rooms and the armor are where kids start; the Impressionists and the Chagall windows are where parents linger. The museum campus adds the Field Museum (Sue the T. rex), the Shedd Aquarium and the Adler Planetarium, plus the Lakefront Trail right out the door.

  • Art Institute: kids under 14 free, Illinois resident free days in winter
  • Field Museum, Shedd, Adler: check each for Illinois free days
  • All of it within a mile of the Green Line, on the Lakefront Trail

Day trips with the bikes on the rack

Thirty to ninety minutes by car. These are the weekend-scale nature trips.

Day trip, 35 min

Waterfall Glen

Darien, about 25 miles southwest. Drive with bikes on the rack.

A 2,500-acre DuPage preserve ringing Argonne National Lab, with almost 11 miles of crushed-limestone trail. The main loop is the best family ride in the region: rolling, wooded, a small waterfall, sawmill ruins, bluffs over the Des Plaines valley. Feels far from the city and is 35 minutes away.

  • Main loop about 9.5 miles, crushed limestone, some real hills
  • Bikes only on designated trails; the loop is designated
  • Bring water; there are few stops on the loop

Day trip, 30 min

Morton Arboretum

Lisle, about 25 miles west.

1,700 acres of trees from around the world, 16 miles of hiking trails, a paved loop road you can bike, and a four-acre Children's Garden that is one of the best in the country. Membership pays for itself in three visits. Fall color here is the family tradition for half the western suburbs.

  • Children's Garden, Maze Garden, big troll sculptures in the woods
  • Bikes allowed on the paved loop roads
  • Winter: sledding hill, snowshoe rental, holiday light walk

Day trip, 35 min

Busse Woods

Elk Grove Village, about 25 miles northwest.

A paved 8-mile loop around a big reservoir inside one of Cook County's largest preserves, with an elk pasture at the north end (yes, real elk). Flat and fully paved, so it is the easiest long ride for a kid on a new bike. Boat rentals in summer.

  • About 8 miles paved, flat, closed to cars
  • Elk herd viewable from the trail
  • Northern end of the Salt Creek greenway

Day trip, 45 min, or ride out

Fox River Trail

Geneva and St. Charles, about 40 miles west. Metra UP-West goes there with bikes.

Roughly 40 miles of trail along the Fox River through Aurora, Batavia, Geneva, St. Charles, Elgin and Algonquin. Old river towns, dams, islands, a lot of ice cream. The Prairie Path connects to it, so the classic big day is: ride the Prairie Path out, Metra home.

  • Bikes ride free on Metra; UP-West stops in Oak Park and Geneva
  • Geneva and St. Charles downtowns are right on the trail
  • Red Oak Nature Center and the Fabyan windmill are stops kids like

Day trip, 75 min

Indiana Dunes

About 55 miles southeast around the bottom of the lake.

A national park with 15 miles of Lake Michigan beach, big dunes to climb, and the Chicago skyline on the horizon. The state park section has the 3 Dune Challenge for kids who need to burn something off. Doable as a long day; better as a night in a Chesterton cabin.

  • West Beach: dune succession trail plus a real swim beach
  • Mount Baldy and the 3 Dune Challenge for climbers
  • South Shore Line train also gets you there from downtown

Day trip, 90 min

Starved Rock

Utica, about 95 miles southwest along I-80.

Eighteen sandstone canyons cut into bluffs over the Illinois River, with seasonal waterfalls and 13 miles of trails and boardwalk. This is the 'real' nature trip, the one people drive to from the whole state. Spring runoff and the days after heavy rain are when the falls run. Matthiessen State Park next door is quieter and just as good.

  • French Canyon and Wildcat Canyon are the short must-dos with kids
  • Lodge with a pool for a one-night stay
  • Eagles on the river in January and February

How it works in practice

  • Bikes on trains. Metra (the UP-West line stops in Oak Park) allows bikes on all trains at no charge; you carry them on yourself. The CTA Green and Blue lines take bikes outside weekday rush hours. This turns every trail into a one-way ride.
  • Bike share. Oak Park tried Divvy from 2016 to 2018 and dropped it for low ridership, so plan on owning bikes. Divvy stations start at the Chicago border a mile east.
  • Getting to the trails. The village has a bike plan with marked neighborhood greenways on quiet streets. Washington and Augusta are the common east-west routes into the city; Thatcher and the Madison bridge get you to the river.
  • Maps that matter. Forest Preserves of Cook County interactive map (70,000 acres, 400 miles of trail), the Prairie Path map, and the OpenStreetMap cycle layer, which shows every trail in the region better than Google does.
  • Honest caveats. The Des Plaines corridor trails flood and get muddy in spring. The Prairie Path's first mile from Maywood is the least scenic bit. Winter shuts most of this down from December to March unless you are into fat bikes and snowshoes, which the Morton Arboretum rents.
