Vent Stack Flashing Rebuild

The confirmed 4th-floor leak source (hose test, wk of 7/6). One repair, ~45 min on the roof, then prove it in the next rain. Companion to the investigation guide.

The materials verdict (updated 7/12 after the mastic question)

The method is the industry-standard "three-course" flashing repair: mastic course → reinforcing fabric embedded → mastic top course. The refinement: gun-grade sealant and brush-grade mastic are different tools, and this repair wants both.

  • Solar Seal #900 (caulk gun) = the gap filler. Injecting deep into the open seam where the flashing pulled off the pipe is exactly what gun sealant is for — precision joint filling. It is NOT the right consistency for saturating fabric over a 5-6" radius; that's fussy, slow, and eats tubes.
  • Henry 289 White Roofing Sealant (quart, brush/trowel grade) = the fabric embedder. This is the mastic in the videos you're watching — and it's literally the designed companion to your fabric: the 296 ElastoTape label and Henry's own instructions specify embedding it "between 2 heavy coats of Henry #289." Elastomeric acrylic, made to wet out fabric, ~$12-20 at the same Home Depot shelf.
  • Skip the BLACK mastic (Henry 208 Wet Patch / asphalt cement) even though videos show it working — asphalt bleeds brown through white acrylic topcoats. Your Phase-4 plan is a white acrylic recoat (Ames Blue Max / Tropi-Cool); the white 289 is formulated to be recoated with acrylic. The black stuff would ghost through your new roof.
  • Your reinforcement is right: Henry 296 ElastoTape (4" × 150 ft spunbonded polyester, in hand). Leave the drywall FibaTape in the drywall stash.
  • Shed water: shape the finished patch as a slight mound so nothing ponds against the pipe.

The failure and the fix — what's happening

Cross-section, before and after. The old coating-flashing pulled away from the pipe, leaving an open seam straight into the plumbing chase. The rebuild is a flexible sealant mound reinforced with buried fabric: 2" up the pipe, 3-4" out onto the roof, edges feathered so water sheds. The fabric (dashed blue) is the skeleton that keeps the sealant from splitting when the pipe expands/contracts.
Why the fabric matters: sealant alone at a pipe base splits within a season or two — the pipe moves with temperature and the sealant takes all the strain. Fabric spreads that strain across the whole patch. Exposed mesh, on the other hand, wicks water and dies in UV — which is why course 2 must fully bury it.
Your material: Henry 296 ElastoTape repair fabric, 4" × 150 ft roll (confirmed 7/12). 100% spunbonded polyester — the right stuff, and 150 ft means you can be generous. Piece 1 (pipe wrap with fingers) cuts straight from the roll: solid top ~2", fingers ~2". Piece 2 is the petal layout shown above since the roll is 4" wide. Ignore the drywall FibaTape — you don't need it anymore.

Step-by-step

Pick the weather windowbefore anything
  • Roof bone-dry — no rain in the last 12-24 hrs. Neither product bonds to damp coating.
  • Because of the 4-hour hold, plan a 3-day dry stretch, not 2 — dry from the Solar Seal bead through 24-48 hrs after the mastic. No rain forecast for 24-48 hrs after — Henry 289 is water-based acrylic; rain before it cures washes it out. 48 hrs of dry is the comfortable window.
  • Morning is best in July — Solar Seal's cutoff is 125°F substrate temp and a sun-soaked roof gets close by afternoon. Cooler surface = less slump, easier spreading.
Gather5 min
Solar Seal tube + caulk gun (gap bead) · Henry 289 White Roofing Sealant, 1 qt (the fabric courses — one Home Depot run) · Henry 296 ElastoTape roll · cheap chip brush or plastic spreader for the 289 · scissors · wire brush · putty knife · rubbing alcohol + rags · nitrile gloves · phone (before/after photos) · small trash bag for the peeled coating flakes.
Strip the failed flashing10 min
  • Pull off every loose, curled flake of old coating around the pipe base by hand — if it lifts with light finger pressure, it goes. Bag it.
  • Stop where the coating is still stuck down hard. Don't peel healthy coating — you're finding the sound edge, not excavating.
  • Wire-brush the exposed pipe metal to knock off surface rust — shiny-ish is good enough, you're not restoring it, just giving sealant something clean to grip.
  • Wire-brush the roof surface in a 4-6" ring around the pipe, including over the sound coating edge you'll overlap onto.
Clean5 min + dry time
  • Brush/blow away all dust and crumbs — debris under the patch = a channel for water.
  • Wipe the pipe and the roof ring with alcohol on a rag. Let it flash off completely (a few minutes). Surface must be clean, dry, dust-free.
Cut the fabric BEFORE opening the sealant5 min
  • Cut Piece 1 (pipe wrap with fingers) + 4-6 petal strips (~8" each) from the Henry ElastoTape roll, per the diagram above. Dry-fit around the pipe now — adjusting fabric with gloves full of sealant is miserable.
Fill the gap (Solar Seal) — then wait 4 hrs / overnight10-15 min
  • Solar Seal first: run a heavy bead directly into the open seam where the flashing pulled away from the pipe — all the way around. Push it INTO the gap with the putty knife; this is the actual hole, fill it. Tool it smooth so no ridge sticks up.
  • Then STOP for 4 hours. Solar Seal is a terpolymer: manufacturer tack-free time is 4 hours (ASTM-C-679) and the label says “paintable after skin forms.” Burying an uncured bead under water-based acrylic risks the bond and seals off its cure. Best practice: run the Solar Seal bead the evening before, then do the whole three-course sandwich the next morning — you get the skin overnight for free and you brush the 289 on a cool roof instead of a hot one.

    Then Course 1 in Henry 289: brush/trowel a heavy coat 2" up the pipe and 3-4" out onto the roof, right over the fresh Solar Seal bead, all the way around. Work in one continuous pass — the fabric goes in while this is wet.
Embed the fabric5-10 min
  • Lay the petal strips first: radiating out from the pipe into the wet sealant, each overlapping its neighbor ~50%, inner ends butted right against the pipe.
  • Then wrap Piece 1 around the pipe: solid half up the pipe, fingers fanned down over the petals. Press every finger flat.
  • Press everything into the corner where pipe meets roof — the fabric must follow the contour. A tented/bridged spot becomes a void that flexes and splits.
  • No bubbles: smooth outward from the pipe like wallpaper.
Course 2 — bury it (Henry 289, immediately)10 min
  • Henry's instructions: apply the top coat of 289 immediately after embedding the fabric — don't let course 1 skin first. Wet-on-wet is how the fabric saturates through.
  • Cover until no fabric pattern is visible anywhere. Exposed fabric wicks water and degrades in UV — this one detail decides whether the repair lasts 2 years or 10.
  • Feather the outer edge to nothing so water sheds over it instead of catching, and shape a slight mound so water runs away from the pipe — no moat.
Same session: wrap the jagged-top stack15 min
Repeat steps 3-8 on the second vent stack (the jagged-rim one from photos 172537/172549) — its lead base flashing looked compromised too. Do NOT cap or seal the pipe opening on either stack — plumbing vents must stay open to the sky; that's their job.
Photograph + cure + provepassive
  • After-photos of both stacks from the same angles as the 7/5 photos.
  • Hands off 24-48 hrs. The 289 top course IS the finished weather surface — no additional coating is needed on the repair. It's UV-stable and designed to sit exposed.
  • Next real rain: check the 4th-floor ceiling. Dry = confirmed fix; log it in the incident table. If you're impatient, a hose test after 48+ hrs of cure is fair game — same test that found it.
  • Whenever the Phase-4 full recoat happens, it rolls straight over this patch — 289 is formulated to take acrylic coating on top. Give the patch a week+ of cure first.

Don't

  • Don't apply to a damp roof — the bond fails invisibly and you find out next storm.
  • Don't leave any mesh/fabric exposed — it wicks water into the patch and UV eats it.
  • Don't glob one thick coat instead of two courses — thick sealant skins on top and stays soft underneath for weeks; it also slumps off the pipe.
  • Don't cap the pipe opening — open is correct for a plumbing vent.
  • Don't peel well-adhered coating looking for a "clean start" — you'll turn a 45-minute patch into a half-roof excavation.
